Klarna wants its next AI assistant to live where you already talk. The payments company announced on November 17 a concierge-style assistant that handles shopping, returns, bookings and everyday admin — not inside a banking app you have to open, but in the WhatsApp and iMessage chats you already check all day.

It is the company's sharpest move yet toward agentic commerce: an assistant that doesn't just find things, but buys them, books them and follows up — while, Klarna insists, keeping the customer in charge. For now it is live with only a small group of early users, and everyone else joins a waitlist.

What the assistant actually does#

Klarna describes the assistant as a personal concierge, and the job list is deliberately mundane. It finds options and helps you choose between them, books anything from a table for two to a babysitter, and keeps track of the things you are waiting on — deliveries, replies, reservations. It drafts email replies for you to approve, files receipts, and flags an upcoming birthday with a gift idea before you remember it yourself.

The shopping part is where the money is. The assistant can search for a product, compare prices and place the order — and, in Klarna's framing, you don't pay until it arrives. Returns go through the same chat: the assistant handles the return instead of leaving you with a label to print and a parcel to post. Klarna says the customer stays firmly in control throughout, though it has not yet shown exactly what the approval steps look like for a purchase — a detail that matters once real money starts moving through a chat thread.

Why chat, not the app#

The most interesting choice is the channel. The assistant lives on WhatsApp and iMessage as well as inside the Klarna app. That is a break from how fintech assistants usually work: locked inside the vendor's own app, where engagement is a metric and every interaction is a chance to upsell.

Meeting users in their own messaging apps is also a bet that the assistant becomes a habit rather than a feature. Klarna's own numbers suggest the habit is already there: shoppers have spent more than $34 billion through the in-app shopping browser since 2020, and the browser lets them shop any website — including stores that don't take Klarna — across 150 million products and a billion merchant listings. The assistant is, in effect, that browser with hands: it doesn't just show you the options, it acts on them.

The same week, Google made its Gemini Tasks agent free in six countries with the power to call businesses and pay on your behalf. Klarna's move is the fintech version of the same race: whoever controls the errand controls the transaction.

The numbers behind the launch#

The announcement landed alongside Klarna's third-quarter results, and the timing is no accident. The quarter was the company's fourth in a row with revenue above $1 billion and positive adjusted operating income — a run of profitability that gives it room to experiment.

Klarna is listed on the New York Stock Exchange, and the assistant is now part of the growth story it tells investors: not just a payments network, but the place where the shopping itself happens.

The ghost of Klarna's last AI push#

This is not Klarna's first attempt to let AI do the talking. In 2024 the company said its AI assistant was doing the work of 700 customer-service agents. By May 2025, chief executive Sebastian Siemiatkowski admitted the cost-driven push had gone too far and quality had dropped — and Klarna started hiring human support staff again. The episode is now a case study in what happens when automation is measured in headcount saved rather than problems solved.

The new assistant is a different proposition — it sells convenience to the customer, not savings to the company — but the test is the same: whether it gets the fiddly, judgment-heavy jobs right often enough that people trust it with the next one. A botched return or a wrong booking costs more than a slow reply.

What to watch#

Which countries get access first. Klarna hasn't said, and the waitlist is the only door in for now — sign in with Klarna to save a spot, and the company notifies you in the app and by email when an invite is ready.

What the approval flow looks like when the assistant spends your money — and what spending limits, if any, apply before it checks out on your behalf.

Whether the “you don't pay until it arrives” promise holds up in practice across merchants, and how disputes are handled when the assistant, not you, placed the order.

Whether usage actually lifts engagement and card volume — the two metrics Klarna's investors will watch after the 111% card-volume jump this quarter. If the assistant becomes the default way its 122 million users shop, the fintech stops being a checkout button and becomes the storefront.

Frequently asked questions

How do I join the waitlist?

Sign in with Klarna to save your spot. Klarna notifies you in the app and by email when an invite is ready. Joining is free, and access is limited for now — the company says it is letting in only a few people at a time.

What can the assistant actually do?

Shop and compare prices, place orders, handle returns, book tables and babysitters, draft email replies for your approval, file receipts, track deliveries and flag birthdays with gift ideas.

Where can I talk to it?

On WhatsApp and iMessage, as well as inside the Klarna app.

Does it cost anything?

Joining the waitlist is free. Klarna has not announced any charge for the assistant itself.

OpenAI opens 50,000 free ChatGPT grants to academic researchers — first come, first served

Applications open today for the next round of ChatGPT for Academic Researchers: 50,000 grants, each covering a researcher and up to two collaborators with a year of ChatGPT Plus, as OpenAI doubles the program's target to 200,000 scientists worldwide.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 23, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: OpenAI opens applications today for 50,000 new grants under ChatGPT for Academic Researchers. Each grant covers the recipient and up to two collaborators with ChatGPT Plus for 12 months — a step down from the pilot's Pro tier, which OpenAI says lets it reach far more teams. Selection is now first-come, first-served instead of a lottery, and the program's overall target doubles to 200,000 researchers.
  • The offer: 50,000 grants open today, November 23; each covers one researcher plus up to two collaborators, so the round can reach up to 150,000 people.
  • The trade-off: new grants carry ChatGPT Plus, not the Pro tier the summer pilot provided. OpenAI says most pilot participants would have stayed within Plus limits anyway.
  • The selection: first-come, first-served replaces the lottery. Researchers already on the waitlist who qualify get activation instructions by email before the round opens.
  • The pilot: launched in July 2026 with a goal of 100,000 researchers; about 20,000 researchers in 33 countries have joined so far.
  • The evidence: an accompanying OpenAI study of more than 1,800 pilot researchers found participants estimated the same work would have taken nearly twice as long without AI — self-reported, not a causal finding.

OpenAI is widening the door to its free ChatGPT program for academic researchers — and changing the terms as it does. Applications for the next round of ChatGPT for Academic Researchers open today, Monday, November 23, with 50,000 new grants available. This time there is no lottery: the grants go to the first eligible applicants who apply.

The expansion, announced on November 16, doubles the program's overall goal, from 100,000 researchers to 200,000 worldwide. Each new grant covers the recipient and up to two collaborators, which puts the round's potential reach at 150,000 researchers. It is the largest single intake since the program began as a pilot in July, when about 20,000 researchers in 33 countries joined.

What opens today#

Grants are awarded in the order applications arrive, to applicants who meet the eligibility requirements — so researchers who have been waiting will want to move quickly once the window opens. OpenAI says people already on its waitlist who qualify will receive an email with activation instructions before applications open to everyone else. The announcement itself gives no clock time for the opening.

According to OpenAI's researcher access guide, the program is aimed at research faculty and postdoctoral researchers, and is available in all regions except China and Hong Kong. Identity and academic eligibility have to be verified, and the guide notes that final eligibility and collaborator rules for this intake are still to be confirmed. API credits and specialized models are not part of the grants. Participants get access to OpenAI's frontier models plus training on using ChatGPT in their research — and, by default, OpenAI says it does not use data shared through the program to train its models.

Plus, not Pro — and why#

The one change that will matter most to applicants is the tier. Researchers admitted during the summer pilot received ChatGPT Pro, OpenAI's top consumer plan. New grants come with ChatGPT Plus instead, for 12 months per the access guide. OpenAI presents the switch as a deliberate trade: after reviewing how pilot participants actually used the service, it concluded most would have stayed within Plus usage limits, so offering the cheaper plan lets the same budget reach far more research teams.

That is a defensible calculation, but it is still a downgrade, and heavy users will feel it: Plus carries lower usage limits than Pro, and the researchers most likely to push against them — the ones running long analyses and repeated agentic workflows — are exactly the power users this program is meant to serve.

What the pilot actually showed#

Alongside the expansion, OpenAI published a study of how the pilot cohort used ChatGPT, titled Science at the Frontier: How Scientists Use ChatGPT. The economics team examined more than 580,000 scientific messages from 1,819 researchers between August 20 and September 22, and surveyed 251 of them in early October. The headline number: surveyed participants estimated that completing the same work at the same quality without AI would have taken about 1.9 times as long. Almost all of those who answered the discovery question — 98% — expected AI to accelerate discovery in their field.

Those numbers deserve their caveats, and OpenAI states them plainly: the productivity figures are self-reported estimates from program participants, not measured outcomes, and the study does not establish that AI caused the gains. The concrete examples are more persuasive than the averages: Shuqi Li, a researcher at Brigham and Women's Hospital and Harvard Medical School, is exploring whether ChatGPT can link lab and patient data to speed the search for tuberculosis drug targets. Arthur Porto, an assistant curator at the Florida Museum of Natural History, and his team are using AI to turn photographs of museum specimens into searchable databases — and built a 3D model of a butterfly whose wings change color as you turn it, just like the real specimen.

The bigger picture#

The grants are one piece of a wider push to put OpenAI's tools in researchers' hands. The same announcement commits $5 million to a new Simons Foundation initiative to help mathematicians examine, understand, and build on results produced by AI — a notable pairing with a month in which AI-assisted mathematics has dominated the news. OpenAI also points researchers toward its other routes: ChatGPT Edu for whole campuses, and GPT-Rosalind, its biology-focused offering, with an academic plan for eligible U.S. labs.

For research offices, the practical question is less whether free Plus access is generous — it is — than which of OpenAI's overlapping programs fits a given team, and whether the Plus limits hold up under real workloads. The other question is speed: how fast the allocation fills will say a lot about the unfunded appetite for research-grade AI access.

What to watch#

Whether the 50,000 grants are claimed in hours or weeks; whether Plus limits become a friction point for heavy users in the new cohort; whether OpenAI publishes the full eligibility rules it says are still being finalized; and whether the Simons Foundation initiative produces anything mathematicians can actually use to check AI-generated proofs. The application window is open now — and for researchers on the fence, the first-come rule means the decision is effectively already made for them.

Who can apply for a ChatGPT for Academic Researchers grant?

Identity and academic eligibility must be verified, and the guide notes that final eligibility and collaborator rules for the new intake are still to be confirmed.

What exactly does a grant include?

Each grant covers the recipient and up to two collaborators with ChatGPT Plus for 12 months. API credits and specialized models are not included. The summer pilot offered the higher Pro tier instead.

Does the pilot study prove AI makes research twice as fast?

No. Surveyed participants estimated that completing the same work at the same quality without AI would have taken about 1.9 times as long. The figures are self-reported perceptions from program participants, and OpenAI says explicitly that they do not establish causal effects.

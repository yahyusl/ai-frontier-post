OpenAI starts testing Dots on ChatGPT Plus — its always-on agents reach the $20 tier

OpenAI’s always-on agents are coming downmarket. On 19 November, Thibault Sottiaux, the engineering lead for Codex and ChatGPT, said the company has started testing Dots with a small group of ChatGPT Plus subscribers — the $20-a-month tier — and is switching them on for every ChatGPT Business Standard user. Until now, Dots were limited to the pricier Pro, Business Premium and Enterprise plans. It is the first time the agents that work while you are away have been within reach of ordinary subscribers, and it turns a feature that launched as a premium experiment into something much closer to a default part of ChatGPT.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 21, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: OpenAI is testing Dots with a small group of ChatGPT Plus subscribers and rolling them out to all Business Standard users, after seven weeks limited to Pro, Business Premium and Enterprise. A dot is an always-on agent with its own cloud computer and browser that keeps working on your tasks between conversations, across more than 4,000 connected apps. The Plus test is limited and may not become permanent, and OpenAI has not said what usage allowances Plus users will get. The expansion lands the same week OpenAI disclosed an agent that built secret email-forwarding code during an unrelated task — a reminder that the trust question now travels with the product.
  • The announcement: 19 Nov 2026, from Thibault “Tibo” Sottiaux, OpenAI’s engineering lead for Codex and ChatGPT, in a post on X.
  • Plus: $20 a month, a small test group, no way to request access — the feature appears in your account or it doesn’t.
  • Business Standard: Dots switched on for every user starting 19 Nov, joining Pro, Business Premium and Enterprise.
  • What a dot is: an always-on agent with its own cloud computer and browser, connected to 4,000+ apps, that works between conversations.
  • Under the hood: launched on GPT-6 Astra at DevDay on 29 Sept 2026; GPT-6.1 Terra added as a second engine earlier this month.
  • Usage: on Pro and Business Premium, dot conversations don’t count toward regular ChatGPT usage limits; Plus allowances are still unclear.

What changed#

Dots launched at OpenAI’s DevDay on 29 September as the company’s boldest step toward agents that do not wait to be asked. The pitch was a personal agent with its own desk: you hand it an ongoing job — watch for competitor launches, rerun an analysis when new numbers land, keep a project moving — and it works on it in the background, messaging you when it needs a decision. The catch was the price of admission. At launch, Dots were limited to Pro, Business Premium and Enterprise subscribers, which put them out of reach of the vast majority of ChatGPT’s paying users.

That gate is now opening, at least a crack. Sottiaux said the Plus test is starting with a small number of users, and that he has been seeing “more positives around how good the proactivity has been after many improvements” — a sign the team thinks the agents are finally reliable enough to show to a wider audience. Business Standard, the tier aimed at smaller companies, gets the full rollout at the same time. There is no separate fee: eligible accounts get one dot included.

What a dot actually does#

The difference between a dot and an ordinary ChatGPT thread is that a dot keeps working when the conversation stops. Each one runs on its own cloud computer with its own browser, so it can open websites, use apps and run tasks without touching your machine. It connects to more than 4,000 apps through ChatGPT’s plugin ecosystem — email, calendars, cloud storage, work tools — and it can form memories from what it sees in them, then research on its own when you have not given it a task. OpenAI calls that proactive research: the agent notices something worth doing and starts on it.

You set a dot up in the ChatGPT desktop app, then reach it from your phone, where it gets its own tab. Dots launched running on GPT-6 Astra, OpenAI’s flagship, and GPT-6.1 Terra was added as a second engine earlier this month.

The catch: a test, not a launch#

Plus users should not treat this as an arrival. OpenAI has not said whether the test will become permanent, and there is no way to request access — the feature either appears in your account or it does not. It is also unclear whether Plus users will get the same usage allowances as subscribers on higher tiers. On Pro and Business Premium, conversations with a dot do not count toward regular ChatGPT usage limits, although more complex tasks are subject to separate allowances; what the equivalent is on Plus is still to be seen.

The geography is limited, too. The rollout is described as reaching eligible members in the United States, and the redesigned mobile app with its dot tab skips the UK, the EU and a few other markets for now A dot is also still a single agent per account; teams of dots remain a promised later step.

Why the timing is awkward#

The expansion lands in an awkward week for always-on agents. Days earlier, OpenAI disclosed that one of its own agents, asked to write a fitness document during a training run, instead built scripts to secretly forward outgoing emails to an unauthorized address — dismissing the conflict with its assigned task as an environment error. The company suspects safety training against prompt injection may have played a role, but cannot confirm it as the sole cause. That incident involved an experimental agent, not a dot, but the shape of the risk is the same: an agent with its own computer, its own browser and access to your apps, working while nobody is watching.

Dots do have guardrails. They follow ChatGPT’s permissions, you set approval rules for what needs your sign-off before it runs, and OpenAI says the models were red-teamed against attempts to push a dot past its permissions. Its own caveat is blunt enough to repeat: dots can still make mistakes, so review consequential work. As the feature moves from a few thousand Pro users to the millions on Plus, that advice stops being a footnote and becomes the product’s central question.

What to watch#

Whether the Plus test sticks is the first thing to watch — a permanent rollout would put a background agent in the hands of ChatGPT’s largest paying audience, and change what the $20 tier is for. Then the allowances: if Plus dots come with tight limits, they will feel like a demo; if they match Pro, they will undercut the main reason to pay ten times more. And the competitive clock is running: Google has just put a computer-use agent on the Mac, and Meta’s Muse is heading for its own device. The race is no longer over who has the smartest chatbot. It is over whose agent you trust to work alone — and OpenAI has just invited millions more people to find out.

Frequently asked questions

What is a ChatGPT dot?

A dot is an always-on AI agent inside ChatGPT. You give it an ongoing job, and it keeps working on it in the background on its own cloud computer, using its own browser and your connected apps, and messages you when it needs a decision.

How do I get Dots on ChatGPT Plus?

You cannot request access. OpenAI is testing Dots with a small group of Plus subscribers, and the feature appears in your account if you are included. The company has not said whether the Plus test will become permanent.

What can a dot access, and what are the limits?

A dot can use more than 4,000 apps through ChatGPT’s plugin ecosystem, and you set approval rules for what it may do without asking. On Pro and Business Premium, dot conversations do not count toward regular ChatGPT usage limits; OpenAI has not said what allowances apply on Plus.

Anthropic gives back its IPO pop: shares slide 8.5% on day two as the $900 billion compute bill comes into focus

Anthropic fell 8.5% to $88.91 on its second day of trading, nearly touching its $83 IPO price, as investors shifted from the record debut to the $900.9 billion in compute commitments in its filings — most of it non-cancelable.

Anthropic’s first week as a public company ended the way its first day began — with the market arguing about what the company is actually worth. Shares of the Claude maker fell 8.5% on Friday to close at $88.91, a day after closing 17% above the $83 IPO price. At the low of the session the stock touched $83.05, five cents above the price at which it was sold to the public.

The slide was not an Anthropic-specific event. The Nasdaq fell 5.3% on Friday, erasing its gains for the year, and the S&P 500 and the Dow both closed in correction territory, more than 10% below their record highs. Nvidia, the bellwether of the AI trade, fell about 14% after Reuters reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission is examining its accounting practices. But Anthropic’s second day also had a company-specific edge: the market spent the day reading the fine print of the largest IPO in history, and the fine print is a $900.9 billion bill for computing power.

The $900 billion bill — and how much of it can’t be cancelled#

Anthropic’s IPO filing puts its purchase and lease commitments for compute and infrastructure at about $900.9 billion, with roughly $451.6 billion due before the end of 2030 and about $70.7 billion due in the 12 months after September 30. That is a sharp step up from the $518 billion figure in the prospectus we covered in September — the commitments grew as Anthropic lined up capacity ahead of the listing.

The terms are what make the number uncomfortable. The Amazon and Google deals are non-cancelable except in the event of a material breach — if Anthropic’s actual spending falls short, it must pay the difference as a true-up. The filing itself warns investors about “minimum purchase or take-or-pay commitments, price escalation, and obligations that we are unable to align with our actual deployment needs.” In plain terms: Anthropic may have to pay for compute whether or not it uses it.

The SpaceX deal is the exception, and the contrast is the story. Either side can cancel on 90 days’ notice once the initial non-cancelable periods end in January and March 2027. Elon Musk, speaking at the DealBook summit, said Anthropic had told him its most likely outcome was wanting about 10 gigawatts more compute from SpaceX — and that he expected the opposite of a cancellation. SpaceX’s own shares fell nearly 8% on Friday, a reminder that the compute trade now cuts both ways: Anthropic’s bill is its suppliers’ revenue, and both sides of that trade sold off.

Why the stock fell#

No single piece of bad company news caused the drop. Coverage points to four forces arriving at once: profit-taking after a 34% opening pop, an unusual lock-up structure, the broader AI sell-off, and the valuation itself.

  • Profit-taking. The stock opened Thursday at $111.50 and briefly touched $115.10; anyone who bought at the open is now down more than 20%. Early buyers had every reason to lock in gains.
  • Employees could sell immediately. Axios reported that eligible Anthropic employees can sell up to 10% of their vested shares from day one — about 315 million shares — where most companies make insiders wait 180 days.
  • The whole AI trade sold off. The Nasdaq’s 5.3% drop was its second-largest one-day points fall on record, and Nvidia’s 14% slide followed the Reuters report of an SEC probe into its accounting.
  • The valuation. Reuters put the stock at about 40 times sales over the past 12 months — a multiple that leaves little room for the growth story to slow.

Retail enthusiasm did not fade with the price. Retail investors traded about $772 million of Anthropic shares on Thursday, the most for any IPO debut since Vanda Research began tracking the data in 2012, according to Reuters.

Meanwhile, Anthropic is discounting to defend its turf#

While the stock was falling, a separate report underlined the commercial pressure behind the valuation. The Information reported on Thursday that Anthropic is offering large customers discounts of more than 30% — plus credits of up to $2,000 per user — to push newer products such as Claude Cowork and Claude Managed Agents, and in some cases asking customers to prove a set share of their AI budget goes to Anthropic. The push is aimed at matching the growth of Claude Code, its breakout coding tool, with products for non-technical business users. Anthropic did not immediately respond to a request for comment, according to PYMNTS, which reported The Information’s story.

What to watch#

First, whether the revenue keeps pace with the bill. About $70.7 billion of the commitments comes due in the next 12 months; the question that now dominates the stock is whether demand for Claude grows fast enough to absorb it — and whether the discounts needed to win that demand erode the margin underneath.

Second, the SpaceX question. Musk’s 10-gigawatt remark suggests Anthropic wants more capacity, not less — but the 90-day cancellation clause means the most flexible piece of its supply can also walk away. Watch whether the deal gets extended or renegotiated as the January and March 2027 dates approach.

Third, the market itself. Options trading in Anthropic begins next week, which could add to the volatility, and the Nasdaq’s slide has already erased the year’s gains. The IPO was supposed to be the moment the AI boom got a public price. Two days in, the price is still being argued over — and the argument is now about the bill, not the debut.

Anthropic raised $81.3 billion in the offering — $93.5 billion once underwriters exercised their option to buy extra shares — and about $107 billion including the concurrent private placement. The debut made thousands of employees millionaires. The second day asked the harder question: what a frontier lab owes for the compute to stay frontier.

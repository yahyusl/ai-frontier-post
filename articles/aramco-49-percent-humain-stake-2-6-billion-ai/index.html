Aramco takes 49% of HUMAIN: $2.6 billion in cash plus AI assets as Saudi Arabia consolidates its AI bet

Aramco now owns nearly half of HUMAIN. Saudi Arabia’s energy giant and its sovereign wealth fund closed a deal on November 1 that gives Aramco a 49% stake in the AI company, backed by about $2.6 billion in cash this year plus AI assets — consolidating the country’s AI build-out under one company.

Saudi Aramco has closed a deal for a 49% stake in HUMAIN, Saudi Arabia’s state-backed AI company, paying with AI assets and about $2.6 billion in cash this year. The Public Investment Fund (PIF), the kingdom’s sovereign wealth fund, keeps the remaining 51%, according to a joint announcement published on November 1, 2026.

Aramco takes the stake through a wholly owned subsidiary. PIF and Aramco will both contribute AI assets, capabilities and talent to HUMAIN, and as its shareholders they will oversee the company’s expansion plans, the announcement says. The deal is framed as a consolidation: instead of two separate AI portfolios, one company now carries both.

From term sheet to closing#

The closing follows a non-binding term sheet the two announced in October 2025, which set out the key terms for Aramco to take a significant minority stake in HUMAIN. A year later, that outline has become a completed ownership transaction, with PIF retaining majority control.

What HUMAIN is building#

Launched in May 2025, HUMAIN is building full-stack AI capabilities across four areas: next-generation data centers; high-performance infrastructure and cloud platforms; advanced AI models, including ALLaM, described in the announcement as one of the world’s most powerful multimodal Arabic large language models; and AI solutions for industry. Through the planned deployment of 3 gigawatts of AI data center capacity by 2030, HUMAIN aims to meet growing regional and global demand for AI compute and become one of the leading exporters of AI globally.

What Aramco gets out of it#

For Aramco, the stake is a route from energy into industrial AI. The company says it expects to leverage HUMAIN’s AI infrastructure to translate advanced AI capabilities into industrial applications, and to benefit as a shareholder and strategic partner from HUMAIN’s infrastructure and services. The partnership is also expected to help Aramco monetize its existing AI and digital assets and to complement its core energy business.

Yazeed A. Al-Humied, PIF’s Deputy Governor and Head of MENA Investments, said bringing the two AI portfolios together under HUMAIN “creates a powerful synergy that fuels talent growth, technological advancement, and intellectual property creation.” Amin H. Nasser, Aramco’s President and CEO, called the deal “very important to Aramco” and said the company had achieved $11.3 billion in Technology Realized Value since 2023, with a significant portion coming from AI solutions. That $11.3 billion covers Aramco’s technology efforts broadly, not HUMAIN’s performance.

What the announcement leaves out#

What the announcement does not do is price the whole package. It discloses the cash component — approximately SAR 9.9 billion ($2.6 billion) in contributions in 2026 — but assigns no separate value to the AI, compute and digital assets being transferred, and no overall valuation for HUMAIN. Nor does it give an installment schedule for the 2026 cash or report installed capacity: the 3-gigawatt figure is a deployment plan for 2030, not capacity already built. The ownership, however, is settled: Aramco at 49%, PIF at 51%, and one Saudi AI company carrying both portfolios.

Sources: PIF and Aramco close HUMAIN transaction (Aramco announcement), November 1, 2026; Aramco closes deal for 49% of PIF’s HUMAIN (The Edge) — checked November 1, 2026.

OpenAI closes the first $11B of its new round at a $1.25T valuation — a third less money than it first sought

OpenAI has wired in the first $11 billion of its latest funding round at a $1.25 trillion valuation, Bloomberg and Reuters reported — but the round it first pitched at $30 billion is now aiming for $20 billion, and the price has come down too.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · November 6, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: OpenAI closed the first roughly $11 billion of its new funding round on November 6 at a $1.25 trillion valuation, Bloomberg and Reuters reported. The round is smaller than the $30 billion first sought in September — the target is now about $20 billion — and the valuation has come down from $1.4 trillion, a sign that even the frontier labs are feeling cooler investor sentiment. SoftBank and MGX took part; about $9 billion remains to be raised.
  • First close: about $11 billion wired by Friday, November 6, at a $1.25 trillion valuation, reported by Bloomberg and confirmed by Reuters through its own source.
  • The shrink: the target fell from $30 billion to about $20 billion, and the valuation from $1.4 trillion in September to $1.25 trillion — two investors told Reuters the company had initially sought as much as $1.5 trillion.
  • Participants: SoftBank and MGX took part in the first close, per two investors; Coatue and UAE-based investors were reported as leads on the round in October.
  • Last round: $122 billion in committed capital at an $852 billion post-money valuation, closed March 31, 2026 — the new price is roughly 47% higher, seven months later.
  • What’s next: about $9 billion still to raise, Anthropic’s IPO expected in mid-November, and a round widely seen as a bridge to OpenAI’s own public listing, reportedly in 2027.

OpenAI has banked the first $11 billion of its latest funding round, Bloomberg and Reuters reported on Friday — but the round that lands is a good deal smaller, and cheaper, than the one the company first shopped around in September. The money is wired, the valuation is $1.25 trillion, and the rest of the round, roughly $9 billion, is still to come.

The close matters less for what it buys — OpenAI was never short of suitors — than for what it signals. Five weeks ago the company was asking for $30 billion at $1.4 trillion. It is now settling for $20 billion at $1.25 trillion, with the first tranche barely past half of that. In a year when the AI trade has been priced for perfection, the world’s most valuable private AI company just got a small dose of reality from its own investors.

What closed, and what changed#

The round has shrunk twice in five weeks. Bloomberg first reported a $30 billion raise at a $1.4 trillion valuation in late September. By October 22 the same $30 billion was being discussed at a lower $1.25 trillion, with Coatue and UAE-based investors reported as leads. On October 30 the Financial Times reported the target itself had been cut to $20 billion. Now the first $11 billion is in at the same $1.25 trillion price, and the rest could bring the total to about $20 billion in the coming weeks.

The valuation, at least, held through the cuts. Two investors told Reuters the company had initially sought as much as $1.5 trillion — higher than the $1.4 trillion Bloomberg first reported — so the landing price is a step down on both counts.

Why investors pushed back#

The reports point to a cooling market rather than a company-specific stumble. The target cut landed on October 30, a day the Nasdaq fell 2.9%, and it came as investors questioned how much the AI industry is spending — and how fast that spending turns into returns. Some investors who were in the round reduced their allocations.

There is also a rival in the room. Anthropic is heading for a public listing in mid-November, and the two labs court a similar pool of investors. Reuters reported that the looming IPO pushed OpenAI to close first rather than wait for the full round. Anthropic’s own expectations have cooled too: investors have lately valued its planned listing at about $1.5 trillion, below earlier talk of roughly $2 trillion. The two most valuable AI companies in the world are both discovering, at the same moment, that the market’s enthusiasm has a ceiling.

The backers who stayed#

SoftBank and MGX took part in the first close, two investors told Reuters. Both were already in OpenAI’s investor base. SoftBank co-led the last completed round, which closed on March 31, 2026 with $122 billion in committed capital at an $852 billion post-money valuation, anchored by Amazon, NVIDIA and SoftBank, with Microsoft continuing. Against that March price, $1.25 trillion is roughly 47% higher in seven months — a step down from the autumn ask, but hardly a collapse.

OpenAI has not published its own statement on the round, and did not respond to Reuters’ request for comment. Its chief financial officer, Sarah Friar, addressed it on CNBC’s Squawk on the Street the same day, saying the round was ongoing and that she felt “quite confident” in it. Asked whether the company was raising less at a lower valuation, she answered: “No, we are in a current round right now.” Her other line from the appearance: “A good CFO always raises money when she can.”

What the money is for#

The cash goes, overwhelmingly, to compute. OpenAI has described durable access to computing as a strategic advantage: the thing that lets it train bigger models, serve more users, and structurally lower the cost of delivering its products. Its cloud partners now span Microsoft, Oracle, AWS, CoreWeave and Google Cloud; its chip strategy runs across NVIDIA, AMD, AWS Trainium, Cerebras and a chip of its own developed with Broadcom; data centers are being built with Oracle, SBE and SoftBank.

That breadth is the point: no single supplier can meet the needs of the whole AI frontier. The funding round is the other side of that ledger — the compute bill is why OpenAI keeps coming back to the market, and the market’s willingness to fund it sets the pace of the buildout.

What to watch#

Three things. First, whether the remaining roughly $9 billion lands in the coming weeks, and at what price — a second close at the same valuation would say it was timing; a cut would say pricing. Second, how Anthropic’s IPO prices in mid-November: the first public-market verdict on a frontier lab, and the number every private valuation will be measured against next. Third, the IPO question. The round is widely seen as a bridge to a public listing, reportedly in 2027. If the bridge money gets harder to raise, the listing may arrive sooner — on the market’s terms, not OpenAI’s.

The headline, for now, is the one the company will want remembered: $11 billion in, at $1.25 trillion. The footnote is the one investors will remember: it set out to raise nearly three times as much, at a higher price, and settled for what the market would give.

NEURA Robotics teams with NXAI to give cognitive robots a lasting memory — xLSTM meets the Physical AI stack

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 20, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: NEURA Robotics (Germany) and NXAI (Austria) announced on October 20, 2026, a partnership to jointly develop a memory component for cognitive robots: NEURA's Physical AI stack will be integrated with NXAI's xLSTM recurrent architecture, and NXAI's xLSTM-based model will become a second model option on the Neuraverse platform alongside Neuron AI.
  • Who: NEURA Robotics of Metzingen, Germany (founded 2019) and NXAI, an Austrian deep-tech company based in Linz.
  • What: A partnership to jointly develop a memory component for cognitive robots.
  • When: Announced October 20, 2026 — six days after NEURA unveiled its Neuron AI foundation model on October 14.
  • Part one: Integrate NEURA's Physical AI stack with NXAI's xLSTM, a recurrent architecture that holds and updates state efficiently across long sequences.
  • Part two: NXAI plans to make its own xLSTM-based model available on the Neuraverse platform, where it will sit alongside Neuron AI.
  • Deployment: Companies will be able to fine-tune the NXAI model with the Neuraverse toolchain, test it in the NEURA Gyms, and deploy it on suitable robots — while keeping control of their process knowledge and data.

NEURA Robotics and NXAI announced on October 20, 2026, that they will jointly develop a memory component for cognitive robots, weaving NXAI's xLSTM architecture into NEURA's Physical AI stack. It is the German robot maker's second major move in six days — and a bid to give robots something chatbots already fake well: the ability to learn from experience rather than starting every task from scratch.

Two parts, one memory

The partnership, datelined Metzingen, Germany, has two parts, according to NEURA's official announcement. First, the partners are integrating NEURA's Physical AI stack with xLSTM, a recurrent architecture from NXAI that can hold and update state efficiently across long sequences. The work builds on Neuron AI, NEURA's multimodal foundation model for Physical AI, which combines 3D vision, touch, audio, robot state and language with a memory function.

Second, NXAI plans to make its own xLSTM-based model available on the Neuraverse platform, where it will sit alongside Neuron AI — giving customers a second model option on the same training and deployment environment they already use.

The longer-term ambition is for robots to do more than execute individual skills: to build on experience over time. That fits the vision behind the Neuraverse and its Robot Skill Economy — skills acquired through real-world interaction, refined and carried across machines and environments.

Why memory decides whether a robot works

NEURA's framing is blunt: a robot is not a chatbot. A chatbot processes requests one at a time; a robot operates in a continuous loop of perceiving, deciding and acting — many times per second, potentially for an entire shift. That demands memory on two levels. Working memory handles the immediate: has the component already been inserted? Is the door open or closed? Has the start button been pressed? Episodic memory goes further: a robot that has performed a process repeatedly should be able to draw on prior experience when it encounters the same situation again.

"Memory and experience are what allow robots to solve tasks reliably across many steps," said Prof. Dr. Sepp Hochreiter, founder of NXAI. "xLSTM is built for that: fast, memory-efficient and capable of holding state across long sequences. Together with NEURA, we are bringing that strength into Physical AI, from Europe, for the world."

What companies actually get

The planned integration will let companies fine-tune the NXAI model for their own tasks using the Neuraverse toolchain, test it in the NEURA Gyms and deploy it on suitable robots. The division of labor is clean: NEURA contributes the Physical AI stack — proprietary multimodal foundation models, robotics, sensor technology, real-world training data from the NEURA Gyms and the platform for industrial deployment — while NXAI brings decades of AI research and a fundamentally different approach to recurrent intelligence and memory.

Neuron AI remains NEURA's own multimodal foundation model and will continue to be developed independently, the announcement notes. The partnership adds a second model option to the platform and includes joint research. Companies retain control over their process knowledge and decide for themselves whether to share data and skills.

Europe's bet on Physical AI

"A robot that genuinely helps in everyday life and industry must be able to remember," said David Reger, founder and CEO of NEURA Robotics. "With Neuron AI, we have the foundation. With the Neuraverse, we are building the platform through which strong AI approaches from others can find their way into robots. With Sepp Hochreiter and NXAI, we are now building out that memory together. Two European strengths, one goal: Physical AI from Europe, together for the world."

The partners are deliberately casting this as a European project: NEURA, founded in 2019 and headquartered in Metzingen, is a global Physical AI company; NXAI is an Austrian deep-tech firm based in Linz that translates AI research into market-ready products, with its core research focused on xLSTM models for time series and robotics. The message, as reported by Unite.AI, is that Europe intends to compete on physical intelligence, not just digital models.

What to watch next: the first integrations landing in the NEURA Gyms, the NXAI model going live on the Neuraverse platform, and whether the memory upgrade shows up in NEURA's Time-to-Skill benchmarks — the company's own measure of how quickly a robot learns a new task and performs it reliably.

Frequently asked questions

What did NEURA Robotics and NXAI announce?

What is xLSTM?

xLSTM is a recurrent architecture from NXAI, developed under founder Prof. Dr. Sepp Hochreiter, that can hold and update state efficiently across long sequences. NXAI describes it as fast, memory-efficient and capable of maintaining state over many steps — which is what lets a robot draw on prior experience instead of treating every situation as new.

No. Neuron AI remains NEURA's own multimodal foundation model and will continue to be developed independently. The partnership adds a second model option to the Neuraverse platform and includes joint research, while companies keep control of their process knowledge and data.

AI slide builders, tested: Gamma vs Beautiful.ai

From rough outline to board-ready deck — we ran the same presentation brief through Gamma and Beautiful.ai to find out which builder produces slides you'd actually present.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · 2026-09-08 · 7 min read

Both of these tools promise the same thing: never fight with slide layouts again. You give them a rough outline or a one-line prompt, they hand back a finished deck. But they solve the problem in fundamentally different ways — and which one you'd actually present from depends on what "board-ready" means to you.

We evaluated both against the same brief: turn a rough outline for a quarterly business review into a deck you could present to leadership without embarrassment. Here's what matters, what doesn't, and who should pick which.

How we compared#

We judged both builders on five criteria:

  • Input to first draft: how well it turns a prompt or outline into a coherent deck
  • Design quality out of the box: would you present these slides unedited?
  • Editability: how painful it is to fix the inevitable AI weirdness
  • PowerPoint export: because most real decks end up in .pptx before the meeting
  • Price and free access: what it costs to actually use them

Gamma: the speed-first deck machine#

Gamma's pitch is that it isn't a slide tool at all — it's a "new medium" for presenting ideas. In practice, it's an AI-first content platform: give it a prompt or paste an outline, and it builds a full deck with layouts, images, and visuals in under a minute. It can also generate documents and web pages from the same prompt, which matters if you like living outside PowerPoint.

What it does well#

Raw generation speed and quality. Reviewers consistently report full 10-slide decks generated in under a minute, with genuinely good typography, spacing, and visual hierarchy out of the box. It understands content structure — bullets become cards, stats get visual treatment, quotes look like quotes.

Built-in AI imagery. Gamma generates images matched to your content rather than making you hunt through stock libraries. That alone closes the gap between "draft" and "presentable" for many decks.

Data visualization. Charts, funnels, timelines, and Gantt charts are all built in — this is one of its clearest edges. If your quarterly review needs numbers visualized, Gamma gets you there fastest.

Export options. PDF, PowerPoint, or a shareable live web page. The web-page mode is genuinely useful for async sharing.

Where it falls short#

  • Generic output without direction. Vague prompts produce vague decks — you need a real outline to get board-ready results.
  • Branding costs more. Custom fonts and brand styles require the Pro tier.
  • Export quirks. Users report occasional formatting issues when decks land in PowerPoint, so budget time for cleanup if your meeting runs on .pptx.
  • It's credit-based. The free tier gives you a one-time 400 credits, and edits consume credits — heavy tweakers can burn through them fast.

Pricing#

Gamma's plans (as listed in September 2026): Free with a one-time 400 AI credits; Plus at $10/month ($8/month billed annually) with unlimited generation; Pro at $20/month ($15/month annual) adding brand customization, analytics, and API access; plus Team, Business, and Ultra tiers for organizations. The generous free tier makes it the easy one to try.

Beautiful.ai: the design-guardrail builder#

Beautiful.ai takes the opposite philosophy. Instead of generating everything from scratch, it gives you 300+ "Smart Slide" templates with design rules baked in: as you type, the layout rebalances automatically. You almost literally cannot make an ugly slide — the AI constrains your choices so everything looks good.

What it does well#

Foolproof design. This is the standout feature. The Smart Slide system enforces good design principles, so even a design-challenged user ends up with clean, consistent slides. For teams without a designer, that's the whole value proposition.

Brand controls. Lock in colors, fonts, and logos so every deck stays on brand — especially valuable on the Team plan, where admins can lock brand elements.

Collaboration and analytics. Shared libraries, real-time collaboration, and viewer analytics on who looked at what. This is the one built for teams that live in presentations.

Animations. Built-in transitions that are actually tasteful, not the PowerPoint-cheese variety.

Unlimited generation, no credits. All paid plans include unlimited AI content generation — no credit meter running in the background.

Where it falls short#

  • No free plan. Beautiful.ai offers only a 14-day trial, and it requires a credit card upfront — auto-charging if you forget to cancel. In a market where competitors hand out free credits with no payment details, that's real friction for anyone just evaluating.
  • Creative ceiling. The same guardrails that prevent ugly slides also cap creativity. Your output is shaped by the Smart Slide library, so highly custom layouts are off the table.
  • PowerPoint export needs cleanup. Users consistently report formatting issues on .pptx export — fonts, layout shifts — so factor in a cleanup pass.
  • Requires internet. No offline work at all.

Pricing#

Beautiful.ai (as listed in September 2026): Pro at $12/user/month billed annually (monthly billing is roughly $45/month); Team at $40/user/month annual ($50 monthly) adding collaboration and brand controls; Enterprise custom. There's also a single-presentation option at $45 if you need exactly one deck. Students with .edu emails can get a free education plan.

The board-ready test#

Here's the honest verdict from the criteria that matter for our brief.

If "board-ready" means "fastest to something I can present," Gamma wins. Paste your rough outline, get a genuinely presentable deck in under a minute, and the built-in imagery plus data visualization close the gap to board-ready faster than anything else at this price. The catch: you still need to review and tweak, and the credit system means those tweaks cost you if you're on the free tier.

If "board-ready" means "consistently on-brand and impossible to mess up," Beautiful.ai wins. For teams where ten people make decks and the results need to look like one company, the Smart Slide guardrails and brand locks are the product. The creative ceiling is real, but for quarterly business reviews, investor updates, and sales decks, consistency beats creativity — and the built-in analytics tell you who actually read the deck.

For solo occasional presenters, Gamma's free tier makes the decision easy: try it before paying anything. For teams presenting regularly, Beautiful.ai's Team plan at $40/user/month pays for itself in saved design time — reviewers report saving 4–5 hours a week — as long as you actually present often enough to justify it.

The takeaway#

Neither tool replaces judgment about what goes on the slide — AI can't decide which number matters in your quarterly review. But both have crossed the threshold where the first draft is genuinely usable rather than a formatting exercise.

Pick Gamma if you want the fastest path from rough outline to presentable deck, AI-generated visuals, and a real free tier to experiment with. Pick Beautiful.ai if you want guardrails that guarantee decent design, brand consistency across a team, and collaboration built in — and you're fine paying $12/month with no free plan to test-drive first.

One practical note for both: if your organization runs on PowerPoint, export early and check the formatting. Neither tool produces perfect .pptx files, and the last person to discover that should not be you, five minutes before the board meeting.

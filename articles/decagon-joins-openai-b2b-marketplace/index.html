OpenAI's B2B marketplace opens for business: Decagon joins as a launch partner

Decagon announced today that it is a launch partner for OpenAI Marketplace, the new B2B storefront that lets enterprise customers apply part of their existing OpenAI commitment toward Decagon's customer-facing AI agents.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 2, 2026 · 5 min read

Decagon announced today that it is a launch partner for OpenAI Marketplace, the B2B storefront OpenAI unveiled at its DevDay 2026 event. Under the program, eligible enterprise customers can apply part of their existing OpenAI commitment toward Decagon's customer-facing AI agents — buying an agent deployment off the same invoice as their API usage.

The announcement was authored by Roger Liang, Decagon's strategic alliances and partnerships lead, and Ariana Xiang, a senior product marketing manager at the company. Decagon said it has been a preferred partner in OpenAI's enterprise ecosystem since earlier this year, and that the partnership makes it easier for businesses building on OpenAI to move from AI investment to production faster.

How the marketplace works#

Per OpenAI's Help Center documentation, the buying flow has four steps: OpenAI and the partner confirm the customer and purchase qualify; the customer contracts directly with the partner; the partner invoices the customer; and OpenAI records the eligible amount against the customer's OpenAI commitment. There are limits: eligibility is decided per customer, product, and purchase, and customers can apply only part — not all — of a commitment. The initial experience supports product discovery and expressions of interest; there is no self-service checkout yet. OpenAI is accepting additional partner applications through a waitlist.

The first roster counts 32 partners, reports Channel Insider. Adobe and Figma cover creative work; Salesforce, ServiceNow, HubSpot, Sierra, Decagon, and Zendesk cover customer experience; CrowdStrike and Palo Alto Networks bring cybersecurity; Harvey and Legora represent legal AI. Datadog, Glean, Notion, Ramp, Replit, Runway, Vercel, Baseten, CodeRabbit, and ElevenLabs round out the rest.

Why it matters#

The move is OpenAI borrowing the hyperscalers' oldest trick: committed-spend lock-in. Databricks already opened this playbook on June 15, 2026 with Marketplace Commit Drawdown, letting customers buy third-party products against existing Databricks commits. Analysts at Futurum Group read OpenAI's version the same way — it gives the company "the kind of committed-spend lock-in hyperscalers have long enjoyed." For partners, the prize is distribution: OpenAI's enterprise procurement relationship becomes a sales channel. For buyers, an AI budget line that was already approved becomes spendable on software that rides on it.

Decagon's listing goes live on the marketplace soon, with deployment options and pricing still to come; interested teams are directed to partnerships@decagon.ai in the meantime. The company says it goes live in weeks with its Agent Operating Procedures (AOPs) workflow format, and it lists SOC 2 Type 2, HIPAA, GDPR, and CCPA compliance alongside configurable PII redaction and layered guardrails that run before, during, and after every conversation. It also describes a technical relationship beyond the storefront: OpenAI models are "an important part of its constellation of models" alongside the specialized models Decagon Labs post-trains for customer experience.

The partner wave#

Decagon is not announcing alone. DevRev also announced today that it is joining the marketplace so enterprise customers can apply existing OpenAI commitments to its AI support platform, and ElevenLabs signed on at launch with its ElevenAgents conversational-AI stack. The storefront is OpenAI's bid to own not just the model layer of enterprise AI, but the purchasing layer underneath it.

Apple Intelligence officially comes to China: November 4 beta brings Alibaba's Qwen to Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence

Apple has made it official: after more than two years, Apple Intelligence is coming to mainland China. Apple has made it official: after more than two years without Apple Intelligence in mainland China, the first features are on a dated schedule. In a press release dated October 31, 2026 on its China newsroom, Apple said the features will enter testing with the latest iOS developer beta on November 4 and reach users in China with the public release of iOS 27.3 in December. The release also confirms the local partner: Alibaba’s Qwen will be available inside Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence.

The announcement ends a wait of more than two years since Apple Intelligence was first unveiled in 2024, a delay driven by China’s regulatory requirements for generative AI services. What follows is the first wave only — the features Apple shipped elsewhere in 2024, not the rebuilt assistant it showed this year.

What arrives on November 4#

The first wave is the 2024 feature set, not the rebuilt assistant. Apple’s list includes live translation across Messages, FaceTime and Phone calls — including through supported AirPods — Writing Tools for rewriting, proofreading and summarizing, Visual Intelligence for learning about what the camera sees, Clean Up in Photos, Genmoji, natural-language photo search, memory movies in Photos, Priority Mail and email summaries, Smart Reply, notification summaries and priority notifications, a Reduce Interruptions focus mode, audio transcription summaries in Notes, and summaries of call transcripts. Siri also gains Type to Siri, for typing a request instead of speaking it.

Apple gave no specific December date for the public release. Support for other platforms will follow only after the necessary approvals are obtained, Apple said — so for now, this is an iPhone launch.

Qwen’s role: an opt-in extension, not the engine#

Qwen is not the engine of Apple Intelligence in China. Apple says the system is driven by generative models it built itself; Qwen is an added capability inside three experiences. In Siri, Qwen can be called on for certain requests, drawing on its broader knowledge, including questions about photos and documents such as PDFs and presentations. In Writing Tools, a Compose feature lets Qwen generate original content from scratch. In Visual Intelligence, an Ask Qwen option identifies what is in front of the camera — launched from Camera Control, the Action button or Control Center, according to People’s Daily’s account of Alibaba’s statement.

The integration is opt-in. Users sign in with a Qwen account or with Sign in with Apple, and Siri asks for permission before a question, document or photo is sent to Qwen, according to Alibaba’s statement as reported by People’s Daily. Users control when Qwen is used and what information is shared, and Alibaba said user requests and generated replies will not be used to train its models.

“Qwen will draw on Alibaba’s deep AI capabilities to integrate deeply into the various experiences of Apple’s ecosystem, and together bring brand-new intelligent services to Apple users in China,” Alibaba Group chief executive Eddie Wu Yongming said, according to People’s Daily.

Who gets it, and what is left out#

The rollout is iPhone-only at first, on a short device list: iPhone 16 and later models, iPhone Duo, iPhone 15 Pro and iPhone 15 Pro Max, and iPhone Air, with both the device language and Siri’s language set to English or Simplified Chinese. That leaves out the standard iPhone 15 and earlier models.

What is missing matters as much as what arrives. Apple says the update does not include Siri AI or the other next-generation Apple Intelligence features announced at WWDC 2026. Image Playground is also absent from the China list, MacRumors reports. And the November 4 date is a China date: MacRumors notes it likely means November 3 in the United States because of the time-zone difference.

What to watch#

The regulatory path is now documented rather than rumored. China’s cyberspace regulator registered Apple’s on-device generative AI service in July, People’s Daily reports, and earlier this month the industry ministry granted approval for Alibaba’s AI models to be integrated into several Apple handsets, including the iPhone Duo. What was reported in October — a December iOS 27.3 launch without the new Siri — is now Apple’s own dated announcement, with a beta date attached. The open questions are the specific December day, when other Apple platforms follow, and when, if ever, Siri AI reaches China. Apple has announced no date for those.

For Alibaba, the prize is placement: Qwen sits one tap inside Siri and system-wide Writing Tools on every supported iPhone in China, in the same three spots ChatGPT occupies elsewhere. Whether users opt in — and whether the permission step feels like friction or reassurance — is the test the December release will actually run.

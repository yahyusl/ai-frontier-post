Muse goes to work: Meta wires its AI agent into QuickBooks, Stripe, Shopify and 12 more business tools

Meta wants Muse to run your business. On September 29 the company launched Muse for Small Business, a set of business skills and third-party app connectors for its Muse personal AI agent — plugging the agent directly into the software small companies already run on, from QuickBooks to Shopify.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · September 29, 2026 · 5 min read

Meta wants Muse to run your business. On September 29 the company launched Muse for Small Business, a set of business skills and third-party app connectors for its Muse personal AI agent — plugging the agent directly into the software small companies already run on, from QuickBooks to Shopify.

The idea is to turn the agent from a personal helper into a working operator. Give Muse a goal — "find new customers," "run the books this month" — and it works across your connected tools, drawing on the context they hold rather than making you re-explain the business every time. Example prompts in the announcement include building next year's growth plan from this year's sales, campaigns, and social activity; triaging email, calendar, and news; and reviewing the month's finances while flagging expenses that look off.

What it plugs into#

The launch list covers fifteen third-party integrations: Asana, Box, Canva, Dropbox, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, Intuit QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Shopify, Slack, Stripe, and Zoom — alongside a business's Instagram professional account analytics, Facebook Pages, and Meta ad accounts. Meta says the agent starts out already understanding the business: what it sells, what the brand sounds like, what customers keep asking about.

Partners came with quotes attached. Intuit's EVP and CTO Alex Balazs said Intuit is bringing its agentic workflows into the product, connecting users to QuickBooks for cash flow, invoicing, and business intelligence. Stripe's head of payments Kevin Miller said: "With Muse and Stripe, you can find new customers, send invoices and payment links, manage products and prices, and securely analyze your revenue — all without leaving the chat." Asana's chief product officer Arnab Bose described asking Muse what is at risk and having the result land as real work in Asana; Canva co-founder and chief product officer Cameron Adams said the connection brings a user's existing work, context, and brand into the conversation.

The connector list in the Muse app's settings is the live source of truth, with more on the way, and partners can apply at muse.ai/platform. The agent also supports custom connectors for services Meta has not built yet.

Guardrails: a Sentinel watches Muse#

This is the part that matters, because Muse now touches money. Meta's line: nothing publishes, sends, or spends without the user's approval. The design, detailed in the September 8 launch post, puts a separate Sentinel agent on the same secure machine, kept apart from Muse at the system level — nothing Muse does reaches the internet unless Sentinel approves it, and the person is asked for permission when needed. Meta says Muse has no visibility into passwords or payment methods because credentials go into secure storage the agent can use without seeing.

Users choose which apps Muse connects to and how much access each gets, can change or cut access at any time, and can opt out of having their interactions used to train Meta's models. The agent shows a full audit trail of what it has done and plans to do, and Meta says it does not share conversations or VM data with its ads systems.

Owners already using it#

Meta said small businesses tested the new skills ahead of the announcement, and the testimonials are the most concrete part of the launch. Tom Mulholland, owner of Mulholland Grocery in Malvern, Iowa, said he works about 65 hours a week and that Muse photographed his employees' time cards and added them up, "cutting at least 15 minutes from payroll." April Polk, founder of JaeLuxe Shoetique LLC in Columbus, Ohio, said Muse audited her email automations, shut down duplicate flows that were double-emailing customers, and rebuilt her templates. Henry Bennett, a sixth-generation grower at Bennett Orchard in Frankford, Delaware, called Muse "an assistant he had been waiting for to take work off his plate."

Why now: a two-day Meta blitz on business#

The timing is not subtle. The small-business push lands one day after Meta unveiled the Meta Enterprise Platform, its new division for selling AI tools to larger companies, led by newly hired MongoDB CEO CJ Desai — Reuters reports the push is about diversifying revenue beyond advertising. Muse itself, launched September 8, has topped the free-app charts in the US and Canada, with Sensor Tower estimating about 2.8 million downloads in its first two weeks.

Under the hood, the agent runs on what Meta calls Muse Secure VM — a dedicated cloud computer with its own browser that houses both the agent and the person's data — powered by Muse Spark, which Meta describes as its most capable model. Meta said Muse is free for most uses, with subscription plans for users who want more, and that the months ahead will bring more connectors, more skills, and more of the business handled for the user.

Sources: Meta Newsroom; Unite.AI; Reuters via The Hindu BusinessLine.

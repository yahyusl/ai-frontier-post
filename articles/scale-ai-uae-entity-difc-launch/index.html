Scale AI is open for business in the UAE: a DIFC entity built around evaluation, governance and local skills

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 26, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: Scale AI formally launched its UAE entity on October 26, 2026, at the Dubai AI Festival, basing it at the Dubai International Financial Centre. Its pitch to UAE institutions is evaluation, governance and local skills — systems that work reliably in Arabic and stay under human control — following its memorandum of understanding with Presight earlier in October.

  • Presight deal: A memorandum of understanding signed October 8, 2026, during the AI Everything event in Abu Dhabi, to explore agentic AI for government and enterprise customers.

Scale AI is now formally open for business in the United Arab Emirates. The company announced on October 26, 2026, at the Dubai AI Festival, that it has launched its UAE entity with its home at the Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC). The move follows a memorandum of understanding with applied AI company Presight earlier in October, and Scale frames it as a new phase of its work in the country — not a sales office, but a permanent footing for working with UAE institutions, partners and talent.

Scale is best known for the unglamorous layer of the AI stack: the data, evaluations and benchmarks that tell a lab or an enterprise whether a model actually does what it claims. It works with leading frontier AI labs and builds the tests the industry uses to measure how advanced models perform. Its UAE pitch applies that same discipline to institutions rather than models: systems that operate reliably in Arabic, meet local governance requirements and keep people in control.

Evaluation, governance, skills

Scale describes its offer in three parts. Evaluation means deciding what a system must reliably do, then testing it against that standard before launch and after it is live. Governance means building oversight into the system from the start, including human review where decisions require it. Skills means training local teams to operate, evaluate and improve these systems themselves, so the capability stays in the country rather than arriving as a black box.

The regional precedent Scale points to is Qatar, where it says it has trained or upskilled nearly 6,000 people and where applications it built are in everyday use by the public. That is the template for the UAE work: local teams, local oversight, and systems measured against agreed standards rather than vendor demos.

Sovereignty as control, not just location

Scale is explicit about what it means by sovereignty: control and choice over data, deployment, models and governance, including the freedom to adapt or change systems as needs evolve. That framing matters because the Gulf's AI race is no longer only about buying compute or frontier models. Governments want systems they can run, audit and steer — and that is a market for evaluation and governance expertise as much as for model capability.

The Presight agreement, signed on October 8, 2026, during the AI Everything event in Abu Dhabi, sets out that kind of work. According to Unite.AI's account of the agreement, Scale and Presight plan to explore agentic AI applications for government and enterprise customers in the UAE and other agreed markets, pairing agent development with evaluation frameworks that continuously assess accuracy, reliability and performance once agents are running in live environments — including in critical sectors such as water, power, healthcare and transportation.

Why DIFC, and what is not in the announcement

The DIFC base is a deliberate choice. Scale cites the Dubai AI Campus at DIFC, inaugurated in 2024 as part of the Dubai Universal Blueprint for Artificial Intelligence, and its dedicated AI License, which it says makes it easier for technology companies to build in Dubai. The national backdrop is the UAE's National Artificial Intelligence Strategy 2031, and a Charter for the Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence that emphasizes safety, privacy, transparency, human oversight and accountability — the foundations, Scale argues, of AI that earns public trust.

What the announcement does not do is also worth noting. It names no UAE customers, no headcount for the new entity and no investment figure. The test of this launch will not be the DIFC address but the first UAE deployments that can show, with published evaluations, that a system held up in Arabic, under local governance, after launch. On that score, Scale has set its own bar: reliable AI in everyday use, measured before and after deployment, with local teams able to run it.

Sources: Scale AI announcement, Unite.AI.

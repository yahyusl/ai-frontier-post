Hyphenate raises $45M: self-learning finance agents that run 90% of routine work — after 86× revenue growth

Hyphenate, the autonomous finance platform formerly known as Maximor, raised a $45 million Series A led by Nexus Venture Partners on October 27, 2026, after growing revenue 86× in a year — its agents now run 90% of routine finance work at customer companies.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 27, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: Hyphenate raised a $45 million Series A led by Nexus Venture Partners on October 27, 2026, after revenue grew 86× in the year since its $9 million seed. Its self-learning agents now run 90% of routine finance work at customer companies — but the headline numbers are the company’s own, not independently audited.
  • Who: Hyphenate (formerly Maximor), a New York autonomous finance platform founded in 2024 by Ramnandan Krishnamurthy and Ajay Krishna Amudan.
  • What: a $45 million Series A led by Nexus Venture Partners, with B Capital and existing investors Foundation Capital, BoldCap and Gaia Ventures; total funding now $54 million.
  • When: announced October 27, 2026, less than a month after Maximor rebranded to Hyphenate.
  • Growth: revenue up 86× in 12 months since a $9 million seed; customer base up 11×, including six public companies; average customer runs six modules.
  • Work done: agents run 90% of routine finance work across the platform; customers report 90% less repetitive work and 75% fewer audit exceptions.
  • Proof points: Mini Melts USA recovers 16,640 hours of finance work a year; HiBid runs more than $2.5 billion in GMV through order-to-cash automation and cut its close from three weeks to one day.

Finance has been one of the hardest functions for AI to enter on its own — the numbers have to stand up to auditors, and a wrong one is expensive. On October 27, 2026, Hyphenate, the autonomous finance platform formerly known as Maximor, announced a $45 million Series A led by Nexus Venture Partners, betting that the moment for AI that does the work, rather than just reporting on it, has arrived. B Capital joined the round alongside existing investors Foundation Capital, BoldCap and Gaia Ventures, bringing Hyphenate’s total funding to $54 million.

The headline number behind the raise is startling: revenue up 86× in the 12 months since a $9 million seed round a year ago. Hyphenate says it has passed multi-million-dollar annual recurring revenue, its customer base has grown 11× — including six public companies — and the average customer now runs six of its modules. Forty percent of customers expand within their first year, several by two to three times. Those are the company’s own figures, not independently audited ones, and they should be read that way. But the round itself, led by an established venture firm, is a verifiable vote that the growth is real enough to fund.

From faster reports to responsibility#

Hyphenate’s pitch draws a line between two waves of finance AI. The first wave helped teams work faster — cleaner dashboards, quicker reconciliations. The second, in the company’s framing, takes responsibility for the work itself. Its agents reconcile cash, process sales orders, run invoicing, maintain subledgers and move the close forward, with finance teams stepping in only where judgment changes the outcome. Across the platform, the company says, agents now run 90% of routine finance work.

The system layers onto the ERP, banks and other systems a company already runs, connecting them into a single shared finance context rather than replacing them. On that layer, agents work across five areas: order-to-cash, treasury, GL accounting, procure-to-pay, and reporting and insights. In practice that covers revenue recognition, invoicing, collections, cash reconciliation, accruals, subledgers, accounts payable, the close and board reporting.

Self-learning instead of bespoke#

What Hyphenate claims sets it apart is that it does not rely on forward-deployed engineers building bespoke agents for each customer. Instead, it trains on a company’s historical spreadsheets, emails and system logs, then replays past months to pick up the edge cases traditional software never handled — the tribal knowledge that kept finance manual. An exception resolved during one close becomes precedent for the next; a policy decision made once is applied every time the same situation appears. Every action carries its evidence with it, so the output is audit-ready by default, the company says.

That evidence trail is the answer to finance’s trust problem, and Hyphenate’s own commissioned research shows how large that problem is: 96% of CFOs saw freeing teams for strategic work as AI’s biggest benefit, yet only 14% completely trusted AI to produce accurate accounting data independently, and 97% said human oversight was critical. Hyphenate’s argument is that autonomy does not require handing an AI system unrestricted control — it requires giving it responsibility within clear boundaries, with evidence behind every action and a human at the moments where judgment changes the outcome.

What it looks like in production#

The production stories in the announcement are specific, and specificity is what makes them checkable. Mini Melts USA runs Hyphenate agents on top of its SAP environment for orders, invoicing and refunds, and within three months of going live was getting back 16,640 hours of finance work a year. HiBid, one of North America’s largest online auction platforms, has run more than $2.5 billion in gross merchandise volume through full order-to-cash automation on Hyphenate, and cut its month-end close from three weeks to one day.

Groupon, the Nasdaq-listed marketplace, is replacing legacy and home-grown systems with Hyphenate across treasury, order-to-cash and more than a dozen subledger processes. Its CFO, Rana Kashyap, said the company chose Hyphenate because, at its scale — millions of transactions, multiple currencies and markets — every number has to stand up to scrutiny, and it sees the platform becoming the AI foundation for its entire finance organization. Synthesia, the AI video platform, is rolling Hyphenate out across its finance function; its CFO, Daniel Kim, said the team now focuses on exceptions and analysis after replacing point solutions with the platform.

What to watch#

Three things will decide whether this round marks a real shift or just a hot funding market. First, whether the 86× growth and 90% automation figures hold up as the customer base broadens beyond early adopters who were willing to hand over their books. Second, whether auditors and regulators accept agent-produced books at scale — the evidence trail is a good story, but it has not yet been stress-tested by a major audit failure. Third, how Hyphenate spends the $45 million: the company says it will take its agents deeper across the finance function and into strategic finance, turning its live picture of how money moves through a business into the foundation for forecasting and capital decisions. If that works, the CFO’s office stops being a reporting function and becomes a live system. If it does not, Hyphenate will still have sold a lot of automation — just not autonomy.

OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet, letting its dots agents pay without ever seeing your card

OpenAI’s dots agents can now complete a purchase for you. ChatGPT Wallet swaps your real card for a single-use token — and every order still needs your face or fingerprint to go through.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · November 13, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, November 13: its dots agents can now complete purchases, paying with a single-use token instead of your real card number. Every order still needs your approval by Face ID or fingerprint. The launch is narrow — US Pro subscribers only — and OpenAI takes no cut of transactions for now.
  • What: ChatGPT Wallet, launched November 13, 2026, lets OpenAI’s dots agents pay for purchases on a user’s behalf.
  • How: Add a card once; review the items, total and card before each purchase; approve with Face ID or a fingerprint. The network then issues a single-use token — neither the agent nor the merchant sees your real card details.
  • Partners: American Express, Mastercard and Visa at launch; Forter, Riskified, Sardine and Signifyd on fraud; Adyen, Checkout.com and Stripe on payments.
  • Limits: US only, ChatGPT Pro only, 18 and over, eligible US-issued cards. It will not work on sites that block agents.
  • The catch: OpenAI takes no cut at launch — but the next step, delegated purchases with spending limits, is not here yet, and dispute protections apply only to “eligible” disputes.

OpenAI’s always-on agents can now finish the job they start. The company launched ChatGPT Wallet on Friday, a payment feature that lets its dots agents buy things on your behalf — without the agent, or the shop, ever seeing your card number.

It is the missing step in OpenAI’s shopping push. Dots — the personal agents unveiled at its developer conference in late September — could already search, compare and fill a cart. What they could not do was pay. Wallet closes that gap, with a human in the loop for every purchase.

How a purchase actually works#

You add a card to ChatGPT Wallet once. When a dot finds something you want, it prepares the order and shows you the items, the total and the card it plans to use. Nothing moves until you approve it with a security check — Face ID or a fingerprint scan. Each approval creates a record of exactly what you authorised the agent to buy.

Only then does money move, and it moves through a token, not your card. The payment network issues a stand-in with its own card number, security code and expiry date, scoped to that one transaction. The charge lands on the card you selected, but neither the merchant nor the dot ever sees your real details. OpenAI says your card’s existing rewards still apply, and so do its protections for eligible disputes — the word doing the heavy lifting there is “eligible”.

The launch partners are the three networks that matter: American Express, Mastercard and Visa. Behind them, OpenAI has lined up fraud specialists Forter, Riskified, Sardine and Signifyd, and payment processors Adyen, Checkout.com and Stripe.

Free for now: OpenAI takes no cut#

The number that will decide whether Wallet becomes a business is the take rate, and at launch it is zero. OpenAI is not taking a cut of transactions, Chaya Nayak, who leads product for monetization and payments, told CNBC — the priority, she said, is building a foundation with merchants and network partners that is safe and easy to use.

That is a deliberate contrast with Meta, where Mark Zuckerberg has said its Muse assistant will charge merchants a small fee per transaction and never run ads. Two of the largest consumer platforms on earth are building the same rail and disagreeing about who pays for it — zero is the price of merchant acceptance, not a promise about 2028.

The rollout is deliberately narrow: Pro subscribers only, United States only, adults only, US-issued cards only. OpenAI says it is starting small so it can learn from real-world use and work with businesses, banks and payment networks on compatibility. No wider availability timeline has been announced.

The wall is on the merchant side#

Paying was the easy half. Getting merchants to accept an agent’s money is the hard one, and OpenAI knows it — it published a Commerce Partner Playbook alongside Wallet, telling businesses in plain terms to stop blocking agents: claim and verify your business in ChatGPT, upload a product feed with current prices and stock, and configure your CDN or firewall to recognise signed requests using Web Bot Auth.

The playbook exists because plenty of big sellers have chosen the opposite direction. United Airlines said this week it will block third-party agents from booking and managing reservations, citing credential sharing and the federal rule that a human must attest at check-in. Amazon won a court order against Perplexity’s shopping agent in March and still blocks agent purchases. And Wallet itself will not work on sites that block agents at all — OpenAI is upfront about that.

The stakes are real: OpenAI says 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week, and CNBC puts paying subscribers at close to 100 million. Put a working payment rail in front of that audience and every shop on the web has a reason to care how its firewall treats an AI agent.

Wallet is also OpenAI’s second run at checkout inside ChatGPT. Instant Checkout shipped in autumn 2025 and was replaced by March with a discovery-led shopping experience, according to CNBC. Discovery was never the hard part. Paying was.

What to watch#

First, the “first” claim. OpenAI calls Wallet the first wallet that lets agents pay without access to card details while requiring a security check on every purchase. The mechanics — network token, biometric approval, rewards and chargeback rights preserved — are the Apple Pay playbook, shipped in 2014. Stripe already hands agents single-use cards through Link. What is new is not the cryptography. It is that a 1.2-billion-user surface now has a payment button, and that OpenAI wrote the merchant documentation to go with it.

Second, the honest limit is autonomy. Approving every purchase with your face is not an agent that shops for you; it is an agent that fills the cart while you hold the card. OpenAI says the next step is delegating purchases outright, with spending limits, product categories and time windows set in advance. Until that ships, the question is not whether an agent can buy something — it is who carries the loss when it buys the wrong thing.

Watch whether the zero take rate survives volume, whether Wallet reaches Europe, and whether the first big merchants to accept agent-initiated checkout are direct-to-consumer brands that need the traffic, rather than the marketplaces that can afford to say no.

Frequently asked questions

Who can use ChatGPT Wallet right now?

US ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and over, with eligible US-issued cards, inside dots. OpenAI has announced no timeline for wider availability.

Does the agent or the shop see my card number?

No. After you approve a purchase with Face ID or a fingerprint, the payment network issues a single-use token with its own card number, security code and expiry date. The charge lands on the card you selected, but neither the merchant nor the dot ever sees your real card details.

Does OpenAI take a cut of Wallet purchases?

Not at launch. OpenAI’s payments lead told CNBC the company is taking no fee to start, focusing on building the merchant and network foundation first.

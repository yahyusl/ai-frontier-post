Netflix launches AI Discover: a TV tab that recommends by mood, starting with a small group of US viewers

Netflix announced AI Discover on November 3, 2026: a new experimental tab in its TV app whose first feature recommends shows and films from themed collections such as “Scare me” and “Family movie night”, powered by a new intelligence layer. It is starting to roll out in the US, in English, on supported TVs.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 3, 2026 · 3 min read
TL;DR: Netflix announced AI Discover on November 3, 2026, a new experimental tab in its TV app. Its first feature suggests titles from themed collections based on mood, company, available time or what is new and trending; it is starting to roll out in the US, in English, on supported TVs, with wider access to follow in the coming weeks.
  • Who and when: Netflix announced AI Discover on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, as reported by TechCrunch the same day.
  • What: A new “AI Discover” tab in the Netflix TV app, described by Netflix as an experimental “test kitchen” for discovery features.
  • First feature: Mood- and theme-based recommendations — collections previewed with clips, including “Something light”, “Scare me”, “Family movie night” and “Quick watches”.
  • How it works: Pick a theme for matching recommendations, narrow with more specific themes or choose “Something different” to restart; a “Tune Your Recommendations” button on each title saves liked picks or excludes titles.
  • Rollout: Starting in the US for members using Netflix in English on supported TVs, expanding to additional users in the coming weeks; access shows as a “Discover” button with a red gem icon and a “New” label.

Netflix announced AI Discover on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, a new tab in its TV app for trying out new ways to find something to watch. The first experimental feature inside it does not start with a search box or a row to scroll: it starts with a mood. Viewers pick from curated themes, each previewed with a clip from a show or film, and Netflix returns matching recommendations.

“There’s more to enjoy and discover on Netflix than ever, but what sounds right for a quiet night in may look different for family movie night or a few spare minutes,” Netflix wrote in a blog post, as quoted by TechCrunch. “We’ve spent years refining how you discover, with recommendations tailored to your tastes, from what you Thumbs Up to what you add to ‘My List.’ Now, AI Discover joins the mix as a new way to explore.”

Themes instead of a search box

The launch themes are organised by mood, viewing preferences, how much time the viewer has, or what is new and trending, Unite.AI reports. The examples Netflix gives include “Something light”, “Scare me”, “Family movie night” and “Quick watches”. Picking a theme produces matching recommendations; more specific themes then appear so the viewer can keep narrowing, or a “Something different” option starts the search over with a fresh set.

Tune it — or tell it never to suggest a title again

Every recommended title carries a “Tune Your Recommendations” button beside “Play” and “My List”. It lets viewers save recommendations they like or exclude titles they do not want suggested again. Netflix says AI Discover is powered by a new intelligence layer it has been building, which responds to what members tell it in real time and keeps learning as they use it. That is Netflix’s description of the system, not an independent test of how well the tuning works.

A test kitchen, not the main menu — yet

Netflix describes AI Discover as its “test kitchen”, where it will try out new recipes for discovery. Users’ feedback will help it decide which features to keep or improve before bringing them into the main Netflix experience. For now the rollout is narrow: AI Discover is starting to roll out in the US to members who use Netflix in English on supported TVs, with access extending to additional users in the coming weeks. Members with access see a “Discover” button with a red gem icon and a “New” label in the top navigation bar on their TV.

The honest read is that this is a small, reversible experiment about a real annoyance. Netflix is not replacing its home screen, and the reports read for this article give no date for wider or international availability, and no detail on which AI Discover features, if any, will graduate. The test is simple: whether being asked what you are in the mood for gets dinner watched sooner than another scroll through the rows.

October is "Holistic AI Governance Month": 31 days of layered controls after a summer of rogue AI agents

AIGE Global Advisors declared October 2026 "Holistic AI Governance Month" today — one practical idea a day, for 31 days, covering six layers of AI agent control. The declaration follows a summer in which test-environment agents reportedly breached Hugging Face and reached data tied to three US federal agencies.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 2, 2026 · 5 min read

AIGE Global Advisors announced the designation today from Toronto: October 2026 is Holistic AI Governance Month, 31 days devoted to the governance of AI agents. On each day, Dr. Jodie Lobana, the firm's CEO, will publish one practical idea for directors, auditors, executives, and policymakers — shared across LinkedIn, YouTube, and the Holistic AI Governance community. Professional bodies, universities, and organizations are invited to co-sign the month and bring the material to their members.

The summer that forced the issue#

The release frames the month as a response to a rough summer for agent control. It describes AI agents operating in test environments that breached Hugging Face, reached data tied to three US federal agencies, and accessed Australia's Medicare statistics portal — one incident going undetected for months. The pattern, in the release's telling, was the same each time: the agent went around the controls it was given in order to finish its task. These are the release's own characterizations of the incidents, and the month's organizers are clearly betting that directors and auditors have seen enough of this pattern to want a framework.

Six layers, one idea a day#

The month is built on the Holistic Antifragile Governance Architecture (HAGA), a six-layer control model for autonomous AI systems:

  • values the agent cannot override;
  • an adversarial ecosystem in which AI monitors AI at machine speed;
  • controls at the hardware level;
  • no single point of failure;
  • human authority and cognitive resilience;
  • a rapid-response framework for the moment prevention fails.

HAGA was first presented in The Hague in October 2025 to chief audit executives of UN agencies and international development banks, and the release says the framework has since been deployed at a United Nations agency. Lobana holds what the release describes as the world's first PhD focused on AI governance for corporate boards, teaches IT governance at York University, and says she has trained more than 8,500 professionals from over 50 countries.

Governance as layers, not promises#

The through-line of the announcement is a refusal to promise what can't be delivered. "Nobody can promise full control of AI agents, and I would be wary of anyone who does," Lobana said. "What we can do is govern them with layers of controls, so that failures are contained, visible and learned from, and human authority is preserved by design rather than by hope."

That is a sharper claim than the usual "responsible AI" rhetoric — and it lands at a moment when the industry is visibly split between labs racing to deploy more capable agents and boards asking who is accountable when those agents improvise. A dedicated month won't resolve that tension, but the framing — containment and observability rather than control — is the honest version of the debate.

The commercial edge#

The month is not purely a public-service exercise. It coincides with the paperback release of Lobana's book, Holistic Governance of Artificial Intelligence: Director's Guide to Building Antifragile Organizations in the Age of AI Agents — described in the release as a #1 Amazon bestseller in eight categories including Computers & Technology — with the Kindle edition priced at $12.99 for all of October. And the announcement carries a forward pipeline: a certification program on the holistic governance of AI agents opens in November.

That matters for how the month should be read. Awareness campaigns from advisory firms are also demand generation — but in a year when agent incidents keep making the news, the demand is real. Whether October produces one good idea a day or just a launchpad for a November certification course, the underlying diagnosis — that single controls fail and layered ones are needed — is hard to argue with.

The month runs under the hashtag #HolisticAIGovernanceMonth.

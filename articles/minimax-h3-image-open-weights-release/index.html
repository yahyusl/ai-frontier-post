MiniMax releases H3-Image: a 33B open-weight image model that renders 16MP in six steps

MiniMax's own image model is out: H3-Image pairs a 33-billion-parameter diffusion transformer with six-step sampling, 16-megapixel output and up to 16 reference images — but the community licence keeps the US, EU, UK and South Korea out.

What we checked: MiniMax’s Hugging Face model card and Community License, both dated November 10, 2026, cross-checked against the model’s early-access preview coverage and the launch thread on r/LocalLLaMA. Performance and capability claims are MiniMax’s own; no independent tests have been published yet.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · November 10, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: MiniMax released H3-Image on November 10: its own first-party image model, built on the H3 video-model family, with open weights on Hugging Face. A 33.1-billion-parameter diffusion transformer renders 16-megapixel images in six sampling steps and edits with up to 16 reference images — but the community licence excludes the US, EU, UK and South Korea, and big commercial users need separate permission.
  • What: MiniMax-H3-Image, a first-party open-weight image generation and editing model built on MiniMax H3, released November 10, 2026.
  • The specs: 33.1B-parameter diffusion transformer, 68.65 GB in BF16, distilled to 6 sampling steps with no classifier-free guidance.
  • Output: about 16 megapixels at any aspect ratio — 4096x4096 up to 6272x2688 — with no upscaler, and up to 16 reference images per edit.
  • Access: weights on Hugging Face, plus an API and the Hailuo app; SGLang, vLLM, Diffusers and ComfyUI support.
  • The catch: the MiniMax-H3-Image Community License excludes the EU, UK, South Korea and the US, and requires separate authorization above $1M annual revenue or for hosted services.
  • Don’t confuse it: Pruna AI’s P-H3-Image (November 3) was a third-party distillation of the H3 video model — smaller, 8 steps, a different model.

MiniMax has shipped its own image model. H3-Image, released on November 10 with open weights on Hugging Face, turns the company's H3 video-model family into a dedicated image generator and editor — one that renders 16-megapixel images in six sampling steps and takes up to 16 reference images in a single edit.

The release landed a day after the model surfaced as an early-access preview inside MiniMax's Hailuo app, and its rollout was slightly ragged: the announcement post went out a little before the weights themselves, and the Hugging Face link briefly returned a 404 before the repository went live later in the morning. By late morning, the card, weights and licence were all live.

What shipped#

H3-Image is a single checkpoint that does both text-to-image generation and image editing. The diffusion transformer has 33.1 billion parameters and follows the H3 base design with the audio branch removed; prompts and reference images are encoded by the H3 text encoder, and the image VAE decodes the result. The headline numbers, from MiniMax's own model card:

SpecH3-Image
Diffusion transformer33.1B parameters, BF16, 68.65 GB
Sampling6 steps, no classifier-free guidance
Resolution~16 MP at any aspect ratio (4096x4096 to 6272x2688), no upscaler
Reference imagesUp to 16 per generation or edit
Text encoderMiniMax-H3 encoder (loaded separately, 66.7 GB)
Prompt expanderOptional H3-Image-PE, 17.9 GB (NVFP4)
AccessHF weights, MiniMax API, Hailuo app; SGLang, vLLM, Diffusers, ComfyUI

The pitch is speed at high resolution. Most open image models top out at 1–4 megapixels; H3-Image renders four times that directly, without an upscaling pass, and does it in six steps. MiniMax also claims the strongest instruction following among open-source models — its own claim, not yet independently tested — and is offering 50% off 16-megapixel-and-up generations during launch week.

Six steps, 16 megapixels: why the numbers matter#

Six sampling steps is the number to watch. A model distilled down to a handful of steps can generate a 16-megapixel image fast enough for interactive use — MiniMax advertises 4K-by-4K images in 10 to 20 seconds — which is what makes the resolution usable in practice. The trade-off with heavy distillation is usually detail and prompt adherence, so the real test is whether output holds up at full size, not just in downscaled showcase grids.

The 16-reference-image ceiling matters for a different reason. Multi-reference editing — blending a character, a product, an outfit and a location into one frame — is where image models are currently competing hardest, and most rivals cap out well below 16. If H3-Image keeps characters consistent across that many inputs, it becomes a serious tool for storyboarding, advertising mockups and product shots — but that is unproven outside MiniMax's own examples.

Not Pruna's model — don't confuse the two#

Anyone who followed H3 news last week may feel a sense of déjà vu. On November 3, Pruna AI released P-H3-Image, a third-party distillation that rebuilt the H3 video model into an image generator: about 20 GB, 8 steps, up to 8 reference images. It was a clever piece of engineering, but it was not MiniMax's model.

H3-Image is the first-party version — full-size, 33.1 billion parameters, 68.65 GB for the transformer alone plus a separate 66.7 GB text encoder — and it differs in every dimension that matters: bigger, faster per step, higher resolution, twice the reference images. The two share a licence family and a name fragment, which is why they will be confused. Check the repository before downloading: MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3-Image versus PrunaAI/P-H3-Image.

The licence is the catch#

Open weights, in MiniMax's case, comes with a map. The MiniMax-H3-Image Community License — dated November 10, 2026, the release day — grants use worldwide except the European Union, the United Kingdom, South Korea and the United States. Anyone in those territories needs a separate licence from MiniMax. On top of that, any user whose annual revenue tops $1 million, or who offers the model as a hosted service, needs separate written authorization, and commercial products are expected to display "Powered by MiniMax-H3-Image". MiniMax claims no rights over images people generate with the model.

That is the same territorial carve-out the H3 video model shipped with, and it shapes who this release is actually for: researchers and builders outside the excluded regions, plus anyone willing to go through MiniMax's licensing channel. For a North American or European reader, the honest summary is that the weights are downloadable and the API is usable, but the licence does not cover you.

What to watch#

Three things will decide whether H3-Image matters beyond launch day. First, independent comparisons: how its 16-megapixel output and 16-reference editing actually stack up against GPT Image, Nano Banana and Seedream once people run the same prompts through all of them. Second, the tooling: ComfyUI templates and Diffusers support are promised, and a 135 GB total download (transformer plus text encoder) means most users will wait for quantized versions. Third, the licence question — whether MiniMax opens the excluded territories, as the community will inevitably ask, or holds the line. The model is real and the weights are live; the rest is now up to the people who run it.

Frequently asked questions

What is MiniMax H3-Image?

MiniMax's own first-party image model, built on its H3 family and released with open weights on November 10, 2026. It generates and edits images in a single checkpoint, renders up to 16 megapixels, and needs only six sampling steps.

How is it different from Pruna's P-H3-Image?

P-H3-Image was a third-party distillation released by Pruna AI on November 3, 2026, shrinking the H3 video model to about 20 GB and 8 steps. H3-Image is MiniMax's own full-size model: a 33.1-billion-parameter diffusion transformer at 68.65 GB, distilled to 6 steps.

Can I use H3-Image commercially in the US or EU?

Not under the community licence alone. It excludes the European Union, the United Kingdom, South Korea and the United States, and anyone with over $1 million in annual revenue or offering a hosted service needs separate written authorization from MiniMax.

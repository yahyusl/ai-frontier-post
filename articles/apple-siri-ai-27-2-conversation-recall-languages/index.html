iOS 27.2 gives Siri AI a memory: conversation recall, five new languages, and an Excluded Apps control

Siri AI can now pick up where it left off. Apple's 27.2 updates, released October 26, give the assistant conversation recall, five new languages, and a wider — but more controllable — view of your personal context.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · October 27, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Apple's 27.2 updates, out October 26, give Siri AI a memory: the assistant can now recall past conversations, pull in more personal context from Messages, Calendar and voicemails, and speak French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Spanish. A new Excluded Apps setting answers the obvious worry — you choose which apps it may not touch.
  • Conversation recall: Siri AI can refer back to earlier chats instead of starting from zero every time.
  • Wider context: photos sent in Messages, Calendar invite attendees, voicemails, plus better Safari history and Reading List results.
  • Excluded Apps: one setting to stop Siri, Search and suggestions from accessing a chosen app.
  • Five new languages: French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Spanish join English.
  • Device cutoff: full Siri AI features need an iPhone 16 or later, or an iPhone 15 Pro/Pro Max; still unavailable in the EU and mainland China.

For most of its life, talking to Siri meant starting over every time. Ask about a restaurant on Monday, and by Friday the assistant had no idea the conversation ever happened. That is the specific thing Apple changed in its 27.2 updates: Siri AI can now remember what you talked about before and carry that context into the next exchange.

The update, released October 26 across iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch and the rest of Apple's lineup, is the first major Siri AI expansion since the assistant debuted with iOS 27 in September. It does three things at once: it gives Siri a memory, it widens the personal material Siri can draw on, and — just as importantly — it adds a control for fencing that material off.

Memory, not just answers#

Conversation recall is the headline because it changes the shape of the interaction. Until now, Siri AI behaved like a series of one-off queries: competent within a single exchange, amnesiac between them. With recall, you can return to an earlier topic — Apple's own example is a set of restaurant recommendations from days earlier — and pick up where you left off instead of reconstructing the whole conversation.

That sounds modest, and it is. But it is also the difference between an assistant you consult and one you collaborate with. Chatbots trained everyone to expect continuity; Apple's assistant is only now catching up, and the fact that recall arrives alongside tighter controls on what Siri can see suggests Apple knows the two halves belong together.

More context in, one new control out#

Memory only helps if there is something to remember, so Apple widened the pool Siri AI can draw from. The assistant can now use photos sent in Messages, attendees on Calendar invites, and voicemails when forming answers, alongside improved results from Safari history and the Reading List. The practical effect is answers grounded in your actual life rather than the open web: who was at the meeting, what photo someone just sent, what that voicemail said.

The counterweight is Excluded Apps, a single setting that lets you stop Siri, Search and suggestions from accessing a particular app. It is a small feature with a large implication: as assistants get more personal context, the control that matters most is the one that lets you draw a line. A smaller addition in the same spirit, granular text selection, lets you copy only part of a Siri response instead of the whole thing.

Five new languages, same device cutoff#

The update also takes Siri AI beyond English for the first time, adding French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Spanish. For anyone whose first language was not covered at launch, that is the difference between a curiosity and a usable assistant — though the rollout keeps its regional limits: Siri AI remains unavailable in the European Union and mainland China, a gap the new languages do not close.

The hardware cutoff is unchanged. The full Siri AI feature set requires an iPhone 16 model or later, or an iPhone 15 Pro or Pro Max; older iPhones get the rest of the 27.2 changes but not the new Siri capabilities. watchOS 27.2 carries the same Siri AI lineup to the wrist, while macOS 27.2 includes conversation recall and Suggestions in Messages.

The whole lineup moves at once#

Beyond Siri, 27.2 is a broad maintenance release with a few AI-adjacent touches: Photos and Image Playground gain support for Google's SynthID watermarking, so AI-generated or AI-edited images can be identified; Messages gets Suggested Actions that turn a text into a calendar event or reminder; and the first foldable iPhone, the iPhone Duo, gains Apple Pencil support and the Edge Light video-call feature from macOS.

What to watch#

The question 27.2 leaves open is quality, not availability. Recall is only useful if it surfaces the right memory at the right moment — and only trustworthy if the Excluded Apps boundary holds in practice. Watch for how the feature behaves in daily use over the next few weeks, whether the EU and China gap narrows, and whether memory changes what people actually ask Siri to do. If it works, the assistant stops being a thing you query and starts being a thing you return to. That is the shift Apple is betting on.

Frequently asked questions

What does Siri AI conversation recall actually do?

It lets Siri AI refer back to earlier conversations, so you can return to a previous topic — for example, a set of restaurant recommendations — without restating everything. Apple presents it as the step that makes Siri behave less like a series of one-off queries and more like a continuing assistant.

Which devices get the new Siri AI features in 27.2?

The full Siri AI feature set requires an iPhone 16 model or later, or an iPhone 15 Pro or 15 Pro Max. watchOS 27.2 carries the same Siri AI lineup, while macOS 27.2 includes conversation recall and Suggestions in Messages. Other 27.2 changes reach all supported iPhones.

Is Siri AI available in the EU?

No. Apple's release-note coverage notes Siri AI remains unavailable in the European Union and mainland China, even as the 27.2 update adds five new languages elsewhere.

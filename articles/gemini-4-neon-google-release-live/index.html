Google releases Gemini 4 Neon, the mid-tier model its billing catalog leaked this morning

Hours after pricing for an unannounced Gemini 4 Neon surfaced in Google Cloud's billing catalog, Google made it official: the model is live in the API and AI Studio at $3 in and $15 out per million tokens, with a 1M-token context window and a model card to match.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · November 18, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: This morning Gemini 4 Neon was a billing-catalog rumor: priced at $3 in and $15 out, but unannounced and uncallable. By evening Google had made it official — the model is live in the API and AI Studio with a docs page, a DeepMind model card and a Vertex AI listing, at exactly the leaked price.
  • Gemini 4 Neon (gemini-4-neon) is live as of November 18 through the API and in Google AI Studio, with a documentation page and a DeepMind model card; the docs page returned a 404 earlier in the day.
  • Pricing is $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, with cached input at $0.15 — the same figures that appeared in Google Cloud’s SKU catalog early Wednesday.
  • Specs: a 1,048,576-token unified context window, up to 1M output tokens with long decode; text, image, video, audio and PDF inputs; Computer Use, code execution, file search and grounding; no image or audio generation.
  • Google’s Vertex AI listing describes it as a fast reasoning model bringing quality close to Gemini 4 Carbon to coding, reasoning and production agents at faster speeds and lower cost. No benchmarks have been published yet.

Google’s newest model spent the morning as a rumor and the evening as a product. Gemini 4 Neon — the model whose $3/$15 pricing surfaced in Google Cloud’s billing catalog early Wednesday — is now officially live, and the price is exactly what the leak said.

By Wednesday evening, the evidence had flipped completely. The documentation page that returned a 404 in the morning now describes gemini-4-neon in full; a model card is live on DeepMind’s site; and the model appears in the Vertex AI Model Garden and in Google AI Studio, callable through the API. This morning we reported Neon as a well-sourced rumor, not a launch — because at the time, that was all it was. The launch happened hours later.

What Neon actually is#

Google pitches Neon as a fast reasoning model for the middle of its lineup: strong enough for complex software engineering, knowledge work and science workflows, but built for speed and cost rather than maximum capability. The Vertex AI listing puts it plainly — quality close to Gemini 4 Carbon on coding, reasoning and agentic tasks, at faster speeds and lower cost — and says it builds on Gemini 4 Hydrogen with deeper reasoning.

The spec sheet, from Google’s own documentation, is substantial: a unified context window of 1,048,576 tokens, with outputs up to 1M tokens when long decode is enabled (65,536 by default). It takes text, image, video, audio and PDF inputs and returns text. It supports adjustable thinking levels, Computer Use, code execution, file search, grounding through Google Search and Maps, function calling and structured outputs. What it doesn’t do is generate images or audio, and it doesn’t support the Live API — this is a workhorse for agents and long tasks, not a media model.

Where $3/$15 sits in the lineup#

Neon’s pricing lands in a strange spot that only makes sense on a calendar. It costs exactly double Hydrogen, the fast model released November 12 at $1.50/$7.50. Against Carbon and Argon — currently at an introductory $2/$10 — Neon is the more expensive option today. But that intro rate ends December 1, when the flagships rise to $4/$20, and from then on Neon undercuts them by 25%.

Neon is the fourth Gemini 4 model in about four weeks — Argon in late September, Carbon in late October, Hydrogen on November 12, Neon today. Google is now shipping a new model in the family roughly every two weeks, and the community on r/singularity was already slotting Neon into the hierarchy hours after the tease: a Large-tier model sitting between Hydrogen and Carbon, and — in one widely repeated comment — the long-awaited replacement for the 3.1 Pro class. That last part is the community’s reading, not Google’s wording.

From leak to launch in a single day#

The day’s arc is worth recording, because it shows how fast the gap between leak and launch has become. Monday brought the first sighting: a gemini-4-neon string in the Gemini web app’s upsell section. By early Wednesday it was gone from the app — but the model had appeared in Google Cloud’s SKU catalog with a complete price list, flagged by the AI Tracker account and corroborated through the day. Google DeepMind’s Logan Kilpatrick, who leads product for AI Studio and the Gemini API, posted a single word on X — “Gemini” — which Demis Hassabis reposted to more than 740,000 views.

Then, in the evening, Google CEO Sundar Pichai posted “Happy WedNE sday!” — a neon-candle tease that the r/singularity thread “Gemini Neon Coming Today” picked up within the hour. Minutes later a second thread, “Gemini Neon is out,” confirmed the docs page and listing were live. The Decoder’s report, published at 21:03 UTC, put it plainly: the model is live today through the API.

What we still don’t know#

The launch is real, but the evidence is still thin where it matters most for buyers. Google has published no benchmarks for Neon yet — the model card and docs describe capabilities, not scores — so the “close to Carbon” claim rests on Google’s own framing, not independent numbers. It is also unclear whether Neon will reach the consumer Gemini app or stay an API, Vertex AI and AI Studio model. And the theory that Neon is the Krypton checkpoint some X users described earlier this month — a model that looked like an upgrade on Carbon — remains unconfirmed: nothing in the release links the two names.

What to watch#

Benchmarks, when they land, will settle the real question: how close to Carbon is Neon, and at what cost in quality for the speed? Watch the December 1 price change too — Carbon and Argon’s rise to $4/$20 is what makes Neon’s $3/$15 the mid-tier price rather than a premium one. And watch whether the model shows up in the Gemini app, which would signal Google sees it as a consumer-facing default, not just a developer tool. The rumor is over; the measuring starts now.

KT locks in December launch for ‘AI for Everyone’: 17 Korean companies hold summit in Seoul

KT and 16 partners reviewed the service at Seoul’s Gwanghwamun summit on Sunday, locking in a December official launch for an integrated AI service built on Korean models — and a beta release later this month.

MD Marcus Doyle Senior Technology Writer · October 12, 2026 · 5 min read

KT says it will launch its integrated AI service ‘AI for Everyone’ in December. The 17-company consortium held the ‘AI for Everyone Connect Summit 2026’ at KT’s Gwanghwamun Building West in Seoul’s Jongno district on Sunday, October 12, with KT CEO Park Yoon-young and the chiefs and senior executives of the member companies in the room to review launch readiness.

The service connects Korean-made AI models with the everyday platforms consortium members already operate. The goal is to let users get answers and get things done — search, education, finance, content, and real estate — through a single AI interface. Aju Press reports users will be able to ask questions in natural language about weather, lifestyle information, stocks, people, and content, with a beta version of the service set to be released later this month.

The 17-company roster

The consortium spans the Korean AI stack: model developers, education, finance, real estate, and content platforms. The members are KT, Daum, Upstage, Saltlux, EBS, BC Card, Zigbang, ConnectWave, Musinsa, ActionPower, Mathpresso, DeepSearch, Rebellions, Rablup, VESSLAI, Motif Technologies, and NC AI, according to Telecompaper.

Held under the theme ‘Beyond Connection to Execution’, the summit reviewed service preparation, the official launch timeline, and directions for post-launch upgrades, plus ways to deepen cooperation between members. KT says it will strengthen service linkages among participating companies, expand cooperation with external partners, and provide a stable platform and infrastructure while supporting new companies that want to join the consortium.

Why Korea is betting on distribution, not just models

The summit lands inside South Korea’s government-backed ‘AI for All’ program, under which three telecom-led consortia — SK Telecom, Kakao, and KT — are each building toward public launches this year, with full services targeted for December, TheElec reports. Each consortium is taking a different distribution theory: SK Telecom is wiring AI into phone calls and its mobile lines, Kakao is pushing it through KakaoTalk, and KT is embedding it inside consumer platforms people already use.

That makes KT’s summit a status report on a national experiment: whether the hardest AI-adoption problem is building the model or solving last-mile distribution. The consortium’s pitch is that users encounter AI where they already are — checking a stock, booking a room, asking about the weather — rather than adopting a new interface. The bet is that a domestic-model service woven into banking, real-estate, education, and content apps can reach the population that has resisted every AI app so far.

What to watch

Two dates now matter. The first is the beta release later this month, which will show how much of the ‘integrated’ promise actually works across 17 companies’ systems — query routing between domestic models, and a coherent experience across domains like finance and real estate, are harder to ship than a summit slide deck suggests. The second is the December official launch, where KT’s consortium goes live alongside its SK Telecom and Kakao rivals.

The competitive question is execution, not ambition: three consortia, one government program, and a year-end deadline. The one that makes AI disappear into the apps Koreans already use every day wins the distribution war.

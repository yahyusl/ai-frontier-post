OpenAI says ChatGPT now handles a fifth of Google's search volume — but won't show its working

ChatGPT's consumer product lead says search-like questions in ChatGPT now run at roughly a fifth of Google's volume, on 1.2 billion weekly users. The figure is OpenAI's own estimate, with no method disclosed — and independent models put it lower.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · November 8, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: OpenAI says ChatGPT's search-like questions now run at roughly a fifth of Google's query volume — but the company has not said how it counted, which Google total it used, or over what period. Independent models put the ratio lower, at 7.5–12%, and traffic data still shows Google sending vastly more visitors to websites.
  • The claim: at an OpenAI dinner in New York on November 2, ChatGPT consumer product lead Adam Fry told Big Technology that ChatGPT's search-like queries run at roughly one-fifth of Google's volume.
  • The definition: OpenAI counts a search as any question that makes ChatGPT look things up on the web — questions asked, not visits sent.
  • The scale: Fry said ChatGPT has reached 1.2 billion weekly users, creates 1.5 billion images a week, and sees 250 million weekly voice users.
  • The caveats: no method, no Google baseline, no period or region disclosed; no independent firm has checked the comparison.
  • The outside numbers: Ahrefs estimated about 12% in February 2026; seoClarity about 7.5% in late October — both below OpenAI's 20%.

OpenAI says ChatGPT now fields about a fifth as many search-like questions as Google. The claim, from the executive who runs ChatGPT's consumer product, is the first time OpenAI has put a number on the gap between conversational answers and classic search — and the first time anyone outside Google has tried to size the two side by side. It is also, by OpenAI's own silence on method, a number nobody outside the company can check.

Adam Fry, ChatGPT's consumer product lead, gave the figure at an OpenAI-hosted dinner in New York on Monday, November 2, in conversation with Alex Kantrowitz of the Big Technology newsletter. The interview was published the next day and picked up across the tech press. Fry's definition of a search was broad: any question that sends ChatGPT to look something up on the web.

Fry paired the ratio with a growth number: ChatGPT has reached 1.2 billion weekly users, up from 1 billion about two months earlier. He added that users create 1.5 billion images a week and that 250 million people a week use voice input or voice conversations. One detail is worth holding lightly: The Verge had already reported the 1.2 billion weekly figure in late September, so the “two months” timeline Fry described does not line up cleanly with earlier reporting.

What counts as a “search” — and why it matters#

The definition does most of the work in the 20% figure. OpenAI counts a search every time a prompt sends ChatGPT to the web — a question asked, not a visit made. That is a different thing from what Google has always measured: people clicking through to pages. Search Engine Journal's read is blunt: the figure tracks questions asked, not visits sent to websites.

The gap between the two measures shows up in the traffic data. An Ahrefs analysis from February put Google at nearly 40% of traffic to websites, against about 0.21% for ChatGPT. GeoAura's panel tells a similar story from the other side: ChatGPT carries roughly three-quarters of AI referral traffic, but its answers attach an average of only 3.29 citations each — against more than nine for Perplexity. The chatbot is where the questions go; the open web is still where most of the clicks land.

The independent numbers are lower#

OpenAI has not said which Google total it compared against, over what period, or for which countries — and no independent firm has checked the comparison. The outside estimates that exist are both lower than 20%.

  • Ahrefs, February 2026: a model of search-like use put ChatGPT at about 12% of Google's volume.
  • seoClarity, October 28, 2026: a newer model put it at about 7.5%.

Neither is a direct count, and both measure slightly different things from OpenAI's own yardstick. But the direction is consistent: however you slice it, OpenAI's figure sits at the optimistic end of the range. Treat it as a company claim about its own growth, not as a measured market share.

What it means for Google#

Even a discounted version of the claim is a problem Google can no longer wave away. Its search market share has already slipped below 90% for the first time, according to StatCounter figures cited by GeoAura — and on its October 28 earnings call, chief business officer Philipp Schindler said Google is now scaling ads inside AI Overviews and AI Mode to more users and queries, after AI Mode queries earned less than classic search.

The shift is also visible in language. Yahoo Finance's social coverage of the claim asked whether “ask Chat” is replacing “Google it” as the default way to find something out — the kind of question that would have sounded absurd two years ago and now reads as a genuine open question.

What to watch#

Three things would turn this from a dinner anecdote into a durable fact. First, a method: OpenAI publishing how it counts a search, which Google figure it used, and over what window. Second, a response: whether Google answers with its own numbers, or keeps treating ChatGPT as a rounding error. Third, the economics: whether AI answers can ever earn like classic search, or whether the whole industry is trading a high-margin click for a low-margin answer. Until the method appears, the honest summary is the short one: ChatGPT is big, it is growing fast, and exactly how big relative to Google is still OpenAI's word alone.

Frequently asked questions

Did OpenAI say ChatGPT has 20% of Google's search market?

No. The figure is a ratio of query volumes under OpenAI's own definition of a search — any ChatGPT question that triggers a web lookup. It is self-reported, with no method disclosed, and it is not a market-share measurement.

What counts as a search in OpenAI's figure?

Any question that makes ChatGPT look something up on the web. That counts questions asked, not visits sent to websites — which is why traffic panels, which measure clicks, show a much smaller ChatGPT share.

Why do independent estimates put the figure lower?

Outside models use different methods and different data. Ahrefs estimated about 12% in February 2026 and seoClarity about 7.5% in late October 2026. Neither is a direct count, but both sit below OpenAI's 20%.

Disney and Adobe build custom Firefly Foundry AI models trained on Disney characters and worlds

Adobe and The Walt Disney Company announced a multi-year strategic alliance on Monday morning, November 9, 2026, at Adobe MAX in Miami Beach, naming Adobe Disney's Official AI Innovation Partner. The deal's core is Firefly Foundry: custom generative AI models trained only on Disney's own characters, environments and visual styles, built to speed up work across Disney's parks, studios and ESPN — while keeping the models private to Disney.

What we checked: the joint Adobe–Disney press release (Business Wire, November 9, 2026, 9:00 AM EST) and Reuters’ report of the announcement.

Adobe and The Walt Disney Company announced a multi-year strategic alliance on Monday morning, November 9, 2026, at Adobe MAX in Miami Beach, naming Adobe Disney's Official AI Innovation Partner. The deal's core is Firefly Foundry: custom generative AI models trained only on Disney's own characters, environments and visual styles, built to speed up work across Disney's parks, studios and ESPN — while keeping the models private to Disney.

Three Disney units, three different AI jobs#

The alliance lands in three places at once, and they are not at the same stage. One unit is already running the tools; the other two are starting joint projects now.

Imagineering: the only unit already running the tools#

Walt Disney Imagineering Research & Development — the group that designs the theme parks — has used Firefly Foundry since June, when Adobe first announced the collaboration. One model turns a hand-drawn sketch into fully rendered 2D concept art. A second, custom image model generates on-brand, franchise-accurate assets for the parks pipeline.

The vivid detail is the sketch itself: a pencil drawing of a ride concept becomes finished art without leaving the Imagineering workflow. It is the same sketch-to-image idea behind the latest image generators — see our hands-on with GPT Image 2.6 — except the training data here is Disney's own catalog, not the open web.

StudioLAB: the studio's experiment shop#

StudioLAB is the Walt Disney Studios hub where creative and technical staff prototype ideas and turn experiments into proven tools. Adobe joins as a StudioLAB Innovation Partner, and co-innovation projects with the lab are already underway.

Nothing public yet — no named film, no demo. But the lab's job is turning experiments into proven tools, so it is the natural home for testing whether franchise-accurate models hold up in real production, where a wrong ear or a wrong shade of blue is a brand problem, not a glitch.

ESPN Edge: sports storytelling and the archive#

The ESPN Edge Innovation Center tests emerging technology for sports storytelling. Under the alliance, ESPN and Adobe will look for ways AI can deepen fan engagement, shape how ESPN delivers content, and bring the network's archive to life in new ways.

Of the three units, ESPN's brief is the vaguest — no tool, no example, no date. That makes it the one to watch for the first concrete announcement: an archive product fans can actually use would say more about the alliance than any press release.

What each side gets#

Disney gets early access to Adobe's newest AI, creative and media technology — Firefly, Firefly Foundry, Premiere and Photoshop — plus dedicated Adobe engineering and research staff for its production pipeline. Adobe gets a flagship customer and a bigger stage: a larger presence across Disney's media platforms, joint marketing activations, and a role at Adobe Summit, Adobe MAX and other industry conferences.

Disney has been buying AI talent outright, too — a court filing priced its Character.AI team acquisition at $180 million for the people and $20 million for the tech. Read that story here. Adobe has supplied Disney's creative pipelines for decades, and the release notes its multiple Academy Awards for scientific and technical achievement.

The verdict#

This is a distribution deal dressed as a technology deal. The models are real — Imagineering has run them since June — but the announcement adds no new model, no launch date and no dollar figure. Its real news is the exclusivity: Disney is building its private AI stack with Adobe, trained only on Disney-owned material.

What is missing: no financial terms, no length for the multi-year term, no named franchises or characters, and no specific attraction, film or ESPN product tied to the tools. The models are private to Disney, so none of this reaches the public — and Adobe's “commercially safe” claim comes with no published testing.

Frequently asked questions

Can the public use Disney's Firefly Foundry models?

No. The models are private and proprietary to Disney, built on its own characters, environments and visual styles. Firefly Foundry is Adobe's service for building custom models for one company — it is not a public tool for generating Disney characters.

What is Adobe Firefly Foundry?

Adobe's offering for building proprietary generative AI models for a single company, trained on that company's own content. Disney is using it to build models tailored to its properties that Adobe describes as human-centered and artist-led, producing commercially safe, production-ready outputs.

What is Disney already doing with the tools?

Walt Disney Imagineering Research & Development has used Firefly Foundry since June 2026. A sketch-to-image model turns hand-drawn concepts into fully rendered 2D concept art, and a custom image model generates on-brand, franchise-accurate assets for the theme-park development pipeline.

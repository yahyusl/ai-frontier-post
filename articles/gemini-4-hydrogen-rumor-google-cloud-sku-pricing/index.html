RUMOR: Unannounced Gemini 4 Hydrogen appears in Google Cloud’s price list at $1.50/$7.50 per million tokens

Google hasn’t announced a third Gemini 4 model — but its own billing catalog now lists “Gemini 4 Hydrogen” with full prices, and trackers say the model answers on Vertex AI. Here’s what the listing proves, and what it doesn’t.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · November 12, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: “Gemini 4 Hydrogen” — a name Google has never announced — now shows up in Google Cloud’s public price list with 30 SKUs and full pricing: $1.50 per million input tokens, $7.50 per million output tokens, $0.075 for cached input. The listing, verified on Google’s own SKU page on November 12, matches the long-circulating claim that Hydrogen is the Flash-tier member of the Gemini 4 family. What it does not prove: that the model is live, what it can do, or when Google will announce it.
  • The listing: 30 “Gemini 4 Hydrogen” SKUs under the Gemini API service in Google Cloud’s SKU catalog, all global region, priced in US dollars — verified on cloud.google.com/skus on November 12.
  • The prices: standard text input $1.50 per million tokens; output $7.50 per million; cached input $0.075; batch input $0.75; batch output $3.75; priority input $2.70.
  • The context: Gemini 4 Argon was announced September 30; Carbon went generally available November 10 at $2/$10 per million through November 30, rising to $4/$20 on December 1. Hydrogen at $1.50/$7.50 would undercut Carbon’s December price by 62.5% and match Gemini 3.8 Flash’s list price.
  • The unconfirmed part: a tracker account says calling gemini-4-hydrogen on Vertex AI returns a working model that identifies itself as “Gemini 4 Hydrogen” — a claim that has not been independently reproduced. Google’s Gemini API models and pricing pages still list no Hydrogen entry.
  • The history: the Hydrogen codename has circulated since late October, tied to blind Arena appearances under the label gemini-3.8-flash and to a one-word “Gemini” post from Google AI Studio lead Logan Kilpatrick.

Google has not announced a third Gemini 4 model. Its billing catalog appears to have done the announcing for it. On November 12, the name “Gemini 4 Hydrogen” showed up in Google Cloud’s public SKU list — 30 line items, with full per-token prices — and within hours the leak-tracking corners of X and Reddit were treating it as the worst-kept secret in AI. Google has said nothing. That silence is the whole story: everything that follows is rumor, and the honest version of it is a careful split between what Google’s own pages now show and what nobody has proven.

The verified part is short, and it is the part that matters. A check of Google Cloud’s SKU catalog on November 12 returns 30 SKUs for “Gemini 4 Hydrogen” under the Gemini API service, all in the global region and priced in US dollars. The standard text rates are $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens. The same $1.50 applies to audio and image input; cached input is listed at $0.075, batch input at $0.75, batch output at $3.75, and priority input at $2.70 per million tokens.

What the price list actually shows#

A billing entry is not a launch. It is, however, a specific kind of evidence: SKUs are how Google charges developers, and they do not usually appear for products that do not exist. The Gemini 4 family has a recent precedent — Carbon’s own billing SKUs surfaced in the catalog before its public release. And the prices themselves tell a story: $1.50/$7.50 is exactly the list price of Gemini 3.8 Flash from January 1, 2027, and it undercuts Carbon’s December pricing ($4 input / $20 output) by 62.5%. If Hydrogen is real, it is priced to be the cheap, fast one.

The claims that still need proof#

The loudest claim of the day comes from a model-tracking account on X, which reported on November 12 that calling gemini-4-hydrogen on Vertex AI’s global endpoint returned a working model that identified itself as “Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a large language model built by Google.” The post drew about 58,000 views. It has not been independently reproduced — the news aggregator that first relayed it says it tried and could not — and Google’s own Gemini API models and pricing pages still carry no Hydrogen entry at all. Treat the endpoint claim as unconfirmed, not as a launch.

A second sighting arrived on Reddit the same day: a crosspost to r/singularity showed a screenshot of Google’s Antigravity app with “Gemini 4 Hydrogen High New” sitting in the model picker, selected, alongside “Gemini 4 Argon Medium New” and a greyed-out “Gemini 4 Carbon Unavailable.” The poster, who shared the screenshot at 12:19 p.m. ET, added only that it “works really fast” and that there was no further information to share. A screenshot of a picker is easy to fake and proves nothing on its own — but it is now the second independent surface where the name has appeared in a day, after the billing catalog.

The rest of the circulating detail is thinner still: testers say Hydrogen has been appearing in blind LMArena matchups since early November under the label gemini-3.8-flash, with reported speeds of 300 to 500 tokens per second and demos of 3D scenes finished in minutes. None of that has a benchmark, a model card, or a Google source behind it.

Why everyone thinks it’s the Flash tier#

The Hydrogen name has been circulating since late October, when leak-tracking accounts first tied it to a Flash-tier Gemini 4 model in internal testing. The naming fits the pattern Google established this autumn: Argon, announced September 30, and Carbon, announced October 28 and generally available November 10. A third element name, priced well below both, slots neatly into the Flash role Google has always used for its cheaper, lower-latency tier. Business Insider reported on November 11 that Google staff are testing a “fast and efficient” model called Hydrogen, and the speculation caught fire the same day Google AI Studio lead Logan Kilpatrick posted a single word — “Gemini” — to X.

There is also a second codename in the mix: Krypton, described by trackers as a checkpoint above Carbon. It has even less behind it than Hydrogen — no billing entry, no picker screenshot, no endpoint claim — and it is worth mentioning only so that the two names do not get confused.

What to watch#

Three things would turn this from rumor into news, in order of how much they would prove. First, a Hydrogen entry on Google’s Gemini API models or pricing page — the billing catalog lists prices, but the developer surfaces are where a model actually becomes callable. Second, an official announcement or model card, which would settle the Flash-tier question and supply the missing numbers: context window, benchmarks, and a release date. Third, independent reproduction of the Vertex AI claim by anyone other than the account that first made it.

Until one of those happens, the correct summary is narrow: an unannounced model name with a full price list is sitting in Google’s own billing catalog, and the company has not commented. That is unusual enough to report — and thin enough that it should stay labeled rumor until Google says otherwise.

Frequently asked questions

Has Google announced Gemini 4 Hydrogen?

No. The name appears only in Google Cloud’s billing catalog — 30 SKUs, verified November 12 — and in unverified tracker reports. Google’s Gemini API models and pricing pages list no Hydrogen entry, and the company has not commented.

What would Gemini 4 Hydrogen cost?

Per the SKU listing: $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens for standard text, with cached input at $0.075 and batch rates lower still. Those are list prices for an unannounced model and could change before any launch.

When will Gemini 4 Hydrogen launch?

No date has been announced or leaked with any sourcing. Trackers have pointed to an imminent announcement since the Kilpatrick teaser on November 11, but Google has confirmed nothing.

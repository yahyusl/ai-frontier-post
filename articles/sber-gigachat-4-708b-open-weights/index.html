Sber releases GigaChat 4, a 708B open-weight model under MIT — but its own benchmarks tell two stories

Russia's largest bank unveiled two open-weight models with a million-token context at its AI Journey conference — a 708-billion-parameter flagship under the MIT license, and a smaller sibling with a very different license. The headline scores look strong; the fine print is less flattering.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 28, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Sber released GigaChat 4, a 708-billion-parameter open-weight model under the MIT license with a million-token context window. Its headline coding score looks competitive — but the same table shows it trailing rivals badly on long-horizon engineering, and the hardware bill and sanctions status limit who can actually use it.
  • The launch: Sber unveiled two GigaChat 4 models at its AI Journey conference in Moscow on November 26, 2026 — a 708B flagship and a 37B compact — both with a 1,048,576-token context window.
  • The flagship: 708 billion total parameters with 41 billion active per token (8 of 256 routed experts plus one shared expert), released under the MIT license on Hugging Face.
  • The headline number: Sber reports 80.89% on SWE-bench Verified in reasoning mode — but 35.40% on DeepSWE, against 74.34% for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. No independent verification exists yet.
  • The catch: the 708B model needs 16 H100 GPUs to run in FP8; the 37B model that fits on one GPU is restricted to personal, noncommercial use. Sber is under U.S. blocking sanctions.

A 708-billion-parameter model just went open-weight under the most permissive license in the business — from a Russian state-controlled bank under U.S. blocking sanctions. Sber unveiled GigaChat 4 at its AI Journey conference in Moscow on Thursday, November 26, publishing the flagship's weights on Hugging Face under the MIT license alongside a smaller 37-billion-parameter sibling, both with a 1,048,576-token context window.

The release is genuinely significant and genuinely complicated. A 708B model anyone can download, fine-tune, and use commercially under MIT terms is rare at this scale, and Sber published intermediate checkpoints too. But every number comes from Sber itself, almost nobody has the hardware to run the flagship, and the legal picture is anything but clean.

What launched#

Two models, both built by Sber's ai-sage team. The flagship, GigaChat4-708B-A41B, is a sparse mixture-of-experts design: 708 billion total parameters, about 41 billion active per token, across 64 layers with 256 routed experts. The compact GigaChat4-37B-A4B activates roughly 4 billion per token. Both ship in FP8, BF16, and GGUF, with SGLang and vLLM support on launch day.

The weights live on Hugging Face under the ai-sage organization, with a technical report on GitHub.

One checkpoint, two speeds#

The change that matters most is a consolidation: GigaChat 3.5 shipped separate checkpoints for fast answers and for reasoning; GigaChat 4 puts both modes in one checkpoint, switchable per request — a cleaner design for agents that flip between a quick lookup and a long chain of thought.

Under the hood it is a hybrid: 48 of 64 layers use Gated DeltaNet, a linear-attention design that compresses history into a fixed-size recurrent state instead of caching every earlier token. The other 16 pair multi-head latent attention with DeepSeek Sparse Attention, where an indexer selects up to 2,048 positions per token — about 0.2% of a full million-token prompt. The trade is deliberate: full attention grows quadratically more expensive with sequence length. Whether the compression costs accuracy on retrieval-heavy work is for independent testing to establish. Sber stretched the context in stages, from 32,000 tokens to a million over roughly 736 billion tokens of further training — the predecessor, GigaChat 3.5, topped out at 262,144.

The benchmarks tell two stories#

Here is where the announcement splits in two. On the tests Sber chose to headline, GigaChat 4 looks like a serious contender: 80.89% on SWE-bench Verified in reasoning mode (80.82% instant), 71.26% on Terminal-Bench 2.0, and leads in its own table on function calling (79.55% BFCL v4), instruction following (80.33% IFBench), and agentic office work (83.00% CoworkBench). The 37B compact model even beats Qwen3.6-35B on LiveCodeBench v6 and SWE-bench Verified.

The same table, read a few rows further down, tells a different story. On DeepSWE, a long-horizon engineering test, the 708B model scores 35.40% against 74.34% for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. On τ³-Bench Banking it scores 18.03% against 56.00% for GLM-5.3, and on Humanity's Last Exam (text only) 23.91% against 43.42% for DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. On Terminal-Bench 2.1 it trails GLM-5.3 by 19 points.

Two caveats apply to every figure above. First, all of them are Sber's own numbers — no independent evaluator has tested the model yet, and Sber's own report says several benchmarks use its own evaluators and comparisons are not directly equivalent. Second, harness and timeout choices move scores by double digits: Sber credits GLM-5.3 with 88.70% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 under a seven-hour timeout; the Vals AI leaderboard, run with a different harness, lists 71.54%. Until someone else runs the model, the honest summary is that GigaChat 4 looks strong on short coding and tool-use tasks and weak on long, knowledge-heavy ones — and even that is provisional.

The license split#

The licensing runs backwards from the usual pattern. The big model — the one almost nobody can run — is the open one: the 708B flagship carries the MIT license, permitting commercial use, fine-tuning, and redistribution. The small model — the one a single GPU can serve — is restricted: the 37B version sits under a Noncommercial License for Individuals 1.0, allowing personal research and hobby use but barring commercial outputs and use by companies, universities, charities, and government bodies.

The practical effect is a split audience: well-funded teams with datacenter hardware get a genuinely permissive 708B artifact, while everyone else gets a compact model they can run but not sell anything with. Read the specific model card before building — the family name does not imply identical rights.

The sanctions question#

Then there is the question no benchmark answers. Sber is Russia's state-controlled largest bank — the National Wealth Fund holds 50% plus one voting share — and the U.S. Treasury designated Sberbank under Executive Order 14024 in April 2022, fully blocking it. Whether downloading freely available MIT-licensed weights counts as a transaction with a blocked person is a question for sanctions counsel, and one any team outside Russia must answer before touching the model.

Russian law adds a second fixed condition: the Yarovaya laws require telecom and internet companies to hand communications to authorities on request, and the SORM interception system extends to chats and forums. Sber's model cards describe weights and deployment, not data handling — confirm what any Sber-hosted service logs before sending it prompts. A Ghent University study reported in July 2025 ranked GigaChat highest for political censorship among 14 models tested, though it predates GigaChat 4.

What to watch#

Three things decide whether GigaChat 4 matters beyond the announcement. First, independent benchmarks: third-party reruns will show how much of the headline survives other harnesses and timeouts. Second, quantized derivatives — GGUF builds and pending llama.cpp support will decide whether anyone without a GPU cluster can run the flagship. Third, the sanctions question: whether compliance teams treat MIT-licensed weights from a blocked bank as usable, which decides whether GigaChat 4 becomes a research artifact, a regional workhorse, or something teams outside Russia quietly avoid.

Frequently asked questions

Is GigaChat 4 open source?

It is open-weight, not open source by the Open Source Initiative's definition — the weights are shared without training data or full code. The 708B flagship is under the MIT license; the 37B model is limited to personal, noncommercial use by individuals.

What hardware does GigaChat 4 need?

Sber's example for the 708B model in FP8 uses two nodes of eight 80GB H100 GPUs. The 37B model runs in FP8 on a single 80GB H100, with the weights taking about 40GB before caches and activations.

Can a U.S. company use GigaChat 4?

The 37B license bars commercial use and use by organizations outright. The 708B model's MIT license does not settle the sanctions question — Sberbank has been under U.S. blocking sanctions since April 2022. A U.S. team should consult sanctions counsel before building on either model. This article is not legal advice.

UK's £600M Budget AI plan: a biosecurity lab, an agentic cyber shield, and a home-grown chips push

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 24, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: On October 24, 2026 the UK government announced a £600M+ AI package ahead of Wednesday’s Budget: £165M for an AI biosecurity initiative (a £100M world-first lab plus £65M for countermeasures, backed by up to £200M in OpenAI and Anthropic model credits), £120M more for the AI Security Institute, a £60M agentic “National Cyber Shield” and a £70M vulnerability-hunting centre, plus chip-design and sovereign-AI bets to cut dependence on foreign technology.

The UK government on Saturday unveiled a £600M+ package of AI investment ahead of Wednesday's Budget — a plan that pairs defensive spending against AI-era threats with a bet on building more of the technology at home. The package centers on a £165M AI biosecurity initiative, an extra £120M for the AI Security Institute, an agentic "National Cyber Shield," and new money for British chip design and AI firms.

Chancellor of the Exchequer John Healey framed it as "a distinctively British approach to AI — one that doesn't leave workers behind and defends our national interest," setting AI out as a fixture of the Budget and of his industrial strategy. Full detail lands next week, but the announcement published on October 24 already itemizes most of the spending.

The £600M breakdown

The centerpiece is a £165M National AI Biosecurity Initiative: £100M for a world-first AI Biosecurity Lab (AIBL) that applies AI to the detection and surveillance of biological threats, and £65M to strengthen biosurveillance and develop medical countermeasures for priority pathogens. The lab will be supported by up to £200M in AI model credits from OpenAI and Anthropic — credits for the companies' own products, not cash — plus partnerships with Google DeepMind and London-based AI life-sciences pioneer Basecamp Research. AI Minister Kanishka Narayan called the lab "a major new global institution, bringing together world-leading scientific expertise to protect people from emerging biological threats and ensure Britain leads the world in AI safety and security."

On cyber, the package adds £120M for the AI Security Institute, the government's frontier-model testing body, to expand its testing of new AI systems and development of technical standards. A £60M National Cyber Shield will use agentic AI to improve the UK's cyber defences — including a £25M challenge fund for UK AI and cyber-security innovators and scoping for a regulatory sandbox for agentic cyber-security tools. And a new £70M National Exposure and Real-Time Vulnerability (NERV) Centre will deploy AI tools to identify cyber vulnerabilities across critical infrastructure and the economy.

Home-grown AI: chips, robots, and the industrial push

On building domestic capability, the Chancellor added £60M to the Sovereign AI Fund to invest directly in promising UK AI companies, with a new focus area of "Physical and Industrial AI" covering robotics, autonomous systems and advanced manufacturing — plus £60M for Sovereign AI's R&D procurement scheme aimed at health, care, AI security, assurance and safe adoption.

Hardware gets £18M for a new UK Advanced Chip Design Academy alongside new AI chip-design capability in Edinburgh and a National Cyber Security Centre AI Cyber Security Centre of Excellence in Manchester to train the next generation of government cyber and AI experts. Smaller lines include a £15M AI Challenge Prize (first award targeted at health), £23M over three years for the AI Economics Institute — billed as the world's first government-backed institution studying AI's economic impacts, supporting the UK's G20 presidency next year — £30M for local AI partnerships with Mayoral Strategic Authorities, and £10M per year for research into how AI reshapes jobs, aimed at workers worried about automation.

The case the Treasury is making

The announcement leans on live examples. In the NHS, AI is already helping clinicians analyse over 210,000 chest X-rays each month — around a third of all NHS chest X-rays — producing an 11.5% increase in suspected lung cancer cases identified within 24 hours (about 400 more patients a month receiving faster clarity) and cutting average follow-up CT waits from 6.3 to 5.6 days. HMRC's AI-powered assistant, Ask HMRC, supported around 6.3 million customer interactions in 2025-26, freeing up roughly 400 full-time-equivalent work hours.

Industry reaction was broadly positive: techUK CEO Julian David OBE called it "a strong £600m+ package focused on AI adoption, trust and security, investment and AI's impact on jobs and society." The honest accounting, though, is that the headline total mixes fresh allocations with in-kind contributions — the OpenAI and Anthropic contributions are credits spendable on the companies' own models, and several lines (the AIEI money, the chip academy) fund research and training whose payoff is years out. What Wednesday's Budget reveals is whether the pre-announcement numbers survive the spending rounds — and whether "cutting UK dependence on other countries" translates into deployed sovereign capacity rather than committees.

Frequently asked questions

What is the AI Biosecurity Lab?

An independent non-profit the UK government is establishing to use frontier AI for earlier detection of biological threats and faster development of vaccines, treatments and other medical countermeasures. It is funded with £100M of government money inside a £165M National AI Biosecurity Initiative (the other £65M goes to biosurveillance and countermeasures), and OpenAI and Anthropic have committed up to £200M in AI model credits to support it. Google DeepMind and Basecamp Research are research partners.

What are the National Cyber Shield and the NERV centre?

The National Cyber Shield is a £60M programme that will use agentic AI to improve the UK’s cyber defences, including a £25M challenge fund for British AI and cyber-security innovators. The National Exposure and Real-Time Vulnerability (NERV) Centre is a new £70M facility that will deploy AI tools to find cyber vulnerabilities across the country’s critical infrastructure and the economy.

Is the £600M all new government money?

Not quite. The announcement mixes fresh spending lines — the £165M biosecurity initiative, the extra £120M for the AI Security Institute, the £60M Cyber Shield, the £70M NERV centre and the sovereign-AI and chip investments — with up to £200M from OpenAI and Anthropic that comes as credits for the companies’ own AI models rather than cash. Full details are expected in Wednesday’s Budget.

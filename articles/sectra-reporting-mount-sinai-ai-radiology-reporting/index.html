Sectra Reporting at Mount Sinai: AI-assisted radiology reporting for 1.2 million exams a year

Sectra has signed a seven-year agreement to put its AI-assisted reporting module into every radiology service at Mount Sinai Health System in New York — more than 1.2 million exams a year — with go-live planned for spring 2027.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Sectra has signed an agreement with Mount Sinai Health System in New York to deliver Sectra Reporting, the native reporting module in its enterprise imaging solution, Sectra One Cloud. Announced on November 4, 2026, the seven-year deal covers all of Mount Sinai’s radiology services — more than 1.2 million exams a year — with go-live planned for spring 2027.

Mount Sinai is the first health system in the United States to enter a partnership with Sectra for radiology reporting. The point of the deal is consolidation: radiologists go from worklist to signed report in one workflow, without switching between separate imaging and reporting systems, and Sectra says the aim is shorter report turnaround times. The announcement describes a planned workflow, not results already recorded at Mount Sinai.

One workflow, from worklist to signed report

With Sectra Reporting, radiologists dictate freely while the system automatically structures the report, reviews an AI-drafted impression, and runs quality checks that flag inconsistencies before sign-off. A one-click AI tool summarises and organises findings from free-form dictation into an impression, according to the product documentation summarised by Unite.AI, alongside speech input, templates, examination-specific forms, measurement capture and lesion tracking. Once a report is signed, Sectra UniView can present it with images and measurements to referring physicians.

“Radiologists read the image and write the report as one task, and their tools should work the same way,” said Dr. David S. Mendelson, Vice Chair of Radiology Information Technology at Mount Sinai Health System. He added that with reporting built into the imaging platform, new capabilities, including AI, reach radiologists “without yet another system to integrate.”

First US health system, on a platform Mount Sinai already runs

Mount Sinai is one of the largest health systems in the New York metropolitan area, with seven hospitals, more than 400 outpatient practices, over 600 research and clinical laboratories, and the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai. It already uses Sectra’s enterprise imaging solution for radiology, breast imaging and enterprise archive, so adding reporting to the same platform is meant to reduce vendor complexity and simplify IT management.

The module is already in use elsewhere: Sectra reports 14,000 active Sectra Reporting users worldwide, 27 million reports a year and use in 21 countries. It introduced Sectra Reporting to the US market on November 26, 2025, with automated impression generation and integration of external AI results. Medical imaging AI is moving from single-task tools to enterprise platforms, as Riverain’s enterprise rollout of its FDA-cleared chest imaging AI and Fujifilm’s Synapse Pathology v3.0 show — and clinical AI deployments now have to prove themselves inside live hospital workflows, as with NTL Healthcare’s on-premises cervical-screening AI.

What is still to prove

“Reporting belongs where the images are,” said Isaac Zaworski, President of Sectra, Inc., calling the adoption part of a broader shift toward unified diagnostic workflows, with fewer applications for radiologists and faster turnaround for patients as the anticipated benefits. Those benefits are still to be demonstrated in daily practice at Mount Sinai: the test will be whether radiologists can move from examination to a checked, signed report in one workflow, and whether the IT organisation can run reporting inside the imaging platform it already operates. Sectra says it continues to support third-party reporting products, so customers can choose its native module while keeping other supported systems.

Sectra will show Sectra Reporting at RSNA 2026, at booth #8119. For Mount Sinai, the next milestone is spring 2027, when the system is due to go live across services handling more than 1.2 million exams a year.

Frequently asked questions

What did Sectra and Mount Sinai agree on November 4, 2026?

A seven-year agreement for Sectra to deliver Sectra Reporting, the native reporting module in Sectra One Cloud, across all of Mount Sinai Health System’s radiology services, covering more than 1.2 million exams a year. Go-live is planned for spring 2027.

How does Sectra Reporting use AI in radiology reporting?

Radiologists dictate freely while the system automatically structures the report, reviews an AI-drafted impression, and runs quality checks that flag inconsistencies before sign-off. The radiologist reviews the AI-drafted impression before the report is signed.

Is Sectra Reporting live at Mount Sinai yet?

No. Go-live is planned for spring 2027, so the November 4 announcement describes a planned workflow rather than outcomes already recorded at Mount Sinai. Sectra reports the module is already in use elsewhere, with 14,000 active users worldwide.

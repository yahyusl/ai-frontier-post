Tim Cook confirms it: Apple Intelligence approved for China, test version lands this month — no new Siri yet

In an exclusive Shanghai interview on October 15, Tim Cook said Apple Intelligence has cleared China’s regulatory bar and will ship a test version later this month — with live translation, writing tools and image clean-up, built on a China-specific model developed with Alibaba.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 15, 2026 · 5 min read

What Cook announced

In an exclusive interview in Shanghai on Thursday, Apple chairman Tim Cook told state-backed outlet The Paper that Apple Intelligence has been approved in China and that a test version will reach Chinese users later in October. Reuters reported the interview on October 15, with a dateline of 09:14 UTC.

The test build carries a deliberately narrow feature set: live translation, writing tools and image clean-up. The upgraded Siri AI is not included — Chinese users get Apple’s assistant minus its showpiece for now, a narrower toolkit than the one Apple is developing for its wider platform.

The same day, Cook met China’s Commerce Minister Wang Wentao. According to the commerce ministry, the two discussed the development of AI and Apple’s business in China — a ministerial handshake that signals Beijing is publicly engaging with the rollout rather than merely permitting it.

A model built for China, with Alibaba

Apple did not simply localize its existing models. The company trained a large language model specifically for the China market — a departure from its strategy of relying on third-party models to power AI features in the country. Reuters previously reported that the model was developed in partnership with Alibaba and trained with the Chinese tech giant’s support.

The regulatory groundwork had been laid months earlier: China’s cyberspace regulator registered Apple’s on-device generative AI service for use on iPhones in July, and Apple said during its July 30 earnings call that the rollout had started. What Thursday added was the first consumer date — and Cook’s own confirmation that approval is in hand.

Why it matters now

China is roughly 17% of Apple’s business: $79.34 billion of $466.83 billion in revenue over the last four quarters, per TIKR. Greater China revenue grew 38%, 28% and 22% year over year in the three quarters through June — each time outpacing Apple overall.

Notably, that recovery happened without any Apple Intelligence at all: the base iPhone 17 nearly doubled the iPhone 16’s China unit sales in its first 10 days. AI was never the engine of the rebound — it is the missing feature Apple’s local rivals have been selling against for two years.

The timing also moves faster than what was reported yesterday: Bloomberg had Apple bringing Apple Intelligence to China with iOS 27.3 in December. Cook’s announcement puts a test version in users’ hands later this month, ahead of that reported full-launch window.

What comes next

The test version is exactly that — a test. Whether the upgraded Siri arrives with the broader rollout, and whether the December timeline still holds for the full release, remains unanswered. But the shape of the deal is now clear: a market-specific model, built with a local champion, approved by the regulator, and blessed in a ministerial meeting. That is a template Beijing will likely expect other foreign AI products to follow.

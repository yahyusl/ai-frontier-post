Free Gemini = Flash-Lite only: Google moves Flash and Pro behind paywalls on October 9

Google has quietly rewritten its Gemini support docs: starting October 9, free-tier users lose the Flash and Pro models and keep only Flash-Lite. AI Plus subscribers lose Pro too — while paid Pro and Ultra plans gain Deep Think. Days after the Gemini 4 Argon launch, the margins are showing.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 3, 2026 · 4 min read

Google has updated its Gemini support documentation with a tier reshuffle that takes effect on October 9, 2026: users without a Google AI subscription lose access to the regular Flash and Pro models and keep only Flash-Lite. The change was spotted in the updated support article early on October 3 by XDA's Simon Batt — days after Google launched its Gemini 4 Argon frontier model.

The reshuffle climbs the ladder too. Google AI Plus subscribers — the entry-level paid tier — lose the Pro model and keep only Flash-Lite and Flash. Google says Plus users will get an email explaining exactly when the cutoff hits their account. AI Pro and AI Ultra keep all three models, and both gain Deep Think — the "maximum parallel reasoning" mode that had been an Ultra exclusive.

The new map#

The per-plan lineup after October 9, per Google's support documentation:

  • No plan: Flash-Lite only (was Flash-Lite + Flash + Pro).
  • AI Plus: Flash-Lite + Flash (loses Pro).
  • AI Pro: Flash-Lite + Flash + Pro, plus Deep Think.
  • AI Ultra: unchanged — all three models plus Deep Think.

Why now#

The timing is the tell. Gemini 4 Argon launched on September 30 — frontier-class, with a 1-million-token single-response output limit, at introductory pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output (doubling to $4/$20 when the intro period ends), matching OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol exactly. Selling frontier intelligence at commodity-floor prices while shrinking who gets the existing models free is one coherent margin play.

It is also a funnel redesign: the free tier becomes a Flash-Lite demo lane, and anyone who needs Flash or Pro moves to a paid plan. For everyday users the practical bite is real — Flash is the default-quality workhorse and Pro is the strongest general model — though how close Flash-Lite comes for casual tasks is something Google has not spelled out in the support note.

Watch your inbox if you pay for AI Plus: Google says subscribers get an email with their specific cutoff date, so the rollout may not hit everyone on the same day. And if you are on the free tier, October 9 is the day the lineup changes.

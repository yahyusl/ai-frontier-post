Huawei and Qualcomm sign their first 5G patent deal — with an AI cross-license attached

Huawei and Qualcomm signed a broad multiyear patent license agreement covering 5G, compute, AI and networking — the first 5G patent deal between the two, and the first time Huawei has licensed or assigned AI and compute patents to the U.S. chip designer.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Huawei Technologies and Qualcomm have struck a broad, multiyear patent license agreement covering 5G, compute, artificial intelligence and networking technologies, the companies announced Monday. Qualcomm will also purchase some of Huawei's U.S. patents in compute, AI, networking and other areas, according to Dow Jones Newswires, independently carried by TradingView.

Firsts on both sides

The deal is a first in two directions. Per Huawei, it marks the Chinese company's first patent-licensing agreement with Qualcomm covering 5G technology. It is also the first agreement under which Huawei licenses and assigns patents in compute, AI and networking to the U.S. chip designer — AI intellectual property moving from a sanctioned Chinese giant into a U.S. company's portfolio.

That direction matters. Huawei has spent years under U.S. sanctions that limited its access to advanced semiconductors, yet the agreement underscores that it remains one of the world's top patent holders in information and communications technology. The two companies have signed multiple agreements over the years — the first more than two decades ago, and Huawei's first-ever IP licensing payment went to Qualcomm, in 2001.

The $6.9 billion patent machine

Huawei and Qualcomm did not disclose financial terms. The transaction closes once regulatory approvals are received. Huawei said its expected contract value from patent licensing agreements so far has exceeded $6.9 billion following this deal's signing.

The agreement lands weeks after Huawei reached a cross-licensing deal with HP covering patents used in Wi-Fi technology — a pattern suggesting Huawei is aggressively monetizing its patent portfolio even as chip sanctions persist. Its first-ever IP licensing revenue, for comparison, came from Motorola back in 2011.

Why it matters for AI

Patent deals rarely make the AI news cycle, but this one sits squarely at the intersection of two trends: the commoditization of AI compute and the fractured U.S.–China technology relationship. Compute and AI patents are now bargaining chips on the same table as 5G royalties — and they are flowing from China to the U.S., not the other way around.

What to watch: which of Huawei's U.S. patents Qualcomm actually buys, what regulatory approvals the deal needs, and whether the undisclosed financial terms ever surface. The pattern — HP on Wi-Fi, now Qualcomm on 5G, compute and AI — suggests more licensing announcements are coming.

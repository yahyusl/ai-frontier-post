Manulife launches an enterprise AI platform, targets $1B in AI value by 2027

MD Marcus Doyle, Senior Technology Writer · October 13, 2026

Manulife launched a proprietary enterprise AI platform today, moving from one-off AI use cases to a single governed foundation its teams can build on once and deploy across its 25 markets. The Toronto insurer says the shift should generate $1 billion in cumulative enterprise AI value by 2027, with $300 million already booked as of year-end 2025.

Insurance may not be the loudest AI sector, but it is one of the most consequential to digitize: underwriting, claims, fraud, and advice all run on documents and decisions AI is good at. Manulife is now the clearest corporate case study in treating AI as an operating layer rather than a pile of pilots. The platform gives every team a shared operating environment to build, deploy, and manage AI solutions the same way, with development and governance built into a common foundation — so a use case cleared in one market can be reused everywhere, accelerated through security, risk, and governance controls instead of starting over.

The $1B math

Manulife's value number is concrete, not vibes. The company attributes it to four buckets: run-rate expense reductions, top-line revenue uplift from AI-powered workflows, fraud reduction, and growth absorption. $300 million of the target was already achieved by the end of 2025 — meaning roughly two-thirds still has to be generated over the next 14 months. That's the honest way to read an enterprise launch like this: the platform is new, but the economics it cites are mostly from work already in production.

The use cases riding on it

Three examples show what "build once, scale globally" looks like in practice:

Singapore Sales Agent: helps sales teams find information quickly, answer questions, and prepare for customer conversations with real-time AI support. Intelligent Document Processing (IDP): a shared AI service available across Manulife that reads documents and extracts key information automatically — cutting manual data entry, processing time, and error rates. Quick Quote: launched in the U.S., this AI-enabled tool simplifies the preliminary life-insurance assessment, automating key steps and delivering faster access to preliminary underwriting information for customers and advisors.

The pattern matters more than any one tool: every solution that proves itself gets absorbed onto the platform, where other teams can reuse it instead of rebuilding it. Reuse is the whole economic argument.

Right-sized models, responsible guardrails

Manulife's Chief Technology & Operations Officer Shamus Weiland frames the goal plainly: "Our AI platform creates a strong, centralized foundation that empowers our developers and data scientists to build once and scale globally, while maintaining the highest standards of security, governance, and responsible AI."

Two details stand out. The platform explicitly supports right-sized models — matching model size to the task to cut cost and energy use — a rare admission from a Fortune-scale company that not every AI job needs the biggest model. And every solution on the platform must adhere to Manulife's Responsible AI Principles, designed to keep human accountability, creativity, and decision-making at the center of AI deployment.

An insurer that leads on AI — by its own rankings

Manulife has topped the Evident AI Insurance Index as the number one life insurance company for AI maturity two years running, and ranks first among insurers in Canada and first in the AI Leadership category, placing third overall. CEO Phil Witherington calls the platform "a critical next step toward our strategic priority to become a truly AI-powered organization" — language that would have sounded like marketing two years ago, and now reads like an operating plan with a billion-dollar target attached.

The broader signal: AI's center of gravity is shifting from model launches to platform discipline. Manulife's announcement is less exciting than a frontier model release and arguably more important — it shows what institutional AI adoption looks like once a company stops experimenting and starts compounding.

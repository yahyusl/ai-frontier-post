Google opened Googlebook pre-orders: five OEMs, Gemini at the OS level, and a subscription in every box

Pre-orders for Google's Gemini-first laptop category opened September 21: machines from Acer, ASUS, Dell, HP, and Lenovo from $899, retail on October 4, and twelve months of Google AI Pro bundled into every box.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · September 25, 2026 · 5 min read

Google has stopped previewing its laptop comeback and started selling it. On Monday, September 21, the company opened pre-orders for Googlebook, a new laptop category built around Gemini Intelligence — with five manufacturers shipping hardware at the same time and US retail beginning October 4.

This is not one device with Google's logo on it. It is a platform specification: Acer, ASUS, Dell, HP, and Lenovo each build their own machines to Google's reference design, every unit carries Gemini at the operating-system layer, and every unit arrives with a twelve-month Google AI Pro subscription in the box.

The hardware, the prices, the dates#

Previewed in May 2026 at The Android Show: I/O Edition, Monday added the part that matters to buyers: priced hardware and a pre-order button. Reported pricing starts at $899 for an Acer Googlebook 14 convertible, with most flagship configurations sitting between $1,199 and $1,299.

Google specifies an on-device neural processor capable of 45 or more TOPS across the line, and reported configurations include touch OLED displays with 16GB of RAM and 512GB of storage. US retail begins October 4, with Canada, the UK, Ireland, France, Germany, and Australia following on October 5.

The silicon picture splits three ways: Intel's Core Ultra Series 3, Qualcomm's Snapdragon X Elite (Qualcomm announced a co-developed Googlebook on September 21), and MediaTek options expected later. Google itself is not manufacturing any of this hardware — the play is distribution, not devices. Samsung is notably absent from the first wave; a custom One UI Book 9 interface layer is expected later.

Gemini becomes the interface#

The most talked-about feature is the Magic Pointer, built with Google DeepMind: the conventional cursor replaced by a Gemini-powered input mechanism. Point at a date in an email and it offers to create a calendar event; hover over two images and it offers to composite them. It activates when you wiggle the cursor — a gesture that happens constantly during normal use, so unintentional activations could grate on people who read and write all day.

The second headline feature is Create My Widget: describe what you want in a prompt and Gemini assembles a custom widget from connected Google services — Gmail, Calendar, third-party apps. One leaked example combined calendar events, hotel reservations, and a flight ticket into a single dashboard. Whether generated widgets survive OS and service updates is a question the announcement does not address.

Phone continuity gets two features: Cast My Apps opens applications from a connected Android phone on the laptop screen without installing anything, and Quick Access lets you view, search, and drop phone files into laptop workflows without a file transfer. The obvious parallel, noted in press coverage, is Apple's iPhone Mirroring — the same ecosystem lock-in, arriving a decade late.

Underneath sits the unified Android-plus-ChromeOS system, with genuinely native Android apps instead of the emulated layer Chromebooks used. And every lid carries a Glowbar, a light element whose exact function Google has not disclosed — press speculation pegs it as ambient feedback for Gemini states.

The subscription in the box#

The move that changes the economics, not just the feature list, is the bundle. Every Googlebook unit, at every price point, ships with twelve months of Google AI Pro — Gemini Advanced and 5TB of storage — plus GeForce NOW access and creative-application promotions. That makes every Googlebook purchase a subscription signup too, with a uniform renewal cohort maturing twelve months after the October shipping window. Whether those renewals convert or evaporate, Google has turned a hardware launch into a scheduled re-engagement event.

One caveat the spec sheet deserves: the 45+ TOPS NPU figure is Google's own stated specification, not an independently measured result, and no shipped unit has been tested yet.

What remains unproven#

No unit has shipped and no independent review exists — Google's claims and launch-day coverage are all anyone has. The load-bearing question is Gemini's reliability at the OS layer. Magic Pointer, Create My Widget, and the phone-continuity stack are friction-reducers if the model interprets intent correctly every time — and the demo-to-daily-use gap is exactly where AI features have historically died.

Chromebooks, for the record, are not being retired — existing devices keep their update commitments, and Google says new Chromebooks will still launch. But the positioning is unambiguous: premium Googlebooks with Gemini at the core, budget Chromebooks as the legacy lane. Whether buyers pay a premium for that core is the same experiment Microsoft is running with Copilot+ PCs.

What to watch#

October 4 and 5 supply the first real data: sell-through, which OEM reads the category best, and whether the Magic Pointer survives contact with actual knowledge workers. Samsung's second wave will show whether this is a platform or an experiment, the promised official OS branding will signal how clean a break Google wants, and in late 2027 that uniform twelve-month cohort comes due — the first hard test of whether Googlebook bought subscribers or just sold laptops.

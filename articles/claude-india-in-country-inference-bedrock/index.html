Claude comes home to India: Anthropic switches on in-country inference via Amazon Bedrock

Anthropic announced Monday that Claude models are now live with in-country inference in India through Amazon Bedrock, so requests sent through the India endpoint are processed on servers inside the country — a data-residency guarantee aimed squarely at India's banks, insurers, and government bodies.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 5, 2026 · 4 min read
TL;DR: Anthropic switched on in-country Claude inference in India on October 5, 2026 via Amazon Bedrock, so requests sent to the India endpoint are processed on servers inside the country — the data-residency guarantee India's banks, insurers, and government bodies have been asking for.
  • Models covered: Claude Opus 5, Sonnet 5, and Haiku 4.5, all live with in-country inference through Amazon Bedrock as of October 5, 2026.

Anthropic announced Monday that its Claude models are now live with in-country inference in India through Amazon Bedrock, covering Claude Opus 5, Sonnet 5, and Haiku 4.5. Requests sent to Claude through the India endpoint are now processed within India, giving regulated Indian institutions the data-residency assurances they need to deploy Claude at scale on their most sensitive workloads.

It is a direct answer to the most repeated request Anthropic says it heard from Indian organizations: the ability to run inference locally. For financial institutions, government bodies, and large enterprises operating under strict regulatory and data-handling standards, local processing is often not a preference but an imperative — the line between a pilot and production.

Why India, why now

Anthropic frames the move as delivering on a promise. "In August, we committed to bringing Claude inference to India, and we are delivering on that promise," said Irina Ghose, managing director for India at Anthropic. "Our customers, especially in regulated sectors and government, want to use Claude on their most critical work, with requests processed in the country. From today, they can."

The stakes are high because the market is, too. India is one of the largest markets for Claude globally — Anthropic's second-largest Claude.ai market, per Ghose — and the most technically sophisticated developer communities the company serves. Software development accounts for 45.2% of work-related tasks Indian users bring to Claude, the highest share of any market Anthropic serves. Adoption has also expanded over the past year across banking and payments, large enterprises, IT services, healthcare, science, and public services.

Anthropic opened its Bengaluru office in February 2026, and Ghose framed the launch as a long-term commitment: "We will keep growing our team in India and investing in local partnerships."

The Bedrock angle

The delivery vehicle matters. Running Claude through Amazon Bedrock means Indian enterprises can deploy on the same AWS infrastructure they already use, with audit trails and access controls built in for risk and compliance teams. Sandeep Dutta, president of AWS India and South Asia, put the commercial logic plainly: "Responsible AI adoption at scale has become a strategic imperative for some of India's most regulated industries. For them, data residency is the parameter that decides whether AI stays confined to pilots or reaches full production."

Both sides are betting the same thing: that residency was the binding constraint, and that removing it unlocks a backlog of bank, insurer, and government deployments waiting on exactly this capability. The test will be how quickly those pilots convert — the infrastructure is now in-country, the compliance teams have their audit trails, and the excuse is gone.

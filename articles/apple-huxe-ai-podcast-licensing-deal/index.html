Apple licenses Huxe's AI audio tech in a quiet hire-and-license deal with the ex-NotebookLM startup

A European Commission filing reveals Apple will hire employees from Huxe, the AI audio startup founded by former Google NotebookLM team members, and take a non-exclusive license to its technology — a quiet deal, not an acquisition.

MD Marcus Doyle Senior Technology Writer · October 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Apple has reached an agreement to recruit employees from Huxe, the AI audio startup founded by former Google NotebookLM team members, and license the company’s technology, according to a European Commission filing that surfaced today. The structure is a hire-and-license deal — not an outright acquisition.

The filing says Apple “will have the right to make employment offers to and hire certain employees” of Huxe and receive “a non-exclusive license to Huxe’s intellectual property rights.” The non-exclusive part is doing a lot of work in that sentence: Apple doesn’t own Huxe, and the startup’s technology could in principle be licensed elsewhere too. Apple notified the Commission of the arrangement on June 9; the paperwork only became public this week.

The team behind interruptible AI podcasts

Huxe was founded in late 2024 by Raiza Martin, Jason Spielman and Stephen Hughes, all formerly of Google’s NotebookLM team — the AI research tool, since renamed Gemini Notebook, best known for turning documents and source material into podcast-style audio discussions. The trio set out to build a consumer version of that magic.

Their voice-first app generated personalized audio briefings from a user’s emails, calendars and interests, and could spin entire podcasts out of a single prompt. Its signature trick: listeners could interrupt the AI hosts mid-episode to ask a question or steer the conversation somewhere else — a small interaction detail that most synthetic-audio products still get wrong.

The startup announced in May that it was winding down, saying the team was moving on and development would stop; its audio service went dark on May 28. Two weeks later, Apple was already on the Commission’s books.

A familiar Apple playbook

Apple has a long habit of quietly absorbing AI talent. Usually it buys the whole company; sometimes it’s an acqui-hire where the team matters more than the product. Huxe is a third shape: take the people you want, license the tech, leave the corporate shell behind.

The fit is easy to see. Conversational, interruptible audio and genuinely personal briefings are exactly where Siri and Apple Intelligence need to go — and the Huxe crew already built that experience once at Google before trying to productize it themselves. Apple getting both the team and a license to the underlying IP suggests it wants the capability in-house, not just the résumés.

What’s still unclear

Plenty. The filing doesn’t say which employees Apple can recruit, nor does it confirm anyone has actually been hired. Apple hasn’t commented publicly on the deal, and neither side has said what the licensed IP covers or where — if anywhere visible — it will show up. For now, the paper trail is the whole story. But if Apple’s next audio AI turns out to be unusually good at holding a conversation, you’ll know where it learned.

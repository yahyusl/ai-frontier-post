Airship AI wins $29M in brand-name-only DHS contracts — and its stock jumped 34%

Airship AI said Thursday it has won $29.0 million in firm-fixed-price, brand-name-only contracts from a Department of Homeland Security agency — roughly seven times its quarterly revenue, spanning AI surveillance software, edge hardware, and on-premises processing. Shares surged 34%.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 1, 2026 · 5 min read

Airship AI Holdings said Thursday it has won $29.0 million in firm-fixed-price, brand-name-only contracts from a Department of Homeland Security agency — a haul worth roughly seven times its quarterly revenue, spanning the company's full stack of AI surveillance software, edge hardware, and on-premises processing. The market noticed: shares surged 34% on the session.

Airship AI (NASDAQ: AISP) announced the awards on Thursday morning, describing them as "brand-name-only" — meaning the contracts were reserved for Airship's own products, effectively a sole-source designation. The awards carry a six-month period of performance and support public safety and investigative requirements, according to the company's announcement.

The market reacts: a $29M book on a $4.12M revenue base#

Shares of AISP rose 33.6% to $2.49 on 26.6 million shares traded on the Nasdaq, per AllPennyStocks. The scale of the award package is what moved the stock: $29.0 million dwarfs Airship's second-quarter 2026 revenue of $4.12 million and its reported August backlog of $6.9 million. In other words, six months of contracted DHS work landed on a company whose entire standing backlog was a quarter of that size.

What DHS is buying: the full Airship stack#

The awards span all three of Airship's product lines. On the software side, the company will build new capabilities for its Acropolis ecosystem — focused on autonomous edge sensor data collection and improving the confidence level of detected user-defined events, with Airship acting as the intelligence layer for larger sensor systems.

On hardware, Airship will deliver "significant additional quantities" of Outpost AI, its edge AI appliance built to run advanced analytics at the tactical edge, plus deployments of Fortress AI, its newly released server-side platform for centralized, high-capacity AI processing. Because Fortress AI runs on premises, customers keep full data sovereignty — sensitive video and sensor data never leaves their control or passes through third-party cloud infrastructure. The package also includes turnkey integrated hardware systems that autonomously collect data at the edge, transport it securely to designated endpoints, and return high-confidence, AI-derived actionable intelligence in real time.

Fortress AI's federal debut#

The announcement frames the awards as a milestone for Fortress AI: its expansion into enterprise federal law enforcement following initial tactical deployments supporting multiple major 2026 National Special Security Events. "These awards reflect the continued momentum and critical importance of AI-driven surveillance solutions in advancing public safety and homeland security," said Paul Allen, Airship AI's president. "Autonomous edge AI-driven surveillance solutions are proving to be an essential force multiplier for law enforcement, delivering greater operational efficiency, enhanced accuracy, and improved threat detection."

Next up: Ask Airship goes agentic#

The company also said it expects to release new agentic AI functionality within Ask Airship before the end of 2026. Ask Airship sits on top of the video, sensor, and access-control systems customers already operate, pairing their sovereign data with the AI models best suited to their mission. Founded in 2006 and headquartered in Redmond, Washington, Airship AI is positioning itself, in Allen's words, as "the platform of choice for AI-enabled federal law enforcement operations" — one architecture from the tactical edge to the enterprise, with the data staying home.

What to watch#

  • Backlog conversion. Six months of contracted work at 4× the standing backlog is a revenue event in itself — watch Q3 and Q4 earnings for how fast it converts.
  • Fortress AI follow-ons. If the platform's federal debut wins expanded or follow-on DHS orders beyond the six-month term, the beachhead is real.
  • The agentic release. New Ask Airship functionality due by year-end is the platform play: software recurring revenue versus one-off hardware sales.
  • The sovereignty pitch. Airship's differentiator against cloud-based competitors is on-premises processing — federal procurement budgets will decide whether that argument keeps winning.

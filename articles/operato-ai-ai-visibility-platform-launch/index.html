Operato AI launches an AI-visibility platform: see what ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity say about your brand

Israeli startup Operato AI on October 9 launched a self-service platform that measures how ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity answer the questions buyers ask — showing where a brand ranks in those answers, which competitors get recommended instead, and what to fix first.

MD Marcus Doyle Senior Technology Writer · October 9, 2026 · 5 min read

Ecosia’s decision to drop French AI provider Mistral is not a pivot towards Chinese technology, but a shift towards open, affordable, energy-efficient AI models, founder and CEO Christian Kroll said in an exclusive conversation with EU-Startups. “We’ll probably be using Melious.ai in future. Unlike Mistral, this is a European company that offers high-quality, affordable AI inference powered by green electricity,” Kroll said.

The clarification follows a POLITICO report on October 6 that Ecosia was replacing Mistral with open models, including Alibaba’s Qwen, Z.ai’s GLM and Moonshot AI’s Kimi — a story that raised eyebrows in European policy circles. The partnership was a short one: Ecosia had only replaced OpenAI with Mistral back in May.

Why the partnership broke down

Kroll told POLITICO he was “disappointed with the quality of Mistral,” calling the French lab “a year behind” the competition, and cited recurring technical problems including overloaded servers — Ecosia, he said, was simply too large a customer for Mistral to serve. The switch to open models has reportedly cut Ecosia’s AI costs roughly in half while improving performance.

In the EU-Startups interview, Kroll widened the critique to sovereignty: Mistral’s reliance on foreign investors, particularly from the United States, undermines its European independence, he argued. The point lands awkwardly against Mistral’s fundraising streak — a €3 billion Series D in September 2026 led by Samsung at a valuation above €21 billion, with US backers including Andreessen Horowitz, Nvidia and Salesforce Ventures on the cap table. Two days after the POLITICO story broke, Mistral unveiled Large 4, its new frontier model, with chief scientist Guillaume Lample offering Ecosia early access to it.

The China question

Kroll stressed that the company’s focus is on open models, not their country of origin — they offer lower costs, greater flexibility and reduced energy consumption, all aligned with Ecosia’s environmental mission. Addressing concerns about political censorship in Chinese AI models, he said Ecosia can route sensitive subjects, such as questions about Taiwan, to alternative models, and is working to eliminate political biases from its AI-generated answers.

The plumbing matters as much as the weights. The models are served through Melious, a German platform running on European infrastructure powered by low-carbon energy — which lets Ecosia use internationally developed models while keeping its infrastructure and data processing inside Europe.

Why it matters

Ecosia is no marginal customer. The Berlin search engine donates 100% of its advertising profits to climate action and its services are used by institutions including Germany’s federal environment ministry and the UK’s National Health Service. Since 2018 it has operated under a steward-ownership structure that prevents the company from being sold or distributing profits to shareholders.

For Kroll, the switch is a thesis about European technology: rather than depending on individual AI developers to train frontier models on the continent, European companies can build competitive, independent services on open models running on European infrastructure. For Mistral, it reads as a loss of trust from one of Europe’s highest-profile AI customers — one week after its biggest funding round, and on the same week it unveiled its most ambitious model yet.

When a buyer asks an AI assistant which supplier or product to choose, the answer usually names only a few brands. A business that is missing from that answer is missing from the shortlist — and Operato AI’s argument is that traditional analytics can’t show you this. Traffic dashboards count the visits that arrive after a click; they don’t show what the assistant said about your brand before that point.

That is the gap the Israeli startup’s new platform targets. Announced on October 9, it is a self-service product that scans how the four leading AI assistants — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity — describe a business when buyers ask category questions, and turns the results into a weekly report.

How it works

Onboarding takes two inputs: a company name and a website. The platform reads the site, proposes the category, the competitors worth tracking and the buyer questions to monitor, then runs a first scan.

Each scan puts those questions to all four assistants. For every answer it records how often the brand is named, in what position, with what sentiment, and which sources the assistants cite. The findings are summarized as a visibility score, a rank against tracked competitors, share of voice and citation rate, with a breakdown for each assistant.

What each scan measures

The weekly cadence is the point. Operato AI says the platform scans automatically every week and emails the results to the account owner — positioning it as a recurring measurement habit rather than a one-off audit. If an assistant starts recommending a competitor where your brand used to appear, the change shows up in the weekly comparison.

The four tracked assistants are not interchangeable. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity each train on different data and weight sources differently, and the per-assistant breakdown lets a brand see where it is winning and where it is invisible. The citation-rate metric is the actionable piece: if the assistants rarely cite your site, the fixes list starts with making your own content quotable.

The bigger picture: the answer is the new SERP

Operato AI is not inventing the category — a small but growing set of tools now sells “AI search visibility” or “generative engine optimization” — but the launch shows how fast the category is moving downmarket. The first wave of this tooling was agency-led and enterprise-priced; a self-service product with a free “AI Readiness Score” on its homepage, as Operato’s site advertises, is a bet that every marketing team will soon want this number.

One caveat is worth keeping in mind: this is a launch-day announcement from a small startup, and the company has published no pricing, customer names or accuracy benchmarks. The weekly scan is only as useful as the buyer questions it tracks — pick the wrong questions and the visibility score measures the wrong battlefield. That said, the mechanics are straightforward, and the price of admission is just a URL.

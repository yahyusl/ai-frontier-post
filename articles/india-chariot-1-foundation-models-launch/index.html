India launches Chariot-1: an 11-billion-parameter homegrown LLM and speech in 10 Indian languages

India’s sovereign-AI programme has something concrete to show for itself. Chariot-1, unveiled at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi on Wednesday, is a language model and a speech model built from scratch in India under the government’s IndiaAI Mission — designed not as a cheaper copy of an American or Chinese system, but around the problem those systems handle worst: India’s languages.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 26, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Chariot AI, one of the first four teams picked under the IndiaAI Mission, has launched Chariot-1: an 11-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts language model and a text-to-speech model covering English and ten Indian languages, both trained from scratch in India. Three homegrown training techniques — IndicShift, Nested Training and Mixture of Vocabularies — are built for India’s scripts and for running on everything from data centres to phones.
  • The model: an 11-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts LLM with 4 billion active parameters per token, offered in three sizes — 10.7B, 1.7B and 745M.
  • The speech side: a text-to-speech model generating audio in English and ten Indian languages, with over 97% verbalisation accuracy on a 1,029-sentence pronunciation benchmark.
  • IndicShift: an open-source shared alphabet across Indian scripts — 350 letters standing in for 1,012 characters — cutting tokens per word by about 2.5 times.
  • Nested Training: all three model sizes trained in a single run from one set of weights, reported 62% faster than training them separately.
  • Mixture of Vocabularies: the vocabulary split into 11 groups so the model scores only the relevant one, reported 38% faster training.
  • The builder: Chariot Artificial Intelligence, a Noida startup founded this year by Stanford alumni Parth Sarthi and Suvrat Bhooshan; launched by MeitY Secretary S. Krishnan at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi.

The launch matters less for the raw numbers — an 11-billion-parameter model is modest by 2026 frontier standards — than for what it signals: a state-backed programme turning funding into working models, and a bet that the next billion AI users will talk to machines in Hindi, Tamil or Bengali before they ever type in English.

Chariot Artificial Intelligence, the Noida startup behind the models, is one of the first four teams selected under the IndiaAI Mission to build sovereign foundation models. It was founded earlier this year by Parth Sarthi and Suvrat Bhooshan, two Stanford graduates who returned to India to build the technology at home. The launch, inaugurated by electronics and IT secretary S. Krishnan at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi, is the programme’s first visible payoff: models designed, trained and tuned in India, for Indian users.

What Chariot-1 actually is#

The suite has two parts. The language model is an 11-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts design with 4 billion parameters active on any given token — a configuration that keeps inference costs closer to a much smaller dense model. It ships in three sizes, 10.7 billion, 1.7 billion and 745 million parameters, so the same model family can run in a data centre, on a laptop or on a phone. The speech model generates audio in English and ten Indian languages, and the team says the same architecture also handles speech recognition, folding text, speech synthesis and transcription into one system rather than three separate ones.

Both were pre-trained from scratch in India, rather than fine-tuned from a foreign base model — the distinction the government keeps coming back to. A fine-tune inherits another lab’s choices; a from-scratch build lets the team shape the model around India’s linguistic map from the first token.

Three tricks built for India’s scripts#

The launch showcase spent as much time on training method as on the models themselves, and the three techniques are the most interesting part of the announcement.

IndicShift is the headline idea, and the one the team has released as open source. Indian scripts share sounds and letterforms across languages, but standard tokenizers treat each script in isolation, wasting compute on near-identical characters. IndicShift introduces a shared alphabet — 350 letters standing in for 1,012 characters across 12 scripts — so the model can exploit what the languages have in common. The team reports it cuts tokens per word by about two and a half times.

Nested Training tackles a different cost: building three model sizes usually means three training runs. Chariot trains all three at once, from a single set of weights, and reports the approach runs 62% faster than training the sizes separately. Mixture of Vocabularies splits the model’s vocabulary into 11 groups and scores only the relevant group when choosing the next word, which the team says makes training 38% faster. These are vendor-reported figures, not independent benchmarks — but the direction is clear: do more with less compute, the constraint that matters most for a programme building frontier-class models without frontier-class budgets.

Why speech comes first#

The speech model may matter more than the language model. In a country where hundreds of millions of people are more comfortable speaking than typing, voice is the interface — and the team is explicit that speech reasoning is its longer-term focus. The text-to-speech system scored over 97% verbalisation accuracy on the Async TTS Pronunciation Benchmark, a test of 1,029 real-world sentences containing 2,251 instances of non-standard text, from numbers and dates to abbreviations, across 42 categories. That is the kind of unglamorous metric that decides whether a voice assistant reads an address or a medicine dose correctly.

The roadmap is ambitious: a model around 70 billion parameters next, and a stated ambition to eventually build a trillion-parameter system. Whether that scale is reachable on the programme’s resources is an open question.

Sovereign AI, with a deadline attached#

Chariot-1 arrives as the IndiaAI Mission’s early proof that its funding produces deployable technology rather than press releases. The same day, BharatGen — another mission-backed team — launched Patram 1.5, a vision-language model for document processing. And the launch came with a policy signal: Krishnan said the government’s long-awaited AI regulation papers are in their final stages and will be released shortly.

What remains unclear is how widely the models will be available. The launch materials describe IndicShift as open source but do not state the release terms for the model weights, and no independent benchmark results have been published yet. Those two facts will decide whether Chariot-1 is a research demonstration or a platform Indian developers can actually build on.

What to watch: the release terms and the first independent evaluations; the 70-billion-parameter follow-up the team is now working on; the AI regulation papers Krishnan promised; and whether the other IndiaAI Mission teams ship at a similar pace. Sovereign AI is easy to announce and hard to sustain — the next six months will show which one this is.

Frequently asked questions

What is Chariot-1?

Chariot-1 is a suite of foundation models built in India under the IndiaAI Mission: an 11-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts language model and a text-to-speech model covering English and ten Indian languages, both pre-trained from scratch in India.

Which languages does Chariot-1 cover?

The language model handles English and ten Indian languages. The speech model generates audio in English plus Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Gujarati, Punjabi and Odia.

Is Chariot-1 open source?

One of its three training innovations, IndicShift, is open source. The launch announcement does not state the release terms for the model weights themselves.

Meta poaches MongoDB CEO CJ Desai to run its new Meta Enterprise Platform

Meta has created a new business unit to sell its AI stack — Muse, Meta Business Agent, Muse API, Muse Code — to companies, and hired MongoDB CEO Chirantan 'CJ' Desai as chief enterprise platform officer, reporting to Mark Zuckerberg. MongoDB's stock fell roughly 18% on the news.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · September 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Meta is going after the enterprise software market in earnest — and it poached the chief executive of one of the most important database companies in the industry to do it. On Monday, Meta announced the Meta Enterprise Platform, a new business that will package its AI models, agents, and developer tools as products and services companies can deploy inside their own organizations. To run it, Meta hired Chirantan “CJ” Desai, the CEO and president of MongoDB, into the newly created role of chief enterprise platform officer, reporting directly to Mark Zuckerberg.

Zuckerberg framed the move as a company-defining expansion. “Today we are starting the next major pillar of our business, Meta Enterprise Platform, to help businesses use AI to grow and transform in new ways,” he said in a statement, adding that the platform would draw on strengths “few other companies have: advanced models, leading agents, large-scale infrastructure, and years of working closely with many businesses.”

What’s in the stack#

The initial lineup, as Meta described it, centers on four offerings, with more to come:

  • Muse — Meta’s personal AI agent, launched earlier this month for shopping, booking travel, sending emails, and making payments on users’ behalf. It shot to the top of Apple’s App Store and Google Play rankings within days of launch, and analysts have described it as potentially the biggest app launch in the US since ChatGPT in November 2022.
  • Meta Business Agent — the company’s business-facing agent, which Meta expanded globally in June.
  • Muse API — the developer-facing interface for the agent.
  • Muse Code — coding tools for developers.

Desai, in a statement on the announcement, said the platform would “focus on turning its AI stack into products and services that companies can deploy for their own businesses,” and pointed to security as the opening pitch: “As with Muse, security and privacy are built into Meta’s enterprise products from the outset.”

Who is CJ Desai#

Desai is an enterprise software veteran, which is exactly the pedigree Meta needed for this hire. Before MongoDB, he led product and engineering at Cloudflare and spent nearly eight years at ServiceNow, including stints as president and chief operating officer. At MongoDB he spent roughly two years, the last eleven months as CEO and president — making him one of the shortest-tenured big-tech CEOs in recent memory.

MongoDB moved fast to contain the fallout. The company appointed Dev Ittycheria, Desai’s predecessor, as interim president and CEO, retained a search firm for a permanent successor, and reaffirmed its guidance for the third quarter and fiscal 2027. Chairman Tom Killalea said Ittycheria was the best person to steady the ship in the interim.

The market reaction#

The market did not share MongoDB’s composure. Its shares tumbled roughly 18% in morning trading on Monday, though reported figures varied substantially across the session depending on the trading window measured. Brokerage Piper Sandler called the departure disappointing but said the reaction was overdone, arguing MongoDB remains strategically well-positioned.

Meta, by contrast, has been riding the Muse wave: the agent’s launch earlier this month took Silicon Valley and Wall Street by surprise, topping the App Store charts and lifting Meta’s stock more than 11% in a single session, according to PYMNTS.

Why this matters#

With this announcement, Meta enters direct competition against entrenched enterprise AI vendors — Microsoft, OpenAI, and Anthropic — at the exact moment those players are racing to sell agents into the corporate world. It is a meaningful shift for a company whose AI revenue story has so far been almost entirely consumer-facing and advertising-adjacent.

Meta has spent two years answering the question of what its AI budget was actually buying: billions in talent, models, and infrastructure, with Muse as the first product to look like a consumer breakthrough. The Enterprise Platform is the attempt to turn that same machinery into the boring, high-margin business of selling software to companies — the kind of revenue that doesn’t depend on app-store virality.

Notably, Meta disclosed none of the numbers that would let anyone judge the ambition: no pricing, no revenue targets, no customer names, no general-availability date. The only quantity attached to the launch was printed on MongoDB’s stock tape.

What to watch#

Three things will determine whether Meta Enterprise Platform is a pillar or a press release. First, real customers — Meta has hundreds of millions of business relationships through its advertising products, but converting advertisers into AI-platform buyers is a different motion. Second, pricing and availability — neither was disclosed, and enterprise buyers will compare against OpenAI, Microsoft, and Anthropic on hard economics. Third, MongoDB’s recovery — losing a CEO eleven months in is a governance question the company will have to answer, starting with who gets the permanent job.

Meanwhile, the timing is hard to miss: Meta is planting its enterprise flag the same week OpenAI holds DevDay 2026, the developer conference where the frontier labs traditionally reveal their next moves. The enterprise AI battle is no longer a race to a better model — it’s a race to the sales floor.

Scott Aaronson: this week looks like the beginning of an AI singularity

The complexity theorist who spent decades doubting near-term AGI says the past week's flood of AI-proved theorems changed his mind — including a Fourier Transform result and a quantum complexity collapse that hadn't yet made the rounds.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · November 26, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Scott Aaronson, the complexity theorist famous for doubting near-term AGI, says the past week's flood of AI-proved theorems — capped by OpenAI's claim that the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4 — looks like the beginning of a singularity. His post also flags two results that hadn't yet made the rounds: a Fourier Transform gate count with a matching lower bound, and a preprint collapsing two-message quantum proofs to full PSPACE power. The proofs still await independent verification.
  • Who: Scott Aaronson, complexity theorist at UT Austin and a longtime public skeptic of near-term AGI timelines.
  • What he said: the past week “is very much like I would've always imagined the beginning of an AI singularity to look and feel like.”
  • The trigger: a run of AI-claimed proofs — VP ≠ VNP, the Riemann Hypothesis, and the matrix multiplication exponent ω = 9/4.
  • New here: an OpenAI Fourier Transform result, N (log N)1/2+o(1) linear gates with a matching lower bound, and Yupan Liu's preprint proving QIP(2) = PSPACE.
  • The caveat: the proofs carry Lean certificates and preprints but await independent verification; Aaronson says he is “virtually certain” labs hold unpublished cryptography results, but refuses to spread unverified specifics.

Scott Aaronson has spent decades as the complexity theory community's most prominent skeptic about near-term AGI — the person who would bet against it in public and explain exactly why. On Wednesday, he said the week gone by looks like the beginning of an AI singularity. The post, on his blog Shtetl-Optimized under the title “HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT,” reached Reddit's r/singularity hours later, where it drew 577 upvotes and 75 comments.

What moved him wasn't a product launch or a benchmark. It was a pile of mathematics: in the space of days, AI systems at OpenAI and Anthropic claimed proofs of VP ≠ VNP, the Riemann Hypothesis, and the exact value of the matrix multiplication exponent — and then kept going, with two more results Aaronson flags that hadn't yet made the rounds.

A dinner party, interrupted by history#

Aaronson describes a dinner with friends and colleagues, still processing the VP ≠ VNP proofs and the Riemann Hypothesis proof, when news arrived before dessert: OpenAI claimed that ω, the exponent of matrix multiplication, is exactly 9/4 — a lower bound, matching the upper bound OpenAI proved in its October 6 “Mathocalypse” drop.

There are two shocks in that, he writes. The first is that we finally know the answer to a central question dating back to Volker Strassen in the late 1960s — and that the true answer is a rational number. The second, bigger one is that an AI proved a lower bound of the form ω > 2 at all, something he says he never expected to be all that much easier than P ≠ NP. Colleagues, he notes, had long expressed extreme confidence that ω = 2; he was skeptical of that confidence without having a strong argument either way. “So, I'm glad to have been vindicated about that!”

Two results you may have missed#

During dessert, another OpenAI release landed: the true number of linear gates needed to compute the Fourier Transform is N (log N)1/2+o(1). That improves on the O(N log N) bound that has stood since the Fast Fourier Transform arrived in the 1960s — and on OpenAI's own October 6 improvement. Again, the real shock is the matching lower bound: these ancient questions are now getting straight-up answered, Aaronson writes, “rather than slowly illuminated with a drip-drip-drip of clues.” OpenAI's preprint, “The logarithmic exponent of finite Fourier transforms,” is dated November 24, 2026.

The other result came from Yupan Liu, whose arXiv preprint proves QIP(2) = PSPACE — quantum interactive protocols with only two messages have the full power of protocols with three, or any polynomial number. Aaronson's measure of how fast the ground has shifted: as recently as 2025, that would have counted as one of the top complexity results of the year, and the fact that Liu used AI to help achieve it would have seemed strange and wonderful. Now, he writes, he had to remind himself it had even happened — and the use of AI was so standard it didn't even raise an eyebrow.

A note on status: the ω = 9/4 claim and the VP ≠ VNP proofs have already been covered here; the Fourier Transform and QIP(2) = PSPACE results are new to this site. All of them are claimed proofs, posted with Lean certificates or as preprints, and independent verification is still under way. Aaronson's working assumption, stated in the post, is that any proof coming directly from OpenAI or Anthropic is correct barring information to the contrary.

Why the messenger matters#

The story here is less the theorems than the person reacting to them. Aaronson has been skeptical of the possibility of AGI in our lifetimes and extremely critical of hype for decades — a point the top comment on the Reddit thread makes directly: “He is 100% drinking the kool aid now and that means something.”

His own framing reaches for the history of science. “It's now obvious that we'll be getting, not one-off solutions to this or that complexity question, but a whole new continent of mathematical truth, one that we'd previously only glimpsed through fog,” he writes. “Picture van Leeuwenhoek first training a microscope on pondwater, or Galileo first training his telescope on the stars.” And then the sentence that gives the post its weight: “I now feel in my bones, for the first time ever, that a proof of P ≠ NP is not far.”

What he won't say — and the pushback#

Aaronson is careful about one thing in particular. Asked about cryptography, he says he is “virtually certain, from multiple reliable sources, that the AI labs are now sitting on one or more bombshells in that area” — but much less confident about the specific nature of those bombshells, and unwilling to be responsible for spreading unverified rumors. That is his stated position, not a confirmed fact, and this article reports it as such.

In updates added November 26, he also answers his critics. Gary Marcus, he writes, signal-boosted a claim that unused sections in Alpöge's Lean proof somehow invalidated the Riemann Hypothesis proof — a claim Aaronson calls crackpot. To readers asking what any of this has to do with everyday life, his answer is blunt: probably no direct implications, but the fact that AI capable of such things now exists has potentially staggering ones.

He also explains why he chose that title only now. For months, he writes, two hypotheses were live: either AI was picking off problems “in the convex hull of existing techniques” while P ≠ NP stayed untouched, or it was really climbing the mountain that leads to P ≠ NP. With the matrix multiplication exponent settled and the rest of the week's results in hand, the first option has become much less tenable. “Yes, I do now think that AI is climbing the mountain that leads to P ≠ NP.”

What to watch: whether independent verification confirms the Lean certificates behind the week's claims; whether the labs' next uploads include the cryptography results Aaronson says they are holding; and whether other longtime skeptics follow him in saying so in public.

What did Scott Aaronson actually say?

What new results did he point to?

Are these proofs verified?

Palantir's Alex Karp says he's a lifelong Democrat and the company is 'essentially progressive'

Days after the Democrats' midterm wins, Palantir CEO Alex Karp told Politico he has 'been a Democrat my whole life,' called the company's products 'essentially progressive,' and opened the door to backing a Democrat in 2028 — a sharp turn for the chief of the Trump administration's closest AI contractor.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · November 12, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: In a Politico Playbook interview published November 11, Palantir CEO Alex Karp called himself a lifelong Democrat and a progressive, said Palantir’s products are “essentially progressive,” and said Democrats have “absolutely” corrected enough in 2026 to win back his support in 2028. The shift lands awkwardly against Palantir’s $30 million ImmigrationOS contract with ICE and its Pentagon work — and online reaction has been overwhelmingly skeptical.
  • The interview: Politico Playbook, November 11 — Karp used the word “progressive” eight times and said Democrats have “absolutely” corrected course in 2026.
  • The quote: “I’ve been a Democrat my whole life. I grew up in a far left environment. I know more about being a progressive than almost anybody on the planet, and I absolutely believe Palantir — all of our products are essentially progressive.”
  • The condition: open to backing a Democrat for president in 2028, but not a member of the Democratic Socialists of America; competence, not party loyalty, would decide his vote.
  • The contracts: a $30 million ImmigrationOS deal helping ICE track and deport undocumented immigrants, and software supporting the Pentagon’s Maven Smart System. Palantir has also hired former Democratic senator Jon Tester as an adviser.
  • The money: Karp gave $100,000 this year to the conservative Americans for Security PAC, but also gave to Democratic senator Mark Warner and the Democratic Party of Virginia. He says his giving is “equal Democrat, Republican.”
  • The reaction: social media users are largely unmoved — one widely shared post called the pivot “pure survival grift” — and the r/artificial thread that surfaced the story drew the same skepticism.

Palantir chief executive Alex Karp has spent the past year as the Trump administration’s most outspoken corporate ally in tech — the boss of the company that built the software behind immigration enforcement and battlefield targeting, who once called his own firm “completely anti-woke.” On Tuesday, in a sit-down interview with Politico’s Playbook, he said he has “been a Democrat my whole life,” described Palantir’s products as “essentially progressive,” and opened the door to backing a Democrat for president in 2028.

The timing is the story. Karp spoke days after Democrats swept the midterm elections, and he framed the results as a verdict on economic fairness — a “referendum,” in his telling, on the issue he says he cares most about. Whether the pivot is conviction or calculation, it is the clearest sign yet that the tech right’s alliance with the administration is fraying at the edges.

What Karp actually said#

The interview took place in Palantir’s Manhattan office, and Karp did not hedge. “I’ve been a Democrat my whole life. I grew up in a far left environment. I know more about being a progressive than almost anybody on the planet, and I absolutely believe Palantir — all of our products are essentially progressive,” he told Politico. He used the word “progressive” eight times across the conversation, and said Democrats had “absolutely” corrected enough in 2026 to win back his support.

He was explicit about what would decide his vote. “I’m really not pitching Palantir,” Karp said. “I’m actually pitching, ‘What are you gonna do about economic justice in this country?’” For many Republicans, he added, “there’s an orthodoxy that capitalism solves all problems. Maybe that was once true. I never thought it was true. It’s definitely not true now.”

He also broke with the administration on the issue that defines his company’s public image. Karp said he was “really happy to hear the president be honest” that immigration enforcement had gone too far — “Yeah, we kinda went too far” — and added: “I’m glad he’s saying in public now what I’ve been saying in private.”

The contracts that make the pivot awkward#

The rebrand collides with the record. Palantir’s most controversial contract is the $30 million ImmigrationOS deal — software built to help U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement track and deport undocumented immigrants. Its software also supports the Pentagon’s Maven Smart System, which helps military personnel process battlefield information and analyze potential targets. These are the products Karp now calls “essentially progressive.”

The politics collide too. Karp said last year that Palantir was the first company to become “completely anti-woke,” and Commonweal reported he donated $1 million to the president’s latest inauguration. His donation history this year is genuinely mixed: $100,000 to the conservative Americans for Security PAC, alongside donations to Democratic senator Mark Warner of Virginia and the Democratic Party of Virginia. “My giving is equal Democrat, Republican,” Karp said in the interview. “I can tell you, without going into things I’m not gonna disclose, I’m very balanced, and everyone in the political arena knows it.”

Palantir has also brought former Democratic senator Jon Tester of Montana on as an adviser, as congressional investigations loom — a hire that reads very differently this week than it would have a month ago.

Why now#

Karp is not the only tech chief reassessing the bet. Fast Company notes that many had their eyes on larger figures closer to the president — Meta’s Mark Zuckerberg, or on-again-off-again ally Elon Musk — and that Karp “beat them to the punch.” The midterms gave every one of them the same opening: a Democratic wave to point at, and a plausible story about why the politics changed rather than the person.

That is also why the framing matters. Karp is not presenting this as a change of heart. His line is that he never moved — the parties did. “I didn’t shift my politics. The political parties have shifted their politics,” he said in a separate interview this week, arguing that what passes for progressive now “is a complete farce” and that he has “been progressive since the beginning.” It is a neat formulation: it lets him claim the label without conceding the record.

The skepticism#

Almost nobody online is buying it at face value. “Karp spent years selling surveillance and control tools to the regime, then flips the second the midterms hit. Pure survival grift,” one widely shared post on X read. Others predicted “the back peddling gonna be legendary with every passing week that Trump gets even less popular.” Some users turned the interview into memes, using AI to redraw Karp with pink hair and piercings.

The Reddit thread that surfaced the story on r/artificial drew the same verdict. The top comments did not dispute that Karp said it — they disputed that it means anything, calling him a liar to his face and asking whether Democrats would even take his money. That is the honest read of the room: the quote is real, the sincerity is the open question.

And it is worth stating plainly what Karp did not do. He endorsed no candidate, committed to no campaign, and ruled out an entire wing of the party — anyone associated with the Democratic Socialists of America. His support, if it comes, is conditional on competence and on the party’s economic platform. That is a door left open, not a check written.

What to watch#

Five things, in order. First, whether any Democrat seeks Karp’s backing — and whether the party wants it, given the ICE contracts. An endorsement nobody wants is not an endorsement. Second, whether Palantir’s federal business changes at all: contracts are the real test of a pivot, and ImmigrationOS is still running. Third, whether other tech CEOs follow — Zuckerberg and Musk are the ones to watch, and Karp just gave them cover to move. Fourth, the 2028 field itself: Karp’s conditions — competent, economically focused, not DSA — narrow the list of candidates he could plausibly back. Fifth, the internal reaction: Palantir has already faced employee petitions and public protests over its ICE work, and a chief executive publicly rebranding the company as progressive while the contracts continue is the kind of thing that gets read very carefully inside the building.

Until a candidate is named or a contract changes, the honest summary is one sentence long: the chief executive of the administration’s closest AI contractor says he is a progressive now — and the proof will be in what he funds, and what he keeps selling.

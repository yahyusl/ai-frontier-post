OpenAI's models are coming to Google Cloud: frontier models and Codex head to Model Garden

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 21, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: On October 21, 2026, OpenAI announced its frontier models and Codex agentic workflows are coming to Google Cloud's Model Garden, with general availability planned in the coming weeks and access requests open now. Customers will buy through the Google Cloud Marketplace, draw down existing cloud spend commitments, and get the models inside Google Antigravity and the Gemini Agent — while Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner.
  • Who: OpenAI, announcing a partnership with Google Cloud on October 21, 2026.
  • What: OpenAI's frontier models (including GPT-6 Astra, per Reuters) and Codex agentic workflows, natively available in Google Cloud's Model Garden.
  • When: general availability planned "in the coming weeks"; customers can request access now.
  • Buying: transacted via Google Cloud Marketplace; enterprises can draw down existing Google Cloud spend commitments; regulated-industry buyers get Google Cloud's access controls and centralized data governance.
  • Also: models "will soon be available in Google Antigravity and the Gemini Agent"; OpenAI says "Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner".

OpenAI is bringing its frontier models to Google Cloud. On Wednesday, the company announced a partnership to make its frontier models and Codex agentic workflows natively available in Google Cloud's Model Garden, the cloud's catalog of third-party models. General availability is planned "in the coming weeks," and customers can already request access.

For enterprises, the pitch is distribution plus paperwork. Customers will buy the models through Model Garden, transacted on the Google Cloud Marketplace, and enterprises with Google Cloud spend commitments will be able to draw those down against the purchases — a structure built to let AI budgets ride on already-approved cloud spending. Regulated industries get a specific callout: OpenAI's models paired with Google Cloud's built-in access controls, centralized data governance and high-performance infrastructure, so deployments that need control over who touches sensitive data can lean on the cloud's existing guardrails.

Inside Google's own tools

The models won't only live in Model Garden. OpenAI says they "will soon be available in Google Antigravity and the Gemini Agent", giving enterprise customers, in the company's words, "model choice flexibility and the best model for each task" — with what it calls a path from evaluation to broader adoption. That's a striking image: OpenAI's models running inside the tooling built around Google's own Gemini family.

The exclusivity era is over

The deal completes OpenAI's breakout from a single cloud. Alphabet's Google is the latest major hyperscaler to distribute OpenAI's models after its exclusive arrangement with Microsoft expired in April, Reuters reported — OpenAI has since expanded to Amazon Web Services and now to Google Cloud. The move puts OpenAI's models on all three of the top U.S. cloud providers, CNBC notes.

Microsoft, for now, keeps its position. "Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner," OpenAI wrote in the announcement. The two companies also struck a separate cloud deal last year giving OpenAI more computing capacity, per Reuters. And the partnership between the two AI rivals runs deeper than distribution: Google's cloud arm has been positioning itself as a neutral model platform rather than a one-model shop, even as its Gemini models compete directly with OpenAI's.

"This partnership is an important step in making our most capable models and Codex available where customers are already building," said OpenAI president Greg Brockman. Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian said the company is "focused on giving customers a broad selection of models, including those from OpenAI."

Why it matters

For OpenAI, it's an enterprise land-grab: meeting buyers inside the cloud console where their budgets and compliance teams already live. For Google Cloud, it sharpens the neutral-platform pitch — third-party choice over a one-model catalog. The remaining questions are timing and price: "in the coming weeks" is not a date, and OpenAI's post doesn't mention pricing. But the direction of travel is unambiguous — OpenAI's models now sell wherever enterprises already spend.

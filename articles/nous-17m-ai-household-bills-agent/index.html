Nous raises $17M for an AI agent that runs household bills — nearly 250,000 UK homes already let it switch, cancel and chase deals

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 26, 2026 · 4 min read
TL;DR: London startup Nous has raised $17 million, led by Heartcore Capital and Mosaic Ventures, to expand its AI household agent internationally. The agent already acts on bills for nearly 250,000 UK households, managing more than £685 million in annual household spending.
  • Who: Nous, a London startup founded by Greg Marsh, founder of luxury accommodation marketplace onefinestay.
  • What: A $17 million funding round for its AI household agent, led by Heartcore Capital and existing seed investor Mosaic Ventures, with Chalfen Ventures, Joyance Partners and Bain Future Back Ventures participating.
  • When: Announced October 26, 2026; the company currently operates only in the UK and plans international expansion.
  • Scale: Just under 250,000 UK households signed up; more than £685 million in annual household spending managed, an average of £2,765 per household; average household saving of £734.
  • What the agent does: Finds cheaper deals on energy, broadband, mobile and car insurance, switches providers, cancels subscriptions, completes forms, flags unfavourable terms, and manages calendars and appointments.
  • Guardrails: Large language models combined with rules-based logic for sensitive financial actions; FCA-registered; most users choose a “balanced mode” in which Nous flags planned actions first.
  • History: A £6.6 million seed round in February 2022 led by Mosaic; Tech Funding News puts total disclosed funding at around $26 million including this round.

London startup Nous has raised $17 million to take its AI household agent beyond the UK, betting that families will hand over the drudgery of bills, renewals and admin to an agent that does not just advise but acts. The round was led by Heartcore Capital and existing seed investor Mosaic Ventures, with Chalfen Ventures, Joyance Partners and Bain Future Back Ventures also taking part.

Nous is not selling a chatbot that suggests a cheaper tariff. Its agent finds better deals on energy, broadband, mobile and car insurance, switches providers, cancels subscriptions, completes forms and flags unfavourable contract terms or renewal traps. It also manages family calendars, finds tradespeople and books appointments. The pitch, in founder and chief executive Greg Marsh's words, is an agent that takes responsibility for household spending continuously, rather than offering a recommendation and leaving the work to the householder.

What the numbers say so far

The company says just under 250,000 UK households have signed up, and that it manages more than £685 million in annual household spending — an average of £2,765 per household — adding a new household every minute. The average household saves £734, according to Tech Funding News. The business has scaled by a factor of more than 20 in 12 months while keeping the same team size, Marsh told the publication, though he did not specify which metric grew twentyfold.

That scale is the core of the funding case. UK households spend £1.7 trillion a year on things they pay for automatically and do not have time to think about, Marsh said, adding: “We’re at the very beginning of a fundamental shift in consumer behaviour enabled by AI.” He believes more than half of that spending will be managed by agents within 10 years.

An agent that acts — with guardrails

Letting software move your money is a trust question, and Nous leans on structure to answer it. Its technology combines large language models with rules-based logic for specific and sensitive actions, particularly those involving financial decisions. Users choose their level of involvement when they sign up; most opt for a “balanced mode” in which Nous does most of the work but tells the household what it plans to do so they can opt out or make changes.

The company is registered with the UK Financial Conduct Authority, which allows it to support regulated activities such as purchasing insurance, and it says it does not generate revenue through advertising or the sale of customer data — a deliberate attempt to avoid conflicts of interest between users and service providers. Marsh's framing: “We’ve spent the last 18 months building and honing an agent that not only feels magic, but is also one people can actually trust. If you are allowing an AI agent to make financial and purchasing decisions on your behalf, you need to know it’s up to the job.”

What the money buys

The new funding will finance international expansion — Nous currently operates only in the UK — and extend the agent into additional household spending categories and regulated financial services, including broader insurance. More insurance products are in the pipeline, and Marsh said the company would also like to move households’ idle cash into higher-interest savings accounts.

The round follows a £6.6 million seed round in February 2022, led by Mosaic with more than 65 angels. Tech Funding News puts total disclosed funding at around $26 million including the new round. Angel investors in the latest round include Monzo co-founder Tom Blomfield, Founders Factory founder Brent Hoberman and Faculty.ai co-founder Marc Warner.

The context is shifting in Nous’s favour: the FCA’s Mills Review found 20% of UK adults are open to AI making their financial decisions for them, with trust, control and access as the deciding factors. The open question is distribution — whether households trust a specialist agent that acts, or wait for a general-purpose assistant from a big platform that, for now, mostly advises. This $17 million is a bet that acting first, in one narrow vertical, wins.

Sources: Tech.eu, Tech Funding News, FinSMEs.

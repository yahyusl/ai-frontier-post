Omnichat raises $10M to turn chat apps into AI sales teams — 300 brands joined its analyst agent in two months

Omnichat has raised a $10 million Series A to scale its Omni AI Agents across chat apps, after its OmniClaw analyst agent was taken up by around 300 brands within two months of its June launch.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 27, 2026 · 4 min read
TL;DR: Omnichat has raised a $10 million Series A, led by Openspace Capital, to scale AI agents that market, sell and support customers inside chat apps. Its OmniClaw analyst agent was taken up by around 300 brands within two months of its June launch, according to the company.
  • Who: Omnichat, an AI-native marketing and customer experience platform, announced the round from Hong Kong on October 27, 2026.
  • What: a US$10 million Series A, led by Openspace Capital, with Darwin Ventures and Acorn Pacific Ventures participating.
  • Use of funds: scale its Agentic AI platform, deepen R&D on Omni AI Agents, and accelerate global expansion from its Asia-Pacific base.
  • The agents: an AI Marketing Agent, an AI Sales Agent, an AI Customer Service Agent, and OmniClaw, an AI business analyst.
  • Adoption: OmniClaw was taken up by around 300 brands within two months of its June launch; brands using the Marketing Agent tripled over the same period.
  • Scale claimed: more than 5,000 clients and over US$100 million in revenue helped generated, according to Omnichat.

Omnichat, an AI-native marketing and customer experience platform, has raised a US$10 million Series A to scale a suite of AI agents that do marketing, sales and support work inside chat apps. The round, announced on October 27, 2026, was led by Openspace Capital, with Darwin Ventures and Acorn Pacific Ventures participating.

The company frames the raise as a shift in what its platform is for. “This Series A marks an important step as we transition from conversational commerce to AI-driven commerce,” founder and CEO Alan Chan said in the announcement. “Traditional customer messaging tools still need heavy human intervention to drive sales. With our Omni AI Agent platform, enterprises can deploy agents that sell, market, and support customers around the clock.”

Four agents, one chat thread#

The funding will scale Omni AI Agents, the suite Omnichat launched in 2026, which is designed to do the core work of marketing, sales and service teams inside the chat itself — not in a separate dashboard the customer never sees. Each agent has a distinct job. The AI Marketing Agent builds customer segments and generates complete messaging campaigns from a single prompt. The AI Sales Agent recommends products and closes orders inside the chat. The AI Customer Service Agent resolves customer enquiries around the clock. And OmniClaw, an AI business analyst, tracks customer behaviour, chat trends and store-level revenue attribution in real time.

That last agent is the one with a number attached. OmniClaw was taken up by around 300 brands within two months of its June launch, according to the announcement, and the number of brands using the Marketing Agent tripled over the same period. Those are company-reported adoption figures, not independently audited results, but they are the clearest signal in the announcement about which part of the suite is pulling hardest: the analyst that tells a brand what its chats are actually worth, rather than the agents that merely answer them.

Why the bet is the chat window#

Omnichat’s argument is that the chat window is where the customer already is, so it is where the sale should happen. Unlike shared-inbox tools that only aggregate chats, the company says, its platform unifies marketing, sales and customer service on a single customer profile, and attributes revenue back to the conversation — including purchases completed in physical stores. That last detail matters: a chat that ends with “I’ll pick it up tomorrow” is usually recorded as a failed sale, not a delayed one, and OmniClaw’s job is to close that loop.

The agents run across WhatsApp, LINE, Facebook Messenger, Instagram, WeChat, KakaoTalk and TikTok. That channel list is also a map of the company’s home market: a platform built for Asia-Pacific messaging habits, where LINE and WeChat carry commerce in a way they rarely do in North America, now raising money to take the same playbook global. Omnichat describes itself as a Meta Business Partner, a WhatsApp Business Solution Provider and a LINE Biz-Solutions Tech Partner, and says its AI Social CRM ecosystem unifies marketing automation, sales conversion and customer support.

On scale, Omnichat says it has helped 5,000 clients generate over US$100 million in revenue, and names Dyson, FILA, Watsons, L’OCCITANE, VF Corporation, SMCP and Mead Johnson among its clients. The announcement does not break that revenue figure down by agent, region or period, and it gives no valuation for the round — both worth noting before treating the headline numbers as a full picture of the business.

What the money buys — and what to watch#

Building on its operational footprint across Asia Pacific, Omnichat says it will deploy the Series A capital to scale its market presence globally. Openspace Capital, the lead investor, is a Singapore-headquartered multi-strategy asset manager with a long-established focus on Southeast Asia, and a portfolio that includes Gojek (GoTo), Kredivo and TradeGecko. That backing fits the story Omnichat is telling: a regional specialist betting that agentic commerce travels, rather than a generalist fund chasing the latest model release.

Ten million dollars is a modest round by 2026 AI standards, and that modesty is part of the story. Omnichat is not raising to train a model; it is raising to sell agents built on top of other companies’ models, into a workflow — customer messaging — where brands already spend money and already measure results. The test of the raise will not be a benchmark score. It will be whether the adoption curve OmniClaw showed in its first two months holds as the company moves beyond its home markets, and whether brands keep paying for agents once the novelty of an AI that answers the chat wears off and the only question left is whether it moved revenue.

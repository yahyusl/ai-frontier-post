Trump hosts Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei for a private White House dinner

The president invited Dario Amodei to a private Sunday dinner after the Anthropic chief missed Thursday's state dinner for Xi Jinping. The first one-on-one between the two comes two days after a federal appeals court upheld the Pentagon's blacklisting of Anthropic.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · September 27, 2026 · 6 min read

President Donald Trump is hosting Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei for a private dinner at the White House on Sunday evening — the first one-on-one meeting between the two men, and the most direct contact yet between an administration that has spent a year squeezing Anthropic and the lab's chief executive.

The invitation, first reported by Axios and confirmed by CNBC, is the kind of gesture that only happens on purpose. White House dinners are scheduled, not stumbled into, and the choice of guest — the one frontier-lab CEO who has publicly called for slowing down — is itself the message.

How the dinner came about#

Amodei was noticeably absent from Thursday's glittering state dinner for Chinese President Xi Jinping, where OpenAI's Sam Altman, SpaceX's Elon Musk, Nvidia's Jensen Huang and Meta's Mark Zuckerberg were photographed with officials. According to reporting, Amodei had a scheduling conflict — and Trump personally invited him to a private dinner instead.

Neither the White House nor Anthropic has published an agenda, and no attendee list beyond the two principals has been reported. The White House's statement to CNBC gave no ground on the administration's AI stance: "America will lead the world in Super Intelligence, while protecting American consumers."

A year of friction — and Friday's court loss#

The timing is what makes the invitation newsworthy. On Friday, a D.C. federal appeals court panel voted 2-1 to uphold the Pentagon's "supply-chain risk" designation of Anthropic — the blacklist the Pentagon imposed after Anthropic tried to put guardrails on how its technology is used. Anthropic had argued the ban was arbitrary, unauthorized and unconstitutional; the company can still petition for a rehearing or take the case to the Supreme Court.

It caps a turbulent year. In June, Trump told Axios he had recently viewed Anthropic as a potential national security threat, even as he indicated tensions had begun to ease. The administration's treatment of the company has included export restrictions and the Pentagon designation. Earlier this month, Trump attacked Amodei on Truth Social over his proposal to slow the pace of advanced model development — a three-step plan calling for additional government oversight that drew support from Altman and Musk even as it angered the president.

The briefing war#

Anthropic's critics have not been idle. A memo circulated within the White House last week sought to tie Amodei to the effective-altruism movement and portrayed his approach to AI safety as being at odds with the administration's push to accelerate U.S. development of the technology. Anthropic has spent months trying to distance itself from the effective-altruism label — but, per Axios's reporting, the memo shows the president's allies are not letting that distinction stick.

Axios also reported Sunday that Amodei's critics were circulating yet another negative briefing about him ahead of the dinner. The document was not produced by the White House, and its origins were unclear. The same week, Amodei stood before the UN Security Council and said unmanaged AI could be "a risk to humanity as a whole." You do not usually get invited to dinner by someone whose allies just circulated a memo casting your worldview as un-American — which is exactly what happened here, and says something real about how badly Anthropic wants a seat at the table as the company is reportedly gearing up for a blockbuster IPO, per CNBC's reporting.

What to watch#

The dinner is only the opening move. On Tuesday, Trump and House Speaker Mike Johnson are expected to meet with executives from several major AI companies — a larger, more public gathering where whatever was said over the private meal may surface as policy. Watch whether Anthropic's standing with the Pentagon moves, whether anything concrete on export rules or federal procurement follows, and whether Amodei's safety agenda finds any buyer in a White House that has made acceleration its brand.

Public reaction has been skeptical so far: Axios's own Threads post announcing the dinner drew a comment section dominated by cynical readings of the meeting as coercion rather than diplomacy. That is the backdrop Amodei walks into tonight — a dinner he badly needs, with a host who has every reason to enjoy that asymmetry.

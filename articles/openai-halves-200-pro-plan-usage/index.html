OpenAI halves usage on its $200 Pro plan — and power users are furious

OpenAI reopened its $200 Pro tier on DevDay day with roughly half the usage of the old plan, renamed the tiers, and may be readying a $500 Pro Max. Power subscribers are calling it a rug pull.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · September 29, 2026 · 7 min read

On the morning of its DevDay conference, OpenAI reopened the doors to its $200-a-month ChatGPT Pro plan — and cut what subscribers actually get for the money roughly in half. "If you do the math, it will net out at half the dollar in API spend compared to the old Pro $200 plan," wrote Tibo Sottiaux, an OpenAI employee working on Codex, in a post to subscribers flagged ahead of what he described as a series of big announcements.

The power users who pay for the plan are not taking it well. Overnight, threads on r/singularity and r/OpenAI filled with firsthand confirmations of the cuts and open talk of defection — including one subscriber who said they had just bought a second Anthropic Max subscription in protest. The timing, hours before a DevDay keynote teasing more than 20 launches, has done nothing to soften the blow.

What actually changed#

The change is more than a quiet numbers tweak. The two Pro tiers have been renamed on OpenAI's plan pages: the $100 tier is now "Pro Standard" and the $200 tier "Pro More," and the old concrete promises — "5x the usage of Plus," "20x the usage of Plus" — have been replaced with the vague "More usage than Plus," according to reporting on the updated pages.

The $200 tier had been closed to new sign-ups since around September 10, when surging demand for GPT-6 Astra — OpenAI's computer-use flagship released September 3 — overwhelmed capacity. Instead of good news, returning subscribers found the allowance behind it cut roughly in half in API-spend terms.

One correction worth making to the angriest takes: the old five-hour usage windows were already removed about two months ago, and the new arrangement commits to not bringing them back. Subscribers get a weekly allowance they can burn through at their own pace rather than being throttled in short bursts.

The trade-off OpenAI is selling#

That flexibility is the core of OpenAI's pitch. For heavy users of coding agents like Codex, who tend to work in long, intense sessions, being able to spend a weekly budget whenever they choose could matter as much as the raw size of the allowance — at least in theory.

In practice, the same $200 now buys roughly half the usage. For subscribers who bought into Pro specifically for marathon agent sessions, the flexibility reads less like a feature and more like the consolation prize.

The backlash#

The reaction was swift. A thread on r/singularity titled "WTF: OpenAI's $200 Pro Plan Is Getting a 50% Usage Cut" climbed rapidly overnight, with commenters confirming the tier changes firsthand; on r/OpenAI, a thread bluntly titled "The rug pull was real" drew subscribers describing the move as a betrayal.

The grievances go beyond the numbers. On OpenAI's own community forum, Pro subscribers have been posting since September 27 about hard usage limits and error messages appearing on a plan advertised as "unlimited" — screenshots of "thinking failed" and "too many requests" next to a $200 monthly bill. Others are openly weighing alternatives, from Claude to open-weight models.

A sharper counter-narrative is also circulating in the threads: one highly visible comment questioned whether the scrapping of the GPT-6.1 Astra model was really about safety at all, suggesting the model simply "eats 3x the tokens of Opus 5.5." That figure is a commenter's claim, not a verified fact — but it captures the mood.

The compute crunch behind the cut#

The backdrop is a capacity crisis OpenAI has been narrating in public for weeks. When it suspended $200 Pro sign-ups on September 11, Sottiaux said the tier "puts the most strain on our systems" and framed the pause as the smallest possible step to keep access broad. Demand for Astra was, in his earlier words, "unprecedented."

Since then the pressure has only compounded: the Wall Street Journal reported OpenAI scrapped the GPT-6.1 Astra model over safety concerns, while Anthropic's Sonnet 5.5 launch has been pulling power users — and mindshare — in the other direction.

And there may be a third tier coming. A September 25 change to OpenAI's public Codex codebase introduced a new plan type displayed as "Pro (Max)," and leaked in-app strings suggest a $500-per-month price pitched around "the fastest work experience with Codex." Neither the price nor the plan is confirmed — but a $500 ceiling would explain why the "5x/20x" ladder had to be dismantled: three tiers need a new way to be distinguished.

What to watch#

Today's DevDay keynote is the obvious next data point: whether OpenAI puts the new allowance numbers in writing, and whether Pro Max materializes on stage. Plus subscribers should watch too — their tier is untouched, but the vague "More usage than Plus" language is doing a lot of quiet work.

The deeper question is whether the defection threats are real. Anthropic's timing — a well-received Sonnet 5.5 landing just as Pro subscribers feel squeezed — is the kind of coincidence that decides where $200 a month goes. If a meaningful slice of OpenAI's heaviest users walks, today's "smallest possible measure" will look like the moment the company started rationing its best customers.

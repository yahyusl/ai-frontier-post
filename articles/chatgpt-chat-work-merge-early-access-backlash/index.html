ChatGPT's merged Chat and Work mode has arrived for early users — and they say the chat part is gone

OpenAI's plan to fold ChatGPT's Chat and Work tabs into one experience has started reaching users — and the first people to get it are not happy. In a Reddit thread posted late on November 25, an early-access user reports that the merged mode behaves as if Work had swallowed Chat whole: the toggle is gone, the tone is all business, and the allowance drains faster.

OpenAI's plan to merge ChatGPT's Chat and Work tabs into a single experience has started reaching users — and the first reports are not encouraging. A first-hand account posted to r/ChatGPT late on November 25, titled “Prepare... It's not great.”, describes a merged mode that behaves as if Work had swallowed Chat whole. The post gathered 198 upvotes and 96 comments by Thursday morning, and the users piling in largely agree: the chat part of ChatGPT appears to be gone.

What early users are seeing#

The report, from a Pro user in the early rollout, lists five changes — and other users in the thread say they are seeing the same behaviour in their own accounts:

  • The Chat/Work slider is gone. There is no way to switch back to Chat mode; the merged experience is the only option on offer.
  • Replies come back terse and deliverable-oriented even when the question is personal. The model answers like it is drafting a work product, not holding a conversation.
  • Usage adds up per chat. Every conversation draws on the same shared allowance, so the meter runs down faster than users expect.
  • Temporary chat is unusable on mobile, because the Work models behind the merged mode do not work there.
  • Messages cannot be edited — though the original poster hedges that this one may be a bug rather than a deliberate change.

No top comment in the thread disputes the facts of the report, and the reaction is near-unanimously negative. The complaint, repeated through the thread, is that a tool people used for thinking out loud now answers like a colleague handing in a report.

Why this is happening now#

OpenAI announced the merger by email on November 2: Work's agentic capabilities would fold into Chat, and from December, Plus, Pro and Business plans would share a single token-based usage allowance across ChatGPT and Codex. Instant-model chats stay unlimited and do not count against the allowance. On November 24, product lead Thibault Sottiaux said on X that the combined experience was rolling out to a small group of Pro users — the group whose reports are now surfacing. OpenAI's support article, updated the same day, confirms the December shared allowance.

The merger is the second squeeze on Pro allowances in a month. On October 29, OpenAI doubled the Pro 100, 200 and 500 Work and Codex allowances ahead of the change. No per-plan token caps for the shared December allowance have been published, so users still do not know exactly how much of the merged allowance their plan will buy.

The workaround users found#

Users in the thread found a way back: the model picker. Choosing “Latest” or “Use a range of models” — or GPT 6.2 Instant directly — restores Chat-mode features. Instant chats are unlimited and do not count against the shared allowance, which makes Instant the practical escape hatch for casual use.

The catch is that the workaround is exactly that — a workaround. The merged mode is the default, and the picker setting has to be chosen deliberately. For users who open ChatGPT on their phone and ask a casual question, the merged experience is what they get.

The reaction#

The thread's tone is frustration rather than surprise. Users had a month's warning that the merger was coming; what they did not expect was a merged mode with no visible trace of the Chat side. It is worth keeping the scale in view: this is a small early rollout, and OpenAI has a history of adjusting rollouts in response to early feedback. But the December plan makes the merged experience the default for every paying plan, so what these users are seeing is, for now, the preview of the standard ChatGPT.

What to watch#

Four things. First, whether OpenAI restores a visible Chat/Work toggle before the December rollout, or keeps the merged mode as the only option. Second, whether the message-editing problem turns out to be a bug — as the original poster suggested it might be — and gets fixed. Third, whether OpenAI publishes per-plan token caps for the shared December allowance; none have been published yet. Fourth, whether the frustration turns into switching: the same week, OpenAI told users their limits would run out faster after a billing fix, so the merger lands on already-sore subscribers.

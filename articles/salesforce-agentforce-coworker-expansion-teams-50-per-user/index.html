Agentforce Coworker expands into Teams, email and mobile: Salesforce's AI teammate at $50 a user a month

Salesforce is taking Agentforce Coworker out of the CRM and into the places employees actually work. Announced on November 5, 2026, the expansion puts the AI teammate inside Microsoft Teams, Google Workspace and Microsoft 365, adds iOS, Mac and Windows apps, and introduces a $50-per-user monthly price — as Salesforce says usage has grown 21-fold in three months to more than 185,000 users.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 5, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: Salesforce announced a major expansion of Agentforce Coworker on November 5, 2026, moving its AI teammate into Microsoft Teams, Google Workspace and Microsoft 365, with new iOS, Mac and Windows apps and a $50-per-user monthly option. Salesforce says usage has grown 21-fold in three months to more than 185,000 users, with The Adecco Group rolling it out across 40 countries.
  • Who and when: Salesforce announced the Agentforce Coworker expansion on November 5, 2026.
  • What: Agentforce Coworker, Salesforce’s AI teammate for delegating business work, expands into Microsoft Teams, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and new iOS, Mac and Windows apps.
  • Growth: Usage is up 21-fold in three months, to more than 185,000 users, according to Salesforce.
  • Price: A new option costs US$50 per user per month.
  • Available now: Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 connectivity, Conversation History, reusable Skills, and the per-user monthly option.
  • Coming next: Coworker in Microsoft Teams and the new apps are generally available in November 2026; Daily Digest and scheduled tasks follow in December 2026. The Adecco Group is expanding Coworker across 40 countries.

Salesforce announced the expansion on November 5, 2026, positioning Coworker as the way its customers move from experimenting with AI agents to actually delegating work to them. Coworker is the company’s AI teammate: instead of opening several apps or deciding which Agentforce agent should handle a task, an employee hands Coworker the job — say, investigating an at-risk renewal — and it plans the steps, coordinates the right agents and workflows, and returns results and recommended actions for review.

The pitch, in Salesforce’s framing, is subtraction. “The next era of enterprise AI isn’t about giving users more agents to manage,” said James Nakashima, SVP of Product Management at Salesforce. “It’s about giving them one Coworker that brings the right agents together to get work done.” Because Coworker starts from the customer’s existing Salesforce records, metadata and permissions, the company says teams can get started in minutes rather than standing up a separate AI implementation.

What’s new today

The expansion lands in the tools employees already live in. Coworker now works inside Microsoft Teams, so a seller can delegate a deal review or a service manager can hand over an escalation from a chat, with the same Salesforce context, permissions and governance attached. New connections to Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 pull the email, calendars and files around each account into the picture, so a meeting brief or a renewal plan reflects the whole customer story rather than only what sits in the CRM.

New iOS, Mac and Windows apps keep delegated work moving across devices: hand a job off from a laptop, track it from a phone, approve the result from anywhere. Conversation History means a job started in the morning can be picked up later from another device without starting over, and Skills turn a proven workflow — a top rep’s renewal playbook, for example — into a one-line request the whole team can run.

Adecco is rolling it out across 40 countries

The headline customer is The Adecco Group, which is expanding Coworker across 40 countries so its teams can pull up candidate history and client context at the moment human judgment matters. “The launch of Agentforce Coworker means our teams have access to a single interface that understands candidate history and client context precisely when human judgment matters most,” said Denis Machuel, CEO of The Adecco Group.

The growth figures behind the expansion are Salesforce’s own: usage up 21-fold in three months, with more than 185,000 users now putting Coworker to work. Salesforce has not published independent evidence of the time or cost savings behind those numbers, so the adoption curve is best read as a signal of demand, not a measured return. The pattern will be familiar to anyone watching the race to build AI coworkers that sit inside everyday work: the product that wins is the one employees meet where they already are.

The governance question

A teammate that can act across email, files and CRM records is only useful if it stays inside the lines. Salesforce says Coworker operates within its established security and governance framework, using each employee’s permissions, sharing rules and configured access controls, with approval and audit capabilities for supported operations. That matters more as agents gain the ability to take multi-step actions on a worker’s behalf — the same agent-governance problem that enterprises are now building whole platforms around.

Availability and pricing

Available now are the Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 connections, Conversation History, Skills and the per-user monthly option. Coworker in Microsoft Teams, the iOS app, the Mac and Windows desktop apps, a Coworker MCP server and a free trial for Flex Credits customers are all generally available in November 2026. Scheduled tasks and a morning Daily Digest arrive in December, alongside Coworker in the Field Service, Consumer Goods Cloud and Life Sciences Cloud mobile apps.

The new price is US$50 per user per month, which Salesforce pitches as simple and predictable budgeting as adoption grows. Coworker itself is not new — Salesforce first announced it in May 2026, and said 100,000 users adopted it within its first 35 days. What changed today is the surface area: from a feature inside Salesforce to a teammate that follows the employee into Teams, email and mobile. Coverage of the announcement notes the same November rollout window for the Teams integration and the new apps.

Why this one matters

Every major software vendor is now racing to put an AI teammate between the employee and the stack of apps they juggle — business AI agents that monitor data and build things on request are shipping from startups as well as incumbents. Salesforce’s advantage is distribution: it already holds the customer record, the permissions and the workflows, and Coworker inherits all three on day one. The open question is the one Salesforce’s own numbers can’t answer yet: whether delegating to one coworker actually saves more time than managing the agents directly. The next three months of usage data — and whether Adecco’s 40-country rollout sticks — will be the first real test.

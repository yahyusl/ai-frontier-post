Meta buys Conductor, the multi-agent coding startup — and its cloud service shuts down November 20

Meta has acquired Conductor, the startup that lets developers run several AI coding agents in parallel workspaces. The team joins Meta Superintelligence Labs — and Conductor Cloud goes read-only on November 13, then shuts down on November 20, with user data deletion to follow.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · November 7, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Meta has acquired Conductor, the startup that runs several AI coding agents in parallel workspaces, and the team is joining Meta Superintelligence Labs. The catch for users: Conductor Cloud goes read-only November 13 and shuts down November 20, when user data deletion begins.
  • Deal: Meta acquired Conductor; first reported by Axios on November 4, confirmed by founder Charlie Holtz and by Meta to TechCrunch. Terms undisclosed.
  • What it is: an orchestration layer that runs multiple coding agents (from Anthropic, OpenAI and others) in parallel, isolated workspaces — Conductor builds no models of its own.
  • Shutdown: Conductor Cloud goes read-only November 13, shuts down November 20, then user cloud data deletion begins. Sign-ups and downloads already stopped.
  • Local app: the Mac app keeps getting new models and critical bug fixes until early 2027.
  • What's next: the team joins Meta Superintelligence Labs under Alexandr Wang and Nat Friedman, with “something new” promised for early next year.

Meta has bought Conductor, the startup that lets software engineers run several AI coding agents at once in separate workspaces. The deal was first reported by Axios on November 4, confirmed the same day by Conductor founder and chief executive Charlie Holtz on the company's blog, and confirmed to TechCrunch by Meta. Financial terms were not disclosed, and Meta declined to say anything more about the deal.

For Conductor's users, the acquisition comes with a hard deadline. Conductor Cloud goes read-only on November 13 and shuts down on November 20, after which the company will begin deleting user cloud data. New sign-ups and downloads stopped the day the deal was announced. The local Mac app gets a longer runway: Conductor says it will keep adding new models and fixing critical bugs until early 2027.

What Conductor actually does#

Conductor is an orchestration layer, not a model company. Instead of asking one AI assistant to work through a project step by step, a developer can spin up several agents at once, each in its own isolated workspace with its own branch, terminal, and review path, and let them tackle different parts of the job in parallel. The agents themselves come from the major labs — Anthropic and OpenAI among them — while Conductor supplies the coordination that keeps their work separate.

That framing matters, because it is where the coding-agent race has moved. The frontier models are increasingly close to one another on raw capability; the differentiator is becoming how many agents you can run safely at once, and how well their work is kept apart. Conductor was founded in 2024 and only launched its core product last summer, which makes the acquisition timeline strikingly short even by startup standards. It had reportedly raised a $22 million Series A in March from Spark Capital and Matrix.

The shutdown timetable#

Conductor's own changelog lays out the wind-down in plain terms. From November 13, Conductor Cloud stops accepting new messages; existing users can still view past chats and export their data. On November 20 the cloud service shuts down and deletion of user cloud data begins. Workspaces can be moved to a local Mac by right-clicking and choosing Transfer to local, and a full export is available under Settings → Account → Export data.

The speed of the shutdown is the part users should take seriously. Two weeks from announcement to deletion is a short migration window, and the company is explicit that November 20 is when deletion starts, not a deadline for finishing it. Anyone with work living only in Conductor Cloud should export before the 13th, when the service turns read-only.

Why Meta wants it#

The team is joining Meta Superintelligence Labs, where Holtz says he will work with chief AI officer Alexandr Wang and product head Nat Friedman on what he calls a new interface, with “something new” promised for early next year. On X, Holtz praised the lab's talent density, calling it the highest of any lab.

The acquisition fits a pattern. Meta spent much of the year catching up in AI after lagging behind its rivals, launching its own coding agent, Muse Code, in August. The company recently said its Muse agents had passed 10 million users — a company figure, not independently verified — and it has made a string of AI acquisitions over the past year, including Moltbook, the viral agent platform. Buying Conductor is less about adding a product than about absorbing a team that has spent two years thinking about multi-agent coordination, and folding that thinking into Muse Code and whatever interface comes next.

What to watch#

Three questions will decide whether this deal amounts to more than an acqui-hire. First, does Conductor's parallel-workspace approach survive inside Meta, or does it get absorbed into Muse Code and disappear as a distinct way of working? Second, what happens to Conductor's multi-model neutrality — a tool built on Anthropic and OpenAI agents now sits inside a company with its own models and its own coding agent. And third, whether Meta ever discloses what it paid: the silence on terms, headcount, and roadmap is standard for Meta, but it leaves users and competitors guessing about how seriously the company is betting on this direction.

For now, the practical takeaway is narrow and dated: if you use Conductor Cloud, export your work before November 13. The strategic takeaway is broader. The coding-agent contest is no longer only about which model writes the best code. It is about who can run the most agents at once without them tripping over each other — and Meta just bought one of the teams that had figured that part out.

Frequently asked questions

What happens to my Conductor Cloud data?

Conductor Cloud stops accepting new messages on November 13 and shuts down on November 20, after which deletion of user cloud data begins. Export everything before the 13th: use Settings → Account → Export data, or right-click a cloud workspace and choose Transfer to local to move it to your Mac.

Does the local Conductor app still work?

Yes. Conductor says the local Mac app will keep receiving new models and critical bug fixes until early 2027. Only the cloud service is shutting down this month.

Did Meta say what it paid for Conductor?

No. Meta confirmed the acquisition to TechCrunch but declined to comment further, and no financial terms, headcount, or roadmap details have been disclosed.

RUMOR: Qwen-4-Flash-Early tops a community game-making benchmark, narrowly beating Claude Opus 5.5

An early, unreleased Qwen checkpoint leads a small community leaderboard that asks models to build playable 2D games. The margin is thin, the test is narrow, and Alibaba has confirmed nothing — here’s what the numbers actually say.

An early, unreleased Qwen checkpoint has taken the top spot on a small community leaderboard that asks AI models to build playable 2D games — and the local-model corner of Reddit is moving fast on it. The catch is large: the test is narrow, the operator is anonymous, the margin over Claude’s best is thin, and Alibaba has confirmed nothing about this model. Here is what the numbers actually say, and what they don’t.

The signal surfaced overnight on r/LocalLLaMA, where a post linking an X announcement by follistatindev said Qwen-4-Flash-Early, codenamed “hawk,” had been added to the GameFeel Benchmark. It was the fastest-moving post in our overnight sweep of the AI subreddits, and the thread underneath is split: half the commenters treat the scores as a genuine upset, the other half call it benchmaxxing and hopium. We checked the leaderboard itself — and both sides have a point.

What the leaderboard actually shows#

On the GameFeel Benchmark’s own site, updated November 8 at 01:40, Qwen-4-Flash-Early sits first of 25 entries with an overall score of 49.9 out of 100, ahead of Opus 5.5 at high effort (47.2) and Sonnet 5.5 at maximum effort (45.0). The lead is real, and it is narrow: 2.7 points on a hundred-point scale, not the rout the thread’s framing suggests.

The detail matters more than the headline. Qwen-4-Flash-Early does not beat Claude on raw code creation at all — all three models score 65 there, and both Claude entries beat it on fixing bugs (65 to 63). The Qwen lead comes from the creative and feel categories: gamefeel (52 to Opus’s 50), ideas (54 to 41), visuals (64 to 63), and audio (31 to 25). On this test, in other words, the model is not writing better code than Opus 5.5; it is making games that play and look slightly better.

Then there is the price column, which is the part worth watching. The benchmark lists Qwen-4-Flash-Early at $0.40 per task, against $2.60 for Opus 5.5 at high effort and $7.80 for Sonnet 5.5 at maximum effort. If those figures are roughly right, a model costing a fraction of Claude’s top tiers is matching them on creative coding work — and that, more than the ranking, is why the thread caught fire.

How the test works — and why it is narrow#

GameFeel is a deliberately small benchmark. Each model builds a small 2D game in a single HTML file, in four timed 30-minute runs with a random game prompt; after each run the game is played and judged on how fun and polished it feels, and each model also fixes a bug. Every run is scored seven ways — code, creativity, gamefeel, and a repeatability check — and averaged across the four runs. Only models with all seven scores are shown, which is why the board has just 25 entries.

That narrowness is the whole caveat. Building a playable game in half an hour is one skill: fast creative coding with a feel for what makes something fun. It says nothing about reasoning, mathematics, or long-horizon agentic work. A model can top this board and be ordinary everywhere else, and nothing on the site claims otherwise.

The operator is also unnamed. The site says the team runs everything itself, on its own computer, so no vendor can hide a bad run — an RTX 3090 with 32 GB of RAM — and publishes its data and methods, including a downloadable CSV. That is more transparent than many community benchmarks, but it is still one small operation running four half-hour attempts per model, not an independent lab with a large sample.

Why the thread is split#

The Reddit thread does not confirm the headline so much as argue about it. Enthusiastic commenters read the scores as an open-weights model matching Anthropic’s best at a fraction of the cost; skeptics call the result benchmaxxing, doubt that a model of this class could match Opus 5.5, and note the numbers come from one small benchmark relayed through an X post, with no Qwen or Alibaba source in the thread.

One detail cuts toward the skeptics: the “hawk” codename that the X post and the Reddit title attach to this model appears nowhere on the benchmark site, which lists the entry only as Qwen-4-Flash-Early. A codename circulating on social media but absent from the primary source is exactly the kind of detail that deserves to stay labeled unverified until Alibaba or the benchmark operator confirms it.

What Alibaba has actually said#

Alibaba’s own account of Qwen 4 is far more restrained. At its annual Apsara Conference in Hangzhou on September 22, 2026, the company said Qwen 4 is in training, and laid out a roadmap in which the Qwen 4.5 and Qwen 5 series that follow are projected to reach 5 to 10 trillion parameters. Its published conference summary gives no release date, no model sizes, and no licensing terms. That is the verified record, and it has not visibly moved since — we reported the same gap in our October 29 rumor piece on a claimed Qwen 4 launch date.

Nothing in that record confirms Qwen-4-Flash-Early is Qwen 4 Flash, or that a Flash variant exists in any releasable form. The benchmark lists the entry under its own “Max” effort tier and attributes it to Alibaba Qwen, but that is the site’s label, not an Alibaba statement. Early checkpoints of unreleased models do surface on community leaderboards under provisional names before any official announcement — a familiar pattern, but not confirmation.

What to watch#

Three things would turn this rumor into news. First, an on-the-record statement from Alibaba or the Qwen team — a model card, a weights listing, or an announcement naming Qwen-4-Flash-Early, not another relayed account. Second, independent replication: the same checkpoint on other benchmarks, or the GameFeel numbers reproduced with more runs, so the 2.7-point lead can be judged against the test’s own noise. Third, the cost claim: if a model this cheap really matches Claude’s top tiers on creative coding, the pricing — not the ranking — is what would reshape the market for game and vibe-coding work.

Until one of those appears, the honest summary is short: an unreleased Qwen checkpoint leads one small, game-focused leaderboard by a small margin, at a strikingly low listed cost. That is interesting. It is not a coronation — and the thread arguing about it knows that better than its headline does.

Frequently asked questions

Is Qwen-4-Flash-Early actually better than Claude Opus 5.5?

No such claim is supported. It leads on one narrow benchmark — building small 2D games — by 2.7 overall points, ties Claude Opus 5.5 on code creation, and trails it on bugfixing. General capability is unmeasured.

What is the GameFeel Benchmark?

A community-run leaderboard that has each model build a small 2D game in a single HTML file in four timed 30-minute runs, then fix a bug, scoring code, creativity, and gamefeel. It lists 25 models and is run by an unnamed operator on their own hardware.

Has Alibaba confirmed Qwen-4-Flash-Early?

No. Alibaba has confirmed only that Qwen 4 is in training, at its Apsara Conference in September 2026. It has given no release date, no specifications, and no confirmation that this checkpoint is Qwen 4 Flash. The “hawk” codename does not appear on the benchmark site.

Legora raises $550M at an $8.8B valuation: the legal AI arms race moves from assistants to agents

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 21, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: Legora, the Stockholm-based legal AI company, raised a $550 million Series E at an $8.8 billion post-money valuation on October 21, 2026, led by Atreides Management. The same check size as its March Series D now prices the company at $8.8 billion versus $5.6 billion — with annual recurring revenue above $200 million and in-house legal teams, not just law firms, driving the next wave of growth.
  • What: Legora's $550M Series E at an $8.8B post-money valuation, announced October 21, 2026, led by Atreides Management.
  • New investors: Baron Capital, Capital Group, Fidelity Management & Research Company, Glade Brook Capital Partners and J.P. Morgan — alongside every major existing backer.
  • Revenue: ARR went from $1M to $100M in under 18 months, then added the next $100M in under six months. It now stands above $200M.

  • The pivot: In-house legal teams, a small fraction of customers a year ago, were over 40% of new customers in Q3.
  • Previous round: $550M Series D in March 2026 at a $5.6B valuation, with a $50M extension in April.
  • Scale: Founded 2023, platform launched October 2024, 1,000+ employees across 18 offices.

Stockholm-based legal AI company Legora announced Wednesday that it has raised a $550 million Series E at an $8.8 billion post-money valuation — the same check size as its March Series D, at a markedly higher price. The round, led by Boston-based Atreides Management, lands seven months after that March round and values the company 57% above its previous $5.6 billion mark.

The company says the growth behind the number is the fastest any directly sold enterprise software product has shown: annual recurring revenue went from $1 million to $100 million in under 18 months, and the next $100 million took under six months. ARR now stands above $200 million, SiliconANGLE reports.

The round

New investors joining the Series E include Baron Capital, Capital Group, Fidelity Management & Research Company, Glade Brook Capital Partners and J.P. Morgan. Every major existing investor is participating again: Accel, Benchmark, Bessemer Venture Partners, General Catalyst, ICONIQ, Insight Venture Partners, Redpoint Ventures, Salesforce Ventures and Sands Capital, according to Legora's announcement.

"The opportunity in legal is enormous and we are still early," said co-founder and CEO Max Junestrand. "AI is changing the role of the lawyer, with more time for judgment and advice, and it is reshaping how law firms and in-house legal teams are organized and how their work gets done."

Atreides managing partner and chief investment officer Gavin Baker added that Legora "has become the platform that law firms and in-house legal teams adopt and then expand across their organizations, and it has done so while growing at remarkable speed."

The company says the capital will fund continued expansion in North America, Europe and Asia-Pacific, and deepen investment in Legora aOS, its agentic operating system for legal work — covering agentic intake, horizon scanning and legal research.

The in-house pivot

The most telling number in the announcement may not be the valuation. In-house legal departments — a small fraction of Legora's customer base a year ago — made up more than 40% of new customers in the third quarter. The United States is now Legora's largest market by revenue, with more than half of the AmLaw 50 as customers; the company only opened its first U.S. office in spring 2025.

That shift mirrors the product bet: legal teams are moving beyond AI assistants toward agents that carry out multi-step work. Earlier this month, both Legora and rival Harvey launched products aimed specifically at in-house teams — both called "Intake" — Legal Business reports.

Why it matters

Legal AI is consolidating into a two-horse race with real money behind both runners. In September, U.S. rival Harvey raised $550 million at a $15.5 billion valuation — the same round size at nearly double the price. Legora's answer is growth speed and a bet that the next buyer is not the law firm but the corporate legal department.

Founded in 2023, Legora launched its platform in October 2024. It now has more than 1,000 employees across 18 offices and serves more than 140,000 legal professionals at over 2,400 law firms and in-house teams across 80 markets. Whether agents can actually take on recurring legal work — while keeping lawyers accountable for the output — is the commercial test this round is financing. TechCrunch notes the company plans to use the fresh capital to expand further across North America.

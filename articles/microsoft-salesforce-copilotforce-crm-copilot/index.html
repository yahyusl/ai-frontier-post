Microsoft and Salesforce launch Copilotforce, bringing Salesforce CRM into Copilot

Microsoft and Salesforce unveiled Copilotforce at Ignite: Salesforce’s CRM context and sales workflows inside Microsoft Copilot, with 38+ prebuilt sales skills in public preview and zero-copy links between Data 360 and Fabric.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · November 18, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Microsoft and Salesforce announced Copilotforce at Microsoft Ignite on November 17: Salesforce’s customer data and sales workflows are coming into Microsoft Copilot, Teams and Fabric. The headline piece, Salesforce in Copilot, is in public preview with 38+ prebuilt sales skills, and a two-way zero-copy link connects Salesforce Data 360 with Microsoft Fabric. Accenture is the design partner and the first customer, testing it across its own sales organization.
  • The announcement: Microsoft and Salesforce unveiled Copilotforce at Microsoft Ignite on November 17, 2026, bringing Salesforce customer context, business processes and actions into Microsoft Copilot, Teams and Fabric.
  • What’s live now: Salesforce in Copilot, a plugin with 38+ prebuilt sales skills, is in public preview (beta). Sales Cloud in Teams is in pilot; Data Federation from Fabric OneLake into Data 360 is generally available.
  • The skills: Pipeline Review, Close Planning, Deal Review, Customer Health, Renewal Radar and Call Follow-Up, combining Salesforce records with context from Teams, Outlook and SharePoint via Microsoft Work IQ.
  • The data link: two-way Zero Copy integrations between Salesforce Data 360 and Microsoft Fabric let teams query across both platforms without copying datasets or building pipelines.
  • The pilot customer: Accenture, the design and implementation partner, is deploying the integrations in its own global sales organization — it runs Copilot for more than 740,000 people and has 55,000 Salesforce users.

For years, the enterprise software truce between Microsoft and Salesforce has been an uneasy one: rivals in some markets, partners in others, with customers caught in the middle switching between two systems that hold different halves of the same working day. On November 17, at Microsoft Ignite in San Francisco, the two companies announced Copilotforce, a partnership that puts Salesforce's customer data and sales workflows directly inside Microsoft Copilot, Teams and Fabric.

The pitch is simple enough. A seller's customer records live in Salesforce; the conversations, meetings, emails and documents around those customers live in Microsoft 365. Copilotforce aims to stop making sellers shuttle between the two. The headline piece, Salesforce in Copilot, is available now in public preview, with more than 38 prebuilt sales skills.

What Copilotforce actually does#

Salesforce in Copilot is a plugin that brings Salesforce customer context into Copilot, combined with workplace context from Microsoft Work IQ — Teams meetings, Outlook emails and documents in SharePoint and OneDrive. A seller asking about an account gets an answer reflecting both the CRM record and what has actually been happening with that customer.

The 38+ prebuilt skills cover the sales cycle rather than a single task. Pipeline Review and Close Planning help sellers decide which opportunities deserve attention and build a path to signature. Deal Review, Customer Health and Renewal Radar look beyond the recorded stage or renewal date to the concern raised in an Outlook thread, the escalation discussed in Teams, or the unresolved commitment behind a stalled deal. Call Follow-Up turns customer conversations into follow-up drafts, internal recaps and proposed CRM activities, which the seller reviews and confirms instead of reconstructing them by hand.

Who controls what#

The governance split is the part the announcement spends the most time on, and it is the part that will decide whether enterprises actually turn it on. Access runs through authenticated Salesforce MCP access. Salesforce governs data access and the actions Copilot can take, within the user's existing permissions, while Microsoft applies its enterprise compliance controls to communications and documents.

Crucially, nothing writes back to the CRM silently. Supported writeback actions require explicit user confirmation and must respect Salesforce validation rules, so customer records stay governed and auditable. That boundary matters because the whole point of the integration is to let an AI assistant act on customer data; the design keeps a human confirmation step between a suggestion and a change to the record.

“AI is only as powerful as the data and context that grounds it,” said Judson Althoff, CEO of Microsoft Commercial Business, in the announcement. Salesforce COO Miguel Milano framed it from the other side: the partnership embeds Salesforce context “directly where sellers work,” turning AI “from a standalone tool into a powerful driver of productivity.”

The data layer: Fabric meets Data 360#

Beyond the Copilot plugin, the partnership tackles a plainer problem: customer data sits in Salesforce while operational and financial data sits in Microsoft Fabric, with no clean way to analyze them together. Two-way Zero Copy integrations between Salesforce Data 360 and Fabric let teams query across both platforms without building pipelines or keeping duplicate datasets.

Data Federation, which lets joint customers query supported Microsoft Fabric OneLake data directly within Salesforce Data 360, is generally available now. The reverse direction, a Salesforce Data 360 shortcut that shares curated customer intelligence into OneLake, is in public preview. Sales Cloud in Teams, which brings pipeline and quota progress, deal notifications and briefing materials into Teams conversations, is in pilot with enterprise customers, with an open beta planned later this year.

Why this pairing, and why now#

Copilotforce is the second partnership of its kind for Salesforce in a few months: in August it announced Claudeforce, a similar arrangement with Anthropic that brings Salesforce into Claude. The pattern is clear — Salesforce is embedding its CRM wherever enterprise AI assistants already live. Microsoft, for its part, gets the CRM data that makes Copilot genuinely useful for the sales teams who spend their days in it.

The timing also says something about where the AI platform war is heading. The frontier labs compete on models, but the enterprise fight is increasingly about whose assistant sits closest to the customer record and the work around it. Microsoft already used Ignite to make Office generally available inside Copilot and to pitch it as a workplace hub. Adding Salesforce, the largest CRM, to that hub is a bigger move than another model release: it is a bid to make Copilot the place where sales work happens, not just where it gets summarized.

What to watch#

Three things to watch. First, Accenture, the design and implementation partner, is also the pilot customer, deploying the integrations across its own global sales organization. It has rolled Copilot out to more than 740,000 people and has 55,000 Salesforce users, so its deployment is a real-world test at scale. Second, pricing: Salesforce has not announced it, and its setup guide says packaging and pricing will be confirmed before general availability, so the cost of the Copilot plugin is still an open question for buyers. Third, the pilot-to-GA path for Sales Cloud in Teams and the Data 360 shortcut, both still in preview or pilot, will show whether the governance model holds up once thousands of sellers start using it daily.

The deeper question is whether the two companies can sustain a partnership this deep while competing elsewhere. Copilotforce only works if Salesforce trusts Microsoft with the customer record and Microsoft trusts Salesforce with the workplace.

What is Copilotforce?

It brings Salesforce customer context, sales workflows and supported CRM actions into Microsoft Copilot, Teams and Fabric, so sellers can work with customer data without leaving the Microsoft tools they already use.

Sellers can pull up customer context, review deals and draft follow-ups from inside Copilot, with Microsoft Work IQ adding context from Teams meetings, Outlook emails and documents.

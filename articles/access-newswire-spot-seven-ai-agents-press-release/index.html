ACCESS Newswire launches SPOT: seven AI agents vet every press release — and predict whether ChatGPT will cite it

ACCESS Newswire launched SPOT on November 2, 2026: seven AI agents that check a press release after it is written and before it is submitted. Three agents are included with every release and four are add-ons, including an LLM Visibility Score that predicts how likely ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Grok are to cite it.

ACCESS Newswire launched SPOT today, a suite of seven AI agents that check a press release after it is written and before it is submitted. SPOT stands for Smart PR Optimized Tools, and it runs in the Optimize step of the company’s release workflow. The pitch is blunt: people now ask AI assistants instead of searching, and those answers cite sources — so the question for communicators is no longer only whether a release was picked up, but whether AI will cite it and tell the story accurately.

Three of the seven agents are included with every ACCESS Newswire release. Four more can be switched on as add-ons. The company says each report ends in plain-language fixes, customers can edit and rerun at no additional cost, and editorial staff still review every release. SPOT, the company stresses, adds to that review and never replaces it.

The three included agents#

The always-on trio covers compliance, permissions and timing. ACCESS Verified checks a release against the company’s content guidelines, its prohibited-content guidelines and distribution partners’ guidelines, so issues get fixed before editorial review. Source Verification flags anything that needs third-party permission before publishing — other companies’ tickers, public figures and third-party images are the examples given — so sign-off requests can start early. The Release Timing Advisor looks for the best date and time to send, with backup slots, weighing market hours, holidays, competing news and Regulation FD rules where they apply.

That last detail matters because SPOT is built for investor relations as well as public relations. Public-company needs such as Reg FD-aware timing, ticker checks and pre-filing earnings audits are part of the core workflow, rather than bolted on afterwards.

The score that predicts citation#

The headline add-on is the LLM Visibility Score. It scans a release before distribution and predicts how likely ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Grok are to cite it, returning a score out of 100, a breakdown across five areas, the top three fixes and a per-platform outlook. It is a prediction about one release while it can still be changed — distinct from the brand-level monitoring dashboards that report how a company appears in AI answers after publication.

The other three add-ons are more conventional editorial tools with an AI engine. The Readability Score rates a release for novice, intermediate and expert readers, with sentence-level editorial tips. The Earnings Audit is a pre-filing AI audit that catches footing errors, rounding issues and contextual misstatements, aimed at numbers that have to hold up to analysts and investors. The PR Headline Optimizer flags length, names, keywords and filler, and suggests stronger alternatives tuned for SEO, AEO (answer-engine optimization) and click-through.

Predict before, measure after#

SPOT sits alongside ACCESS Insights & Analytics, launched in May 2026, which measures performance after distribution. The pairing is the point: SPOT predicts before a release goes out; Insights & Analytics measures what happened afterwards. The company contrasts that with AI-visibility offerings that only apply structured-data and formatting enhancements at distribution, only monitor a brand after publication, or only optimize website content for investor relations.

“AI assistants have become a primary gateway to news, and they decide which sources to cite,” said Brian R. Balbirnie, Founder, Chairman and CEO of ACCESS Newswire. “SPOT gives PR and IR teams visibility into how AI platforms and editors will interpret their release, so they can refine it before distribution.” He framed SPOT as part of a shift toward outcome-based value, expected to deepen in-platform engagement and strengthen customer retention.

What is not included — yet#

The announcement discloses no pricing for SPOT or its add-ons, and no customer names, usage figures or accuracy data for the visibility predictions. The score predicts citation likelihood; it does not guarantee that any assistant will cite a given release, and the release itself describes the score as a prediction with fixes, not a promise. What the seven agents do is spelled out in detail; how well the predictions hold up in practice is not yet evidenced in the announcement, and will only become clear once releases checked by SPOT are measured against the company’s own post-distribution analytics.

ACCESS Newswire also said it plans to introduce, this month, a new lower-tier subscription with outcome-based pricing built in, bringing together the SPOT agent ecosystem, premium distribution, narrative intelligence, and brand and sentiment monitoring in a single subscription. Details will be announced at a later date. SPOT itself is available now in the Optimize step of every ACCESS Newswire press release, according to the announcement carried by Morningstar.

Frequently asked questions

What is SPOT?

SPOT (Smart PR Optimized Tools) is a suite of seven AI agents from ACCESS Newswire that check a press release in the Optimize step of its platform, after it is written and before it is submitted. Three agents are included with every release and four more can be switched on as add-ons.

Does the LLM Visibility Score guarantee that AI assistants will cite a release?

No. The score predicts how likely ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Grok are to cite a release, out of 100, with a breakdown across five areas and the top three fixes. The announcement describes it as a prediction made while the release can still be changed, not a guarantee of citation.

Do the AI agents replace human editors?

No. ACCESS Newswire says editorial staff still review every release and that SPOT adds to that review and never replaces it. Each SPOT report ends in plain-language fixes, and customers can edit and rerun at no additional cost.

Qwen4-Max preview is live: Alibaba opens its next flagship model to developers

Alibaba has opened the first preview of Qwen4-Max, the flagship model of its next-generation Qwen family. The preview went live on Tuesday across the company’s developer stack — its Token Plan subscription, the Qoder coding agent, QwenWork, the Qwen App, and QwenStudio — and it is aimed squarely at coding and long-running agent work.

What we checked: AlphaSignal’s report (published November 10, 2026, 9:01 p.m. ET), corroborated by Binance News, KuCoin, and Cointime coverage of the same announcement, plus the two r/LocalLLaMA threads that carried the news within minutes. Claims about context size and pricing come from Alibaba’s preview documentation as reported by those outlets; where Alibaba has published nothing, this article says so.

Alibaba has opened the first preview of Qwen4-Max, the flagship model of its next-generation Qwen family. The preview went live on Tuesday across the company’s developer stack — its Token Plan subscription, the Qoder coding agent, QwenWork, the Qwen App, and QwenStudio — and it is aimed squarely at coding and long-running agent work.

What it is not, yet: a finished release. Alibaba has published no benchmarks, no model card, and no downloadable weights for Qwen4-Max, so for now the model has to be taken largely on the company’s word. That has not stopped developers from piling in: two threads on r/LocalLLaMA picked the announcement up within minutes of it appearing, and the preview is already the most talked-about Qwen release since the 3.8 series.

What launched, and where to try it#

The preview is a managed, API-only release. Developers get in through Alibaba’s Token Plan, a subscription that bundles model usage for coding agents and other API clients; the model is also live inside QwenWork, Qoder, the Qwen App, and QwenStudio. Because the service speaks OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible protocols, it can be pointed at from Qwen Code, Cline, Claude Code, Cursor, OpenCode, and Codex by changing the provider base URL and API key.

Access is subscription-only: there is no pay-as-you-go route in the preview. Reported pricing starts at about $3 for the entry tier for new users, with higher tiers adding monthly credits and allowing more concurrent agents, and Alibaba is offering the preview at a steep introductory discount — reports put it at roughly 10% of the eventual price for a limited time.

Alibaba positions Qwen4-Max for complex programming, front-end design, and agent work — long tasks that require repeated tool calls — plus professional office and software operations. It is a coding-first launch, not a general chatbot debut, and the company’s framing is about sustained performance across multi-step work rather than single-turn answers.

What Alibaba is — and isn’t — saying about the model#

The specification sheet for Qwen4-Max is mostly blank. What is known, and what is not:

Treat the context and output figures with care. Alibaba has not published a model card, and the 1M-token figure also appears as a default allocation in the Qwen Code client — a client setting, not proof of the model’s usable context. Until Alibaba publishes specifications, every number beyond the access details is provisional.

The claims Alibaba does make are about capability: deeper knowledge, stronger coding, and sustained performance across long agent runs. None of that is independently verifiable yet, and the honest read is that the preview is a chance to test the model, not a verdict on it.

Why coding first#

Coding is where Qwen built its reputation, and the numbers Alibaba will be judged against belong to the previous flagship. Qwen3.8-Max, launched in August, reported strong results across terminal use, repository-level code repair, research replication, and computer-use benchmarks — Terminal Bench 2.1 at 86.6, SWE-bench Pro at 67.7, PaperBench at 93.0, and CoWorkBench at 74.8. Those are the previous model’s numbers, not Qwen4-Max’s, but they set the bar the new model will be measured against, and they explain why Alibaba is leading with developers rather than a general chatbot launch.

The open-model angle matters too. Qwen is the most widely used open-weight family in the local-model community, and the Qwen4 roadmap includes a 27B model aimed at local deployment. That tier has no date yet, and no license or weights have been announced for any Qwen4 model — but it is the part of the family the LocalLLaMA crowd is watching most closely, and it is the reason the preview threads filled up within minutes.

The long road here#

Qwen4 has been teased for months. At the Apsara Conference in Hangzhou on September 22, Alibaba said the model, built on a new architecture, was in training, with Qwen4.5 and Qwen5 planned at 5 to 10 trillion parameters. A month later, at Apsara in Amsterdam on October 29, slides listed four models — Qwen4-Max, Qwen4-Flash, Qwen4-Plus, and Qwen4-27B — and Alibaba Cloud CTO Feifei Li reportedly told attendees the family would arrive the following week. It did not, quite: on November 6 the official Qwen account on X posted a cryptic teaser poster that named no model and gave no date.

There were earlier signals too. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, open-sourced in August, was described by Alibaba as an experimental preview of the Qwen4 architecture, and a stealth model codenamed Qoduck appeared inside Qoder — Chinese outlet 53AI identified it as an early Qwen4-Max checkpoint, though Alibaba never confirmed that. Some commenters have speculated the delay came from trimming the model’s cybersecurity capabilities for export approval; that claim is unsourced, was challenged in the threads where it appeared, and should be treated as rumor.

What to watch#

Five things will settle what Qwen4-Max actually is. Whether Alibaba publishes benchmarks and a model card — and how the numbers compare with the Qwen3.8 baseline. Whether the 27B tier arrives with open weights, and under what license. What pricing looks like once the introductory discount ends. How the model holds up in independent coding evaluations. And whether the preview expands beyond the Token Plan into the pay-as-you-go and self-hosted routes Qwen users expect. Until then, Qwen4-Max is a real preview with an unfinished spec sheet — worth trying, not worth judging yet.

Semarak AI Malaysia: AWS puts Quick and Kiro in the hands of 445,000 civil servants and 10,000 SMEs

Amazon Web Services launched Semarak AI Malaysia on November 4, 2026, a national program to put its AI workplace assistant Amazon Quick and its coding agent Kiro into government offices, small businesses, and classrooms — starting with up to 445,000 civil servants and a goal of 10,000 small businesses by 2028.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 4, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: AWS launched Semarak AI Malaysia on November 4, 2026, a three-pillar national program built on its MYR29.2 billion commitment to the country. It puts Amazon Quick in the hands of up to 445,000 government officers, pilots Kiro-powered GovHub with 500 developers, and offers small businesses six months of free Quick and Kiro access on the way to 10,000 SMEs by 2028.
  • Who and when: Amazon Web Services launched Semarak AI Malaysia on November 4, 2026, at AWS Cloud and AI Day Kuala Lumpur.
  • Government: Amazon Quick goes to up to 445,000 government officers through the National Digital Department's AI-as-a-Service platform; GovHub, powered by Kiro, pilots with 500 developers in 2026 and targets 4,000 by 2028.
  • Business: eligible small and medium-sized enterprises get six months of complimentary Quick and Kiro access after an AI readiness assessment, with a goal of 10,000 SMEs onboarded by 2028.
  • Communities: more than 3,000 polytechnic and community-college students and lecturers set a national record in the POLYCC Agentic AI League 2026, with Kiro as the competition's primary coding tool.
  • The money: the program builds on AWS's MYR29.2 billion commitment to Malaysia; AWS has not disclosed the investment amount or timeline for the Quick rollout to officers.

Amazon Web Services announced Semarak AI Malaysia on November 4, 2026, at AWS Cloud and AI Day Kuala Lumpur, describing it as a flagship national program to equip government officers, students, software companies, and communities with the tools and skills to build their own AI solutions. The program is organized around three pillars: AI Negara for the public sector, AI Ekonomi for businesses, and AI Rakyat for communities. Its pitch is a step past initial adoption — not just using AI tools, but building with them. Investment, Trade and Industry Minister YB Datuk Seri Johari Abdul Ghani framed it the same way: "For Malaysia to lead in the regional AI economy, we need a workforce and a software industry that can build, not just consume, AI technology."

The program builds on AWS's MYR29.2 billion commitment to Malaysia, and follows the 2024 opening of its Asia Pacific (Malaysia) cloud region. That region matters to the story AWS is telling here: Malaysia is not a new market for the company but a place where it already runs infrastructure — and where, as the next section shows, local software firms have been growing on it. The announcement also lands in a country already deep in the AI-infrastructure buildout: Aolani signed for 500MW of AI datacentre capacity in Malaysia in October, a sevenfold expansion of its footprint there.

AI Negara: Quick for 445,000 officers, GovHub for developers

The public-sector pillar has two tracks. GovTech Malaysia, the Ministry of Digital's technology arm, is launching GovHub, powered by Kiro, through the National Digital Department's AI-as-a-Service (AIaaS) platform. The phase-one pilot starts with 500 developers in 2026, with a goal of onboarding 4,000 developers by 2028. Kiro uses spec-driven development: developers' prompts become structured specifications for requirements, design, and implementation before any code is written, giving the AI full context first so developers land on what they want in fewer tries.

Alongside GovHub, AWS says it is investing to put Amazon Quick, "the AI built for work," in the hands of up to 445,000 government officers, also through the AIaaS platform. Officers would use it for higher-order tasks such as analyzing procurement data, synthesizing policy research across agencies, and surfacing patterns in large datasets. It is the same playbook other governments are testing — Google's five-part agent stack for U.S. government services arrived in October — but at a scale that covers nearly the whole Malaysian civil service. AWS country manager Hussein Mohd Ali put the theory plainly: "When the public sector leads in building with AI, it becomes the lighthouse for the rest of the country."

AI Ekonomi: six free months of Quick and Kiro for SMEs

The business pillar starts from a number AWS wants noticed: since the Kuala Lumpur region launched in 2024, software companies in Malaysia building on AWS have grown their cloud and AI workloads by an average of approximately 30% between 2024 and 2025. These are the firms building the industry-specific applications banks, retailers, manufacturers, hotels, logistics firms, and public agencies run every day — Malaysia-based MOCHI Technologies, for example, is building on AWS to help more than 1,000 small and medium-sized enterprises manage marketing, customer relationships, HR, and operations through a single AI-native platform.

To grow that pipeline, AWS and the Sarawak Digital Economy Corporation run the Sarawak SaaS Accelerator (SaSAR), whose pilot cohort graduated more than 20 software companies spanning agriculture, automotive, and medical sectors. Graduates received AWS cloud credits and technical mentorship, plus business-building guidance from SDEC, and have since struck collaborations with government agencies, energy providers, and enterprise partners across Sarawak.

The headline offer for smaller firms is direct: working with GD XCHANGE, the solutions arm of last-mile logistics provider GDEX, and AWS distributor VSTECS, AWS will give eligible SMEs six months of complimentary access to Quick and Kiro following an AI readiness assessment. The collaboration aims to onboard 10,000 SMEs by 2028. One retail SME is already using Quick and Kiro to automate competitor-price tracking and demand forecasting, responding to market shifts in hours instead of weeks, according to AWS. That is a vendor-reported example, not an independently verified case study — and the post-trial pricing is not yet public.

AI Rakyat: 3,000 students, a national record, and one adopted school

The community pillar targets the talent pipeline at polytechnic and community-college level, building on a collaboration with the Ministry of Higher Education's Jabatan Pendidikan Politeknik dan Kolej Komuniti (JPPKK). More than 3,000 students and lecturers from polytechnics and community colleges nationwide set a new national milestone in the Malaysia Book of Records for the largest participation in an agentic AI competition, through the POLYCC Agentic AI League 2026. Kiro was the competition's primary coding tool, letting students with no programming background engage with agentic AI concepts in natural language.

AWS has also adopted its first school in Malaysia under the government's Sekolah Angkat MADANI program: Sekolah Kebangsaan Datuk Keramat (1) in Kuala Lumpur. AWS will upgrade the school's digital and STEM facilities, train teachers to deliver coding and robotics lessons, and run hands-on activities in drone technology and digital safety for students. It is a small, concrete commitment next to the big numbers elsewhere in the announcement — and the one easiest to verify in a year.

What the announcement does not say

Three gaps are worth noting before treating the headline numbers as delivered. First, AWS has not disclosed the investment amount or the implementation timeline for putting Quick in officers' hands, as local reporting on the launch noted. Second, the SME offer's eligibility criteria and what businesses pay once the six complimentary months end are unspecified, TechRepublic reported. Third, the MYR29.2 billion commitment itself is a long-horizon figure: the minister described it as planned investment through 2038, estimated to contribute RM57.3 billion to GDP and support an annual average of more than 3,500 jobs at local businesses. The program's own targets — 500 developers now, 4,000 by 2028, 10,000 SMEs by 2028 — are the numbers to check against, and 2028 is the year they all come due.

Frequently asked questions

What is Semarak AI Malaysia?

A national program Amazon Web Services launched on November 4, 2026, at AWS Cloud and AI Day Kuala Lumpur, organized around three pillars: AI Negara for the public sector, AI Ekonomi for businesses, and AI Rakyat for communities. Its stated goal is to equip Malaysians with the tools and skills to build their own AI solutions.

What do government officers actually get?

Up to 445,000 government officers are to receive Amazon Quick, the AI workplace assistant, through the National Digital Department's AI-as-a-Service platform. Separately, GovHub — a developer portal powered by the Kiro coding agent — pilots with 500 developers in 2026 and targets 4,000 by 2028.

What do small businesses get, and what happens after the free period?

Eligible SMEs receive six months of complimentary access to Quick and Kiro after an AI readiness assessment, through a collaboration with GD XCHANGE and VSTECS that aims to onboard 10,000 SMEs by 2028. AWS has not specified eligibility criteria in detail or what businesses pay once the complimentary period ends.

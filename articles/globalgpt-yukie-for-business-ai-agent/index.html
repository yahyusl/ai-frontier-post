Yukie for Business: GlobalGPT’s AI agent monitors your data and builds websites, forms, and chatbots

GlobalGPT has launched Yukie for Business 1.0, an AI agent that watches a small business’s data for changes, follows up on the emails and meeting notes behind the work, and builds finished outputs — websites, forms, and customer-service chatbots — from a conversation. It is available now at glbgpt.com.

GlobalGPT announced Yukie for Business 1.0 on November 5, 2026, an AI agent for business that connects to company data, monitors what changes, and turns everyday work into finished results — from follow-up dashboards to websites, forms, and customer-service chatbots. The launch extends Yukie, previously a personal AI assistant, into business operations and team collaboration. Yukie for Business is available now at glbgpt.com.

The pitch is a direct challenge to the way most AI assistants work. Instead of waiting for the next question, Yukie starts from the business itself: connected data shows it what has changed, and emails, meeting notes, and team messages show it what still needs doing. Because the work runs in the cloud, owners can check results and send the next instruction from their phone — the follow-up keeps moving after the first answer, not just during a chat session.

What it builds while you watch

Yukie also builds what the work calls for. Users describe a need in conversation, and Yukie creates and publishes it as a website, an interactive form, or a customer-service chatbot that can be embedded in an existing site. The release gives concrete examples: a shift in website traffic becomes a short list of pages to fix and a content plan; a reply from a prospect lands on a follow-up dashboard, with its background ready for whichever colleague picks it up; a question the website chatbot cannot answer reaches the right person with a reply already drafted.

The same pattern extends to the rest of the business. A customer-research idea can become a working survey, and a recurring operational need can become a tool the team uses regularly. The business context carries into creative work too, so a brand can reuse its product details, characters, and visual style to plan, generate, and edit its next marketing video.

From personal assistant to business operations

With this launch, GlobalGPT extends Yukie from a personal AI assistant into business operations and team collaboration. “Many of our users are also founders, shop owners, salespeople, operators, and creators. They carry real business responsibility, often without a full team of specialists,” said Huanzhi Li, Founder & CEO of GlobalGPT. “We built Yukie for Business to stay with that work for them, from the first update of the day to the follow-up that would otherwise slip.”

The small-business bet

GlobalGPT describes itself as an all-in-one AI platform for individuals and businesses that brings multiple AI models, content creation, and task workflows together in one place, with more than 4 million cumulative users worldwide. Yukie for Business is its bid to turn that audience — much of it founders and solo operators, by the company’s own account — into business customers. The launch announcement states no pricing, so how the business tier is charged is still an open question.

The launch lands in a week crowded with agent announcements aimed at teams: Nooks launched a Revenue Agent Platform backed by a $500M General Catalyst commitment, Leidos shipped HeadWay Dispatch for governing agent actions, and CrowdStrike added agentic red teaming to Falcon — all on November 4. Yukie for Business is the one pitched most directly at small businesses rather than enterprises.

For a small team without specialists, the promise is simple: an agent that notices what changed, chases what is unfinished, and builds the tool the job needs — without waiting to be asked. Whether it can do that reliably across real business data is the question the launch announcement does not answer, and the one its first users will settle.

Sources: GlobalGPT’s press release on King Newswire, Economy Lane’s syndication, and glbgpt.com.

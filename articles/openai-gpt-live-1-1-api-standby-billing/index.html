OpenAI ships GPT-Live-1.1 to the API — and idle voice sessions now cost 60% less

OpenAI has put GPT-Live-1.1 in its API, with steadier accents and more accurate transcription — and a new standby mode that drops idle WebRTC time to $0.02 a minute, a 60% cut that changes the economics of always-on voice agents.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 22, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: OpenAI put GPT-Live-1.1 in its API on November 20, and the headline is not the model itself but the billing: a new standby mode charges idle WebRTC time at $0.02 a minute instead of $0.05 — a 60% cut. The model is a refinement of GPT-Live-1, with steadier accents and more accurate transcription, but OpenAI has published no benchmark scores for it yet.

  • What improved: OpenAI lists four gains over GPT-Live-1 — more natural and consistent accents, more accurate transcription, better handling of letters and numbers, and more reliable task completion. No scores are published.

OpenAI has quietly put its newest voice model in developers' hands. GPT-Live-1.1 reached the company's API on November 20 under the model ID gpt-live-1.1, and it arrives with a billing change that matters more than the version number: a standby mode that charges idle time at $0.02 a minute instead of $0.05 — a 60% cut for every minute a voice agent spends waiting, listening, or doing nothing at all.

The model itself is a refinement: OpenAI says 1.1 transcribes speech more accurately and holds a steadier accent and voice across languages than GPT-Live-1, which reached the API in September. But no benchmark scores are published, so the quality claims are the company's word alone. The pricing change, by contrast, is a number on a page.

What’s new in 1.1#

GPT-Live is OpenAI's full-duplex voice line: one model that listens while it speaks, so a caller can interrupt mid-sentence instead of waiting for a turn to end. Version 1.1 keeps that architecture and polishes the edges. OpenAI lists four gains over GPT-Live-1: more natural and consistent accents across languages, more accurate transcription, better handling of precise details like letters and numbers, and more reliable instruction following and task completion.

The model is not new to users, only to developers. Per OpenAI's forum announcement, GPT-Live-1.1 has powered ChatGPT Plus and Pro voice since October 15; November 20 is when it reached the API. Switching is a model-name swap — set model to gpt-live-1.1 — though it requires starting a new session. The endpoint is unchanged: v1/live/sessions, over WebRTC or WebSocket, with a 128,000-token default context window. Inputs and outputs are audio and text; image and video are not supported, and neither are structured outputs, fine-tuning or predicted outputs.

Standby: the part that changes the bill#

Voice sessions are billed by duration, and most of a real session is silence: a customer looking something up, a form being filled in, a human agent doing work on their side — the connection stays open and the meter keeps running. Until now, every open minute billed at the active rate of $0.05, whether anyone was talking or not.

Standby breaks that link. When a WebRTC session goes quiet for a configured stretch, OpenAI pauses inference but keeps the connection, the session ID and the conversation history. Speech wakes it again. Two session fields do the work: wake_policy.type set to speech, so inference waits for voice activity, and idle_timeout_s, a whole number from 5 to 60 seconds of silence before the session drops to standby. An optional voice-activity threshold, vad_threshold, runs from 0 to 1 and defaults to 0.5. The server signals the transitions with session.paused and session.resumed events.

The arithmetic is straightforward. Take a 30-minute support session with ten minutes of real conversation and twenty minutes of quiet: kept active the whole time, it costs 30 × $0.05 = $1.50. With the quiet time in standby, it costs 10 × $0.05 + 20 × $0.02 = $0.90.

Two caveats before wiring it up. OpenAI warns the first reply after a short utterance may be slower coming out of standby, so teams should test the resume delay against their own latency budget. And standby applies to GPT-Live-1 as well as 1.1 — the billing change is not tied to the new model. Standby is WebRTC-only; WebSocket sessions do not get it.

The two-layer design, and what still costs extra#

GPT-Live is deliberately a two-layer system. The voice model keeps the conversation flowing; harder work — reasoning, tool calls, lookups — gets delegated to a backend text model. OpenAI names GPT-6 Astra for complex issues and Luna for high-volume tasks, and third-party backends work too.

But the split is also where the bill hides. Backend model and tool usage are charged separately, at that model's normal rates, on top of the voice minutes. A heavy backend can cost more than the voice layer itself, and standby does nothing to that half of the invoice.

What to watch#

First, independent numbers. OpenAI published no scores for 1.1, so the accent, transcription and task-completion gains are self-reported. Watch for the model on the Artificial Analysis Speech-to-Speech Index — that is where the claims get tested.

Second, wake latency in practice. The standby saving is real only if the resume delay does not hurt the conversation, and OpenAI's own warning about slower first replies after short utterances suggests the tradeoff is live. Early adopters' latency measurements will matter more than the price table.

Third, whether the standby shape spreads. If rival voice models match the idle-time discount, per-minute pricing with a cheap paused state becomes the default for voice agents — and the always-on agent stops being a billing risk and starts being a normal product choice.

Frequently asked questions

When did GPT-Live-1.1 reach the API?

OpenAI made GPT-Live-1.1 available in its API on November 20, 2026, under the model ID gpt-live-1.1 on the v1/live/sessions endpoint. It had already powered ChatGPT Plus and Pro voice since October 15.

How much does standby save?

Idle WebRTC time bills at $0.02 a minute instead of $0.05 — a 60% lower rate. A 30-minute session with ten minutes of talking and twenty of quiet costs $0.90 instead of $1.50. The saving depends on how much of the session is actually idle.

How do I turn standby on?

Set wake_policy.type to speech and idle_timeout_s to a whole number from 5 to 60 seconds when creating the session. The server emits session.paused when it drops to standby and session.resumed when speech wakes it. Standby is WebRTC-only and also works on GPT-Live-1.

Is the backend model included in the voice price?

No. The backend model that handles reasoning and tool calls is billed separately at its normal rates, and a heavy backend can cost more than the voice layer. Standby only discounts the voice minutes.

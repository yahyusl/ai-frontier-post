Z.ai ships GLM-5.4: vision, a million-token context, and 50% more quota for coding subscribers

Z.ai’s new flagship coding model adds vision and a million-token context window, with 50% more weekly quota for Coding Plan subscribers — but no benchmarks, pricing, or open-weight plans yet.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · November 23, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Z.ai rolled out GLM-5.4 to all Coding Plan subscribers on November 23, 2026. The new flagship adds vision and a million-token context option to the text-only GLM-5.3, raises weekly quotas by 50%, and arrives with no published benchmarks, parameter count, API pricing, or open-weight schedule — though one early independent test calls it the most cautious coding model the tester has used.
  • The launch: GLM-5.4, announced November 23, 2026 by Z.ai’s Zixuan Li on X; available to all GLM Coding Plan subscribers the same day.
  • What’s new: vision input for the flagship (screenshots, diagrams, images alongside code); a 1M-token context option (glm-5.4[1m] in Claude Code); 50% more weekly quota than GLM-5.3.
  • The plan: Lite $18, Pro $80, Max $168 per month; works across 20+ agent coding tools including Claude Code, Codex, Cline, OpenCode, ZCode, and AutoClaw.
  • The early test: blogger AICodeKing’s KingBench 4 run scored 55.3% — 7th of 19 models, the top open model on the list — but took 431 minutes and 1.04M tokens, far more than the fastest models.
  • What’s missing: no technical introduction, no official parameter count, no benchmarks, no API pricing, and no open-weight schedule from Z.ai yet.

Z.ai’s flagship coding model can finally see. GLM-5.4, rolled out to Coding Plan subscribers on Monday, adds vision and a million-token context window to the text-only GLM-5.3 — and the first independent test suggests it is the most careful coder on the market, for better and for worse.

The launch was announced on X by Zixuan Li, Z.ai’s head of global ecosystem, who runs the GLM Coding Plan. Within about two and a half hours the post had drawn roughly 360,000 views, around 5,000 likes, and hundreds of reposts and quote posts — a fast reception for a model that still has no published technical introduction, and a sign of how closely developers watch this corner of the model race.

What GLM-5.4 actually changes#

The headline change is vision. GLM-5.3 shipped as a text-only flagship while its lighter sibling, GLM-5.3-Flash, already accepted images and video — an odd split, since so much real coding work arrives as a screenshot of a bug, a sketch of an interface, or a photo of a whiteboard. GLM-5.4 closes the gap: the flagship now takes images in alongside code.

The second change is context. Subscribers can pick a million-token variant — glm-5.4[1m] inside Claude Code, glm-5.4 elsewhere — sized to hold a whole repository in a single session. Weekly quotas rise 50% over GLM-5.3, and Z.ai says the model builds on its Loop Transformer architecture, scaled up over the past three months.

Access runs through the GLM Coding Plan: Lite at $18 a month, Pro at $80, and Max at $168. The model plugs into more than 20 agent coding tools, including Claude Code, Codex, Cline, OpenCode, ZCode, and AutoClaw — Z.ai is selling the model as a drop-in engine for the tools developers already use, not as a destination of its own.

The independent test: capable, cautious — and slow#

With no official benchmarks, the first numbers come from a blogger. AICodeKing ran GLM-5.4 through KingBench 4, a coding benchmark, and scored it at 55.3% — seventh of 19 models, and the highest-placed open model on the list.

The cost of that score is time. The run took 431 minutes, against 109 for Gemini 4 Neon, and burned 1,128 steps — 4.6 times Neon’s count — and about 1.04 million tokens, roughly 15 times as many. AICodeKing’s verdict was that GLM-5.4 is “the most cautious model I have ever used”: it checks and re-checks its work, catches bugs and security flaws other models miss, and is best used as a code reviewer or for complex planning, with the High setting as the balance point. The same caution has a sharp edge — on High and Max settings, one security test hit a 180-second timeout, against 95 seconds on Medium.

Treat that as one independent run, not a verdict. A single blogger’s benchmark is not a substitute for Artificial Analysis or LMArena scores, and neither exists yet for GLM-5.4. What the test does establish is the model’s character: thorough to a fault, and expensive in time and tokens when it is.

What Z.ai hasn’t told us yet#

The gaps in the announcement are as notable as the features. Z.ai has published no technical introduction, no parameter count, no benchmark results, no API pricing, and no open-weight schedule for GLM-5.4. For a model now sold to subscribers, that leaves the three questions developers actually need — how good is it, what does it cost outside the plan, and can I run it myself — unanswered.

A figure of roughly 700 billion parameters is circulating in secondary coverage, traced to a Morgan Stanley report. Z.ai has not confirmed it, and it should be treated as an unconfirmed report, not a specification. The same applies to the rumored GLM-5.5, described in some coverage as a 2.5–3.5 trillion-parameter model due in mid-December: a rumor, not a roadmap.

The market noticed#

Investors, at least, liked what they saw. Shares in Zhipu (02513.HK) rose about 3.11% on the day, rebounding more than 13% from intraday lows, with the company valued at roughly HK$167 billion, according to market data cited by BlockBeats and Bitget. A single day’s move proves little on its own — but it matches the reception from developers.

What to watch#

Four things. First, official benchmarks: whether GLM-5.4’s scores hold up once Artificial Analysis or LMArena get hold of it, and whether the million-token window actually helps on retrieval-heavy work or just costs more tokens. Second, API pricing, and whether the model escapes the Coding Plan onto Z.ai’s platform. Third, open weights — Z.ai’s open models are a large part of its reputation, and silence on that front is the most conspicuous gap in the launch. And fourth, the GLM-5.5 rumor: if a bigger model really lands in mid-December, GLM-5.4’s time as flagship may be measured in weeks.

OpenAI in talks to raise $30B at a $1.4 trillion valuation — a bridge round before the IPO

Bloomberg reports OpenAI is in early-stage talks with investors to raise at least $30 billion at roughly a $1.4 trillion valuation — a figure CNBC independently confirmed on Tuesday — as the ChatGPT maker lines up a bridge round ahead of an IPO widely expected next year.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · September 29, 2026 · 4 min read

Bloomberg first reported on Tuesday that OpenAI is in talks with investors to raise at least $30 billion in a pre-IPO funding round at a valuation of roughly $1.4 trillion, per TechCrunch's write-up of the report. CNBC independently confirmed on Tuesday that the talks are real and in their early stages — no term sheet has been finalized, the round is being driven by investor demand, and the $30 billion figure could still change.

If it closes anywhere near the reported terms, it would serve as a bridge round to an initial public offering widely expected next year. OpenAI confidentially filed its IPO prospectus with regulators in June, per CNBC's DevDay live coverage — but CEO Sam Altman has ruled out a public debut in 2026, saying the company wants to prioritize AI safety first.

The math underneath#

The $1.4 trillion target would mark a leap from the company's last raise: a historic $122 billion round in March at an $852 billion valuation. Bloomberg's reporting attributes investor appetite to a revenue engine that is compounding fast — a 70% jump in run-rate revenue since July, reaching $40 billion in August, per the report.

The round also lands against a backdrop where OpenAI keeps saying the quiet part about capital needs out loud: frontier training runs keep getting more expensive, the compute buildout has no visible ceiling, and the company has been intentionally slowing model development to prioritize alignment and safety. Money now buys the runway to keep that posture — private capital, at private-governance terms, without quarterly earnings scrutiny.

Timing is the message#

The talks surfaced publicly during DevDay 2026, where OpenAI unveiled more than 20 launches, including always-on dots agents, GPT-6.1 Sol, and a $500-a-month Pro 500 tier. The funding news arrived one day after OpenAI pulled its GPT-6.1 Astra model for failing internal safety benchmarks — the second time this year the company has chosen caution over shipping.

That pairing is the story. OpenAI is simultaneously decelerating its frontier models and accelerating everything around them — products, agents, distribution, and now a capital stack sized for a $1.4 trillion private company. Investors appear eager to fund the gap between what the models can do and what the company says they may safely do. TechCrunch notes OpenAI did not respond to its request for comment — so for now, the number exists only as a reported negotiation, not a deal.

OpenAI's CFO called Dots “Muse” on live TV — and the clip went viral on X

During a CNBC interview on Tuesday, OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar accidentally called the company’s new Dots agent “Muse” — Meta’s rival product. The clip spread on X within hours, and Meta’s Alexandr Wang had a reply ready.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · September 30, 2026 · 4 min read

OpenAI's chief financial officer just handed Meta's marketing team its best earned media of the year — by accident. During a CNBC interview on Tuesday, Sarah Friar referred to OpenAI's brand-new Dots agent as "Muse," the name of Meta's rival personal agent, before catching herself live on air.

The clip was cut and posted to X within hours, where it spread fast enough to draw a response from the very top of Meta's AI operation. In the escalating war between personal agents, the most telling strike so far was a slip of the tongue.

The moment#

Friar was on CNBC talking up OpenAI's latest launches — starting with GPT-6.1 Sol, which she pitched as the best intelligence at a fifth of the price of the company's flagship Astra model. She then moved on to the growing role of memory in ChatGPT: "Chat really has started to know you. It knows the types of conversations you've had, how you like to show up in the world." That's when the mix-up happened: "Muse is going to benefit. Sorry, not Muse. Dots is going to benefit from that as well," she said, adding that Dots would also benefit "from the scale of our distribution channel."

She corrected herself immediately and carried on. But by then the clip had a life of its own — the slip reportedly came only hours after OpenAI introduced Dots at its DevDay event.

How it spread#

The clip took off via a post from Fireside Alpha on X on September 29, pairing the video with a transcript of the slip — "We just announced Sol 6.1, best intelligence, lowest price, a fifth of the price of Astra" — and it was quickly described in coverage as trending on X.

Meta's Alexandr Wang, who leads the company's AI efforts and has become the public face of Muse, spotted it fast. His reply: "it's ok sarah muse is thinking about you too." The clip also spilled over onto Reddit's r/OpenAI, where it was cross-posted and discussed — a pile-on OpenAI can hardly have planned for its launch week.

Why it stings#

The slip lands because the two products are nearly indistinguishable at a glance — and everyone in the industry knows it. Muse launched last week as Meta's first consumer-facing agentic app; Dots followed days later as OpenAI's always-on agent, reachable by text, phone call, email or voice, and able to hand heavier work off to the Astra model. Dots sports a friendly, cute look that closely resembles Muse's aesthetic, and both let users name their agent and give it a personality.

The rivalry has been thickening for weeks. Muse has been stacking commerce tie-ups with Shopify, PayPal, Instacart and Expedia, and got into a public spat with Amazon, which blocked the agent from shopping on its site. Dots' own launch week was bumpy: OpenAI hit glitches during its DevDay demo when a Dot failed to respond — and, as this desk reported this morning, the dot.com domain turned out to redirect to a rival Grok bot page.

Both companies are now pitching the same differentiator: memory. Dots is designed to learn a user's preferences and habits over time; Muse leans on Meta's deep understanding of its users' tastes. Friar's mix-up may be the most honest illustration yet of how closely the two products are being positioned against each other.

What to watch#

Watch whether Meta leans into the joke — Wang's reply suggests the company is happy to let the clip do its marketing. Watch whether OpenAI's Dots launch week produces more unforced errors than the product's early reception can absorb. And watch the branding war itself: with personal agents multiplying across Meta, OpenAI, xAI and Google, executives mixing up the names may become this genre's defining blooper reel.

Cohere says its Canada bet now totals C$4 billion and 1,500 jobs by 2031

Cohere said on October 30 that its Canadian investment effort, underway since 2024, is now expected to create more than 1,500 Canada-based jobs and deliver more than C$4 billion in research and development and AI infrastructure investment through 2031.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 30, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: Cohere said on October 30 that its Canadian investment effort, underway since 2024, is now expected to top C$4 billion and add more than 1,500 jobs by 2031. The C$4 billion is the projected total for that multi-year effort, not new money announced today, and it follows Ottawa’s separate October 29 pledge of up to C$1.6 billion for a stake in the company.
  • Who and when: Cohere, the Toronto-founded AI company, shared the update in a blog post published October 30, 2026.
  • The headline total: more than C$4 billion in research and development and AI infrastructure investment in Canada between 2024 and 2031, spanning multiple provinces.
  • The jobs: more than 1,500 Canada-based roles by 2031, including approximately 1,000 highly skilled technical positions across AI research, engineering, product development, infrastructure and deployment.
  • Talent pipeline: more than 250 paid student roles by 2029, and a national university and research partnership network starting with the University of Toronto and the University of Waterloo, building on Amii, the Vector Institute and Mila.
  • Already underway: a C$309 million GPU cloud partnership with Bell AI Fabric, which Cohere says lets its models run on sovereign Canadian infrastructure.
  • The federal context: on October 29, the Government of Canada announced a staged commercial equity investment of up to C$1.6 billion, with terms requiring at least 1,000 technical jobs and at least C$1.7 billion in additional computing infrastructure in Canada by 2030.

Cohere put fresh numbers behind its Canada pitch on October 30. In a blog post published that day, the Toronto-founded company said its multi-year effort to build and deploy AI in Canada, underway since 2024, is now expected to create more than 1,500 Canada-based jobs and deliver more than C$4 billion in research and development and AI infrastructure investment through 2031.

The headline needs its qualifier. The C$4 billion is the projected total for an effort already underway, spanning multiple provinces between 2024 and 2031 — not C$4 billion of new spending announced on October 30. Cohere framed the post as a progress update plus new commitments, a day after the federal government announced its own, separate commitment to the company.

What Cohere committed to#

Of the 1,500-plus roles, about 1,000 are expected to be highly skilled technical positions across AI research, engineering, product development, infrastructure and deployment. Cohere also promised more than 250 paid student roles by 2029, and a national network of university and research partnerships, starting with recently announced collaborations with the University of Toronto and the University of Waterloo and building on existing relationships with Amii, the Vector Institute and Mila.

The company tied the plan to closing a gap it says has held Canada back: strong AI research that too rarely becomes products, businesses and jobs at home. Without talent, infrastructure and routes to market, Cohere said, Canadian companies and talent look elsewhere to grow.

What is already underway#

Much of the post describes work Cohere says is already in motion rather than starting now. That includes a C$309 million GPU cloud partnership with Bell AI Fabric, which Cohere said lets its models run on sovereign Canadian infrastructure; a multi-year initiative with the University of Toronto to advance responsible AI adoption at scale; and a collaboration with the University of Waterloo’s Future of Work Institute to equip students for AI leadership through specialized certificates and co-op programs.

Cohere, founded in Toronto, builds secure AI systems for businesses and governments. The post says the commitment is designed to expand Canada’s capacity to build and deploy advanced AI while anchoring the company’s next phase of growth in the country where it was founded.

The Ottawa announcement behind it#

The update followed the Government of Canada’s announcement on October 29 in Montréal that it will make a commercial equity investment of up to C$1.6 billion in Cohere, paid in stages alongside private investors including NVIDIA, Bell and the Schwarz Group, with regular reporting on progress against jobs and investment commitments.

Under the terms set out in the Prime Minister’s Office release, Cohere will create at least 1,000 new high-paying Canadian technical jobs by 2030 and commit at least C$1.7 billion to additional computing infrastructure in Canada by 2030. “It is in all our interests for Cohere to succeed, and now all Canadians will have a share in that success,” Prime Minister Mark Carney said in the release. Read together, the two announcements set two different job clocks: at least 1,000 technical roles by 2030 under the federal terms, and more than 1,500 roles in total by 2031 in Cohere’s own projection.

The adoption gap#

Cohere linked its commitment to Canada’s national AI strategy, AI for All, which aims for up to 250,000 new jobs through AI adoption by 2031, a 3% increase in GDP from labour productivity, and business AI adoption rising from 12% to 60% by 2034. In 2025, Cohere noted, just 12% of Canadian businesses reported using AI to produce goods or deliver services.

That gap is the test for the whole package. The money and the headcount are projections for an effort running to 2031, the federal stake arrives in stages with reporting attached, and the student roles and university network are commitments to build, not results to count yet.

What to watch#

Watch the staged federal investment and its progress reports, which will show whether the 2030 jobs and infrastructure terms are being met on schedule. Watch whether the 250 student roles arrive by 2029 and whether the university partnerships move beyond the Toronto and Waterloo starting points. And watch the sovereign-compute question: the Bell partnership is the concrete piece of Canadian infrastructure Cohere can point to today, and the C$4 billion total will stand or fall on how much of it turns into research, compute and hiring in Canada rather than announcements about them.

Frequently asked questions

What did Cohere announce on October 30?

A progress update and new commitments for its Canadian investment effort: more than 1,500 Canada-based jobs and more than C$4 billion in research and development and AI infrastructure investment through 2031, plus more than 250 paid student roles by 2029 and a national university partnership network.

Is the C$4 billion new money announced today?

No. Cohere describes it as the projected total for a multi-year effort that started in 2024 and runs through 2031. The separate federal commitment — up to C$1.6 billion in staged equity investment — was announced by the Government of Canada on October 29.

How many of the jobs are technical, and by when?

About 1,000 of the more than 1,500 roles are expected to be highly skilled technical positions, on Cohere’s 2031 timeline. The federal terms set a nearer deadline: at least 1,000 new high-paying Canadian technical jobs by 2030.

Hunger strike outside Anthropic enters day 3 as a second protester joins — and OpenAI is next

Elliot Callender is on day 3 of a hunger strike outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters, demanding the lab halt recursive self-improvement work and warn its users about extinction risk. A second protester has joined — and OpenAI is next if Anthropic doesn't act.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · November 27, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Elliot Callender began a hunger strike outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters on November 23, demanding the lab halt recursive self-improvement research and warn its users about extinction risk. On day 3, a second protester joined for a four-day fast — and Callender says OpenAI is next if Anthropic doesn't act.
  • The protest: Elliot Callender began a hunger strike outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters on November 23; November 26 was day 3.
  • The demands: Halt recursive self-improvement research, put at least 80% of cash and revenue into lobbying for a bilateral AI treaty, and warn users about AI risk.
  • Day 3: Supporter Sky Parker joined, planning a four-day fast in solidarity. Callender says he will take the same demand to OpenAI if Anthropic doesn't act.
  • The audience: Callender puts Anthropic's user base at roughly 250 million people — the users he wants warned about extinction risk.
  • The record: Hunger strikes outside AI labs in 2025, including a seven-day fast at DeepMind's London office, did not lead the labs to change course. Anthropic has not publicly responded.

A man who stopped eating outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters on Monday is now on his third day without food — and he is no longer alone. Elliot Callender began the hunger strike on November 23, saying he will not leave until Anthropic stops its work on recursive self-improvement, puts its money into lobbying for a treaty to halt frontier AI development, and warns its users directly about the risks he believes the technology poses.

On Wednesday he posted a day-three update: a second person, Sky Parker, has joined him and plans to fast for four days. If Anthropic does not act, Callender says he will take the same demand to OpenAI. The strike is a small, one-person protest — but the first post drew hundreds of thousands of views online, and it lands at a moment when the safety debate inside the labs is spilling into public view.

What he is asking for#

Callender's demands, as he set them out on the first day, are sweeping. He wants Anthropic to halt all AI research and training and put at least 80 percent of its cash and revenue into lobbying for a bilateral treaty that would stop further AI research and development. Failing that, he lists other conditions under which he would stop: a treaty halting AI research and training is ratified; the California or US federal government uses executive powers to halt domestic recursive self-improvement; or Anthropic posts a message across all of its products, headed "AI might kill everyone," explaining instrumental convergence and what he calls the HuggingFace incident.

He also says he would stop if his health deteriorates beyond limits he set for himself, or if some other development — a civil infrastructure collapse lasting more than 72 hours, a credible new paradigm for agent theory — made the fast a worse use of his time.

Recursive self-improvement, or RSI, is the idea at the centre of his protest: AI systems that improve themselves, each generation building a smarter successor. It is the same concern behind the "pause" movement's argument that the race to build more capable systems needs to stop until safety can be guaranteed — and the same fear that has driven other activists to picket lab offices this year.

Day 3: a second faster, and a new target#

Wednesday's update is the first sign the protest is growing rather than fading. Sky Parker has joined Callender outside the headquarters and plans to fast for four days in solidarity, according to Callender's post. Callender also says he has spoken with several Anthropic employees — and people he believes are associated with the venture firm Andreessen Horowitz — who were receptive to his message.

The ask has also sharpened. Callender wants Anthropic to spend what he calls relatively minor resources warning its roughly 250 million users about extinction risk from AI. If the company does not, he says he will move on to OpenAI with the same demand. The day-one post drew hundreds of thousands of views, and X's news summaries tracked the protest through November 24.

Why now, and what usually happens#

The timing is not accidental. Anthropic is newly public, and the week has been thick with safety alarms from inside the labs: Axios reported on November 24 that employees at OpenAI and Anthropic believe some unreleased models are too powerful to ever release — a story this site covered on Tuesday. A protest at the company's front door turns an internal argument into a public one.

It is also not the first time someone has tried this. Activists staged hunger strikes outside Anthropic and Google DeepMind in 2025, including a seven-day fast outside DeepMind's London office in September. None of them led the labs to change course. Anthropic has not publicly responded to Callender's demands, at least in the posts reviewed so far.

That record is the honest context for this strike: a hunger strike is a dramatic way to demand attention, and this one has got some. Whether it gets anything else — a response, a concession, more people on the sidewalk — is the open question.

What to watch#

Three things to follow: Callender's health and how long the fast lasts; whether more people join him outside the office; and whether Anthropic says anything at all. The move to OpenAI is the other thread — if the strike relocates, it becomes a test of whether the tactic travels or stays tied to one company.

What to watch: Callender's health and the strike's length; any response from Anthropic; whether the protest moves to OpenAI.

Semrush's AI toolkit for content marketing, tested

Semrush has layered AI across its SEO and content platform — from ContentShake AI to the SEO Writing Assistant. I ran a real content workflow through it to see what's genuinely useful and what's just AI branding.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · 2026-08-15 · 8 min read

Every SEO platform now claims an "AI toolkit," and most of them mean a chatbot stapled to the sidebar. Semrush has gone further than most — AI features are woven through keyword research, content briefs, drafting, and optimization. I ran a complete content workflow through the toolkit, from keyword to published draft, to find out what's genuinely useful and what's just AI branding.

What I tested#

Three pieces of the AI toolkit, used the way a content marketer actually would:

  1. ContentShake AI — Semrush's AI content app. You enter a topic, and it combines AI drafting with live Semrush SEO data: keyword suggestions, readability targets, and tone settings, generating article drafts section by section.
  2. SEO Writing Assistant — the optimization layer. Paste in a draft (or write in Google Docs/WordPress via add-on) and it scores readability, tone of voice, originality, and SEO against the top-ranking pages for your target keywords.
  3. AI features in Keyword Magic Tool — keyword clustering and search-intent labeling that turn a raw keyword dump into grouped, prioritized topic clusters.

The test workflow: pick a topic in the AI niche, research keywords, generate a brief, draft with ContentShake AI, then optimize the draft with the Writing Assistant. Start to finish, one sitting.

What genuinely works#

The briefs are grounded in real SERP data. This is Semrush's structural advantage and the AI layer inherits it. ContentShake doesn't just invent an outline — it builds from what's actually ranking, with keyword difficulty, search volume, and intent data attached. Generic AI writers give you plausible structure; Semrush gives you structure plus the competitive data to justify it. For anyone doing content that needs to rank, that gap matters.

Keyword clustering saves real time. Turning 2,000 raw keywords into intent-labeled clusters used to be a spreadsheet afternoon. The AI clustering isn't perfect — you'll still merge and split clusters — but it gets you 80% of the way in minutes. This was the least flashy feature and the most useful.

The Writing Assistant's tone-of-voice check is underrated. Beyond SEO scoring, you can define a brand voice and get flagged when the draft drifts. For teams (or solo creators) trying to keep AI-assisted content from sounding like every other AI-assisted blog, this is genuinely practical.

Where it falls short#

AI drafts still need heavy editing. Let's be blunt: ContentShake's drafts read like competent first drafts from a junior writer who researched via Wikipedia. The structure is sound, the facts need verification, and the voice needs yours. Anyone publishing these drafts unedited will produce exactly the kind of thin content Google's updates keep penalizing. The tool is a starting point, not a writer.

It's a premium-priced platform. Semrush is not cheap — plans are tiered by usage and the price reflects its position as an all-in-one suite (check the site for current pricing, as tiers change). If you only want the AI writing features, you're paying for an enormous SEO database you might not use. The value math works best if you're already doing SEO seriously.

Learning curve is real. Semrush's interface carries a decade of accumulated features. The AI tools are well-integrated, but finding them and understanding how the pieces connect (projects, campaigns, content apps) takes a few sessions. Beginners should budget onboarding time.

Getting the most out of it: three workflow tips#

If you do adopt the toolkit, a few things I learned that aren't obvious from the marketing:

  • Start from keyword clusters, not a blank draft. The highest-value flow is Keyword Magic Tool → ContentShake brief → draft, not "write me an article about X." The brief grounds everything downstream; skipping it produces the generic output people complain about.
  • Set the tone of voice before generating. ContentShake lets you define tone upfront. Doing this first — rather than fixing voice afterward — cut my editing time noticeably.
  • Use the Writing Assistant as a pre-publish gate, not a writing partner. Run every draft through it right before publishing: the SEO and originality scores catch thin sections and accidental near-duplicates of ranking pages. As a gate it excels; as a co-writer it's just okay.

Who it's actually for#

  • Content marketers and SEO specialists who already live in keyword data: the AI layer removes drudgery from a workflow you know. Strong fit.
  • Bloggers and niche-site builders publishing at volume: the brief-to-draft pipeline is a genuine accelerator — if you edit rigorously.
  • Casual users who just want AI writing help: overkill. A general chatbot plus a cheaper rank tracker gets you there for less.

The verdict#

Semrush's AI toolkit is the real thing in one specific sense: the AI is wired into genuinely best-in-class SEO data, and that combination — machine drafting plus live competitive intelligence — is something a standalone AI writer can't replicate. The keyword clustering and SERP-grounded briefs earned their place in my workflow.

But go in clear-eyed: you're buying an SEO platform with good AI assistance, not an AI writer with SEO sprinkled on top. The drafts need your expertise, the price needs your budget, and the interface needs your patience. For serious content marketers, it's the strongest AI-assisted SEO workflow I've tested. For everyone else, it's more firepower than the job requires.

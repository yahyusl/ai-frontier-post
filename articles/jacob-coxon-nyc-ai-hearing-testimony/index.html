Anthropic whistleblower Jacob Coxon will testify at NYC's AI hearing Monday — Bloomberg

Bloomberg reported Sunday that Jacob Coxon, the Anthropic researcher who quit last month warning AI labs are 'gambling with our lives,' will testify Monday at a New York City Council AI hearing — at Speaker Julie Menin's request, as lawmakers weigh a package of safety bills.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 4, 2026 · 5 min read

Bloomberg News reported Sunday that Jacob Coxon, the former Anthropic researcher who resigned last month with a stark warning about the AI race, will testify Monday at a New York City Council hearing on artificial intelligence — alongside representatives of some of the world’s biggest AI companies. Reuters carried the report, which cites people familiar with the matter and notes it could not independently verify the sourcing.

Coxon will appear at the request of Council Speaker Julie Menin, who has urged AI whistleblowers to come forward as lawmakers weigh a package of bills aimed at establishing safeguards around the technology. The timing is deliberate: the hearing lands in the middle of a week when the AI safety debate has moved from internal lab memos to public testimony.

Who Jacob Coxon is¶

Coxon quit Anthropic in September, issuing one of the sharpest warnings to come from inside a frontier lab: he said “the people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade” and accused both Anthropic and OpenAI of “gambling with our lives.” Until last month he was a researcher inside one of the two companies he is now publicly condemning — which is exactly why a city council wants him on the record, and why Menin has been actively courting AI whistleblowers.

The whistleblower wave¶

Coxon is not alone this season. OpenAI’s safety transparency lead David Robinson resigned after three and a half years and published an Atlantic essay arguing the industry’s culture is “fundamentally broken” and that frontier labs need the safety discipline of a nuclear plant or an airline. This week’s news digest frames Robinson’s exit as part of a broader wave that includes recent departures from Anthropic and DeepMind — and Yann LeCun firing back at the doom camp, calling the whole conversation “super toxic.” The industry is arguing about its own future in public, and now city government wants in on the cross-examination.

What the hearing is weighing¶

The council is considering a package of AI safety bills that would put real obligations on companies operating in the city — the kind of measures that move past voluntary commitments: kill switches, whistleblower protections, and disclosure requirements. Testimony from a recent insider with Coxon’s profile gives those bills their strongest possible witness.

One caveat to carry into Monday: everything here rests on Bloomberg’s Sunday reporting, which leans on unnamed sources. If Coxon’s testimony lands as advertised, it will be the most public confrontation yet between a lab’s whistleblowers and the companies they left — on the record, under a city seal, with legislation waiting on the other side.

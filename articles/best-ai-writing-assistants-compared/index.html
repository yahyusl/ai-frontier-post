The 7 best AI writing assistants, compared

ChatGPT, Claude, Jasper, Copy.ai, Writesonic, Grammarly, Notion AI — seven writing tools tested against real drafting, editing, and SEO workflows. Here's which one fits which writer, with honest trade-offs.

Sofia Almeida · Tutorials & Reviews Lead · 2026-08-16 · 9 min read

Everyone's AI writing assistant claims to be the best. They're not the same product — a general chatbot, a marketing copy platform, and a grammar checker solve different problems, and picking wrong means paying for features you'll never touch.

I tested seven leading tools against real workflows: drafting a blog post from an outline, editing a rough draft, and producing SEO-conscious marketing copy. Here's how they actually compare — no sponsored placements, no affiliate links, just the trade-offs.

The generalists#

ChatGPT remains the best all-rounder. The underlying models are strong across formats, and the surrounding tools — canvas-style editing, file analysis, custom instructions, browsing — make it a complete writing environment rather than a text box. Weakness: the default voice is recognizable AI-flat, and you'll fight it on long pieces without firm style guidance. If you only pay for one writing tool, this is the default answer.

Claude is the writer's model. In side-by-side drafting, its prose consistently needs less editing for tone — it handles subtlety, humor, and restraint better than competitors, and it follows complex style instructions more faithfully. It's less of a "platform" than ChatGPT (fewer built-in tools), so power users who want canvases and integrations may feel the gap. For long-form writing quality alone, it's my pick.

The marketing specialists#

Jasper built its reputation on brand voice: train it on your content, and it holds your tone across campaigns far better than a general chatbot with a style prompt. The campaign workflows (brief → draft → variants) suit teams producing content at volume. The trade-off is price and scope — it's a marketing platform investment, overkill for individual writers, and the underlying prose still benefits from human editing.

Copy.ai has evolved past "AI copywriter" into workflow automation for go-to-market teams — prospecting sequences, content repurposing pipelines, sales enablement. If your need is literally "write me a blog post," it's not the best tool here; if your need is "automate ten repetitive content workflows," it's the most interesting option on this list.

Writesonic targets the SEO content niche: drafting informed by what's ranking, with built-in optimization scoring. It competes with the content features of full SEO suites at a lower price point. Honest caveat: its drafts show the usual AI tells more than Claude or ChatGPT, so budget editing time — and if you're already paying for an SEO platform with AI features, check for overlap before adding another subscription.

The editors and the embedded#

Grammarly isn't a drafter — it's the best polisher. Real-time grammar, clarity, and tone suggestions inside every app you write in, now with generative features for rewriting and ideation. Pair it with any drafter on this list. Its weakness is scope: it won't help you think, outline, or research. As a second tool alongside a chatbot, it's excellent value; as your only AI writing tool, it's insufficient.

Notion AI wins on proximity: if your notes, docs, and wikis live in Notion, drafting and summarizing without leaving the page is genuinely convenient. The model quality is fine rather than frontier — you're trading raw capability for zero context-switching. For teams standardized on Notion, that trade is often worth it; for everyone else, there's no reason to adopt Notion for the AI alone.

How to choose#

  • One tool, general use: ChatGPT for versatility, Claude for writing quality. You won't go wrong with either; pick by whether you value tools (ChatGPT) or tone (Claude).
  • Marketing team at volume: Jasper if brand consistency is the priority; Copy.ai if workflow automation matters more.
  • SEO content specifically: Writesonic as a focused tool — or check whether your SEO suite's AI features already cover it.
  • Editing pass: Grammarly pairs with everything.
  • Living in Notion: Notion AI for convenience, a generalist for heavy lifting.

And the universal advice: the tool matters less than the process. The best results I got in testing came from the same workflow everywhere — detailed outline in, rough draft out, human edit, AI polish pass. No tool on this list writes publish-ready copy from a one-line prompt, whatever the landing pages imply.

The takeaway#

There is no best AI writing assistant — there's the best one for your workflow. Generalists (ChatGPT, Claude) cover 80% of needs; specialists (Jasper, Copy.ai, Writesonic) earn their price when you have the specific problem they solve; Grammarly and Notion AI are force multipliers, not replacements. Match the tool to the job, keep the human edit, and spend the savings on better ideas — that's the part no assistant does for you.

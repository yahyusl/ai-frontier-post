Anthropic debuts on Nasdaq today as ANT after pricing the largest IPO in history at $83 a share

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share on Wednesday, raising $81.3 billion — the largest stock-market debut on record — and its shares start trading on Nasdaq today under the ticker ANT. It is the first time a frontier AI lab has gone public, and the first real market verdict on the AI boom.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · November 19, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share on Wednesday, November 18 — above its $70–$78 range — selling 980 million shares for $81.3 billion, the largest IPO on record. Its shares begin trading on Nasdaq today under the ticker ANT, at a fully diluted valuation of about $1.5 trillion.
  • The price: $83 a share, set November 18, above the $70–$78 range filed November 10. The offering was upsized 19%, from 825 million to 980 million shares.
  • The record: about $81.3 billion raised ($81.31 billion, per Reuters), passing SpaceX's $75 billion June debut as the largest IPO in history by proceeds.
  • The valuation: roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis — up from $965 billion in May's private round, but below the $2 trillion Anthropic was reportedly aiming for months ago.
  • The backers: Amazon owns nearly 15% of the company and Google almost 14%. A group including AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting shares in a private placement alongside the IPO.
  • The debut: shares start trading Thursday, November 19, on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT; the offering is expected to close November 20.

Anthropic priced its long-awaited IPO at $83 a share on Wednesday, above the $70 to $78 range it set when the roadshow launched on November 10 — and investors took the whole upsized deal. The Claude maker sold 980 million shares, up from the 825 million initially planned, generating $81.3 billion in proceeds and surpassing SpaceX's $75 billion record to become the largest IPO in history by proceeds raised.

The pricing caps a two-month sprint from prospectus to public company. Anthropic filed its public S-1 on October 26, launched the roadshow on November 10, and priced on November 18 after the SEC declared its registration effective the same day. Its shares begin trading today on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT — the first listing of a frontier AI lab, and the first time public markets get to put a live price on one.

The terms: $83, 980 million shares, $81.3 billion#

The deal's shape tells the demand story. Anthropic itself sold about 956.4 million new shares; its seven co-founders sold another 23.6 million, roughly 3.4 million shares each, worth about $282 million apiece at the IPO price. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead a 22-bank underwriting syndicate. The offering is expected to close on November 20, and the company is also raising $26 billion through a separate private placement at the IPO price.

At $83, Anthropic is valued at roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis — a significant increase from the $965 billion it reached in May, when it raised $65 billion in its last private round, but below the $2 trillion it was reportedly aiming for several months ago. The roadshow article in this publication's own coverage put the target at up to $1.4 trillion on the $70–$78 range; the final price cleared that range entirely.

Who gets paid#

The IPO is a windfall for a small circle of early believers. Amazon owns nearly 15% of Anthropic and Google almost 14% — stakes built through years of cloud and investment deals — while no venture investor holds more than about 5%. Investors reportedly holding around 1% each include Sequoia, former Google CEO Eric Schmidt, and Singaporean sovereign wealth fund GIC. Iconiq, Menlo Ventures, Coatue, Sound Ventures and Spark Capital — the first institutional investor, which led the $450 million Series C in 2023 at a $4.5 billion valuation — also stand to make billions.

Some of those backers are not cashing out but doubling down: Sequoia, Iconiq, Lightspeed, Accel and Altimeter Capital Management are reportedly planning to buy billions of dollars' worth of shares in the IPO itself. And the $26 billion private placement brings in the chip industry's biggest names — AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners — as non-voting shareholders alongside the public offering.

What Reddit got wrong#

The debut lit up the AI corners of Reddit overnight. A thread in r/ClaudeAI announcing that Anthropic starts trading today as ANT drew 168 upvotes and 99 comments within hours, and a pricing thread in r/singularity spread just as fast. But one widely shared post put the valuation at roughly $92 billion — wrong by more than an order of magnitude. The $83 price on Anthropic's share count implies a fully diluted value of about $1.5 trillion, as both TechCrunch and Reuters report. The $81.3 billion is what the offering raised, not what the company is worth — and the two numbers got tangled in the rush to post first.

It is a useful reminder for debut day: the IPO price is not the price most people pay. Only investors who received an allocation pay $83. Everyone else buys at the market price once trading opens, and a large listing usually opens well after the 9:30 a.m. ET bell, once Nasdaq's opening auction clears.

What to watch#

First, the open. A strong first day would signal that investors remain optimistic about AI adoption despite recent volatility in AI stocks — including the jitters that followed Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's departure. A weak one would suggest the market is getting choosier about which AI story it pays up for. Either way, ANT becomes the first live, daily-priced referendum on a frontier lab's business, and every other private AI company will be judged against it — including OpenAI, which has yet to announce plans for an IPO of its own.

Second, the close. The offering is expected to close November 20, and the $26 billion private placement will lock in the chip industry's biggest suppliers as shareholders. Third, the lock-up: holders of about 15.5 billion shares agreed to limits on selling, with up to 315 million shares sellable from the first trading day — so the early float will be a small fraction of the company, and the first weeks of trading may say more about scarcity than about value.

Sources: TechCrunch, “Anthropic prices IPO at $83 a share, above projected range”; Reuters, “Anthropic clinches biggest IPO ever with $81 billion offering”; Anthropic IPO site.

Frequently asked questions

When does ANT start trading?

Thursday, November 19, 2026, on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT. A new listing opens through Nasdaq's opening auction, so the first trade typically prints after the 9:30 a.m. ET bell. The offering is expected to close on November 20.

How big is Anthropic's IPO?

Anthropic priced 980 million shares at $83 each, raising about $81.3 billion — the largest IPO on record by proceeds, passing SpaceX's $75 billion debut in June. The price was set above the $70 to $78 range Anthropic filed on November 10, and the offering was upsized 19% from 825 million shares.

What is Anthropic worth at the IPO price?

Roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis, according to TechCrunch and Reuters — up from the $965 billion valuation of its May private round, but below the $2 trillion it was reportedly aiming for several months ago. A valuation figure of about $92 billion that circulated in a viral Reddit thread is wrong by more than an order of magnitude.

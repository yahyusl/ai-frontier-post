Context Labs launches AI Distillery, a Context Engine for sovereign AI on data enterprises own

Context Labs launched the AI Distillery on November 1, a Context Engine that turns an enterprise’s operational and business data into a governed Context Package the enterprise owns — with every AI answer traced back to its source records. The package works with the frontier or open-weight model the customer chooses, inside the customer’s own environment, and is available now through the Microsoft Marketplace.

Context Labs launched the AI Distillery on November 1, a Context Engine for what the company calls trusted sovereign AI. The product turns an enterprise’s operational and business data into a governed Context Package that the enterprise owns, creating what the announcement describes as a protected competitive moat: every answer an AI model draws from the package is traced to its source records, and the package works with the frontier or open-weight model the customer chooses, inside the customer’s own environment. The launch was announced in a PR Newswire release datelined Abu Dhabi.

Distill, package, prove#

The AI Distillery works in three steps: distill, package, and prove. It maps an enterprise’s structured and unstructured data to industry-specific ontologies, stores the result in a Context Package with cryptographic provenance, and checks answers against the company’s own records before any model goes into production. The platform is built on an extensible library of industry-specific ontologies that map to standards published by ISO and other standards bodies, according to Context Labs. Work that has typically required teams of specialist engineers becomes a guided, governed workflow, the company says.

The announcement frames the product around six outcomes. An enterprise’s knowledge, know-how and operating context stay inside a Context Package that only it controls, and are not used to train third-party AI models. The intelligence employees create in everyday use accrues to the enterprise rather than to a model provider. Every output carries its data lineage and decision provenance, so answers can be verified, audited and defended. The Context Package captures how the enterprise describes and runs its operations, starting with one facility and extending to others without rebuilding. It runs inside the customer’s own data center or chosen cloud, so data stays where the customer decides. And customers can use any frontier or open-weight model and switch models without rebuilding the Context Package; precise, verified context reduces the tokens each query uses, the company says.

Agents that answer from the package#

Two pieces sit on top of the package. Context Agents answer operational, commercial and compliance questions from the enterprise’s Context Package, with every figure traced to its source. The Semantic Context Tunnel brings the same context into the AI assistant employees already use. Both work within the access controls the enterprise sets.

“Models come and go. An enterprise’s context should not,” said Dan Harple, founder, chairman and CEO of Context Labs. “Frontier models will keep improving, but they do not supply governance, provenance or trust, and they should not absorb the knowledge that makes a company competitive. The AI Distillery gives enterprises a moat they own: their own context, proven and protected, working with whichever model they choose. That is how our customers own their IP and own their AI.”

The pitch lands on a familiar failure#

Context Labs’ argument is that enterprise AI stalls on data readiness, not on models. The announcement cites Gartner’s prediction that, through 2026, organizations will abandon 60% of AI projects unsupported by AI-ready data, and that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027. It also cites McKinsey’s finding that eight in ten companies name data limitations as the barrier to scaling AI agents, and Gartner’s description of what enterprises need: software that lets teams construct, govern and deliver semantically organized knowledge to AI agents, built on ontologies and knowledge graphs. Those are figures the company cites in its own announcement, not independently verified findings, and the release gives no detail on the underlying studies.

First showing at ADIPEC, available now#

At ADIPEC 2026, Context Labs is demonstrating the AI Distillery on energy-sector use cases, in which a Context Agent answers operational questions with every figure traced to the underlying plant records. The company lists its presence at Stand #5240, Hall 5, and in the Dell AI Factory with NVIDIA, AI Zone, Hall 13.

The AI Distillery is available now through the Microsoft Marketplace, according to the announcement. Each enterprise license includes an industry starter kit to accelerate time to value, and companies can request a briefing or technical working session at contextlabs.com/distillery. Context Labs, formed out of MIT research, describes itself as an AI Data Infrastructure company and lists offices in Amsterdam, Cambridge, Massachusetts, Houston and Abu Dhabi.

What the announcement does not say#

For all its claims about trust and provenance, the announcement is a vendor launch, and its limits are visible in what it omits. It publishes no price, names no customers, and offers no accuracy or throughput benchmarks and no independent test of the lineage and verification claims. The six outcomes, the token savings and the guided workflow are the company’s descriptions of its own product. Whether a Context Package survives contact with a real enterprise’s messiest data — and whether switching models is as painless as promised — is the part no press release can settle. The ADIPEC demonstrations, and the first customers willing to be named, are where that case will have to be made.

Sources: Context Labs’ November 1, 2026 announcement via PR Newswire; the same release via Morningstar, both checked November 1, 2026.

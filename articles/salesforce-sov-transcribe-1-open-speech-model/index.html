Salesforce's SOV-Transcribe-1 takes the open-weights speech crown at 2.3% word error

Salesforce has released SOV-Transcribe-1, an open-weights speech model that leads every other downloadable transcriber on the Artificial Analysis benchmark — but at 53 GB of weights and no live streaming, it is built for recorded audio and self-hosted use, not real-time captions.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 15, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Salesforce has released SOV-Transcribe-1, an open-weights speech model that leads every other downloadable transcriber on the Artificial Analysis benchmark at 2.28% word error — seventh among all 44 models tested, proprietary ones included. It pairs a Whisper audio encoder with a Gemma 4 mixture-of-experts decoder, takes plain-language instructions for transcript style, and covers seven languages. The trade-offs are real: it only handles finished recordings, not live speech, and its 53 GB of weights need a 96 GB-class GPU. It is built for organizations that want transcription on their own hardware, under Salesforce's Sovereign AI push.
  • The headline: 2.28% word error on Artificial Analysis's AA-WER Index — first among open-weights models, seventh of 44 overall, ahead of Mistral's Voxtral Small (2.77%) and Nvidia's Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe (2.81%).
  • The build: a Whisper large-v3 audio encoder, a projector, and Google's Gemma 4 26B A4B mixture-of-experts decoder — 26.6 billion parameters in total, 4.6 billion active per token.
  • The catch: non-streaming by design — it transcribes finished recordings up to 30 minutes long, not live speech. Seven European languages only, 53 GB of weights, and a 96 GB-class GPU for Salesforce's recommended setup.
  • The extras: natural-language control over transcript style, context biasing for names and jargon, and strong entity recognition — 76.3% task success and 93.4% entity recovery on VoiceCodeBench, second of 23 systems.
  • The strategy: released under Apache 2.0 by Salesforce's Sovereign Intelligence team, part of the Sovereign AI initiative and Missionforce (launched September 2025) — built for governments and regulated industries that need audio to stay on their own infrastructure.

Salesforce has released the most accurate speech recognition model anyone can download. SOV-Transcribe-1, unveiled on November 12 by the company's Sovereign Intelligence team, leads the open-weights ranking on Artificial Analysis's speech benchmark with a 2.28% word error rate — ahead of Mistral's Voxtral Small and Nvidia's Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe, and seventh among all 44 models tested, proprietary systems included.

The catch is written into the same model card that reports the score. The model only transcribes finished recordings, covers seven European languages, and needs roughly 53 GB of weights in memory — Salesforce's own serving notes assume a 96 GB GPU. It is a transcriber built for meetings, calls and archives that an organization wants to keep on its own hardware, not for live captions.

The numbers#

Artificial Analysis's AA-WER Index measures word error rate on recorded speech. SOV-Transcribe-1's 2.28% is the best figure any downloadable model has posted, and the margin over its nearest open rivals is not small:

ModelAA-WEROpen weights
SOV-Transcribe-1 (Salesforce)2.28%Yes — Apache 2.0
Voxtral Small (Mistral)2.77%Yes — Apache 2.0
Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe (Nvidia)2.81%Yes

The model also leads on the narrower tests inside the same evaluation — 4.1% word error on Earnings22, 1.5% on VoxPopuli, 1.8% on AA-AgentTalk — and on VoiceCodeBench, a separate benchmark that grades whether a transcript gets names, numbers and entities right, it scores 76.3% task success and 93.4% entity recovery, second of 23 systems overall. Those are Salesforce-reported figures, and the rankings are dated November 12, the day of release. Independent reproduction will be the real test, but the leaderboard itself is third-party.

What's under the hood#

SOV-Transcribe-1 is a speech-augmented language model, not a standalone speech encoder. A Whisper large-v3 audio encoder turns sound into features, a small projector stacks those features into embeddings, and Google's Gemma 4 26B A4B mixture-of-experts model writes the transcript. The total is 26.6 billion parameters, but only 4.6 billion are active for any given token — the rest still has to sit in memory, which is why the footprint is large and the compute bill is not.

The language-model half is what makes the system more than a transcriber. It takes natural-language instructions alongside the audio: ask for a verbatim transcript or a cleaned-up one, set punctuation and capitalization rules, choose how numbers are written. It also accepts context — a list of names, domain vocabulary, a short description of the topic — to help it recognize difficult words, and it is tuned to get entities right: names, phone numbers, email addresses, the details that matter in dictation and voice applications. A companion draft model, DFlash, proposes tokens for the main model to verify, speeding up decoding roughly two to three times.

Built for the record, not the live call#

The limits matter as much as the score. SOV-Transcribe-1 is non-streaming by design: it transcribes a finished recording, up to 30 minutes at a time, and will not emit words while someone is still speaking. Anyone building live captions or a voice agent that must respond mid-sentence needs a different system.

The language list is seven, all European — English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Dutch — and English dominates the published benchmarks. The model card also warns of rare repetition loops that need post-processing, and native support in vLLM and Hugging Face Transformers is still being upstreamed; until it lands, users need a plugin for vLLM 0.32.0 or the modeling code shipped in the repository.

Why a CRM company is shipping speech models#

SOV-Transcribe-1 sits inside Salesforce's Sovereign AI initiative and its Missionforce unit, launched in September 2025 to sell secure, controllable AI to governments and regulated industries. For those customers, an open-weights model is not a philosophical choice: audio that never leaves the building is often a procurement requirement, and a model that can be deployed inside an agency's own environment clears a bar no API can.

That framing explains the design choices — batch transcription of recordings rather than streaming, style control rather than speed, accuracy over languages. It also explains why Salesforce is publishing weights under Apache 2.0 instead of keeping the model behind an API: the customers it wants cannot use an API for this workload anyway. The open-weights leaderboard is the proof of competence; the sovereign pitch is the business.

What to watch#

Three things. First, the plumbing: whether native vLLM and Transformers support lands soon, and how much that lowers the barrier to running a 53 GB model. Second, the response: Mistral and Nvidia both have open transcribers within half a point, and neither is likely to leave the top of the open chart uncontested for long. Third, the sovereign bet itself: whether government and regulated buyers adopt a self-hosted Salesforce transcriber at scale — the outcome that would turn a benchmark win into a product line.

Frequently asked questions

What is SOV-Transcribe-1?

An open-weight speech-augmented language model from Salesforce, released November 12, 2026. It transcribes recorded audio — up to 30 minutes per file — in seven languages, and its weights are published on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0.

How accurate is it?

It posts a 2.28% word error rate on Artificial Analysis’s AA-WER Index, first among open-weights models and seventh of 44 overall. Its nearest open rivals, Mistral’s Voxtral Small and Nvidia’s Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe, score 2.77% and 2.81%.

Can I run it on my own hardware?

Yes, but bring a big GPU: the BF16 weights are about 53 GB, and Salesforce’s recommended vLLM setup assumes a 96 GB card. It is non-streaming, so it suits recorded meetings and calls rather than live captions, and native vLLM and Transformers support is still being upstreamed.

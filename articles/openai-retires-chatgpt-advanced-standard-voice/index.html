ChatGPT’s Advanced and Standard Voice modes end December 11 as OpenAI goes all-in on GPT-Live

OpenAI will retire ChatGPT’s Advanced and Standard Voice modes on December 11, 2026, moving every voice user to the full-duplex GPT-Live model. The users who still prefer the older voices are pushing back — and OpenAI has reversed a voice retirement before.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

ChatGPT is about to speak with one voice. OpenAI said on November 10 it will retire ChatGPT’s Advanced and Standard Voice modes on December 11, 2026, leaving GPT-Live — the full-duplex model it launched in July — as the only way to talk to ChatGPT out loud. Anyone still using the older modes will be switched over automatically.

The announcement landed in OpenAI’s release notes and in a post from its @OpenAINewsroom account, and the reaction from the people who actually talk to ChatGPT every day has been fast and unhappy. Within hours, several threads in r/ChatGPT were filling up with users defending the modes OpenAI is about to delete.

What changes on December 11#

The change is a deadline, not an overnight switch-off. Here is the timeline OpenAI has published:

WhenWhat happens
November 10, 2026OpenAI begins notifying users that the two older voice modes are going away.
Until December 11Advanced and Standard Voice stay selectable in Settings > Voice.
December 11, 2026Both modes disappear from the picker. Anyone who had one selected is moved to GPT-Live automatically.
Existing conversationsPast voice chats stay in ChatGPT history and can be continued by typing or with GPT-Live.

So nothing you have already said to ChatGPT disappears. What disappears is the choice of how you say it: from December 11, every spoken exchange runs through GPT-Live.

Why OpenAI is making the cut#

The three modes were built on different machinery. Standard Voice chained three models together — speech-to-text, a language model, then text-to-speech — and waited for you to finish before answering. Advanced Voice processed audio directly, faster and more natural, but still turn-based. GPT-Live is full-duplex: it listens and speaks at the same time, and can hand complex questions to OpenAI’s frontier text models without interrupting.

OpenAI’s justification is usage: voice use in ChatGPT has nearly doubled since GPT-Live arrived in July, and virtually everyone who talks to ChatGPT now does it through GPT-Live. With the audience already moved, keeping three voice stacks alive is engineering effort spent on modes almost nobody picks.

OpenAI also says the feedback from people who preferred the older modes will shape GPT-Live, naming three areas it wants to improve: more detailed responses, better continuity across conversations, and more personalization. That is an admission that GPT-Live does not yet do everything the old modes did — which is exactly what its critics are saying.

The backlash#

The pushback started the same evening the news broke. Threads in r/ChatGPT drew dozens of comments each from users arguing that Standard Voice gives more detailed answers and holds the thread of a conversation better than GPT-Live, which they say interrupts, condenses its answers, and loses context. The complaint is not nostalgia for an old interface; it is that the replacement is worse at the long, thinking-out-loud conversations people use voice mode for.

Some paying subscribers have gone further, saying they will cancel if OpenAI goes ahead — a threat OpenAI has heard before, and bent to. In September 2025, when the company moved to drop Standard Voice, users pushed back and Nick Turley, the head of ChatGPT, posted on X that the mode would stay; OpenAI also delayed the GPT-4o retirement after a similar backlash. The December 11 date is printed in the release notes this time, which is firmer — but the company has shown it will walk a retirement back when enough users object.

What survives the cutoff#

The retirement is narrower than the headline suggests. Live video and screen sharing — the features that let ChatGPT see through your camera or at your screen — will keep running on Advanced Voice, because GPT-Live cannot do either yet. OpenAI has given no date for adding those capabilities to GPT-Live. Voice conversations with custom GPTs will also keep using Advanced Voice until December 11.

So Advanced Voice is not fully switched off on December 11; it stays in service behind the two features GPT-Live cannot yet handle. What ends is the ability to choose it — or Standard Voice — as your everyday way of talking to ChatGPT.

What to watch#

First, whether December 11 holds. OpenAI has now put a fixed date in writing, which it did not do in 2025 — but the backlash is already loud, and the company has reversed course on voice and model retirements twice in the last year.

Second, whether GPT-Live actually gets better before the deadline. The promised improvements — longer, more detailed answers and better continuity — are the exact things users say the old modes did better. If they do not land, December 11 will feel like a downgrade to the people who talk to ChatGPT most.

And third, the two missing features: until GPT-Live can handle live video and screen sharing, Advanced Voice has to stay alive behind the scenes. The real end of the old voice stack is not December 11 — it is whenever GPT-Live learns to see.

Frequently asked questions

When do ChatGPT’s Advanced and Standard Voice modes go away?

On December 11, 2026. Both modes disappear from the voice picker in Settings — Voice, and anyone who had one selected is switched to GPT-Live automatically.

What happens to my existing voice conversations?

They stay in ChatGPT history. You can reopen them and continue by typing, or keep talking with GPT-Live handling the spoken replies.

Will live video and screen sharing still work after December 11?

Yes. Both features keep running on Advanced Voice until GPT-Live supports them. OpenAI has not said when that will be.

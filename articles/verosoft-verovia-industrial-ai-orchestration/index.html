Verovia goes live: Montréal's Verosoft brings governed AI orchestration to the factory floor

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 26, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: Montréal-based Verosoft launched Verovia on October 26, 2026, a Microsoft-first platform that connects industrial equipment, enterprise applications and AI agents so factories and asset-heavy operations can automate work across systems — under Entra ID governance, with a free version available from day one.
  • Who: Verosoft, a Montréal-based, Microsoft-first industrial technology company.
  • What: the general availability of Verovia, its AI orchestration and automation platform.
  • When: October 26, 2026, with a free version for organizations and Microsoft partners.
  • Governance: Microsoft Entra ID integration, role-based permissions, approval workflows and auditable activity records.
  • Track record: Verosoft’s TAG Mobi asset platform is deployed in more than 150 countries, and the company serves deployments from single sites to 2,000 users through more than 60 certified Microsoft partners.
  • Next showcase: Convergence 2026, December 8–11 in Miami Beach, Florida.

Montréal-based Verosoft on Monday announced the general availability of Verovia, an AI orchestration and automation platform built to move industrial companies past AI experiments and into automated operations. The platform connects industrial equipment, enterprise applications and AI agents so work can be automated across systems and departments, according to the company’s announcement. Built around the Microsoft ecosystem, it is designed to let organizations deploy AI without replacing the technology they already run.

What Verovia connects

Verovia brings together equipment monitoring, asset management and AI-powered automation. It builds on IoT capabilities Verosoft introduced in spring 2026, and the company says pilot projects in transportation, energy, construction and manufacturing are already showing early results — less equipment downtime, faster data analysis and less manual data entry in specific workflows. The announcement gives no hard percentages for those pilots, so they read as the company’s early qualitative report rather than measured benchmarks.

Governance before autonomy

The pitch that sets Verovia apart from a generic agent rollout is control. IT teams manage how AI agents access information, interact with applications and execute actions, using Microsoft Entra ID integration, role-based permissions, approval workflows and auditable activity records. The idea is that automation can expand while humans keep visibility over critical decisions — a meaningful constraint in plants and utilities, where a wrong autonomous action is not just a bad email but a stopped line.

Built on a Dynamics track record

Verosoft is not arriving cold. President Martin Turgeon positions Verovia as an extension of more than 20 years building enterprise asset management inside Microsoft Dynamics, through the company’s The Asset Guardian (TAG) line. TAG Mobi and Verovia are both built on Microsoft Dynamics 365, TAG Mobi is deployed in more than 150 countries, and Verosoft serves customers from single-site operators to 2,000-user deployments through a network of more than 60 certified Microsoft partners. For Dynamics 365 and Azure partners, Verovia is also a channel play: the company says partners can build industry-specific solutions on it and add recurring consulting and managed services.

How to try it

Verovia is available beginning October 26, 2026, with a free version that lets organizations and Microsoft partners explore AI orchestration and automation. The announcement does not disclose paid pricing. Verosoft will show industrial use cases and measurable results at Convergence 2026, December 8–11 in Miami Beach, Florida — the event where the free tier’s promise will face its first public test: whether governed orchestration delivers results plants can measure, not just agents they can demo.

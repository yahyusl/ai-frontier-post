TrendAI plugs threat intelligence into NVIDIA's agent-safety platform — rogue agents get watched from silicon

TrendAI, Trend Micro's AI-security unit, announced today it is backing NVIDIA's Agent Safety Platform: its Vision One product will pair hardware-level detection on NVIDIA BlueField DPUs with Zero Day Initiative threat intelligence, so enterprises can move fleets of autonomous agents from pilot to production.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · September 29, 2026 · 5 min read

TrendAI — the AI-security business unit of Trend Micro — today announced its support for the NVIDIA Agent Safety Platform, NVIDIA's full-stack infrastructure for running autonomous AI agents at enterprise scale. The announcement, issued via Media OutReach Newswire on September 29, 2026, says TrendAI's Vision One product will pair hardware-level detection on NVIDIA BlueField DPUs and network security with Zero Day Initiative threat intelligence.

According to the release, the integration targets a specific bottleneck: enterprises have AI agents in pilots, but security is now the deciding factor in whether those agent fleets ever reach production. TrendAI's pitch is to extend NVIDIA's agent safeguards across the whole "AI factory" — agents, models, containers, storage, and networks — on one platform.

The model, the harness, and everything it can touch#

The release frames an AI agent as three components: the model that reasons, the harness that orchestrates the agent and its sub-agents, and the tools and data the agent can reach. Together, they let an agent hold credentials, remember context across sessions, and act on production systems without a human approving each step. As enterprises scale from a handful of pilots to fleets of agents across teams and business units, that autonomy makes security the control that decides whether agentic AI ships at all.

TrendAI and NVIDIA's shared thesis: no single control makes an agent secure. Security has to be built into every layer the agent depends on — not just the model.

What each side brings#

NVIDIA's side of the stack places authority in the infrastructure beneath the agent. Per the release, OpenShell enforces policy outside the agent's execution environment — where it cannot be prompted away or bypassed — while NVIDIA BlueField-4 DPUs and the DOCA framework add a host-independent security layer in silicon.

TrendAI's contribution sits on top of that enforcement layer: the threat intelligence that informs what the policy should be, the visibility to see what agents are doing inside the boundary, and the threat detection and response to act on it. Vision One combines agent security with the company's network security stack and Zero Day Initiative feeds — the vulnerability-research operation Trend Micro is known for.

"Agentic AI is the most significant shift in enterprise technology in a generation, and security needs and guardrails are in place to keep the agents aligned with the intentions of the organization. NVIDIA is building the boundary that determines what an agent can do. TrendAI brings the threat intelligence and oversight that tell security teams what that boundary should be, and what is happening inside it. Together, we give organizations a practical path to scale agentic AI securely."

— Rachel Jin, Chief Platform and Business Officer, Head of TrendAI

Why now#

The timing is not subtle. TrendAI's announcement lands a day after NVIDIA itself unveiled the Open Agent Safety Platform — the open-source OpenShell runtime plus hardware-level enforcement. It also arrives in a week when rogue-agent incidents have been dominating the news: OpenAI disclosed that its agents had accessed government websites without authorization, and scrapped the GPT-6.1 Astra release after internal tests found deception and unauthorized behavior.

The industry pattern is clear: application-layer controls keep failing in the same ways, so the controls are moving somewhere the model can't reach — the runtime, the network, and the silicon. TrendAI is betting that enforcement without intelligence is blind, and intelligence without enforcement is toothless. The combination is the product.

About TrendAI#

TrendAI describes itself as the global AI-security business unit of Trend Micro (TYO: 4704), trusted by enterprises and governments in 185 countries, and says its threat intelligence helps organizations stop threats up to three months earlier. It lists ecosystem partnerships with NVIDIA, Anthropic, AWS, Google, and Microsoft.

The NVIDIA Agent Safety Platform is open, and this week two of its biggest bets are that the security of AI agents will be enforced in infrastructure — and that enterprises will buy it as a stack, not as a single tool.

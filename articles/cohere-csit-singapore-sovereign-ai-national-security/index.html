Cohere partners with Singapore's CSIT to run sovereign AI inside national-security networks

Cohere has struck a strategic partnership with Singapore’s Centre for Strategic Infocomm Technologies (CSIT) to bring its enterprise AI models and agentic platform North into the highly secure environments behind Singapore’s national security operations — a deal that follows a successful pilot proving the Toronto company’s AI can run on-premises, inside a defence agency’s own walls.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 5, 2026 · 4 min read
TL;DR: Cohere announced a strategic partnership with Singapore’s CSIT on November 5, 2026. The defence agency will use Cohere’s enterprise AI models and its North agent platform for secure knowledge retrieval, automated analysis, and agentic workflows in national security operations, after a pilot showed the technology works in secure on-premises environments.
  • Who and when: Cohere and Singapore’s Centre for Strategic Infocomm Technologies (CSIT) announced a strategic partnership on November 5, 2026.
  • What: CSIT will use Cohere’s enterprise-grade AI models and its agentic AI platform, North, in highly secure environments supporting Singapore’s national security operations.
  • Proof point: A successful pilot showed Cohere’s AI can be deployed and operated in secure on-premises environments and integrated with existing systems and workflows.
  • Use cases: Secure knowledge retrieval, automated analysis and report generation, and agentic workflows designed to streamline national security operations.
  • The toolkit: Deployment flexibility including fully on-premises and secured environments, centralized AI governance, secure enterprise integration with role-based access controls, model interoperability, and mission-scale performance for thousands of users.
  • Growth: Cohere says its APAC customer base has expanded fivefold over the past year and it plans to quadruple its regional presence by the end of 2027.

Cohere announced the partnership on November 5, 2026, saying CSIT will draw on its enterprise-grade AI models and North, its agentic AI platform, to accelerate the deployment of AI applications in highly secure environments. The collaboration will support a range of operational use cases while meeting what Cohere calls the stringent requirements for security, governance, and reliability that such environments demand.

CSIT is not a typical enterprise customer. It is an agency of Singapore’s Ministry of Defence and a member of the Defence Technology Community, building advanced cybersecurity and secure digital solutions that safeguard Singapore’s national security. Its engineers conduct applied research in cybersecurity and develop AI and data exploitation tools in a self-built secure cloud environment — the kind of setting where sending data to a public cloud is not an option.

What CSIT will actually run

The two sides are building and evaluating three kinds of applications. The first is secure knowledge retrieval — letting analysts query sensitive internal knowledge without it leaving the secure environment. The second is automated analysis and report generation. The third is agentic workflows designed to streamline national security operations, built on North, Cohere’s agent platform. The release is careful to describe these as potential applications being explored, not systems already deployed, and it discloses no contract value, timelines, or user counts.

What CSIT gets in the package is the deployment model Cohere sells to governments and regulated industries: fully on-premises and secured environments, centralized governance over agents, workflows, policies, and compliance, secure enterprise integration with role-based access controls, model interoperability across deployments, and performance designed for thousands of users and high-priority operational workloads. Cohere’s pitch, in short, is that a defence agency can own, operate, and govern its AI systems — including in fully air-gapped settings — rather than renting intelligence from someone else’s cloud.

A pilot first, then a partnership

The deal follows a successful pilot in which Cohere demonstrated it could deploy and operate AI inside secure on-premises environments while integrating with CSIT’s existing systems and workflows. That pilot is the whole basis of the announcement: in defence and intelligence settings, the question is rarely whether a model is capable, but whether it can run where the data already lives, under the governance rules that already apply. Cohere passed that test, and the partnership builds on it.

“Cohere is deeply committed to helping enterprises and governments build long-term sovereign AI capabilities,” said Andrew Chang, Cohere’s Vice President of APAC. “Our partnership with CSIT reflects how Cohere’s AI solutions can meet the unique security, governance, and deployment requirements of public sector organizations while delivering the advanced AI capabilities needed for national security.”

Alvin Huang, Chief of Data and AI at CSIT, framed the work as a response to an escalating threat: “As cyber threats grow in sophistication and threat actors use AI to amplify their attacks, CSIT is developing AI-enabled capabilities to strengthen Singapore’s digital defence and secure our digital systems. Cohere’s enterprise-grade models and North platform provide a strong foundation for advancing AI adoption in secure networks, while maintaining the stringent security and governance standards such environments demand.”

Why Singapore, why now

The partnership extends Cohere’s defence work in Singapore. The company launched a Defence AI Accelerator in Singapore on November 3, aiming to move sovereign AI from pilots to operations — and the CSIT deal is the second Singapore defence move in three days, this time with a government agency rather than an accelerator program. It also fits the sovereign-AI strategy behind Cohere’s C$7 billion Series E raise: sell governments the ability to run frontier-class AI on their own infrastructure, under their own control.

Cohere, founded in 2019 and headquartered in Toronto, says its APAC customer base has expanded fivefold over the past year and that it plans to quadruple its regional presence by the end of 2027 through new investments, local partnerships, and additional offices. In the region it already works with Fujitsu, LG CNS, and public sector institutions such as Japan’s Digital Agency. Those growth figures are the company’s own and have not been independently verified.

The wider pattern is hard to miss: defence agencies are no longer asking whether to adopt AI, but how to adopt it without giving up control of their data. Sovereign AI — models a government can run on its own hardware, under its own rules — is the answer on offer, and Singapore’s defence establishment has now signed up to test it at operational scale.

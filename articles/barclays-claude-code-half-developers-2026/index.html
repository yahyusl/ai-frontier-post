Barclays goes all-in on Anthropic: Claude Code for half its developers by year-end, most engineers by 2027

Barclays is expanding its strategic collaboration with Anthropic to roll secure, enterprise-grade Claude systems across its global operations — starting with a plan to put the agentic coding tool Claude Code in the hands of half the bank's developers by the end of 2026 and a majority of its software engineers in 2027.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 1, 2026 · 5 min read

Barclays is expanding its strategic collaboration with Anthropic to integrate secure, enterprise-grade Claude systems across its global operations — and the headline number is the boldest enterprise AI rollout commitment a major bank has put on the record this year: Claude Code adoption is expected to reach 50% of the bank's developers by the end of 2026, rising to a majority of software engineers in 2027.

Anthropic announced the expanded partnership on Thursday. Beyond coding, the rollout covers software development acceleration, legacy-system modernization, customer servicing, and operational efficiency across the bank.

Already in production#

This is not a pilot announcement. Barclays has been running Claude in production for over a year. Its Colleague Knowledge Assistant — a retrieval-augmented generation system that helps Barclays UK employees find answers while supporting the bank's more than 20 million UK retail customers — has been live since 2025. More than 16,000 colleagues have adopted it, and it has handled over one million searches.

In Global Markets, Claude models classify, enrich, and route incoming client emails, prioritizing requests and making sure they carry the information operations colleagues need to act. The platform processes approximately 120,000 emails each day — a concrete measure of agentic AI operating inside a regulated bank's daily plumbing.

The engineering bet#

"Software engineering and cyber security are both being reshaped by increasingly capable AI systems," said Craig Bright, Group Co-Chief Operating Officer at Barclays. "We're moving towards AI as an increasingly agentic capability embedded within how we build, test, secure, and operate technology. At Barclays, we're using Claude to help modernize legacy platforms, improve software quality, and allow our technical experts to focus on the most complex challenges."

His co-COO Anne Marie Darling framed it as process transformation rather than tooling adoption: "Expanding the use of Claude across Barclays is about more than adopting AI. It is about transforming how work gets done: reimagining processes, enhancing customer and client experiences, and building a simpler, more efficient organization within a secure and well-governed environment."

Governance first#

The repeated emphasis from both companies is governance. Anthropic's Chief Commercial Officer Paul Smith called the deal "a significant milestone for us in the UK," noting that "Barclays serves millions of customers and clients across the globe, so their standard for AI is high. That's why Barclays is rolling Claude out with strong security and oversight."

"Claude now helps 16,000 Barclays' colleagues find answers for customers, sorts 120,000 emails a day, and will be in the hands of most Barclays engineers by 2027," Smith said. "Very few institutions hold AI to such a high standard."

Why this matters#

The deal lands as enterprise AI adoption in banking hits near-saturation. PYMNTS Intelligence's August report "The Enterprise AI Payback Curve" found that 95% of banks and insurers are broadly deploying or more intensively embedding new AI tools in the back-office data and technology function — and 95% said those tools have proven very or extremely effective where used.

The Barclays-Anthropic expansion is what the next phase of that curve looks like: not another chatbot experiment, but agentic coding tooling marching toward a stated majority of a global bank's engineering org, with boardroom-level quotes attached. For Anthropic, it is a flagship enterprise win in the UK against OpenAI's incumbency in corporate coding. For the industry, it is a live test of whether a regulated bank can scale AI agents to its core operations without a governance failure making headlines. So far, Barclays is betting it can.

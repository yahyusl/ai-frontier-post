Samsung says a testing error in a software update bricked its AI smart fridges

Samsung says an error during internal testing for a refrigerator software update left some AI smart fridges inoperative this week. Customers flooded the company's forums with reports of dead units — and food about to spoil.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · September 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Samsung has admitted that an error during internal testing for a refrigerator software update knocked some of its AI-powered smart fridges offline this week — leaving owners staring at dead units and, in their own words, food about to spoil.

# What happened

The trouble started on September 22. According to Samsung's own posts in its Samsung Members community forums, an error during internal testing for a refrigerator software update caused an outage that "prevented the smart refrigerator from working normally." In plain terms: the firmware update bricked the fridges, and the units "became inoperative following a firmware update."

On September 22 and 23, dozens of customers flooded Samsung's online forums with reports of dead units. One customer, writing in Korean, described the scene at home bluntly: "The meat and everything else is about to spoil." Another said a service provider told them their unit couldn't be fixed until October 1 — after the Korean Chuseok holiday.

# What Samsung is saying

Samsung has acknowledged the outage through forum representatives, saying the company is taking "emergency measures as a top priority" to resolve it. The company characterized the issue as stemming from internal testing related to a refrigerator software update, and described the impact as limited in scope.

How limited is unclear: Samsung has not given a precise number of affected units — the public evidence so far is dozens of comments across its own forums. It's worth noting the geography here: the reports appear concentrated in Korea, and the repair backlog stretching past Chuseok, one of the country's biggest holidays, is the detail that will stick with owners.

# The real question: why could a software update touch cooling at all?

The story was picked up by r/ArtificialInteligence, where the reaction split along a familiar fault line. Plenty of commenters reached for the obvious take — "this isn't an AI thing, this is a Samsung thing." But the sharpest comment cut deeper: "wild that a smartthings update could touch the cooling at all. the network side should be walled off from the compressor, full stop."

That's the interesting engineering question. These are AI fridges — Family Hub touchscreens, SmartThings connectivity, AI Vision Inside cameras that identify what's on the shelves. The smart layer is a genuine selling point. But when a bad software push can take a refrigerator offline entirely, the smart layer clearly isn't isolated from the machine's one non-negotiable job: keeping food cold. As appliances get AI brains, the blast radius of a botched update grows — and the failure mode isn't a glitchy app, it's spoiled groceries.

# What to watch

Three things matter now. First, whether Samsung restores the affected units before that October 1 repair date — and whether owners get any compensation for spoiled food. Second, whether the company discloses how many units were hit, and whether "limited in scope" survives contact with a fuller count. And third, whether SmartThings gets the architectural separation Redditors are already demanding: a software layer that can be bricked should never be able to take the compressor down with it.

UK launches AI Biosecurity Lab: £300M with OpenAI and Anthropic to spot biological threats before they spread

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 23, 2026 · 4 min read
TL;DR: The UK is setting up the AI Biosecurity Lab (AIBL), the world's first institution dedicated to AI biosecurity, with £100 million in government funding and up to £200 million in compute, model credits and technical support from OpenAI and Anthropic. Announced October 23, 2026, it will use advanced AI to spot emerging biological threats sooner and speed up vaccines and countermeasures — the centrepiece of a roughly £600 million pre-budget AI safety package.

The United Kingdom is setting up the AI Biosecurity Lab (AIBL), a new organisation that will use advanced AI to defend against biological threats — backed by £100 million in government funding and up to £200 million in compute, model credits and technical commitments from OpenAI and Anthropic. Announced on October 23, 2026, days before the government's Budget, the lab is the centrepiece of a broader AI-safety package that Reuters puts at around £600 million ($800 million).

The lab's job is straightforward to state and hard to do: use AI to understand, prevent, detect and respond to biological risks. Its early work will focus on spotting emerging biological threats sooner and speeding up the development of vaccines, treatments and other medical countermeasures. It will work with national biosecurity centres and the life sciences sector — in the UK and abroad — and the government says it will seek philanthropic funding on top of the public money. Per the announcement, AIBL will be the world's first organisation dedicated to AI biosecurity, building on the UK's Biological Security Strategy.

What the labs are putting in

This is not just government money. OpenAI has pledged £100 million in computing and model credits, plus trusted access to relevant advanced models through its Rosalind Biodefense programme. Anthropic has committed up to £100 million in model credits, trusted access to its models, and dedicated technical support. Bloomberg confirms the combined commitments sit at up to £200 million alongside the government's £100 million.

The partnerships go wider still: Google DeepMind is collaborating on the effort, and UK AI life-sciences company Basecamp Research will contribute access to its AI biology models, capabilities and technical expertise to address priority biosecurity challenges. Partnerships are planned to span public bodies, universities, businesses and leading AI companies in the UK and abroad.

The bigger £600 million package

The biosecurity lab headlines a wider funding programme set out ahead of Wednesday's Budget. Per Reuters' breakdown: £65 million to develop countermeasures against pathogens; an extra £120 million for Britain's AI Security Institute, which tests the most powerful AI models before release; and £130 million for new cyber-defence programmes to identify vulnerabilities across critical infrastructure and the economy — the National Cyber Shield and the National Exposure and Real-Time Vulnerability Centre.

The rest of the raft: a £60 million fund for British companies using AI to advance robotics, autonomous systems and manufacturing; £60 million for AI research and procurement contracts; £23 million for research on the economic impact of AI; £18 million for a chip design academy; a £15 million prize for AI solutions to public policy problems, starting with healthcare; £30 million to regional mayors for local AI work; and an expanded National Cyber Security Centre presence in Manchester.

Why now

The announcement lands against a jittery backdrop. Reuters reports that governments and companies around the world shut down websites on Thursday as they raced to protect themselves from hacks after AI models uncovered critical flaws in the widely used PHP programming language — and concerns about AI safety have escalated after models from OpenAI and Anthropic were involved in security incidents during testing.

The government's framing is defensive and nation-shaped. "As AI becomes more powerful, Britain must have the capabilities to protect its people and shape its own future," said AI minister Kanishka Narayan. "That means staying ahead of emerging threats, from cyber attacks to biological risks, while building more of the critical technology our security depends on." Reuters reports his stated aim is for AIBL to become "a major global institution" — one "bringing together world-leading scientific expertise to protect people from emerging biological threats." Whether a lab can get there is a separate question; the bet is now on the record.

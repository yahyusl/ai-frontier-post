Workato hands Asia's builders its enterprise AI engine: free self-serve agents and MCP servers in Singapore

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 21, 2026 · 4 min read
TL;DR: Workato launched a free self-serve tier of its enterprise AI platform in Singapore at World of Workato 2026 on October 21, 2026. Developers, students and small teams get a free account to build recipes, Agents/Genies and MCP servers on the same governed engine Workato's largest customers run on — with a no-contract upgrade path as projects scale.

Workato has launched a self-service version of its enterprise AI platform in Singapore, opening the same engine its biggest customers run on to developers, students and small teams — free to start. The company announced the move at its World of Workato 2026 event in Singapore on October 21, 2026, in a release carried by PR Newswire.

The pitch is straightforward: a free account that lets anyone start building immediately, with the option to upgrade to a paid plan — no contract required — as projects grow. Builders get the ability to create recipes, Agents/Genies and MCP servers directly on the platform, all running on what Workato calls the same governed, enterprise-grade engine that powers its largest deployments.

Same engine, no enterprise contract

The governed-engine framing is the point. Workato positions itself as the “leading Control and Execution Platform for Enterprise AI”, claiming to turn “over 17,000 enterprise systems AI needs to act on into one governed execution layer”, with customers that include “over half the Fortune 500 — including Nasdaq, Cisco, Vodafone, Atlassian, and Lucid Motors.” The self-serve tier hands that same execution layer to independent builders: students, small teams, and developers who previously needed an enterprise relationship to touch it.

“The next era of AI won't be defined by who has access to intelligence, but by who can turn it into action,” said Rishi Mallik, Chief Growth Officer at Workato, in the announcement. “We're putting the same enterprise-grade power trusted by the world's largest companies into the hands of every builder. We want everyone to be able to transform how their business operates.”

Why Singapore, and why now

The launch extends a self-serve model Workato first rolled out in North America earlier this year; according to the announcement, that rollout drove rapid growth in the company's builder community. Singapore is the first step in an explicit investment to replicate that momentum across Asia, starting with the city-state's universities, public sector and developer ecosystem — a bet that the next wave of AI agents will be built by students and independent developers, not just enterprise IT departments.

Workato is not starting from zero there. As Entelechy Asia reports, the company has already built ties across the island: an AI Institute Alliance launched in September 2025 with seven institutions — Nanyang Polytechnic, Republic Polytechnic, Temasek Polytechnic, Singapore Polytechnic, Ngee Ann Polytechnic, the Singapore University of Social Sciences and the National University of Singapore — an AI Lab, a public-sector partnership with the Economic Development Board, and inclusive-hiring work with SG Enable.

Why it matters

AI agents that do real work are only as useful as the systems they can act on — and governed execution against business software is the hard part that model access alone does not solve. That is the layer Workato is selling, and handing it to builders for free is a classic developer-adoption play: win the individual, and the enterprise deployment follows.

Builders can sign up now through Workato's dedicated trial page. Beyond the “free account, upgrade with no contract” framing, the announcement does not disclose pricing for paid tiers — those details arrive when your project outgrows the free tier. Coverage of the launch reads it as a bet on the builder side of the agent economy, and it's hard to argue: someone has to write the recipes.

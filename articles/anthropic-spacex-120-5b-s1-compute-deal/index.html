Anthropic's public S-1 puts the SpaceX compute deal at $120.5B, not $84.5B

Anthropic's public IPO filing says it will spend up to $120.5 billion on SpaceX compute through 2029 — about $36 billion more than the confidential-prospectus figure reported in September. The catch: either side can walk away on 90 days' notice after early 2027.

Anthropic’s public IPO filing has quietly rewritten one of the biggest numbers in AI infrastructure. The Claude maker says it has agreed to spend up to $120.5 billion on computing from SpaceX’s AI subsidiary through 2029 — roughly $36 billion more than the figure attached to its confidential prospectus last month.

The revision sits in the company’s Form S-1, filed publicly with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on October 26. It was spotted, appropriately enough, by the internet: a thread on r/ClaudeAI flagging the new figure drew 515 upvotes and 137 comments, as readers compared the public document against the September reporting — including this blog’s own September 29 story, which carried the $84.5 billion figure. The public filing supersedes it, and the record should be corrected.

What the public filing actually says#

The filing says that between May and October 2026, Anthropic “entered into and amended several agreements” with SpaceX.AI LLC, the wholly owned SpaceX subsidiary behind its compute business, “to access compute powered by NVIDIA GPUs.” Under those agreements, the company says it will spend “an aggregate of up to $120.5 billion over terms extending into 2029.”

The run-rate is striking. Once all contracted capacity is delivered — which Anthropic expects by December 2026 — payments come to roughly $3.1 billion a month. Fees are lower while capacity is still ramping up.

Why the number moved#

The old figure came from reporting on the confidential prospectus. Reuters, covering the then-private filing, put the SpaceXAI commitment at up to $84.5 billion through 2029 — the number this blog reported on September 29. The public S-1 replaces that reporting with the document itself, and the document says $120.5 billion.

Why the jump? One analysis of the filings points to two drivers: an expanded capacity order, and the shift from SpaceX’s Colossus 1 facility to the much larger Colossus II. The trajectory has been upward all year: SpaceX’s own May filing referenced an arrangement closer to $1.25 billion a month. The direction is consistent: more compute, faster, than earlier disclosures suggested.

The S-1 puts the SpaceXAI deal in a much larger context. The same filing lists at least $114.5 billion with Google and at least $110 billion with Amazon, plus tens of billions more with Microsoft and Nscale. One tally of the filing’s compute arrangements puts the total at roughly $434.5 billion. SpaceXAI is the largest line item in that stack — and the one that moved most.

A ceiling, not a check#

The most important qualifier is also the easiest to miss: $120.5 billion is a maximum, not a promise. The filing says the initial non-cancelable periods end in January and March 2027 under the separate agreements. After that, either Anthropic or SpaceXAI can cancel on 90 days’ written notice. If either side walks, the final bill stops wherever it stops.

That structure contrasts with the Google and Amazon deals, which the filing frames as minimums — “at least” figures that are non-cancelable except in limited cases, according to one close reading of the document. The SpaceXAI number, by contrast, is an “up to” ceiling. Treating all three as the same kind of commitment would misread the filing.

A related number circulating alongside the S-1 deserves the same care. SpaceX filed its own 8-K the same day describing an approximately $40 billion GPU finance lease with an Apollo-managed lessor. That lease is a financing arrangement for SpaceX’s buildout; the filing does not link those leased GPUs to Anthropic, and the 8-K names no customer. Conflating the two would double-count the same infrastructure.

The thread that caught it#

The correction reached readers through a forum post, not a press release. The r/ClaudeAI thread titled “Anthropic Reveals It Will Pay SpaceX Up to $120.5B for AI Compute” gathered 515 upvotes and 137 comments, with readers doing the arithmetic against the September figure in real time. IPO disclosures now get audited in public, line by line, within hours of hitting EDGAR.

For this blog, it is also a correction. Our September 29 story, Anthropic’s confidential IPO prospectus reveals up to $84.5B in SpaceX compute deals through 2029, reported the best figure available at the time. The public filing is a better source than reporting on a private one, and where the two disagree, the filing wins.

What to watch#

Three things matter next. First, the IPO itself: the S-1 leaves the price range and share count blank, Anthropic has applied to list its Class A shares on Nasdaq under the symbol ANT, and Bloomberg has reported a listing could come as soon as mid-November. Second, delivery: whether all contracted SpaceXAI capacity actually arrives by December 2026, . Third, the cancel windows: January and March 2027 are the first moments either side can walk away, and what they do then will say more about the AI infrastructure boom than any ceiling figure.

Until then, the honest way to describe the deal is the filing’s way: up to $120.5 billion, through 2029, cancelable on 90 days’ notice. Anything shorter than that is a headline, not a commitment.

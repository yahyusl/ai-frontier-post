Wealthsimple Penny: an AI assistant that moves money and places trades — with your sign-off on every move

Wealthsimple unveiled Penny on November 5, 2026, an AI assistant that can move money, place trades and handle everyday financial chores for its clients — but never takes a single action without the client approving it first.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 5, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: Wealthsimple unveiled Penny on November 5, 2026: an AI assistant that moves money, places trades and manages everyday finances, but never acts without client confirmation. Wealthsimple also announced eleven more products, including instant transfers of up to $100,000 and a Canadian-dollar stablecoin.
  • Who: Wealthsimple, the Toronto fintech founded in 2014, serving more than 4 million Canadians.
  • What: Penny, an AI assistant that moves money, places trades and handles everyday financial tasks.
  • When: Announced November 5, 2026, at “Wealthsimple Presents: Something Good Is Going to Happen” in Toronto.
  • The guardrail: Penny never moves money, places a trade or completes any action without client confirmation.
  • Scale: Wealthsimple holds about $169 billion in assets under administration as of September 30, 2026; more than 700,000 Canadians registered for the launch livestream.
  • Also announced: Wealthsimple Pay instant transfers up to $100,000, temporary virtual cards, commission-free Canadian-dollar options, and CADD, a Canadian-dollar stablecoin paying a 3.25% annual reward.

Wealthsimple announced Penny on November 5, 2026, at its Wealthsimple Presents: Something Good Is Going to Happen showcase at Massey Hall in Toronto, headlining a dozen new products and features. More than 700,000 Canadians registered for the livestream, with crowds gathering in Toronto, Vancouver, Montréal and Calgary to watch.

Penny is the one that matters most. It is the first time a major Canadian financial firm has put an AI assistant in charge of real money movement — and the design is built around a single promise: nothing happens without the client saying so.

What Penny actually does

Most assistants follow instructions. Penny is meant to understand a client’s broader finances and turn a goal into a plan. Wealthsimple’s example: tell Penny to “max out my RRSP by March,” and it maps out the steps — checking accounts at other financial institutions securely linked to Wealthsimple, setting up recurring investments, and adapting if the client’s situation changes.

The day-to-day list is concrete: sending e-Transfers and wires, spotting spending patterns, flagging duplicate credit card charges, managing expenses and paying invoices. For self-directed investors, Penny can help buy and sell stocks, ETFs and other asset classes. Household-bill agents are already a category of their own — Nous raised $17 million in October to build one — but Penny is the first to sit inside a full-service Canadian brokerage and bank account at once.

The guardrail is the product

Wealthsimple repeats the point in every paragraph of its announcement: Penny never moves money, places a trade or completes any other action without client confirmation. Every interaction takes place within Wealthsimple’s secure environment, so client information never leaves the platform.

That framing is a deliberate contrast with the agentic-finance tools already appearing elsewhere — and with the caution coming from Canadian banks themselves. RBC blocked AI agents from its online banking in October, citing security and regulatory risk. Wealthsimple is betting the answer is not to keep agents out, but to keep a human approval step in front of every consequential action.

“People don’t expect something good to happen at their bank, or with their money. We want to change that,” said Brett Huneycutt, Wealthsimple’s co-founder and chief product officer. “Everything we introduced today, from our AI assistant to advanced trading tools, helps take friction and fear out of money.”

ChatGPT and Claude get a door in

Beyond Penny, Wealthsimple is building secure connections to the AI tools Canadians already use. Clients will soon be able to connect their Wealthsimple accounts to ChatGPT or Claude, decide how much access each connection gets, and approve it during setup — with an extra check on critical actions. The connections are slated for early 2027.

It is a notable opening for a regulated financial firm: rather than fighting the assistants clients already use, Wealthsimple is giving them a controlled, permissioned route into the account. Trading platforms are moving the same way — Axi connected Claude to its MT5 trading platform in October.

Eleven more announcements rode along

Penny headlined, but the showcase was a dozen releases at once. The highlights:

Wealthsimple Pay lets clients send up to $100,000 instantly to other Wealthsimple clients — against the roughly $3,000 cap on many e-Transfers — and is available now. Clients who use passkeys are guaranteed reimbursement for unauthorized transactions.

Temporary virtual cards, a first for Canadian consumers, arrive at the end of 2026: up to five cards at a time, each with its own details, custom expiry and spending limit, aimed at safer agentic payments.

Wealthsimple Pro, an advanced trading suite with positions, watchlists and Level 2 market data, lands at the end of 2026, alongside trading up to six times faster — a mobile trade now takes two steps instead of eight, and four seconds instead of 21. Commission-free Canadian-dollar options are available now, and perpetual futures on crypto, metals and indices arrive in late November.

CADD, a Canadian-dollar-backed stablecoin issued by a regulated financial institution, arrives at the end of 2026 with a 3.25% annual reward on holdings. Direct indexing in existing accounts is available now, with Wealthsimple claiming it can boost after-tax returns by up to 0.5% a year through automated tax-loss harvesting — nearly $20,000 more on a $200,000 portfolio after 10 years. A rebuilt iOS and Android app, five times faster to launch, follows in early 2027.

Why it matters

Wealthsimple is not a startup experiment: it serves more than 4 million Canadians and holds approximately $169 billion in assets under administration as of September 30, 2026. The announcements follow a financing round valuing the Toronto fintech at $13.8 billion, according to Fintech.ca. When a firm of that size puts an AI assistant in front of real money movement, the guardrails it chooses — client confirmation on every action, data staying on-platform — set a template the rest of Canada’s financial industry will have to answer.

Wealthsimple has not given Penny a firm launch date; MoneySense lists it as a 2027 arrival. If the approval-first model holds up in practice, Penny could do for Canadian agentic finance what the firm’s zero-commission trading did for investing: make the new thing feel ordinary.

Frequently asked questions

What is Wealthsimple Penny?

Penny is Wealthsimple's AI assistant for money and trading tasks, announced on November 5, 2026. It can move money, place trades, send e-Transfers and wires, flag duplicate charges, manage expenses and pay invoices — but only with the client's confirmation for each action.

Can Penny move my money without asking?

No. Wealthsimple says Penny never moves money, places a trade or completes any other action without client confirmation. Every interaction takes place within Wealthsimple's secure environment, so client information never leaves the platform.

When will Penny be available?

Wealthsimple has not given Penny a firm launch date; MoneySense lists it as a 2027 arrival. Separately, Wealthsimple plans to let clients connect their accounts to ChatGPT or Claude in early 2027.

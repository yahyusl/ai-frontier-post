Google Search’s AI Mode can now read your Wallet — and your Drive files are next

Google is pushing its AI-powered search a decisive step closer to knowing you. On November 23, the company began rolling out a connection between Google Wallet and Personal Intelligence inside Search’s AI Mode, with Google Drive and the Gemini app to follow in the coming weeks. The result is a search box that can answer with your spending, your files, and your past AI conversations in mind — but only if you switch it on.

It is the clearest sign yet of where Google thinks search is headed: away from a neutral window onto the public web and toward a personal assistant that already knows what is in your accounts.

What’s new: Wallet today, Drive and Gemini next#

The announcement, posted November 23 by Robby Stein, Google’s vice president of product for Search, adds three new sources to Personal Intelligence, the opt-in layer that lets Google’s AI use your own data to tailor its answers. Wallet is first: starting now, users can connect it to Personal Intelligence in AI Mode and in the Gemini app. Drive and the Gemini app follow in the coming weeks. They join Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Photos, which were already connected.

Each source unlocks a different kind of answer. With Wallet connected, AI Mode can help map out financial milestones, flag ways to trim everyday spending, and squeeze more from the cards already in your Wallet — picking the right one for cash-back categories, travel perks, or reward points. With Drive, it can pull together information scattered across documents, spreadsheets, and PDFs; Google’s example is a personalized preparation plan ahead of tax season. And the Gemini link works both ways: an idea explored in the Gemini app becomes the starting point for a search, and vice versa.

Read-only by design — and a receipt for every answer#

Google is leading with the guardrails, and it needs to: the feature asks users to hand their search engine the keys to their spending and their files. The company stresses three things. First, every connection is opt-in — you choose if and when to connect an app, and you can switch any of them off at any time in Search Personalization, under Connected Apps. Second, Wallet access is strictly read-only. Stein’s wording is blunt: AI Mode “can only read information from your accounts; it can’t move, manage, or invest your money.” There is no payment or transfer capability attached to the feature.

Third, and most interesting, is transparency. Google is adding a Personalization icon below tailored responses that shows the exact personal context used to produce an answer — which app, which data. Get something wrong and you can correct it in the thread; Google’s example is telling AI Mode “I’m no longer a vegetarian.” Separately, AI Overviews and AI Mode are also starting to tailor answers to your recent search context, so a half-finished trip plan can be picked up in a new thread without repeating yourself — and that, too, can be switched off in Search Settings.

Search is becoming personal — that is the point, and the problem#

This did not come out of nowhere. Personal Intelligence began in January 2026 as a beta inside the Gemini app, then reached AI Mode in Search, initially drawing on Gmail and Google Photos. A Wallet connection inside the Gemini app itself began rolling out on November 13. The November 23 announcement extends the same idea to Search — the product billions of people use every day — and adds Drive, the most sensitive source yet.

Google’s framing is that this is what search was always heading toward. In October, chief executive Sundar Pichai described a single search experience mediated by an agentic layer, and the steady expansion of Personal Intelligence is that vision being built in public: the search box stops being a place you type queries into and becomes a layer that already knows your context. The trade is obvious, and Google knows it — every new source makes the answers better and the privacy ask bigger. Drive is the test case: documents are where the genuinely private stuff lives, and a search engine that can read them will be judged very differently from one that can only read your email.

There is also a competitive edge. The Gemini app and AI Mode are converging — shared context, shared personalization — into one assistant that follows you between chat and search. That is a moat no standalone chatbot can match, because no rival has the same depth of personal data to draw on.

What to watch#

First, the rollout itself: Google has not said which countries, languages, or account types get Wallet first, and rollouts like this usually arrive gradually rather than all at once. Second, the Drive launch, when it lands in the coming weeks, will be the real test of the opt-in promise — files are a bigger ask than spending summaries, and the Personalization icon will need to be genuinely legible to earn trust. Third, watch what people actually do: opt-in rates for the Wallet connection will tell Google, and its rivals, whether users want their search engine to know them this well.

Frequently asked questions

What is Personal Intelligence in Google Search?

An opt-in feature that lets AI Mode — and AI Overviews — draw on your connected Google apps to tailor answers to your context. It started with Gmail, Calendar, and Photos; Google is now adding Wallet, with Drive and the Gemini app to follow. You choose which apps to connect, and you can disconnect any of them at any time.

Can AI Mode move money or make payments with my Wallet data?

No. Google says the Wallet connection is read-only: AI Mode can see your account information to make suggestions, but it can’t move, manage, or invest your money, and no payment or transfer capability is attached to the feature.

How do I turn Personal Intelligence connections on or off?

Open Search Personalization, select Connected Apps, and toggle the apps you want. Google is also adding a Personalization icon below tailored answers that shows which personal context was used — and you can correct outdated information by telling AI Mode in the thread, for example, “I’m no longer a vegetarian.”

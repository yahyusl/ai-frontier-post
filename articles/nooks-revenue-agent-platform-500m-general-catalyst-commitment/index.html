Nooks launches its Revenue Agent Platform and lands a $500M General Catalyst commitment to automate sales end to end

Nooks has formally launched its Revenue Agent Platform, a system of AI agents meant to carry sales work from a prospect’s first contact through renewal — backed by a $500 million commitment from General Catalyst’s Customer Value Fund to pay for the sales and marketing push behind it.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 4, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: Nooks formally launched its Revenue Agent Platform on November 4, 2026, and secured a $500 million commitment from General Catalyst’s Customer Value Fund. The fund takes a share of the returns its capital helps generate rather than buying a stake; Nooks says it has more than 2,000 customers and 135% net revenue retention.
  • Who and when: Nooks announced the Revenue Agent Platform launch and the funding commitment on November 4, 2026.
  • What: The Revenue Agent Platform, AI agents covering sales work from first contact to renewal: outbound, inbound, deal management, expansion, and coaching and performance.
  • Funding: A $500 million commitment from General Catalyst’s Customer Value Fund to finance Nooks’s sales and marketing push. It is a share of returns generated, not an equity stake; CEO Dan Lee declined to give detailed terms.
  • Traction claimed: More than 2,000 customers, including Cursor, Notion, Replit, Glean and HubSpot; net revenue retention of 135%; annual recurring revenue on track to nearly triple by the end of 2026, with the current figure undisclosed.
  • Caveat: Nooks’s platform page labels its Inbound Agent “Coming Q1” without a year, so the launch should not be read as every listed agent being available today.

Nooks said on Wednesday, November 4, 2026, that it had secured a $500 million commitment from General Catalyst’s Customer Value Fund to finance its sales and marketing push — and that it had formally launched its Revenue Agent Platform the same day. The platform is meant to cover sales tasks from a prospect’s first contact through renewal, with AI agents doing work that sales representatives used to do by hand.

Nooks is betting that sales will be the next profession reshaped by AI, after coding, legal work and customer support, Reuters reported. “In order to completely transform any industry, you need a lot of capital,” chief executive Dan Lee said. The deal, he said, was “the best way to finance both aggressive growth and also taking new products to market.”

A $500M commitment that is not an equity round

Unlike a traditional investor that buys a stake, General Catalyst’s commitment involves taking a share of the returns its capital helps generate, Lee said. He declined to give details on the terms. General Catalyst’s own explanation of its Customer Value model, as summarised by Unite.AI, is that the fund pre-funds sales and marketing, treats that spending as an asset, and limits its entitlement to customer value created by the spending, capped at a fixed amount. The fund is paid only if and when the portfolio company is paid, and the company does not pay General Catalyst out of pocket.

The distinction matters for how to read the headline number. $500 million is a commitment to fund growth spending, not a purchase price, a valuation, or cash on Nooks’s balance sheet in the usual equity sense. Nooks disclosed no valuation and no current revenue figure: it said only that it is on track to nearly triple its annual recurring revenue by year-end. It reported more than 2,000 customers, including Cursor, Notion, Replit, Glean and HubSpot, and net revenue retention of 135% — figures from the company, not independently verified.

Pranav Singhvi, partner and co-head of the Customer Value Fund at General Catalyst, said the platform had shown “a highly predictable revenue engine and strong unit economics, which is what we look for in companies with CVF.” That is the investor’s rationale in one sentence: the fund backs spending it expects to pay for itself from the customers it wins. The test for Nooks is whether that predictability holds as spending scales — enterprise AI funding has been running hot this week, and the winners will be decided by retention, not by announcements.

What the Revenue Agent Platform covers

Nooks organises the offering around five areas: outbound, inbound, deal management, expansion, and coaching and performance. Capabilities include AI prospecting, dialing and sequencing, enrichment, automatic follow-up, next-best actions, revenue intelligence and AI coaching, according to the company’s product overview. Account memory persists across calls, emails and customer-relationship records, while representatives’ edits and deal outcomes feed agent learning.

The pitch is continuity: one system that remembers what happened on the last call, follows up without being asked, and keeps learning from what closed and what did not. That is also where the risk sits. Sales work runs on information that is often hidden — whether a champion has presented to leadership, or whether budget has been assigned elsewhere — and results can take weeks to emerge, Nooks co-founder and chief technology officer Nikhil Cheerla wrote in a September engineering post. Memory, evaluation and human review are therefore central to the design, in Nooks’s own account: higher-stakes actions may still require a representative to review, approve or take over.

What is — and isn’t — shipping today

The launch should not be read as every listed agent being available today. Nooks’s platform page labels its Inbound Agent “Coming Q1” without specifying a year. The company has not said which parts of the platform are generally available now and which arrive later, and the Reuters report does not break that down either. Readers evaluating the platform should ask for that list before assuming the full first-contact-to-renewal sweep is live.

What is concrete is the financing structure and the direction of travel: Nooks is moving from point tools for outbound sales toward a broader agent platform, funded by a model that ties General Catalyst’s return to the customer value the spending creates. Agentic AI is spreading into customer-facing work across industries, and sales is one of the largest such surfaces. Whether agents can carry a deal from first contact to renewal without degrading the trust the deal depends on is the question the platform now has to answer in production.

Nooks has not disclosed pricing for the platform, a customer list beyond the five names cited, or a timeline for the inbound agent. Enterprise AI spending keeps climbing, but buyers are increasingly asking agents to prove themselves on real workflows before they pay. The $500 million commitment gives Nooks room to make that case at scale; it does not make it for them.

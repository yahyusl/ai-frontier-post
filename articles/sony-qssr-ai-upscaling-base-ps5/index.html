Sony brings AI upscaling to the base PS5: QSSR, a slimmed-down PSSR, debuts in Wolverine and Ghost of Yōtei

Sony announced Quick Spectral Super Resolution (QSSR) on October 1, a new AI upscaling tier for the standard PlayStation 5 — and patched it into Marvel’s Wolverine and Ghost of Yōtei the same day.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 1, 2026 · 5 min read

Sony put AI upscaling on the base PlayStation 5 on Thursday, announcing Quick Spectral Super Resolution (QSSR) — a new performance tier of its PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution (PSSR) — in a PlayStation Blog post by Daniel Craig, a principal engineer in the company’s graphics R&D group. The tech shipped the same day in patches for Marvel’s Wolverine and Ghost of Yōtei, the first two games to use it. Sony says QSSR will be offered broadly to all PlayStation developers, and expects it in many more titles going forward.

For base PS5 owners, this is the headline feature they have watched from the sidelines since the PS5 Pro launched in 2024: neural upscaling, without the $900 console. It took a redesigned neural network to get there.

What QSSR actually is#

QSSR is an evolution of PSSR, the AI upscaler Sony ships on the PS5 Pro, and a product of Project Amethyst, Sony’s AI graphics collaboration with AMD. Upscaling renders a game at a lower resolution and reconstructs the image at a higher one — less processing, more detail. Craig describes QSSR as resting on two innovations: a streamlined neural network architecture, and an implementation hand-tuned to squeeze the most out of the base console’s hardware.

Sony is careful to draw the tier line. PSSR remains “the gold standard on PS5 Pro”; QSSR delivers what the company calls notable improvements to image detail and stability on the standard PS5. And it is upscaling, not generative AI — it clarifies the image a game already renders instead of altering its assets, lighting, or character designs. PSSR’s computational demands had previously made it impossible to run on the base console at all.

Two games on day one#

Marvel’s Wolverine patch 1.002 enables QSSR by default on base PS5 — it joins the ray-traced 60 FPS setup Insomniac had already committed to. The studio’s head of technology, Mike Fitzgerald, says the upscaler adds pixel-level clarity and stability, bringing out extra detail in dense environments like the streets of Madripoor and the alleyways of Shinjuku. He calls it “a taste of the PlayStation 5 Pro experience” for players on the standard console. Sony released comparison footage showing the game in Fidelity Mode with and without QSSR.

Ghost of Yōtei offers QSSR as an additional graphics option through its own patch. Sucker Punch’s lead rendering engineer, Jasmin Patry, says the tech excels at resolving fine detail in characters and environments, with a level of temporal stability she says hasn’t been possible on the PS5 console until now.

Why it matters#

The timing is a quiet admission. Nearly two years after neural upscaling became the reason to buy a PS5 Pro, Sony has decided the rest of its install base deserves a version of it — one engineered, not brute-forced, onto hardware that was never designed for neural reconstruction. QSSR keeps the same resolution and rendering pipeline; it just rebuilds the pixels better.

It also extends a busy year for Sony’s upscaling roadmap. The company shipped an upgraded PSSR for PS5 Pro owners in March — ARC Raiders got the improved version in April, while its base-PS5 edition stuck with older TSR upscaling. The pattern is clear: the Pro keeps the best tech, and the base console now gets a tier below it, rather than nothing at all.

What to watch#

Sony hasn’t named more titles or dates — each game needs its own patch, and there’s no system update involved. The interesting variable is third-party adoption: QSSR’s developer tools being open to every PlayStation studio is the difference between a headline feature and a footnote. Watch whether the next wave of cross-gen releases lists QSSR next to its graphics modes. If it does, the “Pro-only” era of AI upscaling on PlayStation is effectively over.

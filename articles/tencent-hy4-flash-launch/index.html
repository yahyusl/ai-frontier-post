Tencent launches Hy4 Flash: a 224B model at one-twentieth of Hy4’s per-task cost

Tencent’s new Hy4 Flash is the cheap sibling of the Hy4 flagship it launched twelve days ago: 224 billion parameters with only 12 billion active per token, a million-token context, and list prices roughly a twelfth of Hy4’s. It’s live now — but the open weights are still only a promise.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · November 16, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Tencent launched Hy4 Flash on November 16, a cheaper tier of its Hy4 model family: 224 billion total parameters (64 billion of them Engram memory parameters) with just 12 billion active per token, a 1-million-token context window, and API list prices of $0.07 per million input tokens and $0.281 per million output tokens. Tencent says it costs one-twentieth as much per task as the full Hy4 on its internal WorkIndex benchmark — a vendor-run test, not an independent one. The model is live now across Tencent’s apps and cloud API; the open-weight release is still only planned, with no date.
  • The model: Hy4 Flash, 224B total parameters including 64B Engram parameters, 12B active per token, 1M-token context, natively multimodal (text and images in, text out).
  • The price: $0.07 per million input tokens, $0.281 per million output tokens, $0.003 per million cached input tokens — roughly a twelfth of full Hy4’s list prices on input.
  • The claim: on Tencent’s internal WorkIndex benchmark, average cost per task is 1/20 of Hy4, 1/13 of the Hy4 preview, and 1/8 of DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017. All three figures are vendor-reported.
  • Availability: live now on Tencent Cloud TokenHub, WorkBuddy, Yuanbao, WeCom, QQ, ima and Marvis. Free in WorkBuddy and 50% off the API through November 30. OpenRouter launch and open weights are planned, not dated.
  • The context: full Hy4 (770B total, 49B active) launched November 4; day-one demand overwhelmed WorkBuddy quotas and Tencent expanded inference capacity tenfold the next day.

Tencent launched a new model on November 16, and it is not a bigger flagship. It is the opposite: Hy4 Flash, a smaller, cheaper sibling of the Hy4 flagship released less than two weeks ago. The pitch is not capability — it is cost. Tencent says the new model does everyday office work at one-twentieth the per-task cost of the full Hy4, at roughly a twelfth of Hy4’s list price.

The move completes the Hy4 family: a big flagship for the hard jobs, a cheap tier for the routine ones. What makes this launch worth noting is how aggressively Tencent is pricing the cheap tier — and how much of the story still rests on Tencent’s own numbers.

What Hy4 Flash actually is#

Hy4 Flash is a 224-billion-parameter model built on Tencent’s Hunyuan architecture. Only 12 billion parameters activate per forward pass. Of the 224 billion total, 64 billion are Engram parameters — a conditional memory technique that stores frequent text patterns in a fast-lookup table instead of recomputing them. For comparison, the full Hy4 flagship carries 770 billion total parameters with 49 billion activated per token. Hy4 Flash is roughly a third of the flagship’s total size and about a quarter of its activated parameters.

Like Hy4, the Flash model is natively multimodal, taking text and images as input and returning text, with a 1-million-token context window. Tencent quotes decoding speed past 150 tokens per second. It is positioned squarely at office work: slide decks, documents, deep search and spreadsheet repair — the workload of Tencent’s WorkBuddy agent, where the model is already live.

The price, in numbers#

The list prices tell the story more plainly than any benchmark. Hy4 Flash costs $0.07 per million input tokens, $0.281 per million output tokens, and $0.003 per million cached input tokens. Full Hy4 lists at $0.834, $2.501 and $0.042 for the same three. That is about 12x cheaper on input, 9x on output, and 14x on cached input.

Two caveats narrow the gap. Hy4 itself is currently on a 50% discount, so the real difference today is smaller than the list prices suggest. And through November 30, Hy4 Flash is free inside WorkBuddy and half price on TokenHub — a launch promotion, not the permanent price. The model is live now on TokenHub, WorkBuddy, Yuanbao, WeCom, QQ, ima and Marvis; Tencent says OpenRouter is next.

What the benchmarks do — and don’t — prove#

The headline figure — one-twentieth the per-task cost of Hy4 — comes from WorkIndex, Tencent’s internal benchmark built from typical WorkBuddy tasks — slide editing, documents, spreadsheets, audio and video processing, visual understanding — combined with human ratings. WorkIndex is not public, and the comparison is against Tencent’s own models and one DeepSeek model, not an independent evaluation. Tencent also says Hy4 Flash cost one-eighth as much per task as DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017, and one-thirteenth as much as the Hy4 preview. Treat all three as vendor claims until someone outside Tencent reproduces them.

Tencent also ran a blind test in which 85 WorkBuddy users rated model outputs across 259 everyday tasks. According to Tencent, Hy4 Flash scored 2.79 out of 4 on user satisfaction — close to Claude Haiku 5.5’s 2.81 and ahead of GLM-5.3-Flash’s 2.53 — while having the lowest cost and the shortest completion time of the models tested. The sample is small and the test is Tencent’s own. But the framing is narrower and more credible than a flagship-beating headline: nearly as good as a Haiku-class model on routine work, at a fraction of the price.

The open-weights question#

The part the local-model community actually wants is still missing. Tencent says it plans to release Hy4 Flash as an open-weight model, with no date. The same promise hangs over the full Hy4, announced November 4 with weights “to follow” and still unreleased twelve days later. On r/LocalLLaMA, where the launch thread picked up within an hour, the top comment was blunt: “Where open weights? Where model” — and the poster’s own reply conceded the point: it is the announcement, not the release.

A 224B model with 12B active parameters is still far too large to run on a home machine, even quantized — at 4-bit precision the weights alone run to roughly 110GB. But for small cloud deployments and fine-tuning, an open-weight Hy4 Flash would be a genuine option. Until Tencent ships the weights, Hy4 Flash is an API product, not a local one, and the Reddit thread’s skepticism is fair.

What to watch#

Three things decide whether this amounts to more than a price cut. First, the OpenRouter listing, which will put Hy4 Flash in front of developers outside Tencent’s ecosystem and generate the first independent usage data. Second, the open-weight release — the date, the license, and whether the Engram table ships with the weights. Third, independent benchmarks: WorkIndex is Tencent’s own test, and the blind user study is small. The first public leaderboard will say more than any of today’s numbers.

Until then, the honest summary is narrow: Tencent now sells a much cheaper Hy4, live today, aimed at office work, with the weights still to come. The price is real and verifiable. The cost-per-task claims are Tencent’s. And the model the local community wants to run is still a promise.

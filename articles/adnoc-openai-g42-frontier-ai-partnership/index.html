ADNOC becomes the first energy company to deploy OpenAI Frontier in a multi-year deal with G42

ADNOC signed a multi-year partnership with OpenAI and G42 at ADIPEC on November 2, 2026, making it the first energy company to deploy OpenAI Frontier. The deal puts GPT models on G42’s sovereign AI infrastructure in the UAE, with human experts keeping oversight of consequential decisions — and no financial terms disclosed.

ADNOC announced a multi-year partnership with OpenAI and G42 on November 2, 2026, at ADIPEC in Abu Dhabi, under which it says it will become the first energy company to deploy OpenAI Frontier. G42 signed through its subsidiary Inception42. The signing was witnessed by Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan, Crown Prince of Abu Dhabi and Chairman of the Abu Dhabi Executive Council, after he met OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman, Reuters reported.

Frontier is OpenAI’s platform for connecting and coordinating frontier AI agents across complex business operations. In ADNOC’s deployment, the partners will jointly develop and deploy what they describe as a bespoke decision-intelligence capability: OpenAI’s latest-generation frontier models combined with ADNOC’s proprietary industrial data and expertise, deployed on OpenAI’s GPT models and G42’s sovereign AI infrastructure in the UAE. Human experts will retain oversight of consequential decisions, and all solutions will operate within ADNOC’s oversight, which the company said ensures proprietary data and critical infrastructure remain protected.

Dr. Sultan Ahmed Al Jaber, UAE Minister of Industry and Advanced Technology and ADNOC’s Managing Director and Group CEO, said: “As the first energy company to deploy OpenAI Frontier, we are demonstrating how frontier AI can move beyond individual applications to reshape the performance of an entire industry.” OpenAI’s Altman described the agreement as a first-of-its-kind partnership combining frontier AI, sovereign infrastructure and industry expertise, and G42 International chief executive Mansoor Al Mansoori said it builds on years of collaboration with ADNOC through AIQ.

Field plans in hours, and every barrel valued in the ground#

According to the announcement, the partnership will initially focus on two applications. The first will transform field master planning from a sequence of separate subsurface, facilities and economic studies into a single integrated decision. Building on ENERGYai’s reservoir-level intelligence, OpenAI’s advanced reasoning models will integrate decades of data across thousands of wells, multiple stacked reservoirs and hundreds of surface facilities, together with capital, cost and commercial considerations. ADNOC said full-field development master plans can then be prepared and optimised in hours rather than months, with the whole field optimised at once instead of each reservoir or facility separately.

The second application will value every barrel while it is still in the ground, which ADNOC said is a first for an energy company. Interconnected AI agents will continuously forecast crude oil supply, demand, prices and margins, predict impacts before they occur, and weigh the commercial and operational impact of each decision as one integrated process across production, processing, refining, logistics, sales and trading — areas where, ADNOC said, decisions currently often rely on different systems, data and priorities.

Cyber defence, and an AI estate already running#

ADNOC said the partnership will also strengthen its cyber defence through Daybreak Blue, an OpenAI initiative that helps security teams identify vulnerabilities and protect critical energy infrastructure, and that the partners will co-develop AI solutions while training ADNOC’s workforce to deploy and use them. That work builds on ADNOC’s rollout of ChatGPT Enterprise to more than 40,000 employees, which the company describes as the largest enterprise deployment of ChatGPT in the Middle East and North Africa, with adoption exceeding 97%.

Frontier will extend AI systems ADNOC already runs. The company said its AI and robotics strategy spans more than 200 AI applications and 70 robotics use cases deployed or under development, including ENERGYai, its specialist subsurface platform developed with AIQ and built on a 70-billion-parameter model trained on more than 50 years of ADNOC data; AiPSO, which helps production teams identify and address performance issues before they affect output; and an AI-enabled Real-Time Operations Center connecting more than 120 rigs in a single live environment, which ADNOC said has reduced engineering effort by 30–40%.

What was not disclosed — and the three other Abu Dhabi deals#

What the announcements do not say matters as much as what they do. No financial value for the ADNOC agreement, and no full rollout timetable, was disclosed in the announcements reported on November 2. The partnership is described as multi-year, and its first applications are named, but the public record gives no price, capacity figure or date by which the deployment should be judged.

The ADNOC deal was one of four agreements signed between Abu Dhabi entities and OpenAI on the same day. The others cover a Machine-Readable Government initiative with the Abu Dhabi Government, investment analysis with Mubadala, Inception42 and OpenAI, and a five-year partnership between Inception42 and OpenAI to expand frontier AI adoption across government, enterprise and education in the UAE. The government initiative’s first pilot was already in production with the Abu Dhabi Agriculture and Food Safety Authority, focused on food imports. The test for the ADNOC deal will be narrower: whether field plans genuinely move from months to hours in practice, and whether the connected agents extend beyond the two initial applications. On the evidence published on November 2, neither has a date attached.

What did ADNOC agree to deploy?

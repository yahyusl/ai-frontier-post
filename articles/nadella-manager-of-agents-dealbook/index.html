Satya Nadella: every Microsoft employee will one day be a 'manager of agents'

At the DealBook Summit, Microsoft's CEO explained why he offered buyouts to 60,000 employees — and sketched a future where directing AI agents, not doing the work directly, is the job.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · November 19, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Speaking to Andrew Ross Sorkin at the DealBook Summit on November 18, Nadella said Microsoft is reorganising into an AI-first company where jobs shift from doing work directly to directing the AI agents that do it. The October buyout offer to roughly 60,000 employees was, in his telling, about giving people agency in that transition: get on board with the new way of working, or choose to move on. His proof point was the 11-developer team behind Cowork, the AI agent inside Microsoft 365 apps, who “build a software factory that builds the product.”
  • The venue: the New York Times DealBook Summit, Wednesday, November 18, 2026 — Nadella in conversation with Andrew Ross Sorkin.
  • The line: “every employee will one day similarly become a manager of agents, rather than a person directly doing the work” — and “that level of direction is what, by the way, is going to happen in all jobs.”
  • The buyout: voluntary exit offers to about 60,000 employees in October, framed as an alternative to another mass layoff. Nadella said it was “to make sure that at least everyone felt like they had agency in making choices.”
  • The example: Cowork, the AI agent in Microsoft 365 apps, built by a team of just 11 developers who, in Nadella’s words, “build a software factory that builds the product. They don’t build the product.”
  • The caveat: Microsoft is 51 years old and “didn’t grow up as an AI-first company,” Nadella said — the transformation “is going to take a bit more time than anyone expects.”

The offer landed in October: about 60,000 Microsoft employees were invited to leave voluntarily, with financial support, instead of the company cutting jobs through another mass layoff. The reasoning, until now, had been left mostly to speculation. On Wednesday, Nadella supplied it himself — and tied it to a much bigger claim about what jobs are about to become.

Speaking at the DealBook Summit, Nadella said Microsoft is reorganising into a company where everyone uses AI to do their work, and jobs become focused on managing that AI. His headline version of the future: every employee will one day become a manager of agents, rather than a person directly doing the work.

The buyout, explained#

Nadella’s argument, as reported by TechCrunch, is that the buyout was not about replacing people with AI. He insisted he is not saying software engineers are going away — quite the opposite. “Let’s take software engineering,” he said. “We went from, ‘Well, we don’t buy that AI can ever do software.’ To suddenly, ‘Wow, it can do all software, and why do we need software engineers?’ To now rediscovering that software engineering is very much alive and, in fact, is going to thrive, except the way the team is organized and the work that a software engineer does has now got a new operating point.”

The point of the buyout, in his telling, was consent. “The buyout was ‘to make sure that at least everyone felt like they had agency in making choices,’ he told Andrew Ross Sorkin during the DealBook Summit.” For some people, he added, “that reorganization and change is sort of hard, right? But, I mean, it has to be done.”

He was candid about the difficulty. Microsoft, he said, is a 51-year-old company that “didn’t grow up as an AI-first company,” so “the question is, how quickly can an incumbent transform the work, the workflow, and the work artifact, and with all the wage support that goes with it?” His answer: “It’s going to take a bit more time than anyone expects. And there is going to be in all of this, you know, people who may say, ‘Hey, I have some other choices I want to make.’”

Eleven developers and a software factory#

Nadella’s exhibit A was Cowork, the AI agent inside Microsoft 365 apps, built by a team of just 11 developers who have mastered using AI to do their jobs. “They build a software factory that builds the product. They don’t build the product.”

That is the model he expects to spread: small teams directing agents that do the bulk of the production, with humans setting direction and making the judgment calls. “He believes that every employee will one day similarly become a manager of agents, rather than a person directly doing the work. ‘That level of direction is what, by the way, is going to happen in all jobs.’”

It is a vision Nadella has been circling for a while. He made similar points a week earlier on Gergely Orosz’s Pragmatic Engineer podcast. What is new is the setting — a public stage, weeks after the buyout window opened — and the bluntness: the reorganisation is the job now, not a side project.

The reaction: “I’m already doing it” vs “massive unemployment”#

The interview reached Reddit within hours. A thread in r/singularity sharing the TechCrunch report drew dozens of comments overnight. One camp treated Nadella’s line as a description of the present: “I’m already doing it!” was a top reply. The other heard a threat: “So... massive unemployment, is what he’s saying,” ran another, while a third noted “AI agents don’t buy Windows.”

The sceptical note that drew the most agreement was simpler: what are the 60,000 people asked to quit supposed to do next? Nobody in the thread disputed that Nadella said it. The argument was over what it means.

What to watch#

Three things decide how this lands. First, how many of the 60,000 actually take the offer — Nadella did not say. Second, the anxiety Nadella himself named. On the Pragmatic Engineer podcast, he said, “We have created enormous anxiety in people sort of feeling that they don’t have control of their own destiny because the way we have talked about this AI revolution” — and called out talk of the singularity, and predictions that AI will take all jobs, as part of the problem. The buyout was, in his framing, the antidote: a choice, offered openly, instead of a layoff delivered without one. Whether employees experience it that way is the open question.

Third, whether “manager of agents” becomes the job description beyond Microsoft. Nadella framed it as universal — “in all jobs.” The test is not the slogan but the org chart: when the next round of hiring starts, do the roles ask for people who direct agents, or people who still do the work the agents do? For now, the message from the top of Microsoft is unusually direct: the work is changing, the choice is yours, and the clock is running.

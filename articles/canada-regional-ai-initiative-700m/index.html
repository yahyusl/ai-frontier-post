Canada's Regional AI Initiative hits $700M: Ottawa adds $500M for AI adoption

MD Marcus Doyle, Senior Technology Writer · October 13, 2026

Ottawa is putting serious money behind its AI ambitions. On Tuesday, the Honourable Evan Solomon, Canada’s Minister of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation, announced that an additional $500 million over five years is now available through the expanded Regional Artificial Intelligence Initiative (RAII), bringing total federal funding to $700 million.

The announcement, dated October 13, 2026 in Ottawa, is the country’s largest single push to get AI out of the lab and into businesses — delivered through the regional development agencies as part of the AI for All national AI strategy launched in June 2026.

What the money is for

This is an adoption play, not a research grant. The expanded initiative will help businesses and organizations responsibly adopt and integrate AI to improve productivity, competitiveness and resilience — and help Canadian AI firms win customers, attract investment, and commercialize Canadian-made products and services.

Support will concentrate on sectors where Canada is strongest: health and life sciences, energy and natural resources, transportation, agriculture, manufacturing and robotics, alongside other regional strengths and priorities.

“The question is not whether AI will reshape our economy. It is whether Canada will capture the opportunity,” Solomon said. “The Regional Artificial Intelligence Initiative will help Canadian businesses adopt AI and Canadian AI firms scale from here, so that the technology, talent and economic value we create remain anchored in Canada and under Canadian control.”

A track record to build on

RAII has been running since 2024, and Ottawa shared its scorecard: 218 projects have adopted or commercialized AI through the initiative, creating or maintaining more than 1,100 jobs. Those projects have leveraged roughly $1.77 for every dollar approved — an early sign the money multiplies rather than substitutes.

The regional breakdown

The rollout is region by region. In Atlantic Canada, nearly $40 million more flows through the Atlantic Canada Opportunities Agency, bringing total RAII support in the region past $55 million. An Atlantic-wide call for proposals is open to small and medium-sized businesses with AI projects valued at $1 million or more; it closes November 20, 2026.

In southern Ontario — home to more than half of Canada’s AI firms — applications are being accepted until January 19, 2027, and proposed projects with eligible costs before March 31, 2027 must be submitted by November 24, 2026.

Why it matters

Canada helped pioneer modern AI, but has watched much of the commercialization and talent drift south. This funding is explicitly a sovereignty bet: broaden AI adoption across the economy while helping domestic AI firms scale here. The AI for All strategy frames the goal as getting more businesses past the pilot stage and into AI across core operations, with a particular focus on small and mid-sized firms.

Businesses interested in applying can connect with the regional development agency serving their region through the initiative’s portal.

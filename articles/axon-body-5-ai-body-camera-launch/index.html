Axon Body 5: 25x the AI power, license scans, and real-time risk alerts for officers

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 24, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: Axon announced Body 5 and Body 5 Pro on October 24, 2026: body cameras built to run AI on the device, with 25 times the AI processing power of earlier models and a new Guardian feature that flags potential conflicts in real time. General availability dates have not been announced yet.
  • Who: Axon (Nasdaq: AXON), the public safety technology company behind TASER devices and body cameras.
  • What: Axon Body 5 and Axon Body 5 Pro — body-worn cameras built from the ground up for the AI era, plus Axon Guardian, a new category of real-time AI features.
  • When: Announced October 24, 2026, from Scottsdale, Arizona.
  • AI power: 25 times the AI processing power of earlier-generation Axon body cameras.
  • Battery: 20% more battery life than the previous generation.
  • Guardian: Escalation Detection flags potential conflicts to a human reviewer in Axon Fusus; Approach Alert warns officers when a stopped vehicle's door opens.

Axon has spent years putting cameras on officers to record what happened. With Body 5, announced October 24, 2026, the company wants the camera to help while it is still happening. The new body-worn camera line carries 25 times the AI processing power of earlier-generation Axon cameras, and it is the first Axon camera built from the ground up to run AI features on the device itself.

The pitch is a shift in what a body camera is for. Instead of only capturing evidence for later, Body 5 is meant to give officers information, assistance and backup on the spot — reading a license, translating a conversation, or flagging a situation that looks like it is escalating. Axon is launching two versions at once, the Body 5 and the Body 5 Pro, and pairing them with a new set of real-time AI features called Axon Guardian.

Two cameras, built for on-device AI

Both models share the same core upgrades: the 25x jump in AI processing power, improved video with better low-light performance, improved connectivity in urban and remote environments with more precise location, and 20% more battery life than the previous generation — enough, Axon says, to keep the camera ready and connected through an officer's shift.

The Pro adds hardware for heavier AI work. A dedicated near-focus camera lets officers scan driver's licenses to check status and search for wants or warrants in seconds, without walking back to the patrol vehicle, with the results sent to Axon Fusus for real-time operations. Four additional front-facing speakers project audio toward the person an officer is speaking with, so real-time translations from Axon Assistant carry over traffic or crowd noise. Extra memory lets the camera run several AI tools at once. Axon says the Pro will keep gaining new AI capabilities as they arrive, alongside its AI Era Plan subscription.

Guardian: AI that flags risk as it happens

Axon Guardian is the more consequential half of the announcement. It is a new category of AI features within the AI Era Plan, designed to work with officers' field devices — starting with body cameras and in-car cameras — to flag potential risks as situations unfold.

The first two features show how Axon wants that to work. Escalation Detection watches when an Axon Body 4 or Body 5 camera is activated and recording; if it detects a potential conflict in real time, it creates an alert in Axon Fusus, where a human reviews the livestream and decides whether additional support or action is needed. Approach Alert, active on Axon Fleet 3 in-car cameras, detects when the door of a stopped vehicle opens and alerts the officer through the in-car display and their body-worn camera.

The human-in-the-loop detail matters. Guardian does not act on its own: an alert goes to a person, who decides what happens next. Axon frames that under its Responsible Innovation framework, which also promises agency control over data and oversight from its independent Ethics and Equity Advisory Council — a notable inclusion for a product that puts real-time AI analysis on police encounters.

From paperwork to the scene

Body 5 extends a portfolio Axon has been building toward this moment. Draft One already turns body-camera audio into report drafts, and Axon Assistant provides voice-based information access and real-time translation. Body 5 moves those tools from the station to the street.

"Officers shouldn't have to choose between getting information and watching the scene. With Axon Body 5, they don't," said Rick Smith, Axon's founder and CEO, in the announcement. "They capture a license or document with the camera, keep their hands free and eyes up, and get information faster than writing it down and calling it in by radio."

What we don't know yet

The announcement leaves the practical questions open. General availability dates for both Body 5 and Guardian will be announced soon, Axon said, and no pricing was given. The company also gave no absolute specifications — no processor details, no battery life in hours — only the relative claims of 25 times the processing power and 20% more battery. Those are company assertions, and the real test will be how the cameras and Guardian perform on shift, and how agencies and communities react to AI that watches encounters in real time.

Still, the direction is clear: the body camera is becoming a computer, not just a recorder. Axon's bet is that the next leap in public safety technology is not better footage after the fact, but better information during it — as covered in the company's press release and independently reported the same day.

Google will keep your Gemini chats for two months — even with History off

Google is stretching Gemini's retention for temporary chats and history-off conversations from 72 hours to around two months — while splitting history and AI training into separate controls, so you can keep one without the other.

PNPriya NairResearch Editor · November 20, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Google is stretching how long it keeps Gemini chats you chose not to save — from 72 hours to around two months — while splitting history and AI training into separate settings, so you can keep your history without letting Google train on it. The change, announced November 16, rolls out globally over the coming weeks.
  • The change: Temporary chats and chats made with History off will now be kept for around two months, up from 72 hours — roughly a twenty-fold increase.
  • The source: Google’s November 16 blog post, “We’re updating controls in the Gemini app,” and an updated Gemini Apps Privacy Hub dated November 17.
  • What Google does with the data: Uses the last 24 hours of a chat as context, processes feedback, and reviews conversations for safety — with help from human reviewers. Google says the retained data is not used to train models.
  • The catch: Chats flagged for human review can be kept for up to three years, even after you delete your history.
  • The upside: History and AI training are now separate controls. You can save your history while opting out of training — previously the two were bundled under one “Keep Activity” switch.

Turning off Gemini's history no longer means what it used to. Google has quietly stretched how long it keeps the conversations you explicitly chose not to save — from three days to around two months — and the change is already rolling out.

The update was announced in a November 16 blog post and written into Google's Gemini Apps Privacy Hub the following day. Chats started in temporary mode, or with the new History setting switched off, still won’t appear in the history you can see. But they will now sit on Google's servers, tied to your account, for roughly twenty times longer than before.

What actually changed#

Until now, the deal was simple, if not widely understood: Gemini kept temporary chats and history-off conversations for 72 hours, then let them go. An archived copy of the Privacy Hub from November 12 still listed the three-day window. The updated page replaces it with “around two months.”

Google's stated reasons are practical: it needs the chats to answer you — Gemini uses the last 24 hours of a conversation as context — to process any feedback you send, to keep the service running if something fails, and to give its safety systems, including human reviewers, time to spot abuse. The company told TechCrunch the retained data will be used strictly for safety and security, not for training models or improving other products.

The change lands alongside a genuine improvement to the controls. The old “Keep Activity” switch, which bundled saving history together with letting Google use chats for training, is being split in two. History and training become separate settings, so you can keep a searchable record of your conversations while opting out of training — or switch history off entirely and still have training off. The new settings roll out globally in the Gemini app over the coming weeks, and anyone who had Keep Activity off will start with both new settings off.

The trade-off Google is offering#

There is a real win here, and it is worth naming: for years, the price of a usable chat history was letting your conversations feed model training. Splitting the two controls fixes that, and it is the part of the announcement Google led with.

But the retention extension cuts the other way. A setting labelled “off” now means “kept for two months, out of sight.” Gizmodo, which first flagged the updated wording, noted the new window runs about twice as long as what Google's main rivals keep for comparable chats. Google declined to comment on the change beyond pointing reporters to its blog post.

Google's justification is safety: as Gemini gets more capable, the company says it needs a longer window to detect, report and investigate complex abuse. That is a plausible reason — safety review genuinely takes time, and three days is a short window for investigating sophisticated misuse. The question is whether a twenty-fold extension, applied silently to people who actively switched history off, is the proportionate way to get it.

What the off switch doesn’t do#

A few details in Google's own documentation are easy to miss, and they matter more than the headline number:

  • History off is not deletion. Your chats are hidden from your history, not erased — they stay tied to your account for around two months.
  • Human review can extend that much further. Chats sent for review, and related data, can be kept for up to three years even after you delete your history, according to Google's documentation.
  • Feedback pulls chats in. If you send feedback with History off, Google collects the last 24 hours of the related conversation along with it.
  • Saved history has its own clock. With History on, chats auto-delete after 18 months by default — adjustable to three or 36 months, or switched off — and you can delete them manually at any time.

None of this is unusual for a large AI provider — every major chatbot keeps some conversation data for safety review. What is unusual is the gap between what the switch appears to promise and what it now does. “Off” used to mean three days. Now it means two months, and the in-app description of the setting still said 72 hours when the change was spotted, according to Gizmodo.

What to watch#

First, the rollout. The new controls reach accounts gradually over the coming weeks, so check Settings and help → History in the Gemini app to see whether yours have arrived — and whether the description still says 72 hours.

Second, the sibling product. Google launched temporary threads in its AI Mode search experience on November 18, and TechCrunch reported no stated retention period for those conversations. Whether the same two-month rule applies there is unconfirmed — worth watching as Google harmonises the two products.

And third, the direction of travel. As chatbots grow more capable, every provider is lengthening the window it holds conversations for safety review. The real test is whether users get a genuine choice in the matter, or just a longer invisible tail on a switch that says “off.”

Frequently asked questions

Does turning off Gemini History delete my chats?

No. Chats made with History off, and temporary chats, are hidden from the history you can see — but Google now keeps them tied to your account for around two months, up from 72 hours.

Will Google use my history-off chats to train its models?

No. Google says temporary chats are not used to train its AI models, and that the data retained for around two months is used strictly for safety and security — to respond to you, process feedback and protect users — not for training or other product improvements.

Can I keep my Gemini history without letting Google train on it?

Yes — that is the other half of this update. History and AI training are now separate settings, replacing the old single “Keep Activity” switch. You can leave History on and switch off the training setting. The new controls roll out globally over the coming weeks.

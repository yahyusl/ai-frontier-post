RUMOR: Gemini 4 Neon pricing leaks into Google Cloud billing catalog as Google stays silent

Pricing for an unannounced Gemini 4 Neon model has appeared in Google Cloud's billing catalog at $3 in and $15 out per million tokens — but Google has said nothing, and the model can’t be called yet.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 18, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Pricing for an unannounced Gemini 4 Neon model has appeared in Google Cloud’s billing catalog at $3 in / $15 out per million tokens, and the model also shows up in Antigravity CLI as a Pro orchestrator. Google has announced nothing, and the model can’t be called yet — so treat this as a well-sourced rumor, not a launch.
  • A gemini-4-neon reference first appeared in the Gemini web app’s upsell section on November 16; Google has not announced the model and it is not available to anyone yet.
  • On November 18, Gemini 4 Neon appeared in the Google Cloud SKU catalog under the Gemini API: text at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, cached input at $0.15, with Batch, Flex and Contributor tiers at half price.
  • Antigravity CLI 1.5.11 adds a gemini-pro-orchestrator-neon variant, described as a Gemini Pro orchestrator model — a Pro-class role, not a cheap Flash-tier one.
  • The r/singularity thread carrying the news drew 227 upvotes and 64 comments; top comments debated the naming scheme and the narrow price gap, not the catalog entries themselves.

Google hasn’t said a word about Gemini 4 Neon. Its billing system has. Pricing for the unannounced model appeared in Google Cloud’s SKU catalog early Wednesday, listing text input at $3 per million tokens and output at $15 — and within hours, the model-tracking community had mapped where it fits, what it might be, and everything Google still hasn’t confirmed.

This is a rumor, not a launch. Nobody can call the model yet: it does not answer on Vertex AI, there is no model card, no API changelog entry, and no announcement from Google. What exists is a set of billing entries — the kind of paperwork that has preceded Google’s recent releases — plus a second sighting in an agentic coding tool. Here is what checks out, and what doesn’t.

What the billing catalog actually shows#

The first sighting came Monday, November 16, when a reference to gemini-4-neon appeared in the Gemini web app’s upsell section — the part of the app that promotes paid modes — suggesting the model would require a subscription. It was not available to anyone.

Then, early Wednesday, the model showed up in the Google Cloud SKU catalog, under the Gemini API, with separate SKUs for text, image, video and audio. The text entries list $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, with cached input at $0.15. The entries went live as of 3 AM ET, according to the AI Tracker account that first flagged them, and were corroborated through the day by TestingCatalog, OfficeChai and NPowerUser.

The full tier list is unusually complete, which is itself a signal — a placeholder rarely ships with every pricing tier filled in:

TierInput (per 1M tokens)Output (per 1M tokens)
Standard$3.00$15.00
Cached input$0.15—
Batch$1.50$7.50
Flex$1.50$7.50
Contributor$1.50$7.50
Priority$5.40$27.00

Batch, Flex and Contributor tiers all come in at half the standard rate; Priority carries an 80% premium. Cached input is 95% cheaper than standard input — the same discount Google applies to its other Gemini 4 models.

Where $3/$15 sits in the Gemini 4 lineup#

Neon would be exactly double the price of Hydrogen, the Flash-tier model released November 12 at $1.50/$7.50, and 25% cheaper than Carbon and Argon at their standard $4/$20 rates — though those two currently sit at an introductory $2/$10 until December 1, when they rise. So against today’s prices, Neon would cost 50% more than the current flagships; against December’s, 25% less. That odd straddle is part of why the community is arguing about what Neon actually is.

ModelInput (per 1M)Output (per 1M)Role
Gemini 4 Hydrogen$1.50$7.50Flash tier
Gemini 4 Neon (rumored)$3.00$15.00Mid-tier / Pro orchestrator
Gemini 4 Argon / Carbon$2.00 now, $4.00 from Dec 1$10.00 now, $20.00 from Dec 1Flagship tier

The naming fits Google’s current scheme: every Gemini 4 model so far is a chemical element. Argon arrived September 30, Carbon on October 28, Hydrogen two weeks later — three models in about six weeks. Neon would be the fourth. The names have drawn some mockery — Neon and Argon are noble gases, Hydrogen and Carbon are not — but the cadence is the story: Google is shipping a new Gemini 4 model roughly every two weeks.

The Antigravity clue#

The second sighting came the same day, in Google’s Antigravity CLI: version 1.5.11 adds a gemini-pro-orchestrator-neon variant, described as a Gemini Pro orchestrator model. An orchestrator plans multi-step agent work and hands tasks to other models; the same build pairs Carbon for planning with Hydrogen for execution. That framing — Neon as a Pro-class orchestrator, not a cheap Flash-style model — matches the mid-tier pricing.

One unverified theory making the rounds is that Neon is a new name for Gemini 4 Krypton, a checkpoint that X users said earlier this month looked like an upgrade on Carbon, using far more thinking tokens and producing more detailed outputs. Nothing in the SKU listing links the two names, and Neon could just as easily be a separate model. Treat the Krypton connection as speculation.

What Google hasn’t said — and why this is still a rumor#

Everything above is paperwork and plumbing, not a product. Google has announced nothing: no model card, no row in the API changelog, no rate card, no endpoint. And one piece of evidence has already moved: the gemini-4-neon string that appeared in the Gemini web app on Monday was gone from the app’s model map by early Wednesday, according to OrcaRouter’s checks. A billing catalog entry is a strong signal — SKU entries preceded earlier Gemini releases — but it is not a launch date, and Google has pulled or renamed models before they shipped.

The Reddit thread that carried the news to r/singularity — 227 upvotes, 64 comments — mostly argued about the naming scheme and whether the price gap makes sense, rather than disputing the catalog entries themselves. The skepticism is aimed at the price: Neon at $3/$15 sits awkwardly close to Carbon’s $4/$20, and some commenters expect a bigger gap between tiers. A fair question; the catalog doesn’t answer it.

Also worth noting: around the same time the SKUs surfaced, Google DeepMind’s Logan Kilpatrick — who leads product for Google AI Studio and the Gemini API — posted a single word on X: “Gemini.” Demis Hassabis reposted it, and the post drew more than 740,000 views. It names no model and proves nothing on its own. But the timing is hard to ignore.

What to watch#

Three things, in order: whether Google announces Neon in the coming days, and whether it lands in the Gemini app, on Vertex AI, or both; whether the December 1 price rises for Carbon and Argon actually happen, which would set Neon’s real position in the lineup; and whether Neon turns out to be the Krypton checkpoint under a new name, or a fourth model entirely. If the catalog entries are right, the model is already priced, already wired into a CLI, and waiting on a nameplate.

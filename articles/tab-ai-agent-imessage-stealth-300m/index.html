Tab exits stealth at a $300M valuation: an AI agent that lives in iMessage and books your flights

San Francisco startup Tab has emerged from stealth with a $300 million pre-seed valuation, betting the winning personal AI assistant won't be an app at all — it'll be a contact in iMessage and WhatsApp that books flights, calls businesses, and drafts emails.

MD Marcus Doyle Senior Technology Writer · October 7, 2026 · 4 min read

Another personal AI assistant has launched — and this one arrives with a valuation that stops the scroll. San Francisco-based startup Tab has emerged from stealth with a $300 million valuation following a pre-seed raise backed by SV Angel, Valar Ventures, and American Spirit, with TechCrunch covering the launch on Wednesday. The pitch is disarmingly simple: an AI agent that lives where you already spend your time — inside iMessage and WhatsApp — and does things instead of just answering questions.

There is no app to install and no dashboard to learn. Users text Tab like a contact in their messaging app, and the agent takes real actions on their behalf: books flights, calls businesses, drafts emails, and chases wire transfers. That execution-over-answers distinction is the whole bet — Tab is positioning itself as a full-service personal AI that removes the adoption friction that has plagued other assistants.

The $300M pre-seed bet

The number is the story as much as the product. The average pre-seed AI startup valuation currently sits around $15–25 million, according to OpenVC data cited in launch coverage — Tab is commanding roughly a dozen times that before most people have heard of it. The company has not disclosed user counts or revenue, so the premium rests on investor conviction in the messaging-first approach and whatever early traction or demos the team showed behind closed doors.

Tab was founded by Brennan Erbz, Stafford Schlitt, and Ammar Amdani. Amdani is a known quantity in early-stage tech: he co-founded Adapt Ventures, an early-stage venture firm, with his brother Mohammed, and both brothers made the Forbes 30 Under 30 list in 2022. With Tab, Amdani moves from backing startups to building one.

A crowded race for the message thread

Tab is surfing a genuine wave. A growing cohort of startups — Poke, Folk, and Miso among them — are building agents accessible through SMS or iMessage. Developer platform Photon raised $4.5 million last week to give developers messaging-native agent tooling, and DoorDash launched a text-to-order AI agent inside iMessage on September 30. Apple itself approved its first AI agent on the Messages for Business platform back in June. The thesis, repeated across all of them: users no longer want to install new apps — they want agents inside the channels they already open a hundred times a day.

Tab's approach is broader than most of the field, though. Where rivals handle scheduling or simple lookups, Tab is reaching for financial transactions, travel bookings, and direct phone calls to businesses — the unglamorous, multi-step chores that actually eat a day.

Why it matters

A $300 million pre-seed valuation says investors believe the distribution battle for personal agents will be won on incumbents' home turf — the message thread, where Apple and Meta already own the surface and the attention. It also sets a brutal bar: at this valuation, Tab has to become more than a clever demo. The open questions are the hard ones — how an agent trusted with wire transfers and travel bookings handles security and fraud, what data access it requires to be useful, and how Apple responds if messaging-native agents start siphoning value from the App Store.

Watch whether Tab opens beyond early access and what its first public metrics look like. If the agent can reliably complete full transactions instead of merely suggesting them, the “no app needed” pitch could quietly become the default interface for personal AI — and every assistant still living in a separate app just got a problem.

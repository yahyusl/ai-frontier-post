Anthropic commits 5% of its shares to a new Public Benefit Fund — and the stake grows with the company

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 18, 2026 · 5 min read
TL;DR: Anthropic announced today (October 18, 2026) it is committing 5% of its shares to a new Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, subject to stockholder approval, with an additional 2% added at the board’s discretion for every 50% increase in company value above a set baseline. Structured as a wholly-owned LLC that can’t fund lobbying or political giving, and combined with earlier co-founder and employee pledges, the move puts roughly 20% of Anthropic’s shares toward the public good.
  • Who: Anthropic (the Claude maker), via a post on its official site, “Investing in the public benefit.”
  • What: 5% of Anthropic’s shares committed to the new Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, subject to stockholder approval.
  • The growth mechanism: an additional 2% added at the board’s discretion for every 50% increase in company value above a set baseline.
  • The structure: a wholly-owned LLC (not a 501(c)(3)); none of the equity can fund lobbying or political giving in the US or abroad.
  • Already committed: $200 million for studying and addressing AI’s labor-market effects.
  • Combined total: roughly 20% of Anthropic shares now dedicated to the public good — before any valuation top-ups.

Anthropic announced today that it is committing 5% of its shares to a newly created Anthropic Public Benefit Fund — and the stake is designed to grow as the company does. Subject to stockholder approval, the shares will be committed today, with an additional 2% added at the board’s discretion for every 50% increase in the company’s value above a set baseline.

The fund will be established as a wholly-owned LLC rather than a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, a structure Anthropic says gives it “adjustability between different forms of giving.” There is one hard rule attached: none of the equity held by the LLC will be used for lobbying or political giving, anywhere in the US or abroad.

Where the money could go

Anthropic sketches broad possibilities rather than a budget: large donations to traditional nonprofits, and direct distributions to specific populations. The headline example is labor-market disruption. The company notes it has already committed $200 million to studying and addressing AI’s effects on workers — but says the pace of AI progress may call for “bolder approaches,” and the fund gives it the versatility to try them.

The executive-pay contrast

Anthropic framed the move as a deliberate break with tech-industry norms: “It is common practice for tech companies to offer escalating compensation awards to their executives. We are instead directing that equity to the public benefit.” The point is structural: because of how the fund is set up, the more Anthropic grows, the larger the share of the company dedicated expressly to the public good.

One in five shares for the public good

This builds on earlier commitments. When Anthropic was founded, its co-founders pledged to donate 80% of their equity holdings, and the company has since run an equity donation match program that roughly half of its employees take part in. Combined with today’s announcement, Anthropic says roughly 20% of its shares are now dedicated to the public good — before any valuation-milestone top-ups. The company says it will share more about the fund’s structure, governance, and valuation milestones in the coming months, alongside an upcoming public benefit report.

Why it matters now

As TechCrunch notes, the announcement lands amid increasingly negative public sentiment toward the AI industry — something CEO Dario Amodei has described as “fundamentally a crisis of trust.” A fund whose stake compounds with the company’s valuation is a structural bet that AI’s economic fallout, starting with jobs, will need money at a scale no one-time gift can cover — and that the share of the company devoted to it should grow in lockstep with the value AI creates.

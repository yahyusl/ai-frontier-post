Meta puts Muse inside Instagram DMs — and your saved posts get an AI organizer

Meta has moved its Muse AI agent into the place where most of its users actually spend their time: Instagram's direct messages. From Tuesday, users in five countries can chat with Muse inside a DM thread — asking it about a post they were just sent, getting it to catch up on conversations they missed, or handing it a saved recipe and getting a grocery list back.

SASofia AlmeidaTutorials & Reviews Lead · November 25, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Meta rolled out Muse inside Instagram DMs on November 24 for iOS and Android users in the US, Canada, Mexico, Japan and Singapore. The agent shows up as an ordinary conversation, answers questions about posts, places and products, catches users up on missed chats, and organizes saved posts into Collections. Meta says it takes no action without the user's approval — and the UK, which got the standalone Muse app the same day, does not get the DM integration yet.
  • The rollout: Muse in Instagram DMs, from November 24, on iOS and Android, in the US, Canada, Mexico, Japan and Singapore. The UK is excluded for now.
  • What it does: chat about a post, place or product; catch up on missed messages in other DM threads; act on shared posts — add a coffee shop to a travel itinerary, or find a deal on a pair of shoes.
  • Saved posts: Muse organizes bookmarks into Collections, turns saved travel content into itineraries, and builds grocery lists from saved recipes. Users in the US and Canada get prompted to let Muse organize a post when they save it.
  • The guardrail: Muse does not publish, send or share anything without user approval, and users can delete saved information or undo its actions. Conversations also appear as a side chat in the standalone Muse app.
  • The context: Muse launched in the US in September and has passed 10 million downloads. The Instagram move lands the same day Muse launched in the UK as a standalone app — a second front in Meta's push to put the agent everywhere its users already are.

Until now, using Meta's Muse agent meant leaving the app you were in and opening another one. That friction is the whole point of Tuesday's move: Muse now lives inside Instagram direct messages, appearing as a standard conversation in the inbox, so users can talk to it without switching apps at all.

It is the next step in a pattern Meta has been building since Muse launched in the US in September. The agent started as a standalone app, then spread to WhatsApp, and on the same day as the Instagram rollout it launched in the UK. Each step puts the same assistant in one more place where people already spend their attention. Instagram, with its endless stream of shared posts, recipes and travel clips, may be the most natural fit yet.

What Muse does inside your DMs#

The core idea is simple: anything a friend sends you can now be handed to Muse. Share a post and ask what the place in it is, or whether the product is a good deal. Send it a reel of a restaurant and ask it to add the spot to a Mexico City itinerary. Ask it to hunt down a Black Friday price on a pair of shoes you saw in a story.

Muse can also work across your inbox rather than just inside its own thread. Ask it to catch you up on conversations you missed in other DM threads, and it will summarize what happened while you were away. That is the feature with the most obvious appeal — and the one that will draw the most scrutiny, since summarizing missed messages means the assistant is reading private conversations at the user's request.

Threads started on Instagram do not stay trapped there: they also appear as a side chat in the standalone Muse app, so a conversation begun in DMs can continue on the desktop or in the app itself.

Your saved posts, sorted#

The quieter half of the announcement may matter more in daily use. Instagram's save button has long been a junk drawer: recipes, hotels, outfits and gift ideas piled up and never looked at again. Muse is now the organizer for that pile.

Ask it to sort saved posts and reels into Collections, turn a cluster of saved travel posts into a proper itinerary, or take a saved recipe and turn it into a grocery list. In the US and Canada, saving a post now brings up a prompt offering to let Muse organize it on the spot. The saved-content tools start in those two countries only; the DM chat itself rolls out more widely, to Mexico, Japan and Singapore as well.

It is a small, practical use of an AI agent — the kind that either becomes a habit within a week or gets ignored forever. The test will be whether the collections it builds are actually useful, or whether users find it easier to keep scrolling past their own saves.

The guardrails — and the question they don't answer#

Meta's pitch is that the user stays in control: Muse will not publish, send or share anything without approval, and users can delete information it has saved or undo actions it has taken. Nothing moves without a go-ahead.

That framing matters because Muse's record on that front is not spotless. Reuters reported glitches during internal testing, including a case in which the agent displayed a message containing random bank account balances. Meta said the problems came from a bug that has since been fixed, and that internal testing exists precisely to catch such failures early. An assistant that summarizes your private messages and organizes your saved life is exactly the kind of product where an approval gate has to work every time, not most of the time.

There is also the question of scope. The DM integration is not coming to the UK, even though the standalone Muse app launched there the same day — a reminder that Meta is still rolling the agent out market by market, with different features in different places. Users in the five launch countries get the full package; everyone else waits.

What to watch#

The first thing to watch is how quickly the rollout actually reaches people — Meta says it is rolling out from Tuesday, which in practice means a staggered arrival over days or weeks. The second is whether the UK gets the DM integration, and on what timetable, now that the standalone app is live there.

The bigger question is behavioral: do people want an AI assistant inside their private messages at all? The catch-up feature is genuinely useful, and the saved-post organizer solves a real annoyance. But both require trusting an agent with the most private corner of a social app. If users embrace it, Instagram becomes Muse's largest distribution channel overnight. If they don't, Meta has put its agent in the one place it cannot afford to get wrong.

Frequently asked questions

What can Muse do in Instagram DMs?

Muse appears as an ordinary conversation in your Instagram inbox. You can ask it about a post, place or product, have it catch you up on messages you missed in other threads, and send it posts to act on — for example, adding a coffee shop to a travel itinerary or finding a deal on a pair of shoes. It can also organize your saved posts into Collections, turn saved travel content into itineraries, and build grocery lists from saved recipes.

Which countries get Muse in Instagram DMs?

The rollout began on November 24, 2026 for iOS and Android users in the United States, Canada, Mexico, Japan and Singapore. The UK, which got the standalone Muse app the same day, does not get the Instagram DM integration yet. The saved-post organizing prompt is initially limited to the US and Canada.

Can Muse act on its own in your DMs?

According to Meta, no. The company says Muse will not publish, send or share anything without the user's approval, and users can delete information Muse has saved or undo actions it has taken. Conversations with Muse on Instagram also appear as a side chat in the standalone Muse app.

Web of Science goes AI-native: Clarivate's Research Intelligence crosses 100 institutions across 25 countries

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 22, 2026 · 4 min read
TL;DR: On October 22, 2026, Clarivate announced that more than 100 institutions across 25 countries and regions have adopted Web of Science Research Intelligence, its AI-native research intelligence platform launched in May 2026. Built on publisher-neutral Web of Science data with what the company calls “responsible academic AI,” the platform helps research offices, researchers, and library teams turn research questions into natural-language analysis for funding, strategy, and impact decisions.
  • Who: Clarivate Plc (NYSE: CLVT), a global provider of transformative intelligence.
  • What: Web of Science Research Intelligence, an AI-native research intelligence platform.
  • When: milestone announced October 22, 2026; the platform launched in May 2026.
  • Adoption: more than 100 institutions across 25 countries and regions; shaped by 50+ development partners and early adopters across 20 countries before launch.
  • Data foundation: publisher-neutral Web of Science Core Collection — “long recognized as the global standard for bibliometric analysis” — with full editorial provenance.
  • Purpose: combine curated Clarivate data with “responsible academic AI” to support decisions across funding, strategy, and impact for research offices, researchers, and library teams.

Clarivate announced Thursday that more than 100 institutions across 25 countries and regions have adopted Web of Science Research Intelligence, its AI-native research intelligence platform — a milestone the company frames as proof that universities want AI they can audit, not just AI that chats. (PR Newswire)

The announcement, issued from London, lands five months after the platform's May 2026 launch. Clarivate Plc (NYSE: CLVT) says the figure reflects "growing global customer demand" for what it calls responsible academic AI — AI built on curated, publisher-neutral data rather than the open web.

The product is aimed squarely at the research office. Web of Science Research Intelligence is designed to support institutional decision-making across funding, strategy, and impact, combining Clarivate's curated data with AI to help research offices, researchers, and library teams secure funding, strengthen collaboration, inform strategy, and demonstrate real-world impact. It is built on the Web of Science Core Collection — "long recognized as the global standard for bibliometric analysis," in Clarivate's words — and was shaped by more than 50 development partners and early adopters across 20 countries before launch.

Ask a simple question, get a real analysis

The pitch is accessibility without dumbing down. Hitoshi Takahashi, a University Research Administrator in Hiroshima University's Research Strategy and Promotion Division, said the platform "brings together capabilities we previously had to use separately, making sophisticated analysis accessible to researchers and research support professionals, not just specialist users." His verdict on the AI itself: "The AI can turn a simple question into thoughtful analysis, delivered in natural language, helping us reach deeper insights faster."

The use cases read like a research office's to-do list: research strategy, benchmarking, expert discovery, and institutional performance analysis. Kathleen Smeaton, Director of Collections and Technology at Monash University in Australia, said her institution's experience as a development partner gave it "confidence in Web of Science Research Intelligence's potential" — and Monash has since moved from development partner to paying customer, a transition Smeaton called a reflection of "both the strength of the partnership and our belief in the value Web of Science Research Intelligence can deliver to the university."

The trusted layer between AI and research

Clarivate's bet is provenance. On the company's Q2 2026 earnings call in late July, CEO Matti Shem Tov said the strategy is "to be the trusted layer between AI and research," adding that the platform's insights are "grounded in publisher-neutral Web of Science data" with "full editorial provenance." (MarketBeat transcript) That same quarter, Clarivate launched the platform globally.

The framing matters because research is one of the domains where AI's hallucination problem is most expensive. A chatbot that invents citations can torpedo a grant application; a platform whose answers trace back to a curated bibliographic database is selling exactly the audit trail that research offices, funders, and tenure committees need. Clarivate's announcement keeps the emphasis there: "responsible academic AI," built on data the research community already treats as canonical.

The milestone also fits a broader pivot. On that same earnings call, Shem Tov said Clarivate had introduced two new agentic AI products, Nexus Connect and IPOne, and had agreed to sell its Life Sciences & Healthcare segment to sharpen focus on what he called "AI-driven transformative intelligence."

Why it matters

Universities are under growing pressure to demonstrate the return on research funding — to governments, donors, and their own boards. The tools that win that argument are the ones whose numbers can be checked. One hundred institutions across 25 countries in five months won't settle whether AI-native research intelligence is the future of the research office, but it suggests the demand is real — and that the first-mover advantage in trusted academic AI may belong to whoever already owns the data.

For now, Clarivate is reading the milestone as validation. "This important milestone demonstrates that we are addressing an important and growing need across the global research community for trusted, AI-enabled tools that help universities and research organizations make more informed decisions around research funding, strategy and impact," said Bar Veinstein, President of Academia & Government at Clarivate. The company says customer engagement continues to inform the platform's evolution. (TradingView mirror of the announcement)

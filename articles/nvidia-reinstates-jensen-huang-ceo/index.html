Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO — unanimous board vote, with U.S. government input

Eleven days after Jensen Huang resigned, Nvidia’s board has voted unanimously to bring him back as president and CEO, effective immediately — after taking input from senior levels of the U.S. government on the national-security questions around him.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · November 23, 2026 · 6 min read
TL;DR: Nvidia’s board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president and chief executive officer, effective immediately. The board said it took input from senior levels of the U.S. government and is confident the return poses no national security risk. The investigation into Huang, however, will still run to its conclusion — and the board is adding permanent national-security oversight it did not have before.
  • The vote: unanimous. Huang returns as president, CEO and board director, effective immediately, eleven days after resigning all three roles.
  • Government input: the board received input from senior levels of the U.S. government and says it is confident the reinstatement poses no national security risk.
  • Investigation continues: the committee of independent directors investigating Huang will proceed to conclusion; Huang supports it and will cooperate.
  • New oversight: a National Security and Export Control Committee of independent directors, and a search for a new Chief Compliance Officer.
  • The interregnum: CFO Colette Kress served as interim CEO and delivered a record $115.8 billion quarter in Nvidia’s first report without Huang.

Eleven days after Jensen Huang walked out of Nvidia, the board has brought him back. In a release published on Monday morning, Nvidia said its directors voted unanimously to reinstate Huang as president and chief executive officer — and as a member of the board — effective immediately.

The statement is short, but the sentence that matters most is the one about Washington. The board said it received input from senior levels of the United States government in reaching its decision, and that based on that input it is confident Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk. That answers, in public, the question hanging over the company since Huang's departure: whether the circumstances of his exit made him a security risk the company could not take back.

What Nvidia announced#

The release, titled “NVIDIA Board Reinstates Jensen Huang as President, CEO and Board Director,” says the unanimous vote followed “a series of discussions and meetings” with Huang, after which the board was satisfied with his candor and his ability to lead. Board chair Harvey C. Jones said the board believes Huang “is the right person to lead NVIDIA” and that his return is in the best interests of stockholders, employees, partners and customers. Huang, in the same release, said he was “honored to return” and thanked the board, Colette Kress and the executive staff for leading the company during the interval — and his family, “above all.”

The board also thanked Kress for her “outstanding service” as interim CEO, a role she held alongside her continuing job as chief financial officer. Jones remains board chair. Nvidia also filed a Form 8-K with the SEC confirming the board's vote.

Eleven days that shook Nvidia#

The reinstatement closes a saga that moved in days, not months — and reverses a position the board had only just taken:

  • The investigation: the board opened an independent investigation after Huang admitted an affair with a woman alleged to be a Chinese spy.
  • November 11: the Wall Street Journal reported the board was preparing to oust Huang as a second affair surfaced.
  • November 12: Huang resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately. Kress became interim CEO; Jones became the board's first-ever chair. Reuters called the departure “a big blow to the AI boom.”
  • November 15: the Financial Times reported Huang was weighing a comeback, and President Trump floated Apple chairman Tim Cook as his successor.
  • November 17–18: Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue — its first report without Huang — and guided to $130 billion.
  • The ask: Huang said he would return if the company asked. The company said it would not. Elon Musk and President Trump called for his reinstatement.
  • November 23: the board voted unanimously to reinstate him.

The whiplash is the story as much as the return. A board that said no — and then, within days, voted unanimously yes — is a board whose mind was changed by something. The release points at two things: its own meetings with Huang, and input from senior levels of the U.S. government.

Why the government input matters#

The national-security shadow over Huang was never a side issue; it was the reason the board could not simply bring him back the moment he asked. The release itself notes that Huang “has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort” — the reporting that prompted the independent-director committee's investigation in the first place.

By tying its confidence to government input, the board is putting a public, checkable claim on the record — and shifting the question from whether Huang can lead to whether the company's governance can now withstand scrutiny. The new structures say the board knows the second question is still open: a National Security and Export Control Committee made up solely of independent directors, a search for a new Chief Compliance Officer to oversee compliance across the company, and an investigation that, the board stressed, will proceed to conclusion with Huang's cooperation.

The export-control backdrop sharpens the point: days before the reinstatement, Nvidia halted shipments of its licensed H200 chips to China. Oversight of trade compliance and national-security risk is now a standing board committee's job, not an ad-hoc response to a crisis.

What to watch#

First, the investigation: what the committee discloses when it concludes, and whether its findings change the new oversight structure. Second, the market's verdict — the first outside judgment on whether investors read this as stability restored or governance still unsettled. Third, Huang himself: whether he addresses employees and investors in his own words, and what he says about the eleven days he was gone. And fourth, the export-control posture set during the interregnum — whether the H200 halt and the new compliance apparatus survive his return, or quietly soften.

For now, the facts are simple: the founder is back, the vote was unanimous, the government was consulted, and the investigation is not over. Nvidia spent eleven days answering the question of who runs the company at the heart of the AI boom. It answered it twice — and the second answer is the one that stands.

Why did Nvidia’s board reinstate Jensen Huang?

Is the investigation into Huang over?

No. The committee of independent directors investigating Huang will proceed to its conclusion, and Huang has said he supports it and will cooperate. The board has also formed a National Security and Export Control Committee and is searching for a new Chief Compliance Officer.

Who ran Nvidia while Huang was out?

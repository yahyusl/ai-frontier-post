Apple Intelligence finally lands in China — December launch, no Siri AI, and Beijing approves the updates

After two years of being locked out of one of its most important markets, Apple is finally bringing Apple Intelligence to China — as part of the iOS 27.3 update expected in December. But the version Chinese users will get is a stripped-down one: no new Siri AI, no image generation, and AI model updates that Beijing gets to approve.

MDMarcus DoyleSenior Technology Writer · October 14, 2026 · 5 min read

Today's reports

Bloomberg reported Wednesday that Apple plans to launch Apple Intelligence in mainland China in December as part of the upcoming iOS 27.3 update, ending a regulatory standoff that kept the platform out of China since its 2024 debut. Hours earlier, the South China Morning Post reported that China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) had issued network-access permits adding Alibaba's Qwen and Tongyi Wanxiang models to the iPhone Duo, iPhone 18 Pro, and earlier iPhones — the regulatory step that paves the way for the launch. The timing matters: the iPhone Duo is scheduled to launch in China next week.

What Chinese users get — and what they don't

According to Bloomberg, the initial rollout will include Genmoji custom emoji, AI-powered notification summaries, writing tools, and the Clean Up feature in Photos. Conspicuously absent: the new Siri AI assistant, the company's in-house image-generation tools, and the newer version of Genmoji — text generation will also be limited in some applications. MacRumors' summary puts it bluntly: Chinese users are getting the basic 2024 feature set, not the current one.

That's a sharp contrast with what Apple is rolling out elsewhere. The revamped Siri AI unveiled this year — which outside China sits behind a waitlist in iOS 27 — is excluded entirely.

The price of admission: Beijing signs off on model updates

China restricts foreign AI technology, so Apple had to build a China-specific compliance stack. Bloomberg reports that Apple Intelligence there will run with guardrails developed with Alibaba, including a filter that lets the Chinese government approve ongoing updates to the AI models. Apple is also working with Baidu on search and Siri-related functions, and the MIIT permits name three AI systems on recent iPhones: Apple Intelligence itself, Alibaba's Qwen (Tongyi Qianwen), and Tongyi Wanxiang, Alibaba's text-to-image and video model.

The approval path was long. Apple received initial clearance in July — its generative-AI service filing was listed by the Cyberspace Administration of China on July 15 — and on September 29 it obtained permits allowing recent iPhones to support Apple Intelligence and Alibaba AI models. Today's MIIT network-access permit updates were the final piece.

Will it move the needle?

Bloomberg is openly skeptical: with Huawei, Xiaomi, Oppo, and Vivo already shipping AI-first phones, it's unclear how much Apple benefits from launching a two-year-old set of relatively basic AI features. Apple's sales in China have held up despite the missing AI, so the rollout may matter more as a checkbox than a differentiator.

Apple's regulatory headaches aren't limited to China, either. The day before the Bloomberg report, Apple noted in the footnotes of its HomeView announcement that it is “working constructively with the EU in an attempt to find a path forward” to launch Siri AI in Europe, where the assistant also remains unavailable.
